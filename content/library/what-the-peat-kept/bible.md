# What the Peat Kept: Story Bible

## Logline and premise

Logline: When a hand-cut bank on an Irish bog gives up a hanged man who looks two thousand years old, a farming family learns that their land, their neighbours' silence and their grandmother's old promise were all bought with his death, and that the bog is owed one more payment.

In order: Monday 7 October 1974, Mattie Dolan's sleán strikes a naked, tanned, perfectly kept man four feet down in the Lalor bank, a rope round his neck and a twist of white hair in his fist. His mother Honor orders him to cover the body; he doesn't. The guards and a museum archaeologist, Dr Ronan Kielty, say he is Iron Age, and since the museum cannot collect for weeks, the body must be kept wet and dark in a cattle trough in the Dolans' cowshed. Small wrongs begin: black water floods Eamon Brophy's dry field; Eamon sleepwalks to the bank; a face shows in the turf stack; nine-year-old Nuala says "the Guest" has asked her to keep him company.

Truth: the man is Laurence Cashin, 41, a Moate cattle jobber and money-lender, killed 18 October 1922. He held notes on all seven Derrybawn families. Honor, 26, lured him to the Kitchen at dusk with word that the men would pay in cash. Tom Gorman, Jim Brophy, Mick Dolan (Honor's husband) and the young Hession brothers meant to frighten him into burning the notes. Mick tied the halter knot, Jim struck the horse, Cashin died. His fist closed on Tom's hair. They burned the notes, took £86 from his belt, burned his clothes and sank him in the deep cut of what became the Lalor bank. Peg Gorman, 11, watched from the heather. Honor, wrecked by it, invented the Terms (below) from her grandmother's lore of "minding" a drowned stranger, and swore to them. Canon Mulvey heard all seven in confession and made the vigil their penance, passing it off as custom. Honor swore on the first night that when she died "one of mine" would mind him after her. That vow is the debt.

In 1973 the vigil lapsed (Brophy's year; Annie Brophy was ill). The Board then drained near the bank, and Eamon signed an option to sell the seven families' rights. Honor, who never told Mattie the Lalor bank was dead ground, let him cut it, half hoping to be found out.

Ending: the knot, the rope's factory yarn and Peg's memory expose the murder. Eamon dies in the Kitchen. On All Souls' Eve Honor, dying anyway, chooses to stay with Laurence, and the bog keeps her. Her choice, not any child's, settles the debt. Laurence is buried in Kilbride with his name and Nuala's lock of hair. The Dolans give up their turbary. A year on, Nuala keeps the vigil's lamp and hears a step in the fog.

## World and setting

Derrybawn townland, County Westmeath, on the edge of a raised bog of about 600 acres. Seven houses along the bog road, each with a turf bank: Dolan, Gorman, Brophy, Hession, Keegan, Scally and Lalor (empty since 1951, rented to Kielty). Kilbride village (church, school, Garda barracks, creamery) is two miles by road; Moate eleven; Dublin seventy by bus. The only telephones: Hession's shop-and-pub and the barracks. Radio Éireann at six for the Angelus; the church bell carries over the bog. The Board (the national peat board) works the far east edge, 1.5 miles off, with milling machines, drains and a narrow-gauge railway. The summer of 1974 was wet, so the Dolans' cutting ran late.

The bog: sphagnum, heather, bog cotton gone to seed, bog oak and bog deal black in the cuttings, brown water, pale mist at dawn. At its middle stands the Island, a heather knoll, and beside it the Kitchen: a horseshoe mound and trough, a prehistoric cooking-place, with one leaning rowan. The old togher runs under the heather towards it.

Rules of the haunting. The bog keeps whatever is willingly given, and keeps it for ever; the ancient dead of the Kitchen sleep warm. It holds what is taken by force, with a grievance, and that cannot settle until someone chooses to stay with it. Laurence is not evil: he is lonely, unnamed, unburied. He acts only by water, wet boots, faces in peat, sleep and Nuala's ear, never before outsiders. Costs: whoever kept the vigil comes home with a white streak in the hair; the Terms, once broken, make the bog take back; a willing keeper does not come back.

The Terms (Honor's, 1922): each All Souls' Eve one house sits the night at the Kitchen by lot, leaving a lit candle and a turned sod and saying his name at midnight; the seven never sell, drain or cut his ground. Locals call it "minding the Guest" and never say who he was.

Glossary: sleán (turf spade); spit (one cut depth); bank (cutting allotment); the Kitchen; the Island; togher (ancient bog road); the Board; footing (stacking turf to dry); clamp (big turf stack at home); wheeling the barrow; bog deal; the lot (sods marked, drawn on All Souls' Eve); minding the Guest; the Terms; the brack (Hallowe'en barmbrack); the stations (Mass in houses); Hession's (shop and pub); the Angelus; the Renault (Kielty's red Renault 4); the Lalor cottage; the Canon's letter; the trough (galvanised cattle trough); rogue's yarn (coloured strand marking a rope's maker); halter knot (the Dolan double-turn bowline).

## Cast

Mattie Dolan (46), "Mattie". Broad, stooped, a missing top joint on the left forefinger, sandy brows. Speaks slow, under-states, answers questions with questions; habit: "Well now." Wants to keep the farm and be a good son and father; fears his own anger; hides nothing until he learns what he is standing on. Arc: from obedient son to the man who lets his mother go.

Brid Dolan (41), née Cassidy of Kilbride. Small, quick, grey eyes, red raw knuckles; the parish's laying-out woman. Fast, practical, funny: "I'll say this once and then I'll say it again." Wants her children safe; fears Nuala slipping away; hides that she isn't sure she believes anything. Arc: from fixing to bearing.

Tadhg Dolan (17). Tall, bitten nails, a cowlick he fights, Mattie's jaw. Teenage deadpan, rhythm of someone rehearsing sarcasm; "Grand" meaning the opposite. Wants out (Board job, then England); fears becoming his father; hides he sleeps in the shed to guard Nuala. Arc: stays by choice.

Nuala Dolan (9). Fair plait, gap in front teeth, one wellington with a patch, brown eyes. Direct, literal, polite to the dead. Wants to be useful; fears being left; hides her talks with the Guest. Arc: carries the grief and the lamp.

Honor Dolan (78), "Granny", "Mrs Dolan". White hair, hands knotted like roots, black coat green at the seams, a blood-flecked handkerchief she hides. Sharp, formal, short sentences; "We'll see." Wants Laurence covered and her own account closed; fears being found out and not being forgiven; hides everything. Arc: concealment to payment.

Peg Gorman (63), "Miss Gorman". Thin, tall, a bad hip, mannish cardigan, hair dyed black, left unmarried to mind her father. Dry, clipped, fond of asking pointed questions; "I won't pretend." Wants to tell before she dies; fears Honor and her own cowardice; hides that she saw. Arc: testimony.

Eamon Brophy (58), "Eamon". Red face, big moustache stained with tobacco, heavy gold watch. Loud, bluff, deflects with jokes. Wants the Board's money for his son in Birmingham; fears the old thing; hides the option. Arc: dissolves; drowns in the Kitchen.

Fr Colm Heffernan (55), "Father". Round, gentle, bald, thick glasses, shaking left hand. Careful, kindly, speaks in pastoral half-sentences; habit: wipes his glasses. Wants his parish whole; fears being asked for something he can't give; hides the Canon's sealed letter. Arc: from tolerating a custom to standing at a graveside.

Sgt Noel Quirke (44), "the Sergeant". Lean, drooping eyelid, pipe never lit. Patient, dry, courteous interrogator; "Humour me." Wants a quiet pension; fears starting what he can't finish; hides that his own grandfather was in the 1922 Free State army. Arc: chooses mercy over file.

Dr Ronan Kielty (34), "the Doctor". Curly red hair, tweed too thin, spectacles taped at the hinge. Fast, excitable, apologises mid-sentence. Wants the find; fears his superiors; hides ambition behind sympathy. Arc: gives up the exhibit.

Gerry Linnane (38), Board foreman. Neat, moustache, clipboard, Offaly accent; pleasant, relentless. Wants the drain finished.

## Voice guide

Close third, past tense, one rotating viewpoint per chapter (Mattie, Brid, Tadhg, Nuala, Honor, Peg), each with their own register: Mattie in work and weather; Brid in tasks and bodies; Tadhg in sarcasm; Nuala literal and sensory; Honor formal and withheld; Peg precise and wry. Plain midlands English: "himself", "sure", "grand", "I'm after telling you", "so it is". Double quotation marks for speech, single for quotes within. Few Irish words, always correct. Mixed sentence lengths, concrete nouns, dry understatement; people joke at wakes and in fear. Dread comes from implication: wet boots, a counted number, a smell. Dialogue is a third to a half of most chapters. No cheap jumps.

Sample (not part of the novel): The turf came away in wet black loaves, and Mattie Dolan threw each one up to Tadhg without looking, the way a man hands tools to a son he has stopped expecting to thank him. It was the second week of October and the sun had no interest in the job. Out on the bog the heather had gone the colour of a bruise, and the Board's engine grumbled along the far line, a mile and a half off, like a man clearing his throat before an unwelcome announcement. Mattie put the sleán in for the fourth spit. It went in sweet, and then it stopped, and what it stopped on was not wood.

Never write: (1) characters explaining history to people who know it; (2) "suddenly", exclamation marks or screams tearing the night; (3) American idiom (gotten, mom, sidewalk); (4) stage-Irish (begorrah, leprechaun, shamrock, phonetic brogue); (5) therapy language (closure, trauma, boundaries); (6) "ancient evil", "curse" or "cursed" as explanation; (7) lists of feelings ("she felt fear, sorrow"); (8) a scene closing on an aphorism or a joke explained.

## Motifs

Black water running where it should not. Hands and boots stained brown that won't wash. Counting: seven houses, seven sods, seven candles, always one more or fewer. The Angelus bell across the bog. Hair: Tom's white twist, Nuala's plait, the streak of the keepers. Rope and the halter knot. Faces in peat and turf. Tea and the kitchen table as the family's court.

## Timeline and ground rules

1896 Honor born. 1911 Peg born. 1916 Eamon born. 1919 Heffernan born. 1920 Honor marries Mick Dolan. 18 Oct 1922 Cashin killed. 1 Nov 1922 first vigil (Honor), Canon Mulvey receives the seven in confession. 1928 Mattie born. 1951 Mick dies, Lalors leave, Dolans take their bank. 1957 Tadhg born. 1965 Nuala born. 1966 Heffernan arrives; Mulvey died 1965, leaving a sealed letter. 1973 vigil missed; Board drains. 1974 the story.

Distances: Dolan house to Lalor bank 800 yards by the bog lane; bank to the Kitchen 400 yards along the togher; Dolans to Kilbride 2 miles; the Board's edge 1.5 miles. Dr Kielty's licence from the Minister and the museum's conservator will not come before Saturday 2 November. Carbon dating comes back after the funeral. A bog body kept in a trough must be wet, dark and cool.

Who knows what: Honor, Peg and the two Hessions know everything; Annie Brophy and Eamon know only the Terms and that "something is owed"; Heffernan knows the vigil is penance; Quirke learns the truth from Peg in chapter 17; Mattie learns in chapter 15; Brid guesses by chapter 12. Weather: dry and cold after the wet summer, ground frost by 29 October.
