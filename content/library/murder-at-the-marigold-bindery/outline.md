# Outline: Murder at the Marigold Bindery

All chapters are narrated by Dot, first person, past tense.

**Solution.** Morwenna Hext killed Aubrey with the backing hammer at about 10:45pm on Thursday 13 July. His pencilled notes on the pastedowns showed the 1989 sewing was left-handed and ended "Mrs P. in the mor[ning]", which would expose her theft of the Carne Rutter. She burned the pastedowns, took his key and locked up. **Clues and where planted:** Quire settles in her lap (2, recast 25); left-turning kettle stitch, Ned's, copied by a pupil (3); clove-paste smell on the board hinges (3, 6); unexplained feeder of Quire and a sardine tin (2, 8, 25); hammer hung face-out, Ned's left-handed way (1, 6); beeswax on the thread, from her hives, seen at her honey stall (4, 6); charred scrap "K. – cat. 114" and "Mrs P. in the mor" (9); stolen notebook, kind saucer of milk (7, 8); "smelled like church" (10); she pours left-handed (11); she claims not to have set foot in the press room since 1986 (2), yet says the strip light over the nipping press, fitted in 1988, gave her a headache "when I stuck my head in at Easter" (12); Aubrey's honest letter (13); quiz sheet shows she left at 9:50 (15); Tamsin's copied pupil list and Regatta Book spec (20); Loveday's false alibi broken by Jago (21); Pieter's "honey" (23). **Red herrings:** Dutch paper and Pieter; "K." pointing at Kit or Constable Kitto; Nance's candles and Psalter; Jago's wet clothes and silence; Imogen's quarrel and inheritance; Loveday's debts. **Limits:** nobody binds well without years of practice, so the suspect list is short; Dot's proof is craft and testimony, never a villain's speech.

## Chapter 01: The Wrong Nail

Dot. Friday 14 July, 6:50am, the Bindery press room. **Purpose:** hook with the body and the first clue. **Beats:** (1) Dot climbs Fish Cellar Lane with a bag of buns to set the paste before the book fair; the yellow door is locked and Quire is not at the window. (2) Inside she finds Aubrey Trevithick on the floor between the nipping press and the bench, his hat beside him, and the stove warm to her palm in July. (3) The Carne Rutter lies half-unsewn on the bench, its endpapers torn out, thread trailing from the gatherings like roots. (4) She touches nothing but the kettle, rings 999 from the shop telephone and gets Constable Demelza Kitto, who has toast in her mouth. (5) While she waits she notices the backing hammer hangs face-out on its nail, the way Ned hung it and she never has. (6) She remembers the Thursday quiz, and that she walked past this window at eleven last night and saw only dark. **Closing:** the hammer swinging a quarter-inch on its nail, as if someone had only just let go. Plants: warm stove, hammer, key, 11pm.

## Chapter 02: Saffron Cake and Sympathy

Dot. Friday morning, shop and lane. **Purpose:** introduce the village and the suspects; plant the cat. **Beats:** (1) Demelza keeps the lane clear and asks who has keys; Dot admits she lent Aubrey the spare at a quarter to six the evening before, to be rid of him. (2) Kit Pellow, Ned's nephew, arrives unshaven, apologises to everyone, and Demelza turns brisk to hide what she feels about her ex. (3) Detective Inspector Garrow arrives from Truro, calls Dot "the lady of the house", and is made to wait for tea. (4) Morwenna Hext arrives with a saffron cake and takes over the teapot; Quire, who has hissed at constable, inspector and Kit, climbs into Morwenna's lap like a bun into a tin. (5) Morwenna says she has not set foot in the press room since Ned died and hardly comes up the lane now. (6) Tamsin Bray watches from the net-loft steps, then vanishes. **Closing:** Morwenna says, "He always did choose the worst places to be right," and Dot keeps the sentence in her head without knowing why. Dot mentions Quire turned up fat the month Ned died, and that she never worked out who fed her in the weeks Dot forgot. Plants: cat, "not since Ned died", the unexplained feeder.

## Chapter 03: What the Spine Remembers

Dot. Friday, 10am, press room. **Purpose:** inciting incident; Dot reads the book and realises the fraud. **Beats:** (1) Demelza gives Dot ten minutes alone with the rutter before the Truro forensic man bags it. (2) The sewing holes in the folds do not match the stations Ned punched in 1971: the leaves were sewn into this cover by a different hand. (3) At the foot of the last gathering she finds Ned's pin-prick dot and has to sit down on the cutting-board stool. (4) The middle leaves are 1760s laid paper, chain lines too close; only the first two and last two leaves are 1631 rag. (5) The kettle stitches at head and tail turn left, as Ned's did: whoever sewed it was taught by him. The board hinges smell of clove: Ned's paste, a Christmas smell in a room with a dead man. (6) Garrow takes the book, tells her not to leave the village, and calls her "Mrs Pellow, dear". **Closing:** "Someone sewed that book the way my husband taught, with my husband's paste," Dot says, and Demelza, not looking up, answers, "Then someone's been in your shop." (Dot wrongly assumes the work was done at the Bindery.) Plants: Dutch-looking paper, clove, dot.

## Chapter 04: Bunting and Bargains

Dot. Friday afternoon, St Petroc's church hall. **Purpose:** gather the suspects at the book fair. **Beats:** (1) The fair opens an hour late without its chairman; the Reverend Nance reads a prayer and apologises to a congregation of dealers. (2) Pieter de Vries studies a stall of maps with elaborate calm and tells Dot the 1760s paper is probably Dutch, which sounds like an accusation. (3) Morwenna sells honey and beeswax candles for the lifeboat, says she is minding the Regatta Book Ned bound in 1964 and has mended its hinge herself, and presses a jar on Dot. (4) Jago Rowe's regatta bunting falls on Garrow's head. (5) Aubrey's daughter Imogen Mayne arrives in black leather, takes charge of the chairman's cash box and snaps at Kit. (6) Loveday Penhallow, hearing aid whistling, orders Dot to find out who did it. **Closing:** Loveday's hand on Dot's sleeve: "Aubrey valued my library as far as the Carne, and not one inch past it." Pieter buys a candle from Morwenna and will not meet her eye. Kit hands Loveday his typed valuation list, the rutter numbered 114. Plants: beeswax, the Regatta Book, cat. 114.

## Chapter 05: The Quarrel at the Lantern

Dot. Friday evening, The Drowned Lantern. **Purpose:** reconstruct Thursday night; set alibis. **Beats:** (1) Dot walks into the Lantern for the first time since the quiz; Jago serves her in silence and a darts team stops mid-throw. (2) She and the regulars piece the evening together: Quire Straits placed fourth, Morwenna's Jam Tarts at the next table, noise, chips. (3) Several people describe Aubrey and Imogen's row at 8:20, all about money and her mother. (4) Jago admits Aubrey used the payphone at 8:40, said "Half ten, then" to someone he did not name, and left at ten to nine. (5) Nance comes in for his single sherry and cannot say where he was at half past nine. (6) Dot realises only she, Kit and Aubrey knew he had the key. **Closing:** Jago wipes the same glass for a full minute and says, "Leave it, Dot." She does not. Image: the Lantern's ceiling hung with glass fishing floats, one lamp inside each, a drowned lantern for every drowned man. Comedy: the regulars argue whether Quire Straits' fourth place was robbery. Dot notes who left when and writes it on a beer mat.

## Chapter 06: Paste, Thread and Wax

Dot. Saturday morning, press room (reopened after the forensic search). **Purpose:** the craft evidence. **Beats:** (1) Dot makes up Ned's paste with Kit, wheat starch, alum, one drop of clove oil, and the smell matches the hinge. (2) Under her loupe she finds beeswax on the surviving sewing thread; she has only ever used tallow. (3) She tells Kit the hammer hung Ned's way, face-out, because Ned was left-handed. (4) Kit says only he, Dot and Jago ever worked beside Ned, goes pale, and drops a bone folder. (5) Tamsin, hiding behind the shop door, offers to sweep, and Dot says no, then yes. (6) Dot mentions aloud to Kit that Ned kept a notebook of every pupil and job in the locked cupboard (she lent it to Tamsin in May and never read it herself); Morwenna, calling from the shop doorway with saffron buns, hears it. **Closing:** Dot locks the cupboard and takes the key home on its bootlace, feeling foolish and not foolish enough. Plants: wax, notebook, left hand.

## Chapter 07: Somebody Else's Key

Dot. Saturday evening and night. **Purpose:** second crime. **Beats:** (1) The fair's Saturday auction: comedy over a stuffed otter in a glass case, Pieter bidding with ghastly calm against a woman who wants it for a hat stand. (2) Nance asks Dot, too lightly, whether stolen books can be bought back. (3) Dot walks home at midnight past the Bindery and sees the press-room window forced. (4) Quire is shut in the paste cupboard with a saucer of milk and is furious. (5) The cupboard lock is unbroken but the notebook is gone; someone had a second key, and Dot's is still on her bootlace. (6) Demelza arrives in pyjama bottoms under her coat. **Closing:** the saucer, one of Dot's own blue-rimmed set, with a teaspoon laid neatly beside it. Plants: kind to the cat; a second cupboard key (Ned's, kept by Morwenna since 1958, revealed 25). Image: the otter, glass eyes blazing under the hall lights. Dot tells nobody about the key on her bootlace, and that choice matters later. Turn: the Bindery is no longer a crime scene but a place someone enters at will.

## Chapter 08: Quire Gives Evidence

Dot. Sunday, shop and church hall. **Purpose:** complications between allies. **Beats:** (1) Demelza and Dot argue over the notebook: Dot should have told her it existed, and Demelza has had to lie to Garrow. (2) Garrow scoffs and suggests Dot staged the burglary for sympathy. (3) Kit's alibi for Saturday night rests on a darts match nobody remembers. (4) Dot reasons that the thief cared more about the notebook than the till, knew the cupboard had a second key, and was kind to a cat. (5) At the fair's Sunday close Pieter, ordered by Garrow to stay in Trevenna, sulks over his packed trunk in the Lantern's back room. (6) Dot, exhausted, falls asleep in the shop chair and wakes with Quire on her knee. **Closing:** Demelza says, "If you find something, you tell me first," and Dot, who has never promised anything lightly, promises. Texture: Sunday service at St Petroc's, the choir flat, Garrow in a back pew with a notebook. Dot finds a sardine tin licked clean behind the dustbin and does not know why it nags her. Dot realises Morwenna's saffron cake was left on her step at dawn, as every crisis, and eats it standing up, tearful and furious.

## Chapter 09: Ash in the Stove

Dot. Monday morning, press room. **Purpose:** find the burned endpapers. **Beats:** (1) Dot rakes the press-room stove, which the police ignored because it was July. (2) A charred fragment of pastedown survives under the grate. (3) Aubrey's pencil is legible under the lamp: "K. – cat. 114 –" and, lower, "Mrs P. in the mor". (4) She realises Aubrey meant to show her the book on Friday morning and was waiting for her to come. (5) She brings it to Demelza, who sees "K." and goes still: Kit, or Kitto? (6) Kit and Demelza meet in Dot's shop, their first real conversation in a year, savage and short. **Closing:** Dot folds the scrap into Ned's tin of bone folders and says, aloud, "He was waiting for me." Plants: "K.", "Mrs P." Texture: the dust smell of cold ash in July, the lamp tilted so the pencil shows silver. Dot reasons that the killer burned the endpapers, not the whole book, because the book was worth money and the notes were not. Payoff of ch 1's warm stove.

## Chapter 10: Smelled Like Church

Dot. Monday afternoon, the net loft. **Purpose:** an eyewitness appears. **Beats:** (1) Dot climbs the outside steps and finds Tamsin's sleeping bag, torch, and exercise books full of drawn book-spines. (2) Tamsin admits she watched the lane on Thursday night from the loft window. (3) She saw Nance at 9:30 pleading at the door, then Imogen at 10:05, who left at 10:20 wiping her face. (4) At 10:30 a hooded woman in a mac went in without knocking. Tamsin only smelled her as she passed below: "like church. Polish, candles." (5) Dot insists Tamsin tell Demelza; Tamsin refuses, afraid her mother will learn she sleeps out. (6) They bargain: Dot will speak to Sheila Bray herself. **Closing:** Tamsin says, "Can I watch you do corners next?" and Dot says yes before she has thought about it. Red herring: church smell, which fits Nance's vestry as well as every churchwoman. Image: a torch beam through the loft's salt-stiff nets and a blue exercise-book drawing of a sewing frame. Comedy: Tamsin sulks eloquently and eats Dot's last bun. Turn: Dot now has a timeline and a smell, and Nance and Imogen both have explaining to do.

## Chapter 11: Tea at Penhallow Court

Dot. Tuesday afternoon, Penhallow Court. **Purpose:** the big house and its secrets. **Beats:** (1) Dot walks up with Morwenna, who has WI business with Loveday and a tin of shortbread. (2) The library: gaps on the shelves, tide-marks of damp, Loveday's pride in her ancestors. (3) Loveday tells the story of the Carne pilot drowned off the Longships in 1631, who left his book. (4) Morwenna pours the tea left-handed, then corrects herself. (5) Dot glimpses a pair of size-twelve boots in the back passage; Loveday says they are the gardener's. (6) Loveday volunteers that Morwenna sat up with her until midnight on Thursday. **Closing:** the hall clock strikes eleven minutes after Dot's watch, and nobody in the house seems to mind. Plants: left hand, alibi, Jago. Image: a library with a bucket under every third shelf, rain in July. Comedy: Loveday's hearing aid whistles whenever anyone lies, which is often. Morwenna handles a first edition like a woman who has handled many. Plant: Morwenna visits weekly to read to Loveday and has the run of the shelves.

## Chapter 12: The Strip Light

Dot. Tuesday evening, WI hall. **Purpose:** comic set piece with the killer's slip. **Beats:** (1) The regatta bunting committee: eight women, three sewing machines, one scandal and a quarrel about triangles. (2) Morwenna passes round the Regatta Book to admire her hinge repair; Dot weighs it and thinks it heavy for a ledger. (3) Imogen wanders in; the committee closes ranks, then pounces with questions and sandwiches. (4) Morwenna, complaining of headaches, says the fluorescent strip over the Bindery nipping press gave her one "when I stuck my head in at Easter". (5) Dot, laughing at the tea urn, registers nothing. (6) Nance and Jago quarrel over who will carry the Book to the finish line, and Dot slips her pocket gauge onto its boards as a joke: 3.5mm. **Closing:** the bunting falls in a heap, and Morwenna says, "That's the third time this week things have come apart." Plants: strip light slip, board thickness.

## Chapter 13: The Honest Man

Dot. Wednesday 19 July, morning, shop. **Purpose:** midpoint reversal. **Beats:** (1) Imogen comes to the Bindery with a letter she found sealed and unposted in the lining of Aubrey's case while packing his things. (2) It is addressed to Loveday's solicitor, dated Wednesday 12 July: "The Carne Rutter is not what it seems." He will not put it to auction. (3) So Aubrey was not a crook who fell out with a partner; he was the detective, and honest. (4) Dot revises everything: the forger knew Aubrey knew, and killed to stop the word spreading. (5) Imogen confesses she was at the Bindery at 10:05 and left him alive, furious. (6) She asks Dot to help her bury him properly, not as the fraudster Garrow thinks he was. **Closing:** Dot writes "Who knew Aubrey knew?" inside the cover of Ned's last order book and underlines it twice. Image: the letter, in Aubrey's fountain pen, a coffee ring on the corner. Dot is ashamed of every unkind thing she thought about him. Imogen hands over Aubrey's signet ring and Dot sets it on the paste shelf beside the Pellow dot tin.

## Chapter 14: Question Time

Dot. Thursday 20 July, 8pm, The Drowned Lantern. **Purpose:** quiz comedy, rising pressure. **Beats:** (1) Quire Straits (Dot, Kit, Tamsin, Nance) face the Jam Tarts and the Lantern Regulars across a sea of crisps. (2) Round one, general knowledge: Nance knows every saint and no footballer. (3) Round two, book trivia: the last set Aubrey wrote, which Jago reads out through a lump in his throat while the pub stands. (4) Tamsin and Morwenna argue over a bookbinding term and both turn out to be right. (5) The music round: Morwenna's team does well, then badly. (6) Pieter appears at the door as the tiebreak is read and stands there as if undecided, looking at the jar of Morwenna's honey on the bar. Jago announces a lock-in. **Closing:** the tiebreak is "Name the Trevenna lifeboat lost in 1910," and nobody in the room knows. Comedy: Kit's answer sheet becomes a love letter to Demelza, who is at the next table as a spectator. Nance and Tamsin form an unlikely alliance against the Regulars. Dot watches who laughs a beat late. Plant: Morwenna's captain's script is quick and neat.

## Chapter 15: The Music Round

Dot. Thursday night, the Lantern. **Purpose:** break Morwenna's alibi, partly. **Beats:** (1) Jago lets Dot sort his biscuit tin of old answer sheets. (2) Last Thursday's Jam Tarts sheet is in two hands; the captain's neat script ends at the music round. (3) Dot asks Morwenna, lightly, and she says she left at half past ten to sit with Loveday. (4) But the sheet shows she left at 9:50. (5) Morwenna smiles, says her memory is not what it was, and offers cake, and Dot, who loves her, lets it go. (6) Pieter, who has watched Morwenna all evening, slips out at ten to eleven with his collar up, to meet someone by the quay. **Closing:** from the quay, a cry and a splash. Texture: the biscuit tin of Huntley and Palmers, forty-one weeks of Thursdays, ringed with beer. Each round on the sheet is timed in the captain's hand, and the music round closed at 9:50, confirmed by when Jago switched off the jukebox. Cost: she lies to Demelza for one more day.

## Chapter 16: Man in the Water

Dot. Thursday, 11pm, the Hard. **Purpose:** third crime and action set piece. **Beats:** (1) Dot runs down Pilchard Steps; Jago, already in the water to his chest, is hauling at something pale. (2) Pieter floats face-down and they drag him between a moored boat and the wall, scraping the quay. (3) Dot, who learned first aid in the Girl Guides in 1943, works on him beside the lobster pots. (4) The ambulance takes forty minutes; Demelza arrives first and takes over. (5) Jago, soaked, will not say what he was doing near the harbour. (6) Pieter's pockets hold a hotel key, a Dutch paperback and a folded leaf of old paper, which Dot slips into her cardigan before Garrow comes. **Closing:** the leaf, in a careful seventeenth-century hand, "Cape Cornwall to the Longships", folded in four. Image: the tide a young flood over the mud, green weed on the quay steps, a gull on Pieter's trunk. Jago says nothing, only breathes. Dot's knees give way afterwards on Pilchard Steps, and a stranger's hand under her elbow turns out to be Nance's.

## Chapter 17: Wet Boots

Dot. Friday 21 July, morning, shop and police station. **Purpose:** suspicion shifts. **Beats:** (1) Garrow holds Jago on suspicion of attacking the Dutchman. (2) Dot realises the folded page is one of the sixty missing leaves. (3) She shows it to Demelza, who takes it as an off-books lead. (4) Under a lamp the ink and rag match the rutter's first leaf, not the day-book. (5) Garrow declares Pieter the forger who fell out with a partner, Jago or Kit. (6) Loveday telephones to say Jago is her man of business, which no one has ever believed. **Closing:** Demelza holds the receiver away from her ear and says, "Is she lying, or is she in love?" Texture: a grey police station with a ticking clock and a plastic ficus. Dot lies, sleepless, about where she found the leaf, to protect Demelza. Garrow's theory is plausible, which is the problem. Turn: the case is now officially about Pieter.

## Chapter 18: Kit's Mistake

Dot. Friday afternoon, shop. **Purpose:** the descent begins. **Beats:** (1) Kit confesses he flagged the rutter's paper to Aubrey on Wednesday and was told to keep quiet. (2) He was also at the Bindery on Thursday at six to return a book and left prints on the bench. (3) The "K." in the burned scrap could be him. (4) Dot admits she has held the scrap since Monday, and Demelza, who hid it from Garrow, is compromised. (5) Garrow arrives with a warrant. (6) Kit asks Dot to feed Quire. **Closing:** the police car's tail-lights going down Fish Cellar Lane while Quire sits on the step and watches. Image: a warrant read aloud in the shop, over the paste pots. Dot recalls Ned teaching Kit to sew at ten years old, sitting on a cushion. Demelza, standing in the doorway with her notebook, doesn't look at either of them. Turn: the one person Dot would have trusted is gone.

## Chapter 19: Nothing Left on the Bench

Dot. Friday evening, press room and lane. **Purpose:** the low point. **Beats:** (1) Garrow seals the Bindery for a second sweep and Dot is shut out of her own bench. (2) The fair's last stalls pack up in the church hall; the village avoids her eye. (3) Demelza is reprimanded and takes her warrant card out in front of Dot, then puts it back. (4) Dot sits on the front step among the paste pots and counts what she has: a dead husband's mark, a burned scrap, a cat. (5) She remembers walking past the dark window at eleven on Thursday and not looking in. (6) Tamsin brings tea and says nothing for twenty minutes. **Closing:** Tamsin says, "I've got a copy of Ned's notebook. Some of it." Texture: the lane at dusk, children playing sardines on the Hard as if nothing had happened. Dot's despair is practical: the rent, the commissions, the cat's tin. Image: the Bindery's yellow door with a police seal across it like a plaster on a graze.

## Chapter 20: Tamsin's Exercise Book

Dot. Friday night, Dot's kitchen. **Purpose:** the key document. **Beats:** (1) Tamsin explains she copied two pages in May for a school project on old trades. (2) Page one: Ned's pupils list, 1955-58: "M. Hext (left-handed, forced right at school), best I've had." (3) Page two: the specification of the Regatta Book, boards 2mm, half calf. (4) Dot refuses to believe it of Morwenna, who held her hand at Ned's funeral. (5) Tamsin lists what the hooded woman smelled of, who poured left-handed, whom the cat chose. (6) Dot, cold, reads the pupil entry aloud twice. **Closing:** "Morwenna Hext was a better binder than I am," Dot says, and the kettle boils over. Comedy: Tamsin's handwriting is better than Dot's and she knows it. The exercise book carries a drawing of Ned's sewing frame with notes in brackets. Dot remembers the funeral, Morwenna holding her hand through the hymn. Turn: Dot stops asking who and starts asking how.

## Chapter 21: What Jago Couldn't Say

Dot. Friday late night, Jago's kitchen and Penhallow Court. **Purpose:** break Loveday's alibi. **Beats:** (1) Demelza releases Jago for lack of evidence. (2) Dot confronts him: he has spent every Thursday night for three years at Penhallow Court, and the boots were his. (3) He was there from 9:30 till midnight on 13 July, drinking tea with Loveday, a love the village never forgave in 1956. (4) Dot walks up to Penhallow in the dark. (5) Loveday admits Morwenna asked her to say she was with her, and she did, out of friendship and shame. (6) She says Morwenna had "borrowed" small things from the library for years. **Closing:** Loveday, in nightdress and gumboots, asks, "Will they put her in prison?" and does not wait for an answer. Image: Jago's kitchen with the lobster pots stacked to the ceiling, an old photograph of Loveday at nineteen on a horse. Comedy: Loveday's gumboots on the wrong feet. Jago's alibi destroys Morwenna's and exposes his own love. Payoff: size-twelve boots, ch 11.

## Chapter 22: Measuring the Boards

Dot. Saturday 22 July, 5am, Dot's kitchen. **Purpose:** plan the trap. **Beats:** (1) Dot, Demelza, Imogen and Tamsin sit round the table with the exercise book. (2) The spec says the Regatta Book's boards should be 2mm; Dot measured them on Tuesday at 3.5mm. (3) Twenty thin leaves could lie in that gap. (4) Demelza cannot get a warrant on this, and Garrow will not hear it. (5) Dot proposes the prize-giving: Morwenna will present the Book, and Dot will ask to see it in public. (6) Imogen brings her father's pencilled list of the Penhallow shelves, which Garrow cannot ignore. **Closing:** Dot lays out her dividers and bone folder like a woman setting a table for a guest who will not leave. Texture: dawn, the harbour already hung with bunting, tea going cold. Tamsin does the arithmetic aloud. Demelza argues, then folds. The plan is Dot's but the timing is Tamsin's. Cost: if they are wrong, Dot will lose the Bindery's good name for good.

## Chapter 23: Regatta Morning

Dot. Saturday, 8am to 2pm, harbour (low water 10:40, gig race at noon). **Purpose:** the big public set piece. **Beats:** (1) Bunting, stalls, a brass band, gigs on the water; Jago back at the starting gun. (2) Demelza calls from the Truro hospital: Pieter has spoken, one phrase, "the lady with honey". (3) Loveday arrives in a bath chair pushed by Nance. (4) Morwenna, in her WI sash, arranges the prize table with the Regatta Book. (5) Garrow agrees only to stand at the back. (6) The gig race: Trevenna wins by a length and the harbour roars. **Closing:** at the finish gun Dot looks at Morwenna and finds she is already looking back. Image: gig crews in white jerseys, oars shipped, the harbour a mosaic of flags. Comedy: the brass band plays three different tunes and ends on the right one. Dot watches Morwenna press a sandwich on the Reverend. Turn: Pieter's phrase confirms what Dot has already begun to dread.

## Chapter 24: The Tea Tent

Dot. Saturday, 3pm, tea tent. **Purpose:** the reveal. **Beats:** (1) Dot asks to see the Regatta Book in front of forty people. (2) Morwenna hands it over smiling and offers cake. (3) Dot lays out the clues in order: hammer, left-turning stitch, paste, wax, a cupboard key only a pupil would have kept, saucer, strip light, and Kit's catalogue number 114, which is all "K." ever meant. (4) She measures the boards with her dividers; Morwenna says Ned bound them thick. (5) Dot reads Ned's pupil line aloud. (6) Quire, uninvited, jumps into Morwenna's lap again and stays. **Closing:** Morwenna lifts the cat gently to the grass, takes the Regatta Book and walks out of the tent. Image: the tea tent, wet canvas, a long table with the Regatta Book among the scones. Dot never raises her voice. Garrow steps forward at the dividers. Quire's second lap-sitting recasts the cat scene of ch 2. Turn: the village sees Dot for the first time in three years. Comedy: Garrow tries to stop the sandwiches being cleared. Dot's hands shake and she steadies them on the table edge like a woman at a bench. Morwenna answers every point with courtesy, which is worse. Cost: Dot must say aloud she loved her.

## Chapter 25: High Water on the Slip

Dot. Saturday, 4pm, the lifeboat Slip. **Purpose:** confrontation and confession. **Beats:** (1) Dot follows Morwenna down to the lifeboat slipway. (2) Morwenna sits on the rail with the Book on her knees. (3) She tells it in order: Ronald, the leaves, Aubrey, the hammer, Pieter on the quay. (4) She admits she kept Ned's cupboard key since 1958, and fed Quire sardines for three months. (5) She says she loved Ned and Dot did not need him as she did. (6) She admits she heard Dot pass the dark window at eleven and held her breath with Aubrey at her feet. She flings the Book into the high water; Jago, in the gig, hauls it dripping out. **Closing:** laid on the slipway, the boards split and twenty leaves of a 1631 rutter spread like a hand of cards in the sun. Image: high water lapping the Slip, streaked green, the lifeboat doors shut. Morwenna does not weep. She answers every question in the same level voice, like someone reading out her own minutes. Payoff: the key, the saucer, the sardines, the strip light, the dot. Cost: she tried to kill Pieter.

## Chapter 26: A Second Dot

Dot. Late July, the Bindery. **Purpose:** emotional resolution. **Beats:** (1) Kit is released; Demelza visits the shop, then Kit. (2) Pieter, recovering, hands back his forty leaves. (3) Loveday sells the library openly and keeps the rutter. (4) Dot rebinds the book with Tamsin pasting at her side. (5) She makes a pin-prick beside Ned's. (6) Quire sits on Tamsin's lap. **Closing:** Dot hangs the backing hammer on its nail, her way, face to the wall, and tells Tamsin which way to hang it next.
