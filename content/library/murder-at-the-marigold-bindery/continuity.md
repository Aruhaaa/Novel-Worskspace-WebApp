# Continuity log: Murder at the Marigold Bindery

Each writer appends the facts a chapter establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: The Wrong Nail

- Fri 14 July, 6:50am: door locked, Quire not at window; spare key (brass Yale, green wool loop) lent to Aubrey Thu 5:45pm, now missing.
- Aubrey on his side between nipping press and bench, back of head dark, cane and Panama on the boards; Dot touched only the stove.
- Stove (lit only for paste, let die Wed) warm; yard door bolted, window and skylight shut. Rutter on bench, pastedowns torn out.
- Hammer found on Ned's left nail, face-out; Dot's nail (hammer face to wall) was empty. It swung a quarter-inch at the shop bell.
- Aubrey, Thu: "Tomorrow I shall have something to say to you... rather be accurate than prompt." Dot passed the dark window about 11pm, told no one.
- 999 reached Kitto at home, mouth full of toast. Fair opens noon; Dot booked to mend hinges there. Third net-loft step creaks.

## Ch 2: Saffron Cake and Sympathy

- Demelza: short, wide-shouldered, tight plait, notebook with elastic band; taped the lane. Wears Kit's old grey jumper cuff (Dot's darn) under uniform.
- Kit: 34, ginger, bitten left thumbnail, "Sorry" before everything, "Auntie Dot". Garrow: raincoat, "bound in rexine", says "lady of the house".
- Tamsin Bray (16, black bob, bleached stripe, inky; mother cleans at Penhallow Court) watched from the loft steps, then vanished.
- Aubrey's body carried out about 9:15. Morwenna came 9:20 with saffron cake and took over the teapot.
- Quire hissed at Demelza, Garrow and Kit, then sat in Morwenna's lap "like a bun into a tin". Quire arrived fat in March 1986; Dot never learned who fed her.
- Morwenna: not in the press room since Ned died, rarely climbs the lane (knees). "He always did choose the worst places to be right."

## Ch 3: What the Spine Remembers

- Fri 10am: ten minutes alone with the rutter, Demelza's back turned; forensic man and Garrow arrive about 10:10.
- Only the first and last gatherings carry the 1631 and Ned's 1971 holes; the middle gatherings have clean new holes, never sewn before.
- Middle leaves: 1760s paper, lion-in-a-fence watermark (Dutch-looking), farm accounts. First two and last two leaves are 1631 rag.
- Ned's pin-prick dot at the foot of the last gathering; Dot never knew of it and told nobody.
- Kettle stitches in the middle turn left like Ned's; hinge smells of fresh clove paste. Both pastedowns torn out.
- Garrow bagged the book, "Mrs Pellow, dear", told her not to leave the village. Demelza: "Then someone's been in your shop."

## Ch 4: Bunting and Bargains

- Fair opened about 1pm; Nance prayed ("There will be no ribbon"). Dot at a repair trestle by the tea urn; Jago's bunting fell on Garrow.
- Pieter de Vries: Rotterdam map dealer, white linen jacket, round tortoiseshell glasses, grey beard. Told Garrow the lion paper is probably Dutch. Bought a taper from Morwenna, avoiding her eyes.
- Morwenna's stall: honey, beeswax tapers and wax cakes for the lifeboat. She minds the Regatta Book (Ned, 1964) and mended its back joint; Dot found it heavy.
- Imogen Mayne: leather jacket, cropped hair, nose stud; took the cash box from Kit. Aubrey called Dot the one person who did not flatter him.
- Loveday: gumboots, whistling hearing aid, blackthorn stick. She told Dot to find the killer: "Nobody looks at you." Dot did not tell her the rutter is false.
- Kit's valuation list ends at lot 114, the Carne: "as far as the Carne, and not one inch past it." Sale in September.

## Ch 5: The Quarrel at the Lantern

- Fri 7pm, the Lantern: 140 glass floats with lamps; Jago served Dot cider in silence. Aubrey held the Lantern's lease.
- Thursday row, 8:20: Imogen about her mother's money ("You sold Mother's piano"); she left 8:25. Aubrey left at ten to nine.
- Jago: Aubrey phoned at 8:40, "Half ten, then", no name. Jago said nothing of his own evening.
- Nance missed the quiz ("detained by the roof"), cannot say where he was at 9:30.
- Quiz ended 10:30; Dot left 10:50. Quire Straits fourth; Jam Tarts third, Morwenna holding the pencil.
- Only Dot, Aubrey and Kit knew of the key. Close: Jago wiped a tumbler a full minute: "Leave it, Dot." She did not.

## Ch 6: Paste, Thread and Wax

- Sat 15 July: press room reopened; Dot and Kit made paste on the shop gas ring because Dot could not touch the powdered stove.
- The clove smell matches the rutter hinge exactly; only Dot, Kit and Ned made that paste.
- Dot found a thread tail in the cutting board, golden, honey-scented: beeswax (she and Ned used tallow). She keeps it sealed in an envelope.
- Police took the hammer. Dot told Kit it hung Ned's way, face-out, because Ned was left-handed; Kit (right-handed) went white at "Only me and you and Jago."
- Ned's green notebook (jobs and pupils) is in the paste cupboard; Dot lent it to Tamsin in May and never read it. Morwenna arrived with saffron buns just as Dot spoke of it.
- Tamsin swept the yard unpaid. Dot locked the cupboard and wears its key on her bootlace.

## Ch 7: Somebody Else's Key

- Sat 15 July, church-hall auction: Jago auctioneer (Aubrey's gavel, waistcoat). Lot 41, stuffed otter with amber glass eyes and a trout, went for 135 pounds to Ada Bolitho (post office, raspberry felt hat, hat stand plan) after Pieter bid with one raised finger and bowed out. Pieter: "People forgive a great deal in a man who wants an otter."
- Nance asked Dot, "too lightly", whether a book that left its owner's keeping by irregular means can be bought back ("a friend of a friend"). Dot: possible, the money is the difficulty. Roof stain shaped like Ireland.
- Kit left for the Lantern (darts) at nine; Imogen counted takings in the vestry; Dot walked home about midnight (tide on the ebb).
- Press-room sash forced from outside with a thin chisel; till (14.60 pounds and a button) untouched. Strip light over the nipping press (fitted 1988) comes on in three stages.
- Quire shut in the locked paste cupboard with a blue-rimmed saucer of milk (from Dot's shop tea set) and one of Dot's teaspoons laid straight beside it, handle to the door. Green notebook gone; lock unmarked.
- Dot opened the cupboard with her own key from the bootlace, then told Demelza only that the notebook was gone and the key once hung on the third hook (now bare). She did NOT say her key is on her neck, so Demelza believes one key was taken from the hook. Demelza came in tartan pyjama bottoms, green gumboots, police coat, wearing Kit's grey darned cuff. Truro forensic man due 8am Sunday.

## Ch 8: Quire Gives Evidence

- Sun 16 July: Morwenna's saffron cake left on Dot's step before six (third such cake: 1962, March 1986). Dot ate it standing up, weeping and furious.
- Demelza told Garrow by phone she had seen and dismissed the notebook during the Friday search (a lie; she never opened the paste cupboard). Dot nodded to it in front of Garrow. Garrow suggests Dot staged the burglary for sympathy: "Mrs Pellow, dear."
- Kit's alibi: darts at the Lantern from nine, Porthcurno or the Seconds, nobody remembers; Eddy Tonkin went home about ten to feed his ferrets; Jago saw Kit at nine and at eleven and was in the cellar changing a barrel about an hour.
- Dot's reasoning (written on a hymn sheet): till, key, cat. Thief preferred the notebook to the till; a second key exists (Ned bought two of everything); Quire (who bites everyone) was carried and fed without a scratch. Dot still tells nobody about the key on her neck.
- Sardine tin licked clean found behind the dustbin in the yard (Portuguese lettering); it nags Dot. Pieter lodges at the Lantern, trunk packed, ordered by Garrow to stay, missed his Tuesday ferry.
- Close: Dot asleep in the shop chair, Quire (unusually) on her knee; Demelza: "If you find something, you tell me first. Not Garrow. Not Kit. Me." Dot promised.

## Ch 9: Ash in the Stove

- Mon 17 July, 7:30am: Dot raked the stove (Truro man's fingerprint powder all over it). Kindling box (fish crate) down by a double handful; stove had been warm Friday though it died Wed.
- Found a charred corner of pastedown/flyleaf (buff laid paper, board fibres on the back) under the grate; Aubrey's 2B pencil, crossed sevens, shows silver under a low lamp: "K. - cat. 114 -" and lower "Mrs P. in the mor". Dot takes Mrs P. as herself and the K. as Kit's catalogue number for the Carne (lot 114).
- Dot reasons the killer burned the notes, not the book, because notes are a rope and the book is cash. Aubrey was waiting for her on Friday morning.
- Demelza saw it and said "Kit. Or Kitto." She did not bag it: left it with Dot, "I'll come with a bag when I know what I'm going to say and to whom." Not to be shown to Garrow or Kit yet.
- Kit and Demelza's first real exchange in 14 months: she was passed over for sergeant in March last year and he said sorry; she wanted a plate thrown; "Don't be kind to me. I can't do the job if you're kind." Dot shelved the scrap in Ned's Silver Jubilee toffee tin of bone folders. Close: "He was waiting for me."

## Ch 10: Smelled Like Church

- Mon 17 July, 2:30pm: net loft up eleven granite steps; padlock broken since 1979; three tarred seine nets; Tamsin's army-green sleeping bag, rubber torch, library books, jam jar of pencils, four blue exercise books of drawn book spines and a drawing of a sewing frame ("Ned's? ask"). No candle. She steps over the creaking third step. She sleeps there since the exams; five in two rooms, stepfather Terry on lates, a dog.
- Tamsin's Thursday 13 July, by her father's watch: 9:30 Nance at the door ("you know what it's worth, Aubrey"; Aubrey: "Not tonight, Arthur"); 10:05 Imogen banged in, left 10:20 wiping her face and kicked the wall twice; 10:30 a hooded woman in a pale belted mac with a big bag on her arm walked in without knocking (door on the latch), short from above, "carrying something that mustn't spill", smelled "like church. Polish and candles". Tamsin slept and did not see her leave; woke at the twelve o'clock chime.
- Tamsin also saw Dot pass about eleven, humming, not looking in at the press room (light was off). She will not tell if Dot does not tell her mum.
- Tamsin refuses to talk to Demelza (fears Auntie Jean's launderette in Redruth). Bargain: Dot will speak to Sheila Bray herself. Dot told her yes to watching corners; not yet told Demelza.

## Ch 11: Tea at Penhallow Court

- Tue 18 July, rain: Dot and Morwenna walked up in pale belted macs and headscarves (about 25 minutes). Morwenna: Ronald knew her Sunday and asked "who's died". She said of the cake, "It was flour."
- Back passage: six pale macs on pegs; size-12 black rubber sea-boots (white 12 on each heel, salt rim) which Loveday called "the gardener's". Sheila Bray's mop stopped. Loveday's hearing aid whistles whenever someone lies.
- Library: forty feet, eleven buckets, brown tide-mark at knee height, pink slips of Kit's. Portraits: Sir Ambrose, Captain Hannibal, Honor Penhallow, her father. Loveday told the story: Jory Carne, Sennen pilot, ketch Hopewell of Bideford, Longships, Nov 1631; first line "From Cape Cornwall to the Longships, two leagues and a half, keeping the Brisons well open to the west." Widow sold it to Sir Ambrose for coal and boots.
- Handled the rutter since Christmas: Kit, a record-office man, Aubrey three times, Morwenna reads to Loveday on Tuesdays for eleven years and has the run of the shelves. Morwenna took down a Hakluyt like a practised binder. She poured with her LEFT hand then switched to her right.
- Loveday has not been told the rutter is false; Garrow said "interfered with". Sheila Bray knows Tamsin sleeps in the loft ("they smell of tar and cat") and asks Dot to give her a bench; Dot agreed she may work days for her.
- Loveday volunteered an alibi unasked: Morwenna sat up with her until midnight Thursday, playing piquet (hearing aid squealed). The Institute may borrow the long tables and big urn for the regatta tea. Hall clock struck four eleven minutes late.

## Ch 12: The Strip Light

- Tue 18 July, 7pm, WI hall (green tin hut, Net Loft Row). Eight women: Morwenna (president), Edna Chegwidden (templates), Miss Iris Hocking (retired arithmetic teacher), Ada Bolitho, Doris Rosevear (Pengelly's of Truro cake scandal at the Chapel anniversary tea; Morwenna: "a cake is a promise"), Gwen Trewhella (deaf), Jenifer Opie, and Dot.
- The Regatta Book: foolscap ledger, half calf, marbled sides, red label; lived in a flour sack; Morwenna's back-joint repair has no visible join; she said she had "watched a good deal of mending". Dot found it far too heavy for a ledger and put it down to Ned. Dot measured the board with her brass pocket gauge: 3.5mm, as a joke.
- Imogen brought Aubrey's regatta folder; the committee pounced with sandwiches; she agreed to present the cup at prize-giving. She is staying at the Lantern. Dot kept Imogen's Thursday visit to herself.
- Morwenna said the strip over Dot's nipping press gave her a headache "when I stuck my head in at Easter"; Dot answered idly, then the urn sprayed Mrs Trewhella's feet and she laughed.
- Nance and Jago quarrelled over carrying the Book to the finish (Chairman's job; no chairman). Morwenna slid the Book back into its sack. The bunting fell on the committee; Morwenna: "That's the third time this week things have come apart."

## Ch 13: The Honest Man

- Wed 19 July, rain stopped. Tamsin's first day at the Bindery (arrived 8am unasked; Dot has her tearing waste paper with and across the grain). Takings since the fair: 11 pounds 40.
- Imogen came 10:10 with Aubrey's brown leather document case (since Cambridge). Letter found sealed in the perished lining: cream laid envelope, 19p stamp already on, coffee-ring on one corner, to H. R. Tregear Esq., Tregear & Polglase, Boscawen Street, Truro (Loveday's and the family's solicitor). Imogen slit it with a nail file; took it to Constable Kitto at 1pm.
- Letter dated 12 July, "Dear Hugh": "The Carne Rutter is not what it seems"; not for the September sale; nobody to handle it; Loveday not to be told; he would have it taken apart Friday before Mrs Pellow, who "has not flattered me once in twenty-seven years". Signed "Yours ever, Aubrey". Never posted.
- Imogen at the Bindery 10:05-10:20: Aubrey in shirtsleeves, pencil behind ear, lamp on, said he had "somebody coming at half past ten" and meant to be kind to them. Last words: "Tomorrow, then. I shall be accurate about the piano tomorrow." She kicked the wall twice. Dot told her only that "someone" saw her; Tamsin (in the yard) overheard.
- Garrow thinks Aubrey was in the fraud ("Trevithick's hands weren't clean") and has asked Imogen twice who inherits (she does). Funeral Monday 24 July if Garrow releases the body; Nance to give the hymn. Imogen wants his name cleared; Dot said she would do what she could.
- Aubrey's gold swan signet ring (in a police envelope) is with Dot, on the paste shelf beside Ned's Silver Jubilee toffee tin (bone folders and the burned scrap). Quire sat by it.
- Dot's inference: the false book's maker learned Aubrey knew between Wed and Thu night; the letter never left the case. She wrote WHO KNEW AUBREY KNEW? in capitals inside the front cover of Ned's last order book (black, cloth spine, she cannot bear his hand) and underlined it twice.

## Ch 14: Question Time

- Thu 20 July, the Lantern, quiz began 8:20; low water 9:25. Quire Straits: Dot, Kit (writes), Tamsin (lemonade), Nance (sherry, cycle clips). Jam Tarts: Morwenna, Edna Chegwidden, Doris Rosevear, Gwen Trewhella, Iris Hocking. Regulars: Wilf Hosken (71, ex-pilot's boy) and Eddy Tonkin (caught with a 1962 Pears' Cyclopaedia, five points docked). Jory is the potman. Demelza attended off duty.
- Nance and Tamsin formed an alliance (saints and footballers). Round 2 was Aubrey's book round in an envelope marked "Jago, for Thursday the twentieth, in order", read by Jago in glasses; last note "read the answers slowly. People are generally cleverer than they look". The pub stood. Morwenna comforted Imogen at the bar.
- Gathering/signature/quire question: Aubrey's note accepted all three and "quire from one team in particular"; Tamsin and Morwenna argued and were both right. Morwenna's hand is quick, upright, neat; the Jam Tarts write the time at the foot of each round (Miss Hocking's rule since 1987): 8.40, 9.12.
- Dot noticed who laughed a beat late: Nance, Jago, Morwenna.
- Kit's margin notes to Demelza (the jumper, ransom-note handwriting, "sorry about March last year") were read aloud by Wilf; Demelza folded the sheet into her cardigan breast pocket.
- Music round: Jam Tarts took the first six then collapsed; Jago pulled the jukebox plug at 9:50 (Eddy: robbery).
- 10:25 Pieter de Vries appeared at the door and stared at Morwenna's jar of heather and gorse honey (lifeboat tin) on the bar; sat at the far end. Three-way tie on 71; tiebreak "Name the Trevenna lifeboat lost in 1910": nobody knew. Jago called a lock-in; Jory bolted the door.

## Ch 15: The Music Round

- Lock-in from about 10:35. Jago gave Dot the Huntley & Palmers tin (stagecoach in snow on the lid) of 41 weeks of answer sheets since 1 Oct 1988 (Quire Straits fourth 31 times), then left by the back in his coat "to see to a barrel".
- Jam Tarts sheet for 13 July: Morwenna's hand to the foot of the Music round, "9.50"; then Doris Rosevear (round hand, circle dots): "Capt. changed 9.50. M.H. gone up to Loveday's. D.R. now holding pencil. (J.R.'s rule.)". Kit confirms the plug was pulled at ten to ten. Dot pocketed the sheet.
- Morwenna first said she left at 10:30; shown the sheet, said it was ten to ten and her memory was going (Ronald asked if she was the new nurse; tablets in the wrong pocket). She offered saffron cake from the handbag tin (horn-handled knife); Dot let it go. Morwenna smelt of woodsmoke and wax.
- Jory carried Pieter's trunk to the quay steps for a five-pound note; Pieter said he would collect it himself.
- Jam Tarts left 10:45 (the Institute does not hold with lock-ins). Demelza, off duty, asked Dot "You said first. If there's anything." Dot said "Not yet" (a lie: the fourth thing she has kept from Demelza). Demelza left.
- 10:50 Pieter turned up his collar and left by the front door, going downhill towards the Steps and quay. Dot went out for air at 10:55; Nance stayed in the inglenook with lemon barley; Tamsin asleep on the settle. Close: a man's cry from the quay, cut off, then a heavy splash. Dot did not follow Pieter.

## Ch 16: Man in the Water

- Pilchard Steps: 43 steps in three flights, rope rail on the third. Margery Jane (Wilf Hosken's boat) moored under the wall; lobster pots on the Hard; Pieter's strapped steamer trunk (brass corners) at the head of the quay steps with a herring gull on it. Tide about 1.5 hours on the flood over mud.
- Jago in the water to his chest in shirt and trousers, coat in a heap on the stone. Dot used a boat hook; they dragged Pieter (face-down, linen jacket ballooned) between the boat and the wall; his head knocked on the granite twice; cut behind his right ear.
- Dot gave artificial respiration (Guides 1943 under Miss Treffry; St John Ambulance course 1972 with Ned and a rubber doll named Annie). He coughed and breathed but did not wake.
- Demelza arrived first at a run and took over; sent Kit to ring 999 (ambulance and Garrow). Kit, Tamsin (blanket), Wilf and Nance (praying) came. Ambulance and Garrow's Rover arrived 11:40.
- Jago would not say what he was doing there ("Walking"; "I heard a man go in and I went in after him. That's all I've got"). Demelza noted his door was behind him and he had his coat on. His whole statement to Garrow was silence.
- Pieter's pockets: Lantern room 2 key (brass tag), wallet (given to Demelza), handkerchief, a Dutch paperback (woman looking over her shoulder at a house) and, inside the cover, a leaf folded in four. Dot pocketed it unseen before Garrow came.
- Dot's knees gave way on the third flight; Nance helped, walked her home wheeling his bicycle ("I am sure it is nothing"). Leaf: laid rag, wide chain lines, brown ink, long s, running head "Cape Cornwall to the Longships", creases soft from handling. She put it between blotting paper under Ned's Cruden's Concordance.

## Ch 17: Wet Boots

- Fri 21 July, 5:10am Dot realised the leaf is a continuation leaf (pilot's running head; the rutter's first two leaves carry the same head). Kit's list gave 64 leaves: 4 in the false book, 60 missing. Quire slept on Dot's bed.
- 7:30 Kit brought news: Garrow holds Jago (since about 2am) in the cell of the police house (front room, foot of Church Lane between chapel and Co-op; ficus, photograph of the Queen). Pieter is at Treliske hospital, expected to live. Jago's sea-boots stand stuffed with newspaper by the cell radiator.
- Dot lied to Demelza: said she found the leaf on the quay steps in the weed about seven (to protect Demelza, who was on the scene). Demelza wrote it in her notebook, then agreed not to log it: "I'd like another day." Dot kept it (told to keep it out of the Bindery).
- The false rutter is in the police-house safe in an evidence bag, key on Demelza's chain, awaiting the county record office man "Thursday week" (27 July). Dot matched the leaf under the swan-neck lamp: chain lines a quarter inch wider than the day-book's, pepper-flecked rag, brown ink, leaning long s, no lion watermark.
- Garrow's theory (noon, DC Pascoe with him): Pieter (known to Dutch police for tax; volunteered the Dutch paper; trunk on the quay; cash-paid berth on the Margery Jane for 5am) forged or paid for the false book and fell out with a partner, Rowe or Kit. Garrow to Truro for the hotel room, bank and the magistrates at two. "Wet boots... oldest evidence in the book."
- Loveday phoned: Jago is her man of business, keeps the estate's books, was on his way to her at eleven with the quarter's rents ("accounts are best done at night, nobody interrupts"). Her hearing aid whistled down the wire. The village has never believed the books story; Jago adds on his fingers (he has nine).
- Close: Demelza covers the mouthpiece: "Is she lying, or is she in love?"

## Ch 18: Kit's Mistake

- Fri 21 July, 3:10pm, shop: Dot had a pan of paste (clove) on the shop gas ring; Tamsin at the Co-op for sugar. Ned taught Kit to sew in summer 1965, aged ten, sitting on a St Petroc's hassock (PRAY FOR THE NAVY) that Ned never returned.
- Kit confessed: Wed 12 July, collating the Carne for the valuation, he found the lion-in-a-fence watermark and told Aubrey, who said to say nothing ("Christopher"). Thu 13 July about 6pm he came to the Bindery to return Ned's Cockerell, found Aubrey happy at the bench with the rutter open, put his hand on the bench and moved the lamp, left. On the 14th he told Demelza he had not been in the press room since Easter last year.
- Dot told Kit Aubrey's letter was dated the 12th ("Then he believed me"). She then showed him the burned scrap, "K. - cat. 114 -" and "Mrs P. in the mor"; the K. is his own catalogue number. She admitted she has held it since Monday and that Demelza saw it and did not bag it. Kit begged her not to give it to Garrow because it would give him Demelza.
- Dot moved the scrap from Ned's toffee tin to her cardigan pocket. The leaf stays at her cottage. Police have neither.
- About 3:30 Garrow arrived with DC Pascoe, a Truro uniformed constable and Demelza, with a search warrant (Truro justices; shop, press room, net loft). He read it aloud over the paste pots and arrested Kit for the murder of Aubrey, citing Kit's left thumb on the underside of the lamp over Aubrey's work against his statement to Demelza.
- Dot kept stirring the paste ("if I stop it will be glue"). Kit asked her to feed Quire the "good tin" (pilchards, at six); Quire hisses at him, "how you know she's fond".
- Demelza stood in the doorway looking at the lintel, not at Kit or Dot. Garrow rode alone in the first car; Kit in the back of the second (a white Ford) with Demelza in front beside the driver. A police search team remains at the Bindery.
- Close: tail-lights down Fish Cellar Lane; Quire on the step asks for her tin; Dot does not know which is the good one.

## Ch 19: Nothing Left on the Bench

- Fri 21 July evening. The "good tin" is the pilchards (fish on label, 42p); the tin with a cat in a basket is "the other" and Quire refuses it. Garrow back 6:30pm (Kit left at Truro). Pascoe sealed the shop door, sash and yard door with white gummed strips with a red stamp; Scenes of Crime man due 8am Sat. Everything inside is an exhibit: Dot's bone folder, the Chapel pulpit Bible (spine gone at Leviticus), nine commissions. Paste pan on the gas ring set to rubber.
- Dot's canvas tool roll (two bone folders, dividers, awl, tallow, needle book, brass pocket gauge) was fetched from her trestle in St Petroc's hall; the police do not know it exists.
- Hall: last dealers packing (held a week by the frozen cash box); Pieter's map stall under a dust sheet, card BACK SHORTLY; the Institute borrowing tables for the regatta tea. Gwen Trewhella shouted about Kit; Doris relayed Morwenna's message (thinking of you, something hot, only her knees); Nance: "Forgive me, Mrs Pellow, if there is anything..."
- Demelza, about 8pm at the foot of the lane: reprimanded at Truro for an undeclared personal association (on her file Monday); left minding the police house and Jago; told to keep off civilians in the inquiry. She showed her warrant card, then put it back. Jago has said eleven words since 2am Friday. Dot still holds the burned scrap and told her "Nothing".
- Dot's worries: Michaelmas rent 92 pounds to the Harbour Trustees (let to the Pellows since 1951), takings since the fair 11.40, nine jobs behind the seal. The search put the eight paste pots out on the step. Children played sardines at the old lifeboat shed (door not shut since Suez).
- Thursday 13th: the church clock was striking eleven as Dot reached the old pilchard cellar corner, humming; the press-room window was black; the doctor said death between ten and midnight.
- Tamsin brought Dot's brown teapot, two mugs and the unopened Co-op sugar and sat silent on the step below for twenty minutes (her sleeping bag was put back by police with the zip wrong). Close: "I've got a copy of Ned's notebook. Some of it."

## Ch 20: Tamsin's Exercise Book

- Fri 21 July about 10:15pm, Dot's kitchen. Tamsin's red school book (TREVENNA, VANISHING TRADES, T. BRAY 4B; Mr Lanyon: B plus, "Neat. More sea next time.") copies two pages of Ned's green notebook, made one Saturday in May at the shop desk. Her hand is better than Ned's; her notes are in square brackets. Her drawing of Ned's oak sewing frame notes that Ned says there were TWO: where is the other?
- Page one: J.R. from 1951 holds the paste, "fingers are for rope". "M. Hext (left-handed, forced right at school). 1955 to 58. Best I've had. Hook key to her for Saturdays. Fetch back." "Second frame to M.H., wedding present, 1958. She will use it." C.J.P. 1965, aged ten, "sews like a wet Tuesday". Ink changes at the Hext entry (written up fair after the wedding).
- Page two: Regatta Book 1964, foolscap ledger, 200 leaves, five cords, half calf, marbled sides, red label, boards 2 mm "and not a hair more" (Hocking's father dropped the old book in the harbour in 1931). Nine pounds and a pint (J.R.). Dot felt a snag at the figure and let it go.
- Tamsin's list: the hooded woman's church smell is beeswax polish and tapers (Morwenna sells the tapers, does the church brass, keeps eight hives); she pours and cuts cake left-handed, signs right; Quire went into her lap. Dot admitted the 9:50 quiz sheet and Loveday's squealing alibi.
- Ned's funeral, a Tuesday in March 1986: Morwenna held Dot's left hand through "Abide With Me" (ring left a mark two days) and said in the porch, "He was a careful one, Dot."
- Dot concludes the book was not made at the Bindery and asks how. Ned never wrote "best" of her. Close: she reads the Hext line aloud twice: "Morwenna Hext was a better binder than I am"; the kettle boils over. Tamsin sleeps in Dot's box room (loft sealed).

## Ch 21: What Jago Couldn't Say

- Sat 22 July, 12:10am: Demelza freed Jago from the police-house cell for lack of evidence. Dot promised to tell her first: five o'clock, Dot's kitchen. Jago's sea-boots have a white 12 painted on each heel.
- Jago's cottage, harbour end of Net Loft Row: lobster pots stacked to the ceiling; photograph of Loveday at nineteen on a grey horse. Thursdays since the winter Ned died he leaves Jory the cash-box key and jukebox plug at 9:30 for Penhallow Court: piquet for coppers, eleven buckets to empty.
- 1956: Jago 28, Loveday 41, a hay barn, the Chapel's scandal; her father paid him off with a fortnight's wages and a Falmouth coaster berth; three years away; thirty years of nodding. Ned never spoke of it.
- 13 July: Jago at the Court 9:30 to midnight, home by half past; nobody rang the bell. The piquet was with him (he lost 60p). Morwenna did not come: Loveday put her in Jago's chair.
- Loveday (about 1am, flannel nightdress, gumboots on the wrong feet): Morwenna came before nine on Fri 14th with a cake tin and asked her to say she sat up with her. Loveday agreed from friendship and shame. Small things "borrowed" from the house for years: snuffbox, Hakluyt plates, a Book of Hours. She guesses the Carne is ruined.
- Close: "Is it Morwenna?" "I don't know yet." "Will they put her in prison?" She walked off. Dot carries Jago's black rubber torch.

## Ch 22: Measuring the Boards

- Sat 22 July, 5am, Dot's kitchen: Dot, Demelza, Imogen (fetched by Tamsin's note to Room 3 at the Lantern), Tamsin. Jago hung forty yards of bunting overnight. Imogen recalled Morwenna's strip-light headache at the WI (strip fitted last October; Morwenna said on 14 July she had not been in that room since Ned died). Demelza underlined it.
- Dot measured Pieter's leaf at 3am: 0.15 mm. Boards 3.5 mm against spec 2: 3 mm extra over two boards; Tamsin worked 3 / 0.15 = 20 leaves. Theory: boards split and relaid round twenty leaves. Sixty leaves missing, four in the false book.
- No warrant possible on thick boards; Garrow will not hear it. Plan: ask to see the Book at the prize-giving at ten past three, when the cox waits to sign (Tamsin's timing). Demelza agrees only to stand at the back. If wrong, Dot loses the Bindery's name for good.
- Imogen's Penhallow shelf schedule (41 sheets) in Aubrey's 2B pencil: M.H. "to mend" against lot 31 Hakluyt, lot 57 Book of Hours, almanacs, snuffbox; last sheet "Mrs H. is kind & careful. Cf. all of the above. Ask Mrs P." Demelza to give it to Garrow.
- Dot gave Demelza the burned scrap; she bagged and signed for it and Dot asked her to record that Dot held it five days. Close: Dot laid out dividers, bone folder and gauge on her table; Quire sat among them.

## Ch 23: Regatta Morning

- Sat 22 July. Trevenna's gig is the Provident; Ada Bolitho's raffle table has the otter in her raspberry hat. Low water 10:40; race at noon.
- About 9:15am Demelza (at Treliske since seven) rang the Lantern payphone: Pieter spoke, twice, "The lady with honey"; asked the day and for his glasses. Morwenna's jar HONEY FOR THE LIFEBOAT, M.H. stood on the bar. Only Dot and Demelza know.
- 10:30 Loveday arrived in a wicker bath chair pushed by Nance; gumboots on the right feet; her aid shrieked at Morwenna's "How lovely to see you so well". "I've come to see whatever it is you're going to do."
- The tea tent: Morwenna in green WI sash squared the Regatta Book on the prize table with her left hand and asked if "that poor Dutchman" was mending. Dot lied "Still asleep" and ate her saffron bun.
- 11:45 Garrow has read the list and been given the scrap; he will stand at the back of the tent at three.
- Noon: Jago fired the starting gun; Trevenna won by a length; the band played three tunes and ended on "Trelawny". Close: Morwenna pressing an egg sandwich on Nance, already looking back at Dot.

## Ch 24: The Tea Tent

- Sat 22 July, 3:10pm, about forty present; cox Kerensa Rosevear (12); Imogen holds the cup; Demelza at the back pole, Garrow behind a trestle. Edna Chegwidden began clearing the sandwiches and Garrow shouted that nothing be moved.
- Morwenna handed Dot the Book smiling and offered cake. Dot gave the clues in order (hammer, left-turning stitch, Ned's paste, beeswax, the hook key, saucer and teaspoon, strip light, K. = cat. 114). Morwenna answered each courteously; on the strip light: "a figure of speech... I do see how it looks". Demelza held up the bagged scrap; Dot took blame for holding it five days.
- Dividers read 3.5 mm on both boards; Morwenna: "Ned bound them thick." Dot read the pupil line aloud; Gwen Trewhella: "OF COURSE SHE CAN, SHE DID THE BANNER."
- Dot said aloud: "I loved you, Morwenna... I think I still do." Morwenna: "That is very kind of you, Dot."
- Quire (shut in Dot's kitchen at noon) climbed into Morwenna's lap. Morwenna set her on the grass, took the Regatta Book (dividers slid onto the cloth) and walked out; Jago held the flap. Nobody stopped her; the Book is with her.

## Ch 25: High Water on the Slip

- Sat 22 July, about 4pm to near high water (4:50). Demelza gave Dot "ten minutes", stayed at the head of the Slip (sees, cannot hear); Edna Chegwidden kept Garrow busy with an egg-and-cress. Slip: 1923 concrete ramp, galvanised rail on the east side, lifeboat house with green doors shut; the gig Provident lay afloat at its flank and Jago sat in her unasked.
- Morwenna sat on the rail with the Regatta Book on her knees, offered saffron cake (handbag tin, horn-handled knife); Dot refused for the first time since 1962. She told it level, "like reading out her own minutes". Ronald: Gwithian House 380 pounds a week; the County alternative was Bodmin. She counted regatta money honestly since 1971 and says "it was only my own that ran out".
- Method: small borrowings first (snuffbox, three Hakluyt plates); took the Carne in March under shortbread; put the 1763 Penhallow estate day-book into Ned's cover by the Tuesday before Easter, sewn on Ned's second frame (his 1958 wedding gift, carried up Church Lane on a handcart) in her honey-house. Sold 40 leaves to Pieter in three goes ("a lady with old leaves", Lemon Street book sale, Truro); 20 hidden by splitting the Regatta Book boards with wheat paste. Dot told her it was the best sewing she had seen in a forgery.
- Thu 13 July: Aubrey's "Half ten, then" was said into the payphone with his back to Morwenna in the Lantern passage; she answered "Half ten, Aubrey" and wrote 8.40 on the sheet. She left 9:50 for her mac, watched Imogen from the old pilchard-cellar doorway, entered 10:30 (door on the latch). Aubrey offered to stand beside her before Loveday and Dot if she returned the leaves. She struck him once with the hammer from the nail at her left shoulder as he bent to write; hung it back face-out by habit; put out the lamp.
- She heard Dot pass at eleven, humming, and held her breath; Dot did not look in. Then she put on the strip light (the true headache, moved to Easter), burned the pastedowns in the stove with Dot's kindling, took the spare key (green wool) from the bench and locked up. She could not burn the rutter.
- Ned lent her the cupboard key in 1955 ("fetch back"), never returned; first used on Sat 15 July after overhearing Dot in the shop. Ned's green notebook is unburned, in a biscuit tin with Christmas paper in her honey-house; Dot may have it back ("It isn't a bad hand"). She fed Quire sardines on the back step at six for three months in 1986; the cupboard milk was "only manners". She loved Ned; Ned told her (not Dot) about the dot, and she left it in the Carne's last gathering hoping to be found.
- Pieter put a folded note in her handbag (quay, 10:55); she pushed him in at a young flood: "That was the idea." He had one leaf in his paperback and meant to write to the police from Rotterdam. She saw through Dot's "still asleep" lie, and said she is glad he woke: "the one thing in all this I would have back". She says she would like to think she would have spoken up before Kit was charged.
- She threw the Regatta Book left-handed over the rail; Jago hauled it out of the water by the spine from the gig ("No. Not that, Morwenna"). Garrow (half an egg sandwich in hand) said "Constable Kitto. If you would." Demelza arrested Morwenna for the murder of Aubrey and the attempted murder of Pieter. Morwenna gave Dot the cake tin and walked up the Slip on Demelza's arm.
- Close: Dot opened the back board with her bone folder (wheat paste let go in the water) and laid twenty leaves of the Carne in a row on the Slip in the sun; the ink had not run; the tide stood at the top.

## Ch 26: A Second Dot

- Last Sunday in July (30 July), steady rain, Bindery smelling of clove. Seals removed Mon 24 by DC Pascoe. Garrow phoned Tue and called Dot "Mrs Pellow" with no "dear". Morwenna made a signed 11-page statement and is committed for trial at Truro in the autumn. Kit came home Sat night in the white Ford, slept 14 hours on Dot's settle. Garrow's report credits Constable Kitto with the arrest.
- Aubrey buried Mon 24 July; Nance gave the hymn; Dot gave Imogen the swan ring (she wears it on her thumb; said "Accurate"). Imogen sold Nance back the Psalter for 900 pounds, payable when the roof is mended. The Institute voted to pay Ronald Hext's Gwithian fees from the bunting fund until Christmas. Takings that week 143.20 pounds; Dot is now looked at and does not entirely hate it.
- Pieter (plaster behind right ear, glasses wired at one hinge) brought forty leaves in a Delft-blue biscuit tin, flown from Rotterdam by his assistant, counted and photographed by police; will not ask for his money back. Gift: his grandfather's Delft bone folder (long, pale, pointed one end, thumb notch). Tamsin counted 64 leaves in all (4 + 20 + 40).
- Demelza, out of uniform in Kit's grey darned jumper, returned Ned's green notebook (photographed). Dot has not opened it; it sits on the paste shelf by the toffee tin and she means to read it one wet Sunday. Demelza to Kit in the doorway: "Thursday. Seven. Mine." (she cannot do mince; bring nothing). Kit apologised for apologising; Dot pretended to be ruling a line.
- Loveday arrived with Jago in a mud-coloured Land Rover, openly. The library sells at Truro on 23 September, damp and all, off Kit's list; the Carne stays, bound plain (no gift-shop calf), to sit on her hall table; Jago drives her "in the front, in the daylight". Her hearing aid whistled once only (at "I should have been" in the quiz).
- Jago brought the lifeboat station's service book 1884-1922 (sailcloth, damp) for rebinding: 31 pounds plus a quadruplicate form (first order from the Institution; Wilf Hosken is honorary secretary). The 1910 lifeboat wrecked off the Brisons (crew saved) was the Ellen Penhallow, Loveday's great-aunt's gift of 1894: the answer to the unanswered tiebreak.
- Dot and Tamsin sewed the Carne into the 1853 calf with Ned's 1971 spine: 64 leaves, 8 gatherings, 5 cords on Ned's frame; Dot's kettle stitches turn right. Morwenna's second frame is under a tarpaulin in her honey-house with a police label. Tamsin's first corner was lumpy; Dot said nothing; "It's the first one."
- Dot pricked her own dot a quarter-inch right of Ned's at the foot of the last gathering. Quire sat on Tamsin's lap (Tamsin has put out the good tin at six since Fri 21 July). Police returned the backing hammer Thu 27 July in a paper bag. Close: Dot hung it on her own nail face to the wall; Tamsin may use either nail or put up a third, hung her own way each night; "Where do you keep the nails?" "I showed her."
