# Bible: Murder at the Marigold Bindery

## Logline and premise

A fifty-eight-year-old bookbinder who feels invisible finds the village collector dead in her press room and reads, from thread, paste and paper, that the rare manuscript beside him has been quietly gutted by someone who learned the trade from her late husband.

**What happens, in order.** Friday 14 July 1989, Trevenna, Cornwall. Dot Pellow finds Aubrey Trevithick (67, collector, regatta chairman) dead on the floor of the Marigold Bindery's press room on the morning his three-day antiquarian book fair opens. On the bench lies the Carne Rutter, a c.1631 manuscript pilot's book belonging to Loveday Penhallow of Penhallow Court, half-unsewn, its endpapers torn out. Dot sees that it has been rebound twice: once by her husband Ned in 1971 (his pin-prick "Pellow dot"), and again this spring by someone else, who put a worthless 1760s estate day-book into Ned's cover and kept only the rutter's first and last leaves. Aubrey, valuing Loveday's library for sale, had spotted the fraud and gone to the Bindery to take the book apart and show Dot in the morning. He was an honest man, which nobody in the village believed.

The forger and killer is **Morwenna Hext**, 63, Women's Institute president and regatta treasurer: Ned's secret pupil in 1955-58, a better binder than anyone knows. Ned gave her his old second sewing frame as a wedding present in 1958; she did the 1989 rebinding on it in her honey-house, not in the Bindery. Since her husband Ronald's dementia put him in Gwithian House (£380 a week; he still knows her on good days), she has sold the rutter's middle leaves through the Dutch dealer Pieter de Vries ("a Cornish lady with honey") and hidden twenty in the boards of the Regatta Book. Aubrey, who wished to give her a chance to return the leaves before he told Loveday, rang her from the Lantern payphone at 8:40 ("Half ten, then"). On the quiz night of Thursday 13 July she slipped out at 9:50 (to fetch her mac and wait out Imogen's visit), met him, begged, was refused, and struck him with the backing hammer, burned his pencilled notes (the torn endpapers), took his key and locked up. She hung the hammer on the nail Ned's way. She later steals Ned's notebook (it names her as pupil and logs the 1971 job), shuts the cat in the cupboard with a saucer of milk, and pushes Pieter off the quay when he works out who she is. Dot proves it on regatta day, Saturday 22 July. The harmless early scene recast: in chapter 2 Quire, who hisses at everyone, settles in Morwenna's lap "like a bun in a tin". Quire knew her because in spring 1986, after Ned died and Dot forgot to feed anything, Morwenna left sardines on the back step for three months and Dot never knew. Dot takes on Tamsin Bray as apprentice.

## World and setting

**Trevenna**, a Cornish fishing village of 600 on a steep harbour, summer 1989. Granite, slate, hydrangeas, gulls on every chimney. Lanes named for what was once done in them: Fish Cellar Lane, Pilchard Steps, Net Loft Row. The **Marigold Bindery** (Ned's mother's favourite flower; yellow door) sits at the top of Fish Cellar Lane above the harbour: two rooms below (shop at the front, press room behind with a Rayburn-type iron stove and a skylight), a net loft above reached by outside steps, Dot's cottage twenty yards uphill. Press room fittings: lying press, nipping press, plough, sewing frame, backing hammer on its nail, paste pots, a cutting board scarred like a map. Fluorescent strip over the nipping press (fitted 1988). Third stair creaks.

Elsewhere: **The Drowned Lantern** (pub; Thursday quiz, 8 till half ten; landlord Jago Rowe keeps every answer sheet in a biscuit tin); **St Petroc's** church and its hall (the fair, 14-16 July); **Penhallow Court**, the damp granite big house on the hill, Loveday's; **Gwithian House** care home in Truro (forty minutes by bus); the **Hard** and **the Slip** (lifeboat slipway at the harbour mouth); the WI hall; the Chapel Methodists who disapprove of the Lantern.

**Rules.** No mobiles. One BT payphone in the Lantern, a landline in the shop. Truro police station is 40 minutes' drive; Constable Kitto is the only officer resident in Trevenna. A forensic man comes from Truro the same day. 1989 forensic limits: fingerprints and photographs, no DNA. Tides: low water about 10:40 on regatta morning.

**Glossary.**
- *Rutter*: a sailor's handwritten pilot book of coastal directions.
- *Gathering*: folded sheets forming one section of a book.
- *Pastedown*: endpaper pasted to the inside of a board; *flyleaf*: the free leaf after it.
- *Kettle stitch*: the knot at head and tail linking gatherings. Ned's turned left.
- *Sewing stations*: holes along the fold; old holes stay, like scars.
- *Chain lines / laid paper*: ribbed pattern of old paper; spacing dates it.
- *Millboard*: thick board for covers; *Davey board* is its modern cousin.
- *Backing hammer*: round-faced hammer for shaping the spine. Hangs on its nail.
- *Bone folder*: smooth bone paddle for creasing.
- *The Pellow dot*: Ned's pin-prick at the foot of the last gathering, a signature he never mentioned.
- *Ned's paste*: wheat-starch paste with a pinch of alum and a drop of clove oil against mould. Smells of Christmas.
- *Waxing thread*: Dot uses paraffin-tallow; beeswax is a hobbyist's choice.
- *Nipping press*: iron press that squeezes books flat.
- *Tight back / hollow back*: spine structures; Ned's 1971 repair is a tight back.
- *Jam Tarts*: the WI quiz team. *Quire Straits*: Dot's team. *Lantern Regulars*: Jago's.
- *Gig*: six-oared Cornish pilot boat; the regatta's gig race is the day's event.
- *The Regatta Book*: ledger of winners, half calf, bound by Ned in 1964, kept by the treasurer.
- *Trevenna time*: everything starts twenty minutes late except the tide.
- *Saffron cake*: Morwenna's famous yellow loaf, brought to every crisis.

## Cast

**Dorothea "Dot" Pellow** (narrator), 58. Born Plymouth 1931 as Dorothea Quick; came to Trevenna on marrying Ned in 1962; widowed March 1986. Tall, bony, grey bob cut with kitchen scissors, reading glasses on a bootlace, left thumb flattened by thirty years of bone folder. Speaks dry and level, in understatement, asides in brackets; says "Well." to close a topic. Example: "I have been ignored by experts, Constable; I know the difference between not noticing and not caring." Wants: to keep the Bindery solvent and to be taken seriously. Fears: that she is no longer needed. Hides: that she walked past the Bindery at 11 on Thursday night and did not look in. Arc: from invisibility to being the one everyone listens to; takes on an apprentice and puts her own dot beside Ned's.

**Aubrey Trevithick** (dead; seen in memory), 67. Tall, silver moustache, summer tweeds, signet ring with a swan, bad left hip, ebony cane. Pompous, precise, kind when unobserved. "My dear Mrs Pellow, accuracy is a form of courtesy." Collector, regatta chairman, Loveday's valuer. Wanted to expose a fraud against a friend. Estranged from his daughter.

**Morwenna Hext**, 63. Small, round, apple cheeks, silver perm, pearl studs, a large handbag with a tin in it always; keeps eight hives. Brisk, warm, bossy: "Mind", "my handsome", "Sit. Eat." Writes right-handed (forced at school) but pours, cuts and stirs left. Wants: Ronald's care paid, her standing intact. Fears: being known as a thief. Hides: that she was Ned's pupil and in love with him; everything else. Arc: ally to killer; confesses with composure.

**Kit Pellow**, 34. Ned's nephew; ginger, gangling, bitten left thumbnail, flat Cornish vowels. Mumbles; "Sorry" before every sentence. Cataloguer for Aubrey; right-handed. Wants: Demelza back and a trade of his own. Fears: being useless. Hides: that he flagged the rutter's paper to Aubrey on Wednesday and was told to say nothing. Arc: accused, cleared, confident.

**Constable Demelza Kitto**, 31. Short, wide-shouldered, tight plait under helmet, notebook with an elastic band. Clipped: "Right. Facts." Kit's ex of fourteen months. Wants: to be believed by her inspector. Fears: being sidelined (DI Garrow of Truro). Hides: that she still wears Kit's old jumper under the uniform. Arc: bends the rules once, makes the arrest.

**Jago Rowe**, 61. Huge, bald, anchor tattoo faded to a blur, missing the little finger of his right hand (winch), voice like a slow tide. "That's the size of it." Landlord, regatta vice-chairman, Ned's best friend. Wants: Aubrey's hold on his lease gone. Hides: that he has spent Thursday nights for three years with Loveday Penhallow.

**Reverend Arthur Nance**, 66. Thin, stooping, wispy white hair, bicycle clips, a cough drop in his cheek. Apologises constantly: "Forgive me, I am sure it is nothing." Wants: St Petroc's roof mended. Hides: he sold the church's 1520 Psalter to Aubrey for £900, a third of its worth; visited the Bindery lane at 9:30 on Thursday to beg it back.

**Tamsin Bray**, 16. Wiry, black bob with a stripe bleached at the front, ink on her fingers, bitten pen. Quick, blunt: "Right. So." Her mother cleans at Penhallow Court. Wants: to make books; leave school. Hides: she sleeps in the net loft some nights and watched the lane on Thursday. Arc: the apprentice.

**Pieter de Vries**, 54. Rotterdam dealer; huge pale linen jacket, round tortoiseshell glasses, clipped beard. Dutch word order: "Very interesting, this." Honest-ish: bought leaves from "a Cornish lady" and did not ask. Pushed from the quay after asking Morwenna to meet him there at 10:55 on Thursday 20 July.

**Loveday Penhallow**, 74. Thin, gumboots under tweed, hearing aid that whistles, beautiful long hands. Barks. "Don't fuss, Pellow." Wants: the roof mended and a clean death. Hides: Jago; and that she suspected Morwenna of "borrowing" and said nothing.

**Imogen Mayne**, 38. Aubrey's daughter; London costume-maker, cropped hair, nose stud, smokes. Dry and defensive: "Daddy, in the voice he hated." Quarrelled with him Thursday, came to the Bindery at 10:05, left at 10:20. Hides: she did.

**Quire**: black cat, white bib, one torn ear; hisses at everyone, including Dot when hungry.

## Voice guide

First person, past tense, Dot, telling it a year or so afterwards. Dry, observant, practical; humour from understatement and from comparing people to bindings. Medium sentences with the occasional very short one. Asides about being invisible at fifty-eight. Dialogue: double quotes, British spellings, "said" mostly, village speech lightly marked ("my handsome", "dreckly", "proper job"). Never explains clues to the reader; sets them down as ordinary detail.

**Sample (not part of the novel).** I will say this for a dead man: he was perfectly still, which is more than can be said for most of Trevenna. He lay between the nipping press and the bench with his hat beside him, and the first thing I noticed was the hat, because Aubrey Trevithick would no more have taken it off indoors than he would have apologised. The second thing was the stove, which was warm. It was July. I stood in my own doorway, a woman in a cardigan holding a paper bag of buns, and understood that nobody was going to ask me anything for at least the next minute, and that I should use it.

**Never writes:** a villain's speech about their plan; "little did I know"; a gut feeling standing in for a clue; modern slang or a mention of mobiles or the internet; the word "closure"; a joke at the expense of grief; medical or police detail Dot could not know; a clue explained at the moment it is planted.

## Motifs

1. Thread and sewing: what holds, what was cut. 2. Paste smell of clove. 3. The dot: a small mark proving someone was here. 4. Hands: who is left-handed, whose are stained. 5. Tide and harbour, times by the water. 6. Quire's opinions. 7. Cake as weapon and comfort. 8. Being unseen; the mac and headscarf that make women invisible.

## Timeline and ground rules

- 1631 Carne Rutter written. 1853 bound in calf. 1955-58 Morwenna is Ned's pupil. 1958 marries Ronald Hext. 1951 Ned takes over his late father's bench in the building (unnamed). 1962 Ned marries Dot and names it the Marigold Bindery. Morwenna keeps the cupboard key Ned gave her as a pupil in 1955 and never asked back. 1964 Ned binds Regatta Book (boards 2mm). 1971 Ned rebacks the rutter. March 1986 Ned dies at the bench. 1987 Ronald enters Gwithian House. 1988 Loveday asks Aubrey to value the library for a September sale. Jan-June 1989 Morwenna swaps the rutter's leaves, sells 40 to Pieter in three batches, hides 20 in the Regatta Book boards, keeps 4 in the fake.
- Wed 12 July: Kit flags the rutter's paper to Aubrey. Thu 13 July: 5:45pm Aubrey borrows the Bindery; Dot lends the spare key. 8:00 quiz begins; 8:20 Imogen and Aubrey quarrel in the Lantern; 8:40 Aubrey on the payphone ("Half ten, then"); 8:50 he leaves; 9:30 Nance in the lane, sent away; 9:50 Morwenna leaves the quiz (end of music round); 10:05-10:20 Imogen at the Bindery; 10:30 hooded woman enters (Tamsin sees from the loft); ~10:45 Aubrey killed; Morwenna burns the pastedowns, locks up ~11:15; Dot passes ~11:00 and sees the window dark. Jago is at Penhallow Court 9:30 till midnight.
- Fri 14 July body found 6:50am. Fair Fri-Sun. Sat 15 break-in. Thu 20 quiz; after the quiz lock-in, 10:55pm, Pieter pushed. Fri 21 Kit arrested. Sat 22 regatta; low water 10:40, high water about 4:50pm; gig race at noon; prize-giving 3pm. Thu 20 July low water is about 9:25pm, so at 11pm the tide is a young flood over mud.
- "K. – cat. 114" is Kit's own catalogue number for the rutter in the Penhallow valuation: Aubrey noted that Kit flagged it. It is not an accusation.
- Distances: Bindery to Lantern 4 minutes downhill; to church hall 6; to Penhallow Court 15 on foot.
- Who knows what: nobody except Morwenna knows she can bind. Dot never read Ned's notebook; In May Dot left Ned's notebook with Tamsin for an afternoon (a school project on old trades; Dot cannot bear Ned's handwriting), and Tamsin copied the pupil list and the Regatta Book spec without grasping them. Tamsin began sleeping in the net loft only in June. Pieter wakes on Fri 21 July but speaks only on Sat 22 morning.
