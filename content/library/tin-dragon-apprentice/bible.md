# Bible: The Tin Dragon's Apprentice (for writers only)

## Logline and premise

Sixteen-year-old canal mechanic Odile Marchetti becomes apprentice to the Dragon-Warden of Gantry because three better candidates quit, then discovers that the forty-foot tin dragon heating her city is a caged living sea-drake that wants only to see the sea.

**What happens, in order.** Nov 3: Odile, in hock to the Fuel Commission over her father's barge licence, is bullied into the apprenticeship. Nov 5: she asks the sleeping dragon "Does that hurt?" and it answers. Old Ferrous only answers sincere questions, never orders, which is why the three candidates (who gave commands) heard nothing, and why Odile hears everything. Warden Bastien Orlowe, who lulls it nightly, is horrified: nineteen years ago it woke, walked toward the water, and he talked it back by promising the sea, then lulled it anyway on the Guilds' orders. Commissioner Hartwell Gast wants it dismantled for scrap and for his Gast Cells (a hundred small stoves fed from its ember, which would kill it), because coal now costs the poor more than they can pay. Odile finds a torn Founders' Ledger page: founder Tibor Hask did not build the dragon, he caged a wild sea-drake caught in the estuary 212 years ago inside a tin shell (the Cage), sealed by five rivets. Midpoint (Dec 1, the Winter Assay): the awakened dragon, starved by the Cage, unintentionally drains warmth from everyone within the Reach; Pim collapses. Freeing it is dangerous, but the cure for the hunger is the sea's warm current. The crew steals four of the five rivet spanners, loses them and Dario and Bastien to Gast, are tried, and escape. On the night of Dec 20-21 they float the dragon down the Great Cut, open the Tidegate in a flood Gast's sabotage turns deadly, and Odile gives her own warmth to hold the dragon steady until the salt water touches the Cage at the Bar. The shell opens; the sea-drake swims free, saves Gast from drowning, and leaves a spark of heart-fire in the empty shell, now the Bar Beacon, on new terms: Gantry keeps the channel open and stops charming coal into it; the drake returns each Long Night to talk. Odile becomes Warden alongside Bastien.

## World and setting

**Gantry**: smoke-and-slate port city on the Brack, a brackish estuary five miles from open sea. Soot-snow falls from November (fine grey flakes from the furnace stacks). Brass pipes, riveted steam mains, gaslamps, tide-clocks on every square, pigeon post, canal barges. Year 212 of the Kindling. Currency: crowns (20 bits each). A day's labour is about 2 crowns; a sack of coal is 9 bits.

**Districts and places.** *The Furnace Cathedral*: slate-and-iron nave on Ember Hill, doors forty feet high, warm to the touch outside; Ferrous lies coiled around the central Hearth Pit. *Cinder Row*: poor, low-lying streets downhill, heated by shared steam standpipes; first to feel the Reach. *Pennywhistle Lock*: where Odile starts; her barge *Marguerite's Patience* is moored at Number Four Basin. *The Great Cut*: straight canal, 2.5 miles, Ember Hill to the Tidegate, 40 ft wide, 9 ft deep. *The Tidegate*: huge sluice-gate keeping the Brack out of the Cut, wound by capstan, tended by Lorcan. *The Bar*: sandbank and open sea three miles below the Tidegate. *The Charmery*: spell-engineers' college, green-glass lanterns, Provost Eberhard Vaszary. *Hall of Rivets*: Boiler-Wrights' Guild. *Gast Hall*: Commission headquarters, marble, new.

**Rules and costs.** *Charms* are chalk-and-solder glyphs; the caster pays in body heat, called the Chill (grey lips, numb hands, cold-sick for days if overspent). Casters wear wool and carry hot-bricks. *The Lulling*: a nightly charm at midnight at the Ember Bell, which keeps Ferrous dozing; it costs Bastien's warmth, so his hands are always cold. *Awake rule*: a waking dragon is hungry and draws warmth from living things within the Reach (about 400 yards). Bound, it starves; the Reach is a symptom of the Cage. *Hear rule*: Ferrous answers only sincere questions; once it answers someone, that person can keep hearing it, through the bellows-throat, as a low voice in the floor. *The Cage*: tin shell, 206 jaw rivets, five Binding Rivets at head, tail, and each flank. Each takes one of five Spanners, turned together at the same moment, and only when the Cage is wetted by seawater.

**Glossary.** Hearth Ticket (rationed heat chit); standpipe; hot-brick; Quarter-Day (quarterly rent and licence day); Long Night (Dec 21 festival of lamps); the Assay (public winter heat test); Gast Cell (Gast's patent stove); Hundred Hearths Scheme (his plan); the Ember (the heart-fire); the Kindling (calendar era); lanternman (lamp-lighter); the Goose (Marguerite's Patience's brass whistle); three-knock (father's valve trick: three taps with a spanner handle frees a stuck gland); cotter pin (Odile pins her braids with them); tide-clock (shows tide state, not hour); soot-snow; bits and crowns; Founders' Ledger; lullcoal (sleepy-smelling charmed coal); Warden's Spanner.

## Cast

**Odile Marchetti** (16, narrator). Called Odile; "dear" makes her bristle. Two tight braids pinned with cotter pins; a comma-shaped burn scar on her left wrist; gap between her front teeth. Speaks fast and dry, in lists of three, answers insults with measurements. "Eleven inches of pipe, nine of excuses." Wants to prove she is worth more than her debt. Fears being ordinary and the barge being repossessed. Hides how deep the debt is. Arc: from fixing machines to asking after the people inside them.

**Bastien Orlowe** (52, Dragon-Warden). Ex-sapper. Grey moustache stained by tobacco; two brass fingers on his left hand; limps on a bad right knee; hands always cold. Speaks in orders, then apologises sideways ("Boots. Off. ... The floor is cold. Wear the socks."). Wants the dragon safe and asleep. Fears waking it again. Hides that he promised it the sea and broke the promise. Knows the Lulling is an order, and that orders cannot reach the dragon, only a sincere question under it. Arc: stops lulling and keeps the promise.

**Old Ferrous** (212, dragon). Forty feet, pewter-bright tin scales, a clouded blue glass left eye, a boiler-heart glowing orange behind the ribs. Speaks slowly, formal, no contractions, with questions of its own, counting things. "I do not know the word salt. Is it large?" Wants the sea. Fears being unmade and hurting people again. Hides the Frost Strike's real cost: three people frostbitten. Arc: from captive to guest to free creature making a bargain.

**Hartwell Gast** (58, Fuel Commissioner). Plump, immaculate, bowler hat, pince-nez, smells of bergamot; warm handshake. Speaks in committee phrases: "Let us be sensible." Calls Odile "my dear." Wants cheap heat for the poor and his name on it. Fears the cold he grew up in. Hides the forged draw on the Guild's funds that pays for the Cells. Arc: stays proud, but is saved by the dragon and learns her name.

**Ottoline Brandt** (71, Guildmistress, Boiler-Wrights). Tiny, ear trumpet, white hair in a bun held with two chisels. Barks. Wants the guild's coal contracts. Fears being the last to run it. Hides that the Guild has always known about the Cage. Arc: gives up her spanner.

**Casimir Vaszary** (19, Charmery student). Tall, floppy blond hair, silk cravats in an absurd range of colours, a chilblained nose. Over-speaks: "Admittedly. Obviously. Inevitably." Wants to be taken seriously. Fears being ordinary without his uncle's name. Hides that he quit because the dragon spoke to him and he fled. Arc: from vain to brave.

**Fenwick Mott** (63, head stoker). Huge, bald, forearms like hams, knits waistcoats for the Cathedral pigeons. Slow gentle speech: "Well, now. Well, now." Runs a soup boiler in the vestry. Wants everyone fed. Fears being fired. Hides nothing, which is his weapon.

**Pim Rooke** (11, messenger boy). Freckled, missing one front tooth, a jacket six sizes too big, carries a satchel of coloured tickets. Fast chatter, sells everything including secrets. Wants a pair of boots and a room. Fears Cinder Row's winters. Hides that Gast pays him for reports. Arc: confesses, becomes a double agent, testifies.

**Lorcan Kessler** (41, Tidegate keeper and tug captain). Broad, sunburned in winter, a pipe he never lights, tug *Persimmon*. Laconic, dry: "Tide doesn't care." Wants his permits. Fears the sea. Arc: opens the gate.

**Dario Marchetti** (48, Odile's father). Thin, stooped, big laugh, grease under every nail, wears his late wife's silver thimble on a string. Gentle, jokes when scared. Wants Odile out of his debts. Hides the size of the note (320 crowns). Arc: stops shielding her; helps with the barge.

## Voice guide

First person, past tense, Odile telling this a few years later, with her mechanic's eye: every machine is named by part and torque. Register: quick, funny, precise, warm under the sarcasm. Sentences vary: long tumbling ones with lists, then short ones. Humour from understatement and exact numbers. Dialogue in double quotes, new paragraph for each speaker; the dragon's speech also in plain quotes, with no contractions. Thoughts in italics sparingly. Chapters mix talk (a third to half), set piece, and quiet.

**Sample paragraph (not part of the novel).** The Cathedral smelled the way a new boiler smells the first time you light it: of hot dust and of something that had been waiting a long time to be asked. I stood in the nave holding a broom, which is a ridiculous thing to hold in front of a forty-foot dragon, and counted the rivets in its jaw, because counting is what I do when I would rather run. Two hundred and six. Warden Orlowe pretended not to watch me. I pretended not to be frightened. The dragon, as far as I could tell, was pretending to be a statue, and doing it better than either of us. Somebody had to blink first, and my tea was going cold.

**Odile would never write:** (1) "shimmering", "ethereal" or any gushing magic adjective; (2) an emotion named flat ("I felt sad") instead of shown by action; (3) "dear reader" or foreshadowing asides; (4) modern words (okay, computer, scientist, adrenaline); (5) a machine described without naming a part; (6) a speech about destiny or being chosen; (7) a villain explaining his scheme; (8) a joke followed by an explanation of the joke.

## Motifs

Hands and warmth (cold hands, mittens, the Chill). Questions ("Ask me another"). Tide-clocks showing the water, not the hour. Soot-snow. Rivets and spanners. The word *salt*. Lamps (lanternmen; Long Night). The thimble.

## Timeline and ground rules

Year 212: Dragon caged when Tibor Hask lured a wild sea-drake up the Brack on a spring tide and sealed the Cage with five rivets, 212 years ago. Nineteen years ago (Bastien 33): the Frost Strike; Ferrous woke, walked to the Great Cut's end, split the first Tidegate wall; Bastien talked it back by promising the sea, three people suffered frostbite; Guilds ordered the Lulling made nightly. Odile born 16 years ago; mother Marguerite died four years ago of lung fever.

Story: Nov 3 Quarter-Day, Odile appointed. Nov 5 first speech. Nov 8 city market. Nov 10 Gast's visit. Nov 16 inside the ribs. Nov 22 chalk lesson. Nov 25 Charmery stacks. Dec 1 Assay and Cinder Row chill. Dec 4 Bastien's full story at the Tidegate. Dec 6 Pim confesses. Dec 9 the plan. Dec 14 Provost's Lantern night. Dec 16 Ottoline. Dec 18 Gast Hall Gala. Dec 19 low point. Dec 20 Inquest. Night Dec 20-21 the passage; spring high tide 3:10 a.m. Dec 21 (Long Night); dawn 8:05. Epilogue the following spring.

Distances: Cathedral to Great Cut 0.3 mile; Cut 2.5 miles; Tidegate to the Bar 3 miles. A pontoon raft with the dragon makes 2 knots, but the Cut is 2.8 miles from the Cathedral, so allow about 1h45 to the gate (leaving 12:30 a.m., arriving about 2:30 with the boom delay). The tug then needs until about 8 a.m. to reach the Bar through debris and slack water; sunrise is 8:05. The spanners are five: Warden's (Bastien), Charmery's (Provost), Guild's (Ottoline), Commission's (Gast), Harbour's (Lorcan). Who knows what: Bastien and Ottoline know of the Cage (Ottoline since taking office); Gast knows nothing of the Cage; he believes it a machine. He holds the Guild's spanner as loan collateral (hinted ch 6, used ch 18) and has the Commission's in his strongroom without knowing what they are. The Guild handbook records the rule: five spanners together, on wet metal. The dragon remembers the rivets going in wet (ch 14). Casimir heard it once, whispering "Is anyone there?" alone in the nave, and fled; he hears it again from ch 17. Gast hears it first at ch 12 but dismisses it as a charm trick.
