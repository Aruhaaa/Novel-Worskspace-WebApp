# Outline: The Tin Dragon's Apprentice

All chapters: first person, Odile, past tense. Refrains: "Ask me another"; Gast's "my dear"; cold hands; Fenwick's soup boiler.

## Chapter 01: The Seized Valve
Place and when: Pennywhistle Lock and Number Four Basin, Nov 3, Quarter-Day, sleet. Purpose: hook Odile's voice, her skill and her debt, and force her into the post.
1. Odile, arm in a lock-gate gearbox, fixes a seized valve with the three-knock while the lock-keeper insults her.
2. Pim arrives with a pink ticket: the Commission wants her at Gast Hall by noon.
3. Clerk Anselm Roux offers the Dragon-Warden's apprenticeship: three candidates quit, and unless the Cathedral has an apprentice by sundown the Quarter-Day heat tithe lapses.
4. She refuses; Roux produces her father's 320-crown note and offers to freeze interest.
5. Gast himself passes in the corridor: "Splendid, my dear." She bristles.
6. Her father Dario laughs it off on the barge, hiding the figure on the note; plant: he wears Marguerite's thimble.
7. At dusk she climbs Ember Hill. The Cathedral doors are warm as bread.
Closing beat: a cloudy blue eye, high in the dark, opens a crack and shuts. She tells herself it was a rivet catching light.

## Chapter 02: Cold Hands, Warm Tea
Place and when: Furnace Cathedral, Nov 3-4, night.
Purpose: introduce Bastien, Fenwick, the Lulling and its cost.
1. Bastien meets her with boots-off orders and a limp; his brass fingers click on the teacup.
2. Fenwick's vestry soup boiler (plant: fat enough to hide five spanners) and his knitted pigeon waistcoats.
3. Bastien's rules: never touch the Hearth Pit rail, never order the dragon, never skip the midnight Lulling.
4. She notes his hands: blue at the nails, always cold.
5. A tour of the nave: the 206 jaw rivets, which she counts.
6. Casimir's discarded cravat and gloves in a locker; the previous candidates' names scratched inside.
7. Midnight: Bastien chalks and chants at the Ember Bell.
Closing beat: from the Hearth Pit comes a sound like a pot lid being lifted, then a small "no". Bastien does not stop chanting; she is the only one who notices he flinched.

## Chapter 03: A Question for the Furnace
Place and when: Cathedral, Nov 5, night; the inciting incident.
Purpose: the dragon speaks to her.
1. Day two: sweeping ash, black coal, the cheap work. Fenwick counts the coal hoppers: six tons a day; the poor get Hearth Tickets and queue.
2. Odile fixes the left jaw hinge on a ladder, talking to herself as she always does.
3. She asks, idly, "Does that hurt?" because it is what you ask any machine that squeals.
4. A voice like warm gravel answers: "Yes. Thank you for asking."
5. She drops her wrench; it rolls under the dragon; she does not fetch it.
6. They talk: it has no name it remembers; it calls itself "the one who is kept."
7. It asks "What is the salt?" and she does not know.
Closing beat: "Again?" it says. "Ask me another." She backs out of the nave, and the wrench stays where it fell. Plant: the dragon only answers questions.

## Chapter 04: The Hinge of the Jaw
Place and when: Cathedral, Nov 6, pre-dawn and day.
Purpose: show the friendship's first ease and Bastien's fear.
1. She returns for her wrench at 4 a.m. and asks the dragon three questions; each answer is odder than the last.
2. Comedy: she tests: "Stop the clock." Nothing. "What would you like to be called?" A pause, then a rumbling "Not Ferrous."
3. She repairs the sticking jaw hinge with a cotter pin from her braid; plant: pins.
4. Fenwick walks in and hears nothing, only Odile addressing a hinge.
5. Bastien arrives, sees the wrench by the Pit, and goes pale. He demands: "What did you say to it?"
6. She tells him; he tells her she is lying; she repeats the exact words.
7. He sits on the Hearth Pit rail, which he forbade her to touch.
Closing beat: "Who else have you told?" She says nobody. He says: "Keep it that way, or they will put you in the Pit with it."

## Chapter 05: Soot-Snow Market
Place and when: Cinder Row and the tide-clock market, Nov 8.
Purpose: show the city, the coal crisis, Pim, and Casimir's return.
1. Odile goes for hinge-grease and sees the Hearth Ticket queue in soot-snow; a woman rations her baby's blanket.
2. Pim sells her a map of every standpipe and tells her coal is up to 14 bits.
3. Cinder Row knows her: Marguerite's Patience has hauled their coal free every bad Quarter-Day, and the Goose has meant "help is coming" to the Row for ten years (plant). Bargaining with a stall holder; the tide-clocks all disagree and she fixes one for a pie.
4. Casimir, shivering, arrives in a lime cravat, announces he will take back his place: "Obviously, the post is mine."
5. They fight about who deserves it; she calls him "pretty"; he calls her "dear".
6. Cold hits Cinder Row: a short, slow chill, a standpipe pale with frost. (Plant: the Reach.)
7. Pim passes a folded note to a bowler-hatted man, unnoticed by Odile.
Closing beat: Odile counts the marks on the standpipe: eleven where the frost has reached, in a perfect circle around Ember Hill.

## Chapter 06: The Reasonable Man
Place and when: Cathedral and the Hall of Rivets steps, Nov 10.
Purpose: introduce Gast as sympathetic, with a real argument.
1. Gast arrives with surveyors, praises Fenwick's waistcoats, shakes everyone's hand warmly.
2. In the nave he lays out the Hundred Hearths Scheme: dismantle the dragon, melt 80 tons of tin, build the Gast Cells so every street has a stove. Costs: coal is ruining the poor.
3. Bastien and Ottoline Brandt (arrived with her ear trumpet) object; she shouts, he is polite and wins the room.
4. Gast tells Odile in private that he grew up in a cold cellar in Cinder Row, and that "my dear" is only warmth.
5. He drops that the Guild of Boiler-Wrights owes the Commission a great deal and he is "holding some collateral" (plant). He offers to erase her father's note if she reports on the Warden.
6. She refuses; he says "Think it over." Ottoline eavesdropping says only: "Don't."
7. The dragon, listening, asks her later: "What is scrap?"
Closing beat: Odile answers truthfully. The Ember glow dims two shades.

## Chapter 07: Inside the Ribs
Place and when: inside Old Ferrous, Nov 16, night.
Purpose: set piece; reveal the Cage's structure.
1. The left foreleg seizes; Bastien orders it repaired before the Lulling or Ferrous may wake in pain.
2. Odile and Fenwick climb through a hatch behind the shoulder; Fenwick brings a lamp and soup in a flask.
3. Interior: ribs of tin plate, copper veins, the Ember glowing like a lit stove behind a grille, and a cold spot nearer the heart.
4. Dragon's voice inside: warm and louder; she asks directions; it counts her steps.
5. She finds five heavy bolts (head, tail, flanks) with keyholes shaped for spanners, not for wrenches.
6. The foreleg gland is fixed with her three-knock.
7. Scratched on a rib in tiny letters: "T. HASK. FORGIVE ME."
Closing beat: Fenwick reads it aloud, takes off his cap, and says nothing for the first time since she met him. The dragon asks: "What does forgive mean?"

## Chapter 08: What the Warden Left Out
Place and when: the Cathedral vestry roof, Nov 19, wet dawn.
Purpose: Bastien's first half-confession.
1. Odile confronts Bastien about the rib scratch and the five keyholes.
2. He deflects with orders; she keeps repairing his stove to make him listen.
3. He tells her about the Frost Strike nineteen years ago: it woke, walked to the Great Cut, cracked a Tidegate wall; he stopped it by talking.
4. He does not tell her about the promise.
5. She tells him it asked what salt is. He puts his tea down very slowly.
6. A comic interlude: Fenwick brings soup and a pigeon in a waistcoat, and both pretend not to cry.
7. Bastien says the Guilds will never allow it near water, and neither will he.
Closing beat: Odile notices his brass fingers have made dents in the cup. She decides to find out what the Founders knew. Plant: Ledger.

## Chapter 09: Chalk and Chill
Place and when: Charmery practice yard and the barge, Nov 22, evening.
Purpose: show magic's cost; father's debt; Casimir as comic ally.
1. Casimir, swallowing pride, agrees to teach her a warming charm; chalk-and-solder demonstration with cravat flourishes.
2. She casts a tiny glyph to warm the dragon's eye; her fingers go grey. Casimir fetches a hot-brick, scolding, and then rubbing her hands.
3. The cost explained: every charm borrows body heat from the caster.
4. Odile goes home to Marguerite's Patience; Dario coughs; the thimble.
5. She sees a Commission letter on the stove: final notice, licence forfeit on the Long Night unless 320 crowns are paid.
6. Dario jokes, then admits it; they eat kippers in silence.
7. Odile resolves to say nothing and win. Plant: the Long Night forfeiture date, and the thimble, which Dario later pawns and she later redeems.
Closing beat: she warms her father's hands with her own, as she once warmed her mother's.

## Chapter 10: The Founders' Stacks
Place and when: the Charmery library, Nov 25, night.
Purpose: comedy set piece and first real clue.
1. Odile and Casimir sneak in by the coal chute; his uncle the Provost's lantern-lit stacks.
2. The Founders' Ledger is kept in a glass case guarded by a clockwork warden, a brass owl on a rail.
3. Comic chase: Casimir's cravat is used as a lasso, and the owl hoots on a rail across the gallery.
4. Ottoline Brandt arrives to read a ledger of her own, catches them, and lets them go, saying "I was never here."
5. The Ledger has pages torn out, all from the entries for the Kindling year.
6. Odile finds one page wedged in the binding: a drawing of a creature with scales and a clouded left eye, labelled "Brack-drake, caught."
7. They flee through the coal chute, soot-black.
Closing beat: in the lamplight outside, Casimir looks at the drawing, says "Obviously this is wrong," and then goes quiet.

## Chapter 11: A Page Torn Out
Place and when: the Cathedral, Nov 26, night.
Purpose: revelation: the Cage; Odile's moral turn.
1. She shows the dragon the drawing without knowing what she asked.
2. Dragon: "That is the shape I was before." It remembers the sound of waves, not the sight.
3. It remembers being called into the estuary on a spring tide by lamps and singing, and the five rivets, and the cold.
4. It asks again what the salt is; she guesses: tears, soup, the sea.
5. Fenwick's lamp swings in the vestry; they are nearly caught.
6. Odile realises she is the first person to learn the Cage was a trap, other than the Guilds.
7. She lies to Bastien at breakfast: "Nothing new."
Closing beat: the dragon says, very softly, "You do not have to promise." She promises nothing, but takes the page into her pocket where it burns against her ribs.

## Chapter 12: The Winter Assay
Place and when: Furnace Cathedral, Dec 1, noon, public.
Purpose: set piece; Gast's test goes wrong.
1. A crowd of Councillors, guildsmen and journalists fill the nave; Hearth Tickets for front rows.
2. Gast's engineers bore a tap into the dragon's flank to measure the heat for the Cells.
3. The dragon wakes in pain, the nave shakes, steam, a roar like a boiler letting go.
4. Odile, forbidden, climbs the rail and asks it quietly: "Can you tell me where it hurts?"
5. It answers aloud, through the bellows, for the whole nave to hear. A gasp.
6. She talks it back down by asking, one question at a time.
7. Gast stands very still, looking at her, with his pince-nez off.
Closing beat: outside, the Cathedral windows are rimed on the inside. Pim runs past down the hill toward Cinder Row, and the clock above the doors reads the wrong hour.

## Chapter 13: Cold Comes to Cinder Row
Place and when: Cinder Row and Ember Hill Infirmary, Dec 1-2.
Purpose: MIDPOINT REVERSAL. The dragon is a victim and also a danger.
1. In the street: fourteen people lie on the cobbles, grey-lipped, shivering; Pim among them, small and rigid.
2. Odile runs for Fenwick's soup, hot-bricks and a barrow; she carries Pim up the hill herself.
3. In the infirmary, a nurse explains: the same cold-sick as spent casters. The Reach had drawn their warmth.
4. Bastien's face: this is what he was lulling it against.
5. Odile confronts the dragon; it is silent, then ashamed: "I did not choose. I was hungry. I am always hungry."
6. It says the sea is warm. There is so much warm there. In the Cage it starves, and out of the Cage it would not take from any one.
7. Gast at the infirmary door, newly grave, announces the dismantling is advanced to dawn on Long Night.
Closing beat: Odile no longer sees the dragon as a prisoner only. She sits beside Pim's bed with the torn page and chooses anyway.

## Chapter 14: The Frost Strike Told Plainly
Place and when: the Great Cut, the Tidegate and the Cathedral, Dec 4, night.
Purpose: Bastien's full truth; he joins; geography and Lorcan planted.
1. Odile confronts Bastien with the page and Pim's bed; he says nothing, takes a lantern, and walks her down the Great Cut to the Tidegate, limping.
2. At the Tidegate he shows the old crack in the wall, patched with riveted plate, where it stopped nineteen years ago. Lorcan Kessler, tending the capstan with an unlit pipe, tells them to go home; he and Bastien share a silence like two men with one bad memory.
3. Bastien admits the rest: it woke, walked here, begged to see the sea, and he talked it back with a promise: "I will take you." Then the Guilds ordered the Lulling.
4. He explains the Lulling is an order laid on the dragon, and an order is the one thing it cannot answer. Odile sees it: a sincere question might reach underneath (plant for ch 20 and 22).
5. Fenwick meets them back at the Cathedral with soup; all three sit on the Pit rail. Bastien asks whether the dragon could go without hurting anyone if it had warmth enough.
6. The dragon, through Odile, says: on the way it will be hungry. It will need something to hold. Salt, it adds, is what the Founders used to shut it in: the rivets went in wet and must come out wet (plant).
7. Bastien agrees on a condition: if it takes anyone's warmth against their will, he lulls it for good.
Closing beat: he takes off his glove and puts his cold brass hand against its flank. "Nineteen years," he says. "I am sorry." The dragon answers: "Ask me another." Bastien laughs, a cracked sound.

## Chapter 15: Pim Sings
Place and when: Ember Hill Infirmary and the roofs of the Cathedral, Dec 6.
Purpose: Pim's confession, a chase, and the double-agent plan; action after two talky chapters.
1. Pim, recovered but coughing, slips out of the infirmary; Odile, who has noticed the bowler-hatted man at the Cathedral gate twice, follows him up the vestry roof.
2. He is tying a note to a pigeon. She grabs; he bolts across the slate roofs in soot-snow, Odile after him, the Goose-whistle on her belt, a gutter giving way under her.
3. She catches him at the lead flat above the nave; he breaks: Gast pays two bits per report and promised him boots.
4. She is furious; Bastien wants him gone, and Odile weighs the betrayal against his cough and the fact that he is eleven.
5. Fenwick suggests feeding Gast false information; they invent a decoy: the crew means to move the dragon on Dec 22, after the dismantling is "stopped".
6. The pigeon code: red ring means true, green means a lie. Plant: Pim keeps his Hearth Tickets sorted by colour, which later helps at the Gala.
7. Pim writes the first false report; the dragon asks him how it is to be so small, and he asks the dragon how it is to be so big.
Closing beat: Pim sends the green-ringed pigeon into the soot-snow, and Odile finds she has forgiven him without noticing when.

## Chapter 16: Borrowed Tools
Place and when: Marguerite's Patience, Dec 9, night.
Purpose: assemble the crew; the plan; Dario's choice.
1. Odile gathers Casimir, Fenwick, Bastien and Pim (in a blanket, now trusted, chatty) on the barge. Dario's thimble is missing from his neck; she notices and says nothing.
2. She chalks a map of the Great Cut and Tidegate. Plan: the spring tide at 3:10 a.m. on Long Night; the Cut is 2.5 miles, about an hour and three-quarters at a raft's pace, then the Brack to the Bar.
3. The five spanners and why: Warden's (Bastien), Charmery's (Provost), Guild's (Ottoline), Commission's (Gast), Harbour's (Lorcan). Bastien: all five turned together, wetted, as the dragon said.
4. Debate and comedy: Casimir wants a ladder, Fenwick wants a stew, Pim wants boots. Odile assigns the jobs.
5. Dario comes home mid-planning; hears everything.
6. He refuses to let the barge be used. Odile says it is her barge as much as his.
7. They argue; neither speaks of the note or the thimble.
Closing beat: Dario, at the hatch, says "I'll lend the barge. Not my daughter." She says "I'm not yours to lend", and then puts her arms around him.

## Chapter 17: The Provost's Lantern
Place and when: the Charmery, Dec 14, Lantern Examination night.
Purpose: first heist, comic; Casimir's courage.
1. The Charmery's Examination is the one night all lanterns are lit and the Provost's study is empty.
2. Casimir goes in as a candidate in full robes; Odile as a waitress with a tray of tea.
3. The Provost's spanner is in a lantern-glass case with a charm that chills thieves.
4. Casimir confesses the reason he quit: alone in the nave at night he whispered "Is anyone there?", it said "Yes", and he ran.
5. He asks it a real question this time; it answers, and Casimir stays to listen.
6. Casimir takes the Chill himself to break the charm, losing feeling in his hands, using his cravat as a pad.
7. They slip out with the spanner past the owl.
Closing beat: Casimir drops the spanner into Odile's hand, shaking, and says, "Well. That was inevitable." His lips are blue.

## Chapter 18: Ottoline's Terms
Place and when: the Hall of Rivets and the Cathedral, Dec 16.
Purpose: Ottoline's turn and a complication.
1. Odile asks Ottoline Brandt for the Guild's spanner. She barks and refuses: the guild lives on coal contracts.
2. Ottoline reveals the Guilds have always known about the Cage.
3. She adds that the Guild keeps the rule in its master's handbook: five spanners, turned together, on wet metal. Odile brings her to the Cathedral; she lifts the ear trumpet to the dragon's jaw.
4. The dragon asks her a sincere question: "Did you ever hear the sea?" She cries, and tells it about her childhood on the Bar.
5. She promises her spanner on one condition: the Guild keeps its stoker posts.
6. She writes a note, then reveals that the spanner is not in her hall; Gast called in the Guild loan (see ch 6) and took it as collateral into his vault.
7. They must take two from Gast's strongroom.
Closing beat: Ottoline hands Odile the vault key-card, a faded Guild badge, and says, "He keeps his sums in the same place as his shoes."

## Chapter 19: The Gala at Gast Hall
Place and when: Gast Hall, Dec 18, evening.
Purpose: big heist set piece.
1. Gast hosts a Gala unveiling the first Gast Cell; Councillors, bands, champagne, Hearth Tickets as door prizes.
2. Odile, Casimir and Pim go in as caterers; Fenwick is a footman; Bastien holds the cloakroom.
3. Pim reads Gast's schedule from the ticket box; the decoy date has held.
4. A mix-up with a tray of kippers causes a diversion; the band plays through it.
5. The strongroom: two spanners in a case, behind a steam-valve lock that Odile three-knocks open.
6. Gast catches her on the stair, kindly: "My dear, you look lost." She says her name is Odile. He lets her pass, unwitting, then sees the empty case.
7. Constables storm the cloakroom; Bastien is arrested.
Closing beat: Odile reaches the barge with four spanners, Dario's arrest note nailed to the door, and no Bastien.

## Chapter 20: Silence in the Nave
Place and when: Cathedral and Fenwick's vestry, Dec 19.
Purpose: LOW POINT. The plan collapses.
1. Gast's constables seal the Cathedral. Odile, finding the arrest note on the barge door on the night of the Gala, had run the four spanners to Fenwick's soup boiler (her choice; plant ch 2); the constables search everything but the soup. The fifth, Lorcan's, is untouched.
2. Dario, arrested for the 320-crown note, is in the debtors' ward.
3. Bastien charged with theft and sedition.
4. Gast brings hired charmers who recast the Lulling at triple strength; the dragon goes silent.
5. Odile begs; no answer. Fenwick leaves soup. Casimir leaves his cravat.
6. Pim delivers news that the dismantling is not moved; it stays at dawn on Long Night, as Gast promised.
7. The Cathedral is cold for the first time in 212 years, and Fenwick's pigeons huddle in their waistcoats on the Bell. Payoff: the hidden spanners wait in the soup.
Closing beat: alone, in the dark, Odile talks to the dragon anyway, asking it questions with no hope of answers. After an hour, a very small voice: "Ask me another."

## Chapter 21: The Inquest of the Engine
Place and when: the Court of Charters, Dec 20, day.
Purpose: the trial.
1. Odile, Bastien (in manacles) and Fenwick are tried for theft and sedition before the Council.
2. Gast's advocate argues the dragon is property.
3. Casimir stands up as a character witness and reveals what the dragon said to him.
4. Pim testifies that Gast paid him to spy, and the courtroom murmurs; Gast stays reasonable.
5. Ottoline reveals the Founders' page and the Guilds' long knowledge.
6. Odile argues: you cannot own what can answer a question.
7. Gast, still reasonable, offers Odile a last chance to recant; she declines, naming the Cell's true cost. The Council votes 5 to 4 to uphold the dismantling at dawn; Odile and Bastien are remanded but the crowd is shouting.
Closing beat: Dario, out on parole thanks to a Guild payment, sits in the gallery. Odile catches his eye and taps three times on the dock rail, the three-knock; he goes white, then nods. He will blow the Goose.

## Chapter 22: Lamps Along Cinder Row
Place and when: Cinder Row and Ember Hill, night of Dec 20.
Purpose: the escape and the march.
1. Odile's plan, whispered to Fenwick and Pim in the gallery: the remand wagon crosses Cinder Row Bridge at dusk, and Fenwick's soup cart and Pim's ticket-runners tangle it. Odile and Bastien tumble out.
2. Fenwick lifts the four spanners from the soup boiler; Dario, on the Goose, calls the Row.
3. In the Cathedral, Odile reaches under the triple Lulling (ch 14, 20) with a sincere question, "Do you want to go?" It answers "Yes."
4. Bastien opens the nave doors; the dragon walks.
5. The Goose sounds (planted ch 5); Cinder Row opens every window; families line the street with lamps and hot-bricks, giving warmth to ease the Reach.
6. Gast's Fuel Guard blocks the Row; the dragon, hungry, slows.
7. Odile asks it to hold on; Casimir links hands in a chain of warm bodies, and the dragon takes only what is offered.
Closing beat: the dragon steps into the Great Cut, its tin belly sluicing, and the lamps along the Row reflect in the black water like a second street.

## Chapter 23: The Great Cut
Place and when: the Great Cut, Dec 21, 12:30 to 2:30 a.m.
Purpose: the passage and the obstacle.
1. The dragon is floated on pontoon rafts lashed to Marguerite's Patience; Dario and Odile run the engines.
2. Soot-snow, lamps, bridges rattling as they pass.
3. A Commission boom of chained barges blocks the Cut at Gaol Bridge.
4. Odile and Dario work out a way: reverse the engines, use the barge's rudder hinge as a lever, and Casimir's lantern charm cuts the chain.
5. The Reach follows: the barge's crew grow cold; Bastien keeps feeding Odile hot tea.
6. At the Tidegate, the gate is locked.
7. Lorcan stands in the sluice-house with the Harbour spanner and a pipe. He will not open it; his permits depend on Gast.
Closing beat: Odile does not argue. She asks: "What do you think the sea would say to you?" Lorcan takes the pipe out of his mouth.

## Chapter 24: Tidegate
Place and when: the Tidegate and the Brack, Dec 21, 2:30 to 3:30 a.m.
Purpose: the flood.
1. Lorcan opens the capstan; the Brack roars in, the Cut's level surges.
2. Gast, who followed in a rowing boat to forbid the opening, had earlier ordered the gate wheel and lower sluice jammed to keep the dragon in the Cut. Lorcan forced the capstan, so the surge cannot drain and now threatens Cinder Row. Gast never meant that.
3. The dragon is torn loose from its raft; the barge is thrown against the wall.
4. Odile and Bastien must choose: stop the flood, or reach the Bar. The dragon says: "Hold the water. I can."
5. The dragon throws its coils across the breach like a dam, drawing hard on everyone's warmth.
6. Odile gives her own, hands on its jaw, as the Chill climbs her arms. Bastien gives his too, then Casimir, then Fenwick.
7. Gast's rowing boat is capsized by the surge and he is swept into the water.
Closing beat: the flood falls back. The dragon, shaking, asks: "Is that what warm is?" Odile cannot speak; her lips have gone grey.

## Chapter 25: The Bar at Dawn
Place and when: the Brack and the Bar, Dec 21, 3:30 to 8:05 a.m.
Purpose: climax: the sea.
1. Lorcan's tug *Persimmon* tows the dragon down the Brack against debris and slack water, reaching the Bar near 8 a.m.; Odile lies wrapped in blankets, half-frozen.
2. The dragon refuses to feed on anyone further; Bastien holds its jaw.
3. Gast, clinging to wreckage near the tug, is lifted out by the dragon's own coil; it saves him although it is starving. He says, "Why?" It says: "You asked."
4. At the Bar, the first salt wave touches the Cage. The five rivets glow.
5. The five spanners, turned together by Bastien, Casimir, Fenwick, Lorcan and Odile, with Gast, silent, holding the lamp.
6. The shell opens; a long bright creature of living flame and steam lifts into the surf.
7. It speaks one word: "Salt."
Closing beat: it tastes the sea, and the sun rises; the empty tin shell sits on the sand, warm.

## Chapter 26: Terms of the Fire
Place and when: Gantry and the Bar, Dec 22 and the following spring.
Purpose: resolution and the last emotional note.
1. The Council hears the terms the sea-drake dictates through Odile: keep the Tidegate open, stop charming coal into it, and it will leave a spark.
2. The shell becomes the Bar Beacon, with a spark of heart-fire in its chest; Gantry's mains are half-powered by tide engines.
3. Gast resigns, with grace. He addresses Odile by name; she lets him.
4. Dario's note is paid by the Guild, and Odile redeems his thimble from the pawnbroker with her first Warden's wage; Pim gets boots; Fenwick knits waistcoats for the whole Row.
5. Bastien, warm for the first time, retires to the barge; Odile is made Warden.
6. Spring: the sea-drake returns on a still night; Odile asks it the first question.
7. They talk until dawn.
Closing beat: "Ask me another," it says, and she holds up her hands, warm, and does.
