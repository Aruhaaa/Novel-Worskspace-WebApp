# Continuity log: The Tin Dragon's Apprentice

Each writer appends the facts a chapter establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: The Seized Valve
- Nov 3, Quarter-Day, sleet then soot-snow by four. Odile frees Pennywhistle's lower-gate paddle valve with the three-knock ("first knock introduces you, second makes your case, third is a bluff"); lock-keeper Ludo Platt pays 7 bits.
- Pim Rooke: coalman's jacket, gap at top front tooth, sacking tied on with twine for boots. Ticket colours: pink Commission, white Ticket-huts, blue Council, yellow Tide Office.
- Clerk Anselm Roux, Gast Hall (marble, Treadle Street): wage 4 crowns a month and keep. Earlier candidates: Ignatz Boll (11 days), Perpetua Wray (6), Casimir Vaszary (4); none said why. Odile is "the fifth name on the list".
- Charter: no apprentice on the books by sundown on Quarter-Day and the heat tithe (paid out next day as Hearth Tickets) lapses.
- Dario's note: 320 crowns, secured on Marguerite's Patience and her fuel licence; interest frozen while Odile holds the post. She had been told it was 40. Dario calls it "a Quarter-Day number" and turns the thimble (dented crown) on its string when he fibs.
- Gast: plump, bowler, pince-nez, bergamot, warm dry hands. "Splendid, my dear." Odile's name "has five letters".
- Barge: 70 ft iron, two-cylinder compound engine, brass Goose whistle. Dario hands her a hot-brick in a grey sock.
- Ends at dusk: Cathedral doors warm as bread, wicket ajar; a skimmed-milk eye with blue in it opens a crack high in the dark and shuts. "A rivet catching the light."

## Ch 2: Cold Hands, Warm Tea
- Night of Nov 3-4. Bastien Orlowe: tobacco-pouch moustache, army greatcoat, bad knee, two brass fingers (ring and little, left hand, hand-cut lentil screws), hands cold with blue nails. "Boots. Off." then "Wear the socks" (grey wool, heels darned in two colours).
- Nave floor is dished toward the Pit; rail waist-high; head rests on the highest coil about 12 ft up, tin lid over the clouded left eye. Odile counts 206 jaw rivets (Wray quit counting at 60, Boll at 109).
- Fenwick Mott: huge, bald, seven-colour waistcoat, "Well, now." 41 pigeons, 38 waistcoats; Dorcas (herringbone) bites. His 30-gallon copper vat once swallowed a watch, tongs and Constable Dray's whistle for a Quarter-Day: "a man could drop a small anvil in the bottom".
- Rules: never touch the Pit rail; never give the dragon orders (cover reason: they can cross the Lulling charm); never miss the midnight Lulling.
- Locker three: Casimir's plum cravat, rabbit-lined gloves, violet pastilles, uncracked glyphwork primer; Boll, Wray ("NO") and Vaszary (4 strokes, fifth unfinished) scratched inside. Odile will not scratch hers.
- Lulling at the Ember Bell: chalk-and-solder glyph, lullaby chant, Bastien frosts over; Fenwick's two hot-bricks barely help.
- Ends: a pot-lid scrape and a small "No." from the Pit; Bastien's chalk snaps in his left hand. Only Odile notices.

## Ch 3: A Question for the Furnace
- Nov 5 into 1:30 a.m. Nov 6. Coal: three hoppers of two tons, six tons a day; one shovel in ten is grey lullcoal (hay and cough-syrup smell, makes you yawn). Hearth Ticket = four minutes at a standpipe after an hour's queue.
- Left jaw-hinge pin has walked a quarter-inch out ("eleven winters") because its cotter pin is gone. Odile packs the bush with tallow and graphite from a ladder set outside the rail.
- "Does that hurt?" / "Yes. Thank you for asking." The voice comes up through the floor; orders and statements get no reply, questions do. Nobody has asked it that in 200 winters.
- It calls itself "the one who is kept"; "Ferrous" is the men's word. Bastien "has not asked me anything in nineteen years". It counts everything: 206 rivets, 11,004 slates, her breaths. It learns her name, Odile.
- It asks "What is the salt?" (heard through the floor: "the salt is up", "salt on the gate"). Odile has never seen the sea or been below the Tidegate. "I don't know."
- Her 14-inch ash-handled wrench (father's initials in the grip) rolls to the foot of the Pit rail and stays. Ends: "Ask me another."

## Ch 4: The Hinge of the Jaw
- Nov 6, 4 to 6:30 a.m. Three questions: "Are you awake?" ("the way a kettle is awake before it sings"); "What do you do all night?" ("I count"; the Bell "sounds like being sat upon"); "What would you like to be called?" ("Not Ferrous... Ask me when I do.").
- "Stop the clock" (steam-fed escapement over the nave doors) and other orders do nothing. "Can you stop the clock?" "It is the only one in the building that talks to me at night."
- Odile fixes the left hinge with a cotter pin from her left braid (two pins in her hair, one per braid). "It stops the big thing from walking away." / "From the place where it belongs." / "From the place it's been put."
- Fenwick hears nothing and thinks she is talking to the hinge. Only those the dragon has answered can hear it.
- Bastien reads the Ember a shade brighter, sees the wrench by the rail, goes white, sits on the forbidden rail. She tells him the exact words, not the salt question or "Not Ferrous". Her wrench stays by the rail.
- Ends: "Keep it that way, or they will put you in the Pit with it."

## Ch 5: Soot-Snow Market
- Nov 8, Slake Street and the market under Cinder Row Bridge. Odile talks to the dragon most nights at 4 a.m.; it asks about drains and hot-bricks, not salt again.
- A mother warms her baby's blanket a quarter at a time at a standpipe, counting ninety, never warming herself.
- Pim sells a pencilled map of 131 standpipes for 2 bits and a promise of boots. Coal is 14 bits a sack.
- Hester Pallitt calls Odile "the Goose girl": Dario has hauled free coal to the Row every bad Quarter-Day for ten years; the Goose at the foot of the Row means "somebody's coming". Odile buys grease (4.5 bits) and a dozen 3/16 cotter pins; wonders how much of the 320 crowns was coal.
- Orrin Gunn prices eel pies by the tide; Odile fixes his tide-clock for a pie. The Row's pawnbroker's window holds its only hour-clock.
- Casimir: about 19, yellow hair, lime cravat, chilblained nose, "Obviously, the post is mine." She calls him "pretty", he calls her "dear". He denies anything frightened him ("It is a tin dragon. It says nothing") and blurts "heat-draw".
- A 4 min 10 sec chill; frost climbs the Slake Street standpipe; locals blame the pipes. Pim passes a folded note to a bowler-hatted man unseen by Odile. Ends: eleven frosted standpipes form a ring round Ember Hill.

## Ch 6: The Reasonable Man
- Nov 10. Gast, Roux, surveyors Pettifer and Quane. Gast takes off his boots unasked (silk socks, darned at the heel), praises Dorcas's herringbone, tells Bastien his hands are "like a winter pump".
- The Hundred Hearths: melt the 80 tons of tin, pour a Gast Cell (iron stove with a glass eye in the door) for every street, the Ember "divided". Coal was 9 bits a year ago, 14 now. He thinks the dragon is a boiler ("as if it were a bear").
- Ottoline Brandt: 4 ft 10, ear trumpet, two chisels in her bun, shouts about 1,100 stoker posts. Bastien wins the room quietly and gets the Charter's public Assay on Dec 1, with Gast's measuring-tap in the dragon's flank.
- On the 31 steps of the Hall of Rivets Gast says he grew up in a cellar on Drum Alley (eight children, burned the stairs); "my dear" was his mother's warmth. He still calls Odile "my dear" after she gives her name.
- Gast says the Guild owes the Commission heavily and he is "holding some collateral". He offers to erase Dario's note if Odile reports on the Warden. She refuses; "Think it over, my dear." Ottoline, listening through the door, says only "Don't."
- Night: password "Are you awake?" The dragon asks "What is scrap?" Odile tells it the truth and the Ember dims two shades. She tells nobody of Gast's offer.
