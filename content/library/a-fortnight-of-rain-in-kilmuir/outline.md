# Outline: A Fortnight of Rain in Kilmuir

Odd chapters are Nell's, even chapters Calum's. Closing images never repeat. The song's fourth verse is the book's mystery: its solution, clues and red herrings are at the end.

## Chapter 01: The Last Sailing

Nell. Sat 7 June, the *Morven Lass* and Camas Fada pier, afternoon. Purpose: hook us with her ears and put her on the island just before the door shuts.
1. On the crossing Nell records the engine and the rain on the car deck; a big man in oilskins lifts her flight case and she snaps at him; he shrugs, "Aye, well," and puts it down exactly where it was.
2. Her right ear rings; she explains to no one that silence is worse than noise. In the saloon a hen-night's worth of old women gossip about "the girl from the archive".
3. The ferry docks; Fraser lowers the ramp wrongly and is shouted at in Gaelic with great gentleness. The big man is the skipper.
4. Beaton's Store: Morag has the kettle on, reads Nell's name off a parcel, and announces the manse roof came down on Thursday.
5. Morag phones around. Nell insists she can sleep at the hall; Morag says the hall has a bucket in every corner.
6. Calum, delivering the evening post, is volunteered for a bed.
Plant: the gale card on the pier Board; Bosun the dog; Nell's headphones. Closing beat: the 4 p.m. horn sounds behind her, Morag says "that's the last till Thursday, maybe", and Nell, who has been wanting quiet for a year, hears the whole island go quiet and flinches.

## Chapter 02: A Spare Room

Calum. Sat evening to night, Seal Cottage.
Purpose: show his grief by what he has not touched.
1. Calum drives her up the road in the van with the dog between them; neither speaks; he answers "How far?" with "Far enough for rain."
2. At the house he makes her tea he does not drink; Bosun takes to her feet.
3. The spare room: Ailsa's study, shelves of school jotters, a small keyboard. Nell does not ask. He moves a jotter box into the porch and finds he is shaking.
4. Morag arrives uninvited with a clootie dumpling and a second dinner; she and Calum argue in whispers in the porch about "that room"; she hints she knows about "the thing on your phone".
5. Alone, Calum scrolls to Voice Memos, sees *14 Mar* and the length, 4:12, and puts the phone face down.
6. Through the wall he hears Nell testing her microphone: a soft hush, a hiss, and the rain played back at him in her headphones' leak.
Plant: the memo; Ailsa's jotters; Morag's knowledge. Closing beat: he finds himself standing in the hall with the kettle in his hand, listening to her listen to the rain.

## Chapter 03: Psalm for a Wet Sunday

Nell. Sun 8 June, St Muir's kirk and Càrn Liath.
Purpose: inciting incident. She hears the voice she came for and learns she cannot leave.
1. Morag marches Nell to kirk. Eleven in the pews. Rev. Donald Iain Macrae reads the forecast along with the lesson.
2. Iseabail Gillies precents the psalm, line by line, the congregation answering in a long ragged drift; Nell, forgetting herself, records on her phone under her coat and is caught by Iseabail's eye.
3. After kirk Iseabail says she will sing in her own kitchen after dinner on Wednesday, never in a hall, never "with that man".
4. The Board at the pier says no sailings; Calum, grave, says the gale will last till Tuesday at best.
5. She climbs Càrn Liath for signal; Gethin on the phone, apologising: twelve singers by 30 June or the funding tranche goes. She says "Right, so, I'll do the twelve", and hears herself.
6. At the stone she records the wind in it, a slow low note.
Plant: "that man"; Clach na Gaoithe; the 30 June deadline. Closing beat: she takes her headphones off on the hilltop for the first time in a year and the ringing in her ear drops, as if somebody had turned down a tap.

## Chapter 04: Force Nine

Calum. Mon 9 to Wed 11 June, pier, shop, Seal Cottage.
Purpose: establish his work, his pressures and the ferry as the island's heartbeat.
1. Gale: Calum walks the pier at dawn, chains the gangway, tells Fraser the gale card is the only decision he's making today.
2. Hector Campbell arrives with a folder: Western Crossings would run the route from September with a bigger boat. Calum says no, politely, three times.
3. Dinner at the cottage: Nell asks where to set up; Calum offers the hall and its generator; she stares at the roof and says it will do.
4. Wed: calm enough for a trial run with Fraser; midway the starboard gearbox starts a deep, slow rumble. He turns back. Fault unknown.
5. Tony at the Port Ardnish yard (on the phone from the shop) says it is the reduction gearbox: a part from Glasgow, eight days.
6. Back home, Nell plays the engine recording from Saturday and Calum, who has never heard the Lass from outside, goes still.
Plant: the engine recording; Hector's offer; the Trust vote. Closing beat: he fits the gale card to the Board with a drawing pin and the pin bends.

## Chapter 05: What Iseabail Wouldn't Sing

Nell. Wed 11 June, Iseabail's kitchen, Glen Road.
Purpose: first great recording and the first hint of the feud.
1. Nell sets up in a small kitchen: room tone, a stopped clock, Iseabail turning off her hearing aid to sing.
2. Iseabail sings a psalm, a lullaby, a lament; Nell realises she is crying only when the monitor smears.
3. Nell asks for "Òran an Uisge" because the archive catalogue lists it. Iseabail's face closes. "That is a song for two. I have only the half of it."
4. A comic interruption: Dougie, her grandson, brings in a lobster in a bucket and sings along off-key.
5. Over tea Iseabail says "Seonaidh MacLeòid would sing it if he were asked properly, which he will not be, by me."
6. Dougie drives Nell home and mentions, innocently, that the late Ailsa used to visit his gran on Thursdays.
Plant: Ailsa's visits; the song for two. Closing beat: Iseabail, alone at her table after Nell leaves, takes a folded letter from a tea tin and does not unfold it.

## Chapter 06: Passing Places

Calum. Thu 12 June, island roads and the hill.
Purpose: show them as company; seed her London offer.
1. Calum drives Nell between singers (a bagpipe-playing brother pair, the knitwear ladies); she asks short sharp questions; he answers with weather.
2. They meet a flock in a passing place and a farmer who will not move; Nell records the stand-off.
3. Midges: Nell in a net she hates, Calum without one, immune, smug.
4. Up the hill at her request, she takes a call from Tamsin: Head of Audio at Kestrel Lane Sound, start 1 September, answer by 4 July. Calum hears half and says nothing.
5. On the hill she says she has not played a note she liked since August; he says Ailsa taught P3; "I'm not much of a talker."
6. They stop at a peat bank and listen to it drip, five minutes, no talk.
Plant: 4 July deadline; the dripping peat bank, which returns in ch 24. Closing beat: she names the pitch of the drip, he says "G", and she looks at him, startled.

## Chapter 07: Seonaidh's Test

Nell. Fri 13 June, Glen Dubh, Seonaidh's weaving shed.
Purpose: Seonaidh refuses, tests her, and passes her.
1. The shed: a loom, tweed hanging like flags, a stove. Seonaidh sits in a high-backed chair and says no before she opens her mouth.
2. He makes her sit for ten minutes in silence. He makes her tell him, eyes shut, what the room holds.
3. She names the loom, the stove tick, a rain gutter, Seonaidh's breathing with a catch in it, a ewe three fields off; he grunts, "The last one was a woman from Glasgow who heard nothing at all."
4. He asks whose house she sleeps in; at "Calum MacAskill's" he goes silent a long time.
5. He says he sings after dark only, and not for the archive: "A wee box in London is a coffin for a song."
6. As she leaves he calls her back and tells her to bring the box to the hall on the first night the rain stops.
Plant: his reaction to the MacAskill name (Calum's family had nothing to do with the feud; it is Ailsa he is thinking of); the "last woman" from Glasgow. Closing beat: she cannot decide if she has been dismissed or given a date.

## Chapter 08: Buckets in the Hall

Calum. Sat 14 June, morning to night, the ceilidh hall.
Purpose: comedy set piece; the hall as the community's instrument.
1. A storm leaks through the hall roof in nineteen places. Calum is summoned; Morag, Rev. Donald Iain and Fraser are already there with every bucket on the island.
2. Nell, appalled and delighted, starts recording the drips and realises the buckets are tuned differently.
3. She orchestrates it: Morag in the tin bath, the Rev. on the piano, Fraser conducting a pan and wooden spoons. A genuine, ridiculous, beautiful piece.
4. The generator fails, and in the dark the room goes entirely silent; the buckets keep going.
5. Calum fixes it in six minutes on his knees and comes back to find her playing the recording through her headphones, grinning.
6. She offers him one ear of the headphones. He hears it. "That's us," he says.
Plant: the bucket piece (ch 24); Fraser's talent. Closing beat: Fraser, red-faced, hands Nell a memory stick of his own beats without looking at her.

## Chapter 09: The Wrong Bearing

Nell. Sat 14 to Sun 15 June, the engine room and pier.
Purpose: her skill saves his ferry; they open up.
1. Calum takes her to the engine room because she can't stop asking what the Lass sounds like.
2. With a contact mic taped to the shaft housing she hears a once-per-turn click he has missed, under the gearbox's rumble.
3. It is the intermediate shaft bearing, cheaper, faster. Calum rings the yard; the wrong part is cancelled and a bearing is ordered for Wednesday.
4. They eat Morag's tattie scones on the pier. She tells him about the festival, the ringing, the walk-out.
5. He tells her one sentence about Ailsa, that she used to hum when she thought nobody was listening.
6. Fraser hovers: he wants to apply for the sound-engineering course in Glasgow; Nell helps him draft the personal statement.
Payoff: her ear. Closing beat: Calum starts to say something about his phone, says "It's nothing", and goes off to feed the dog.

## Chapter 10: Ailsa's Aunt

Calum. Mon 16 to Tue 17 June, Camas Fada and the cemetery by Tràigh Bhàn.
Purpose: Morag's and Calum's grief meet; the memo gets named.
1. Calum helps strip the gearbox, bruises a knuckle, realises he has not felt this well in a year.
2. Morag fusses in the shop, hinting that Nell might "do" for him.
3. He snaps. Morag, shaken, says she was Ailsa's aunt before she was anyone's postmistress.
4. He walks to the grave on the machair with the dog; the rain stops for the first time in nine days.
5. Morag follows with an umbrella and tells him that Ailsa told her about the memo; Ailsa said he would play it when someone made him laugh.
6. He says Nell makes him laugh; Morag says, "I know it, you great lump."
7. A one-minute argument about who lost more.
Plant: the memo's content, partly. Closing beat: they stand under one umbrella and the sun comes out on a wet gravestone, a single bright bar.

## Chapter 11: The First Lament

Nell. Tue 17 June, Glen Dubh at night.
Purpose: Seonaidh sings; Gethin's catalogue entry; first hint of verse four.
1. The rain stops at 9 p.m. Nell sets up in Seonaidh's shed by lamplight; Fraser holds the boom.
2. Seonaidh sings three laments from the 1940s; his voice is a cracked bell, and Nell's levels peak.
3. Mid-session he stops and asks if the archive has "the old reel, March of seventy-six". Gethin has mentioned it that morning on the phone: a folklorist's tape, two voices, ending mid-verse.
4. Seonaidh denies it, then asks who is on it. Nell does not know.
5. He speaks of "the song that needs two" and gets angry.
6. Fraser fixes the lamp, defusing it.
7. Walking home in the simmer dim, Nell hears her own footsteps for the first time.
Plant: the 1976 reel. Closing beat: Seonaidh at the door, staring at the empty road: "Is she well?" and Nell does not know whom he means.

## Chapter 12: The Ferry Goes

Calum. Wed 18 to Fri 20 June, the pier and the Lass.
Purpose: midpoint reversal: the ferry runs; Nell stays by choice; the kiss.
1. Wed: Dougie delivers the right bearing in a creel boat, to cheers. Calum and Fraser fit it Thursday under Nell's headphones.
2. Fri 9 a.m.: first sailing, a crowd with trolleys and prams. Nell arrives with her bags and the case; Calum assumes she is going.
3. He lifts the case on and she lifts it off again: Seonaidh has said yes to the full repertoire; Gethin has extended her to 12 July; she has decided.
4. Calum takes the Lass out in sun and rain, furious with relief.
5. At the evening return the pier is full; Morag is crying with the cold. In the wheelhouse, after, Nell kisses him; he kisses back; the horn sounds by accident.
6. Fraser's shout from the deck breaks it up.
Payoff: the ear. Closing beat: she leaves a set of headphones on the throttle; he does not move them for a week.

## Chapter 13: Breakfast After

Nell. Sat 21 to Sun 22 June, Seal Cottage and the hall.
Purpose: tenderness with consequences: she tells him about London.
1. Breakfast: two people who have kissed and now have to pass the butter. Bosun is the only honest one.
2. She tells him about Tamsin's offer, with the 4 July deadline.
3. He says, "You should think about it", which is fair and the wrong thing.
4. Waulking-song session with the knitwear ladies, thumping wet tweed on the hall table; Nell records the thump pattern.
5. Seonaidh arrives unannounced to listen; Iseabail leaves the hall; they do not meet in the door.
6. Morag announces at the Store that the whole island knows.
7. A closed-door evening of tea and tenderness, the dog in the middle.
Closing beat: his hand is on her ankle under the quilt, and his phone, face down on the shelf, lights up with a notification from the Trust.

## Chapter 14: What the Boat Is Worth

Calum. Mon 23 to Tue 24 June, the Trust office and the Lass.
Purpose: Hector's offer, Calum's own want, and her gift.
1. Hector shows Calum the accounts: loss of £41,000 a year; Western Crossings will keep Calum on as mate. Calum is stung.
2. Fraser plays him a track Nell helped him make; it contains the horn and the engine and Calum's own voice calling the ropes.
3. Nell proposes a sound portrait of the ferry for Saturday's meeting: sixty seconds, no speech.
4. Calum at first refuses, then records the voice-over lines himself.
5. He catches himself laughing at one of them.
6. Hector, listening, takes his glasses off.
Plant: the Saturday meeting. Closing beat: Calum steps off the Lass at dusk and, for once, does not look back at her.

## Chapter 15: The Notebook

Nell. Tue 24 to Wed 25 June, Seal Cottage spare room and Iseabail's kitchen.
Purpose: she finds the key to the song.
1. Looking for an extension lead in the spare room, she knocks over a box and a music jotter falls open: Ailsa's neat writing, three verses of Òran an Uisge with chords, and under them "v4: ask S."
2. She sits on the bed with her pulse in her throat. Does she tell him?
3. Iseabail's kitchen: Nell shows the page. Iseabail reads it, takes off her glasses, and tells the truth: 1976, the ceilidh, the reel, Seonaidh's fourth verse, her wedding to Hamish.
4. "He wrote it for me. I married another. It was a long time ago, and I am old."
5. Iseabail is not asking Nell to fix it, but her hands shake.
6. Nell leaves the jotter in her bag, unspoken.
Plant: the jotter; Ailsa's visits. Closing beat: Nell in bed in Ailsa's study, the quilt on the wall, the jotter in the dark beside her like a small animal.

## Chapter 16: The Skerry

Calum. Wed 25 June, night, Sgeir Mhòr.
Purpose: action set piece; Calum at full competence; intimacy.
1. At 11 p.m. the radio crackles: Dougie's creel boat has lost its engine off the Sgeir Mhòr in a rising swell.
2. Calum takes the Lass out with Fraser; Nell, volunteering, takes the radio.
3. A tow from a rolling deck in the dark; Nell reads the sea state from the sound of the swell, by ear, and calls the lull.
4. Dougie is aboard, white-faced, alive.
5. On the pier, Iseabail is waiting, in a coat over her nightdress, singing the metrical psalm under her breath.
6. Seonaidh, who has been driven down by a neighbour, stands twenty feet from her. They do not speak.
7. Afterwards, shaking in the wheelhouse, Calum tells Nell in plain sentences how Ailsa died.
Plant: Seonaidh and Iseabail on the pier. Closing beat: the engine ticks as it cools, and Nell puts her hand flat on the deck as if to feel the boat breathe.

## Chapter 17: Not Yours to Find

Nell. Thu 26 June, Seal Cottage.
Purpose: the real fight: she means well and has still overreached.
1. A bright morning after the night. They wake easily.
2. She gives him the jotter; tells him about "v4: ask S."
3. He reads Ailsa's handwriting for a long time. "You were in her room."
4. "I fell over a box."
5. He says she has been carrying it for two days; she says she needed to know what to do.
6. He says she should not have been the one to find it, and she is hurt.
7. He walks out to the Lass.
8. Tamsin phones: Kestrel Lane wants an answer by Saturday, not the 4th.
Closing beat: she sits on the kitchen floor with Bosun's chin on her knee and the kettle boiling dry.

## Chapter 18: Take the Job

Calum. Fri 27 to Sat 28 June, Camas Fada.
Purpose: low point: he pushes her away, and the island watches it happen.
1. Rev. Donald Iain fishes beside him on the pier and tells him that you can ask someone to stay or ask them to go; the one thing you cannot do is pretend you have no say.
2. Calum cannot say it. He goes to Morag, who says nothing, which is worse.
3. Saturday 11 a.m.: the Trust meeting in the hall. Fraser plays Nell's sixty seconds of the ferry; the room is silent; Hector moves to postpone the vote to September for a community trial.
4. Calum says to Nell on the steps, "You should take the job. I mean it. It's a good job."
5. She says, "Right. So." and nothing else.
6. At 4 p.m. he takes the Lass out with her at the stern rail; she doesn't turn round.
Closing beat: the wake, long and white, closing over the place where the pier was.

## Chapter 19: Six Seconds of Tape

Nell. Sat 28 to Mon 30 June, the crossing, the train, Edinburgh.
Purpose: her skill gets the missing thing, and she learns what she wants.
1. On the crossing she hears the Lass from outside, the engine, the horn, and the sound of a person at a wheel who is not turning round.
2. The train south; tinnitus roaring on the platform.
3. In Edinburgh, Gethin, apologising, brings her the 1976 reel in the Cutting Room.
4. After a night's work she lifts six seconds of Seonaidh breathing in and beginning "Ged a théid an t-uisge..." under the hiss. A man of 42, singing to a woman, and holding something back.
5. Gethin weeps at the vowel.
6. She asks for the whole reel to be copied and sent to the island; he says it is hers.
Payoff: the 1976 reel. Closing beat: she sits in the Cutting Room in headphones, the six seconds looping, and takes them off for the second time.

## Chapter 20: Fourteenth of March

Calum. Sun 29 June to Tue 1 July, Seal Cottage and the wheelhouse.
Purpose: he plays the memo; he acts.
1. Sunday: the cottage with the spare room door open and the quilt folded.
2. Fraser comes to collect Bosun's lead and says, flatly, "You're an idiot, Skip."
3. Morag asks him to come to tea and tells him the whole of what Ailsa said.
4. Monday night, in the wheelhouse, he plays the memo: Ailsa humming, three verses, a gap, "I haven't got the last one. Get it from Seonaidh, would you? Don't leave the kettle on."
5. He laughs and then cannot stop crying.
6. At dawn he drives to Glen Dubh and asks Seonaidh, as a man, not as a MacAskill.
7. Seonaidh agrees, if the girl brings the reel.
Closing beat: Calum unplugs the phone from the dashboard and the wheelhouse is full of the sound of rain.

## Chapter 21: The Quiet Room

Nell. Wed 2 to Thu 3 July, London.
Purpose: she chooses on her own terms.
1. Kestrel Lane's quiet room: dead silent. Her tinnitus is deafening.
2. Tamsin offers the job again with a bigger salary. Nell says no to the post and yes to a different arrangement: six weeks a year in London, the rest remote, from an island schoolhouse.
3. Tamsin: "Island broadband is a myth." Nell: "So is a job that doesn't kill you."
4. Tamsin agrees to a trial through December.
5. Nell phones her mother in Hull, then her father, who says "go where the sound is."
6. She buys a ticket for the 4 July sailing.
Closing beat: on the Tube she hears the rails sing a clean fifth and almost smiles.

## Chapter 22: The Evening Sailing

Calum. Fri 4 July, the last sailing.
Purpose: reunion, with terms.
1. Calum skippers the 4 p.m. back to the island; she is not on the manifest; he checks it twice.
2. At the pier at Port Ardnish she walks up the ramp in a wet coat with her case and her headphones round her neck.
3. They don't speak for the first twenty minutes.
4. On deck, in the rain: he apologises, plainly, with no speech.
5. She says she is staying, with terms: she gets the schoolhouse, he lets her find things, and she'll be away six weeks a year.
6. He asks if she'll listen to the memo with him.
7. Fraser, at the stern, trying to look busy, drops a rope.
Closing beat: the Lass comes round the point into the harbour with the whole island on the pier.

## Chapter 23: The Fourth Verse

Nell. Sat 5 July, Iseabail's kitchen and Seonaidh's shed.
Purpose: climax: the two old singers.
1. Morning: Nell goes to Iseabail first. She plays the six seconds on a speaker.
2. Iseabail listens to a young man's breath. "He is going to sing it to me," she says.
3. In the shed, Seonaidh will not look at the speaker; then he does.
4. "I wrote it in the ditch behind the byre. I was a fool."
5. Iseabail has come up the glen in a taxi; she stands in the doorway.
6. They argue for a long time, tenderly and ridiculously, about Hamish.
7. He sings the fourth verse, in English and Gaelic, in a cracked bell; she answers.
Payoff: the 1976 reel. Closing beat: Nell, recording, forgets to look at the meters.

## Chapter 24: The Song of the Rain

Calum. Sat 5 July, evening, the ceilidh hall.
Purpose: land the emotional note: the song, finished.
1. The hall full; buckets still in the corners, tuned; Fraser on the desk.
2. The Rev. plays the piano; Morag has made forty clootie dumplings.
3. Seonaidh and Iseabail sit on two chairs, a foot apart.
4. Calum asks Fraser to put his phone through the PA; Nell stands beside him.
5. Ailsa's voice sings verse three, the tune of every kitchen in the island, and then stops where she stopped, and Seonaidh begins verse four in the quiet.
6. Iseabail takes up the answer. The roof starts to rain; the buckets play along.
7. Calum puts his hand over Nell's on the desk.
Closing beat: the song ends; the rain doesn't; nobody claps for a long, long moment.

## Mystery: the fourth verse

**Solution.** In 1976 Seonaidh wrote verse four for Iseabail to sing at the ceilidh and meant to ask her to marry him; the reel ran out as he began it, and she heard enough to understand. She was already engaged to Hamish Gillies. He never offered the verse again; she never asked. Ailsa heard of it from Dougie's gran, and spent her last winter trying to broker it; Seonaidh refused her three times.
**Clues.** Ch 3 Iseabail refuses "with that man"; ch 5 "a song for two" and the tea-tin letter; ch 7 Seonaidh goes quiet; ch 11 the 1976 reel and "Is she well?"; ch 15 the jotter and Iseabail's account; ch 16 the pier; ch 19 six seconds of tape; ch 20 the memo.
**Red herrings.** Seonaidh's reaction to the name MacAskill (suggesting a family feud, but it is Ailsa he thinks of); Dougie's remark in ch 5 (suggests Ailsa was ill-advised).
**Costs.** No magic or technology rules beyond the archive's. The cost of the song is that two people have to risk being refused again.
