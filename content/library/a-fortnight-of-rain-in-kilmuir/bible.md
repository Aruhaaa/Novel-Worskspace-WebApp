# Bible: A Fortnight of Rain in Kilmuir (for writers only)

## Logline and premise

A burned-out London sound engineer sent to record an island's last great Gaelic singers is stranded by the weather with the widowed ferry skipper who cannot play the last recording of his wife, and the song that links them has a missing verse that two stubborn old singers have kept apart for fifty years.

**In order.** Sat 7 June: Nell Okoye-Brandt arrives on the last sailing before a gale. The manse roof is leaking, so Morag Beaton, the postmistress, puts her in the spare room at Calum MacAskill's house, Seal Cottage. The ferry, the *Morven Lass*, is then held by gales and a rumbling bearing, so a fortnight's job becomes a month's stay. Nell is meant to record twelve singers by 30 June for the Northern Voices Archive (Edinburgh). Iseabail Gillies (78) sings only if she is never asked to sing with Seonaidh MacLeòid (91), and Seonaidh will not be recorded at all. They are the two voices of "Òran an Uisge", a waulking song with three known verses and a fourth that only Seonaidh knows. Nell's ear finds the real fault in the ferry (a worn intermediate-shaft bearing, not the gearbox), which brings her and Calum close. When the ferry runs again (Fri 20 June) Nell chooses not to board; they kiss. In the spare room Nell finds the school music book in which Ailsa, Calum's late wife, copied out the three verses with the note "v4: ask S." Nell tells Calum; he feels his grief has been handled without leave, and later, afraid, tells her to take the London job (Head of Audio, answer by 4 July). The islanders' ferry-trust vote is postponed to September. Calum himself steers her away on Sat 28 June. In Edinburgh, Nell restores the last six seconds of a 1976 reel and hears young Seonaidh begin verse four. Back on the island Calum finally plays Ailsa's voice memo of 14 March, in which she sings the song, admits she lacks verse four, and tells him to get it. Nell negotiates her own terms in London (an island studio, six weeks a year in the city) and returns on Fri 4 July. She plays the six seconds to Seonaidh and Iseabail. He wrote verse four for Iseabail in 1976; she married Hamish Gillies instead; he never offered it and she never asked. They sing it at the ceilidh on Sat 5 July, and Ailsa's recorded voice carries the third verse. Nell stays, on new terms. The last chapter is the song.

## World and setting

**Kilmuir**: fictional island west of Mull, eleven miles by four, 212 residents, about 40 per cent Gaelic speakers, most of them over sixty. Gaelic is the first language of the singers and used in the home by the old; English is everyday. Rain falls on 24 of 30 days in June; the light stays until nearly 11 p.m. (the *simmer dim*). Peat smoke, wet wool, diesel, kelp, sheep. Midges in still damp air.

- **Camas Fada**: the main township. The pier, Beaton's Store and Post Office, the ceilidh hall (tin roof, so rain is loud), the manse, St Muir's kirk (white, plain, no organ).
- **Seal Cottage (Tigh nan Ròn)**: Calum's whitewashed croft house above the pier road. Kitchen with a cast-iron range, a wheelhouse-style porch full of boots, a spare room (formerly Ailsa's study).
- **The old schoolhouse**: closed 2019, one big room, good acoustics; Nell's eventual studio.
- **Càrn Liath** (396 m): hill above the township. The standing stone **Clach na Gaoithe** at the top is the only place with mobile signal. A walk of 25 minutes from the road end.
- **Tràigh Bhàn**: white west-coast beach and machair. **Sgeir Mhòr**: dangerous skerry off the south point, five miles from the pier. **Glen Dubh**: inland glen where Seonaidh lives, nine minutes by van.
- **Ferry**: *Morven Lass*, 12 cars, 60 passengers, 70 minutes to Port Ardnish on the mainland. Sails Tue, Thu, Sat (out 9:00, back 4:00) when the wind is below force 8. Crew: Calum (skipper and engineer), Fraser (deck). Owned by the Kilmuir Ferry Trust; the contractor Western Crossings wants the route.
- **Phone/power**: landline at the shop; mobile only on the hill. Broadband slow. Hall generator in a shed. Power cuts two or three times a month.

**Rules and costs.** The archive requires a signed consent form, a minimum 48 kHz/24-bit recording, and a session log for each singer. Singers set their own rules: Iseabail sings only before 3 p.m.; Seonaidh sings after dark. Nell's ears are the cost of her trade: tinnitus in the right ear, worst after silence and after London.

**Glossary.** *Òran an Uisge*: "Song of the Rain", waulking song, call and response, four verses. *Luadh*: waulking (thumping wet tweed) and its songs. *Precenting*: leading the psalm line by line; Iseabail does it. *The simmer dim*: June twilight. *The Board*: chalkboard on the pier with the day's sailing. *Gale card*: Calum's laminated sailing-cancelled sign. *Piece*: a snack. *Creel*: lobster pot. *Machair*: the flowery coastal grass. *Peat bank*: where peats are cut. *Passing place*: single-track lay-by. *The Store*: Beaton's. *Dram*: a whisky. *Clootie dumpling*: boiled fruit pudding, Morag's. *The Trust*: ferry committee. *The Cutting Room*: archive digitisation suite in Edinburgh. *Room tone*: a room's own quiet, which Nell records first everywhere. *Midgie net*: a head net. *Tattie scones*: potato flatbread. *Mo chridhe*: my heart. *Slàinte*: health, a toast.

## Cast

**Nell Okoye-Brandt** ("Nell", "Nelly" to her mother), 34. British-Nigerian father (Dr Emeka Okoye, retired GP), English mother (Hilary Brandt, Hull). Tall, close-cropped hair, a gap between her front teeth, a pale scar across her left thumb from a cable winch. Wears big over-ear headphones round her neck always. Speaks fast, bright, self-mocking, in lists; says "right, so" before bad news. Example: "Right, so, I can fix the hiss, I can't fix the weather, and I can absolutely not fix your face." Wants: quiet, and to love her work again. Fears: that her ears are finished. Hides: she walked out of a festival job mid-set after her right ear rang for a week. Arc: from running to choosing a place.

**Calum MacAskill** ("Calum", "Skipper", "the Skip" to Fraser), 37. Ferry skipper and engineer; widower. Big, sandy-grey beard, pale grey eyes, left ring finger missing above the knuckle (winch, aged 22). Speech: slow, dry, short; answers a question with a weather report; says "aye, well." Example: "It's wet. It's been wet. It will be wet. That's me done on the subject." Wants: to be left alone and keep the ferry in the island's hands. Fears: forgetting Ailsa's voice, then remembering it. Hides: the voice memo on his phone, unplayed. Arc: from guarding the silence to letting it be filled.

**Morag Beaton**, 62. Postmistress, Ailsa's aunt. Small, round, white perm, reading glasses on a chain, flour on her cardigan. Speaks in questions and asides; reads postcards "to check the address". "Is that so? Well, I'd not say it, but I'll tell you what I'd not say." Wants Calum looked after; fears he will forget Ailsa or, worse, not. Hides that Ailsa told her about the memo. Arc: from matchmaking by stealth to blessing in the open.

**Seonaidh MacLeòid** ("old Seonaidh"), 91. Weaver, bard, last man who knows the full waulking repertoire. Frail, big hands, milky left eye, flat cap indoors. Speaks in comic gruffness, mixing Gaelic and English, calls everyone "a luaidh" (love, once) and refuses to answer the question asked. "I'm not dead. I'm resting my voice for the funeral." Wants to be asked properly; fears the song dying with him; hides verse four. Arc: gives up verse four.

**Iseabail Gillies** ("Iseabail", "Bella" only to Seonaidh), 78. Precentor of St Muir's. Straight-backed, silver braid, strong forearms, hearing aid she turns off to sing. Speaks exact, formal, English with Gaelic grammar ("It is not a thing I would do."). Wants to sing with him; says she wants never to. Fears she gave her life to a second choice; hides that she kept his letter. Arc: from refusal to asking him.

**Rev. Donald Iain Macrae**, 58. Minister. Wide, bald, fisherman's hands, terrible knitted hat, plays the hall piano badly. Quotes the shipping forecast as if scripture. "Blessed are the Fisher, Viking, North Utsire." Wants Calum back in the pews; fears being merely kind. Hides his own divorce.

**Fraser Beaton**, 19. Morag's grandson, deckhand. Gangly, jug ears, a different pair of earbuds in each pocket, ginger stubble. Speech: mumbled, "like," "man", sudden bursts of eloquence. Wants the audio course in Glasgow; fears leaving Calum short. Arc: applies and goes (in September), and records the song.

**Hector Campbell**, 66. Chair of the Ferry Trust. Retired grocer, rugby build, red braces. Speaks in committee-ese; "with respect." Wants the ferry safe, and not to see Calum worked to death. Backs selling to Western Crossings; changes his mind. Not a villain.

**Tamsin Orrell**, 45. Owner of Kestrel Lane Sound, London. Sharp bob, silver ear cuff, no small talk. Speaks in bullet points. "Three things. One: money. Two: money. Three: you." Wants Nell as head of audio; honest about it. Respects a clear no.

**Gethin Prosser**, 52. Archive curator, Edinburgh. Cardigan, dishevelled, weeps at good vowel sounds. Speaks in overlong apologies. Wants his twelve singers by 30 June.

**Ailsa Beaton MacAskill** (dead, aged 35, 23 November, 19 months ago). Primary teacher and Trust secretary. Fair, freckled, loud laugh, sang in the kitchen. Heard only by memo and in memory. Dry, teasing: "Don't leave the kettle on."

*Dougie Gillies* (Iseabail's grandson, 31, creel fisherman) and the dog *Bosun* (Calum's collie, one blue eye) recur.

## Voice guide

Close third, past tense, alternating chapters: odd Nell, even Calum. Warm, wry, concrete, sound-rich. Nell's chapters notice sound first (frequencies, room tone, rhythm, what is under the rain); Calum's notice weather, engines, hands, tides. Sentences mix long, rain-like runs with short blunt ones. Humour comes from character, never jokes for their own sake. Dialogue in straight double quotes, one speaker per paragraph; Gaelic sparingly and correct, usually clear from context. No dialect spelling beyond "aye", "wee", "cannae" for Morag only.

*Sample (not part of the novel):* The rain on the hall roof had a pitch. Nell found it in the second hour, a sort of grey F-sharp with a loose tooth in it, and by the third hour she could have told you which gutter was failing. "You're doing that face," said Calum from the door, with a flask under his arm. "What face?" "The one the dog does at the kettle." She held up a hand without turning round. Somewhere under the roar there was a drip with a different shape, slower, heavier, a bucket filling toward the edge. She counted eleven. "Don't move," she said. He did not move. A man who had been told to stand still in a doorway in the rain for twenty seconds, and who did it, was worth knowing.

**This narrator never:** explains a joke; names an emotion when a gesture will do; uses "suddenly"; opens with weather as a summary; lets Gaelic go unglossed by context; gives a character a speech about grief; writes a sunset as the end of a chapter; says "little did they know".

## Motifs

Rain's many pitches. Buckets and gutters. The tape/recording and what it keeps. Hands (Calum's missing fingertip, Seonaidh's weaver hands, Nell's thumb). Kettles and tea. The ferry's wake and horn. Headphones round the neck (she takes them off at the end). Doors left ajar.

## Timeline and ground rules

Backstory: 1934 Seonaidh born. 1947 Iseabail born. 1976 (March): folklorist's reel records Seonaidh (42) and Iseabail (29) singing at a ceilidh; the reel runs out as Seonaidh starts verse four. Iseabail marries Hamish Gillies in September 1976; Hamish dies 12 years ago. Calum born in Kilmuir; Ailsa his classmate. They marry 12 years before the story. Ailsa diagnosed with cancer in January, makes the voice memo on 14 March (4 min 12 sec), dies 23 November, 19 months before the story. Nell's festival walk-out: last August.

Story present: Sat 7 June Nell arrives (4 p.m. sailing). Sun 8 gale begins. Wed 11 trial run reveals a rumble; wrong part ordered. Sun 15 Nell diagnoses it with a contact mic. Wed 18 correct bearing arrives by Dougie's creel boat. Thu 19 fitted. Fri 20 first sailing. Wed 25 night: Sgeir Mhòr rescue. Sat 28 Trust meeting (11 a.m.); Nell leaves on the 4 p.m. sailing. Mon 30 Edinburgh. Wed 2 July London. Thu 3 offer answered. Fri 4 July last sailing back. Sat 5 July the ceilidh.

Who knows what: Calum learns of the missing verse and the notebook in ch 17 (Nell tells him); Nell learns of "ask S." in ch 15; Morag knows about the memo from ch 2; Seonaidh and Iseabail both know the history. Crossing is always 70 minutes; van to hill 4 minutes then 25 on foot; Glen Dubh to Camas Fada 9 minutes.
