# Unit Tour

Espie switched on the ring light at 9:05 on Tuesday, and the lobby of Harwick Terrace, which has not been flattered since 1986, took it like a patient told to say ah. The brown-and-cream terrazzo came up the color of caramel. The brass H in the floor lit like a coin at the bottom of a fountain. The dead doorman's desk gleamed in a manner I would have said it did not deserve.

"Ok so we're giving original features," said Espie, to a phone on a gimbal. Her hair was up and her gold hoops were in and her nails were still pumpkin, with the bats. "No because look at this floor? It's giving 1927. It's giving someone important got married in here."

"Nobody got married in here," I said.

"Dev. You're in my shot."

"I'm at the mailboxes."

"You're in my shot with your *face*." She lowered the phone. "Ok so the thing is, I need a rent guy. For the background. The building has a person, the person is handling something. Mood: responsible. Do you have a lanyard?"

"I have a lanyard."

"Put it on. Walk from the office to the mailboxes with a folder and look like you're about to evict someone, but warmly."

"I've never evicted anyone."

"Then do the warm part."

We did it five times. On the third take I held the folder wrong, on the fourth she said I was giving hostage, and on the fifth she said that was literally it, and I understood I had been cast. In the last take I stopped at the mailboxes, because it was where the script told me to stop, and for the first time in fourteen months I looked at the box for 4B. It was so full of circulars that its small brass door had given up closing. The name card in the slot said OCCUPANT, in blue ink, in a round hand. I thought of Joyce, saying *round*, and went back to the office with my folder.

* * *

The rest of the tour was Noreen's idea, in the sense that Noreen had said yes to "a tasteful walk-through, no tenant faces" without reading the word *walk-through* as a plan. We did 5D first, vacant since spring, with new vinyl plank and a ficus that was not a ficus. "And this is our biophilic moment," Espie told the phone, touching a leaf. "It's fake but it's trying. That's so real." I stood in the doorway being responsible.

"Ok, and now, for a sneak peek of the vibes," she said, in the elevator, "we're doing four."

"Why four?"

"The carpet. It's old hotel. Old hotel is literally an aesthetic now."

"It was a hotel."

"Exactly. It's giving provenance."

She walked the corridor slowly, holding the gimbal low with both hands like a woman carrying a full bowl, narrating over her shoulder. I followed with my folder, where the shot would not catch me, and counted. Past the clogs at 4A, past the cabinet, to the bicycle at 4C, and what I got in my head was seventeen. The smell of toast came up around the cabinet and went again. "Is someone making toast?" said Espie, without stopping. "Ok that's giving home." My lips must have been moving, because she said, "Stop moving your mouth, you're giving ventriloquist," and I stopped, and seventeen stayed where it was.

At the cabinet she slowed even further. "And this is a fire safety feature, which, important. We love safety." She held the lens on the red glass for a full second, and I felt the carpet under my shoes go on being carpet, and she said, "Ok that's the tour, besties, link in bio," and lowered the phone.

* * *

At 10:20 she propped her laptop on the leasing desk and played it back, and I stood behind her chair with my folder against my chest. Joyce came to the edge of her cubicle without being asked, which she does for fire drills and cake.

It was a good video. The lobby looked like money. The ficus looked almost real. At 0:12 the picture went into the fourth-floor corridor, low and slow, the maroon diamonds sliding underfoot, and at 0:14, between the clogs on their mat and the bicycle by the far door, there was something beige.

I would like to put this carefully. It was there for one frame, or two, and then it was a red cabinet with a hose in it, and the video went on to the stairs. The first time I told myself it was the gimbal. The second time it was a shape, a tall pale rectangle with an edge. The third time it had brass on it, two small numerals at the height of a face, a 4 and a B, the B slightly out of true. On the fourth time I saw that it stood open a finger's width, and on the fifth there was a lamp in the gap, a thin vertical line of amber the color of strong tea.

"Is it a jump cut?" said Espie. "I didn't cut. I don't even have an edit. I've literally never cut anything in my life."

"Noted," said Joyce, from behind us, very quietly. Then, which she never does: "Play it again."

Espie played it again. When she stopped it on 0:14 and zoomed, the frame went soft and gray and showed us a cabinet and a hose. Only when the video moved did the door arrive, like a word you know on a page you have been staring at.

"Dev." She had turned the chair around to look at me. Without the ring light she looked younger and more tired, and her eyes were doing something I had not seen them do in fourteen months, which was not performing. "You saw it right away. Not like, what is that. Like, there you are."

"I was squinting."

"Mm-hm." She picked up her phone and held it against her chest, and I saw the thumb move on the glass. "I have a note about this building."

"What kind of note?"

"Later," said Espie. Then, being Espie, she brightened. "Ok, but this is content. This is, like, so much content. Dev, people make whole careers out of one door."

"Please don't post it."

"It's a corridor."

"It's a corridor with a door in it, which you just said."

She posted it at 10:31 as The Mystery Unit.

> @harwickterrace | The Mystery Unit | Live at Harwick: a sneak peek of the fourth floor. Rewatch 0:14. No, don't. Yes, do. 1BRs from $1,395, tours daily | #oldbuilding #apartmenttour #mysteryunit

> pauline.reads: what is the door at 0:14??

> bigmike_1987: lol it's a fire cabinet, you people need sleep

> tessaroo: ok i watched it 3 times and the gap gets WIDER

It had eight hundred views by 10:50, when the keys in Noreen's office stopped, and her door opened and nothing chimed. I knew before I looked at her. The color went out of her face in sections, forehead first. She came and stood behind Espie's chair and watched 0:14 four times with the sound off, and her hands found the lanyard and closed on it, all fourteen keys, until they were silent.

"Take it down," said Noreen.

"It's doing numbers, Noreen. It's my best day since the pumpkin."

"Esperanza." She almost never uses the whole name. "Take it down. Now. Okay. Okay. Okay."

Espie took it down at 10:52. By noon a stranger who had recorded it off her screen had put it up under the title MYSTERY APARTMENT IN OLD HOTEL, and that one had eight thousand views, and Noreen had shut her door.

* * *

At 1:41 an email came, in capitals.

> From: Randall Pugh | To: Noreen Whitmore; Devendra Mahajan | Date: Tue 14 Oct, 1:41 PM | Subject: UNIT 4B DOES NOT EXIST (ACTION REQUIRED)

> Team, following this morning's social post Larchmont has asked for written confirmation that no unit 4B exists at Harwick Terrace. It does not. Going forward the post stays down, the line comes out ahead of the 10/31 data-room freeze, and nobody posts about 4B again. Zoom at 2:00 PM, please come with a date. Randall

We took the call in the filing room, on Noreen's laptop, propped on the stool that tips. Randall appeared in front of Positano. In my experience the backgrounds change by the call and the man does not: the quarter-zip, the tan, the white square of a ring light in each eye. Behind him a hundred pastel houses were stacked up a cliff toward a sky that nobody would ever need to account for. He looked like someone in excellent health who had never once been rained on.

"Team, thanks for jumping on. I'll be brief, I've got Larchmont at 2:30." He steepled his hands. "I want to be transparent that I have watched this video eleven times."

"Eleven," said Noreen. Her hands were out of the frame, below the shelf of the stool, and I could see her elbows working.

"And every time, what I see is a fire-hose cabinet. Dev, you're closest to the data. Do you see a door?"

There are questions you can answer truthfully and questions you can answer in good standing. "Technically," I said, "what I see is a compression artifact."

"Love that. Compression artifact. I'm going to use that." He leaned in. "Here's what's keeping me up, and it isn't a door, because there is no door. It's eight thousand people in a comment section asking about a unit that Larchmont's data room says we don't own. Their integration lead sent me the link with one question mark. One. I have equity that vests at closing and I'm being asked about a question mark."

"We understand," said Noreen.

"Going forward I need the line out, I need it before the freeze, and I need us to not boil the ocean here. It's one row." Randall put his palms on the desk in Positano. "Four-B does not exist, Dev. That is the whole point of the exercise."

I thought of asking whose exercise. I said "Noted," in Joyce's voice, which is the only time I have borrowed it.

"Super," said Randall, who had been in the Tenantry training too. "Let's circle back Friday."

When the screen went blank Noreen did not move. The stool tipped a little under the laptop and she put out one finger and steadied it.

"It's a row, hon," she said, to the wall. "It's a row."

* * *

I left at twenty past five, in rain that had settled in for the evening, and I crossed 31st Street not to the bus stop but to Halloran's, because at about 2:40 I had decided I needed a notebook and I had not had the courage to say so to myself until I was holding my coat. Halloran's sells ham on rye, lottery tickets, umbrellas on a cardboard spinner, and, next to the batteries, a small yellow notebook with a rubber cover and pages made for surveyors and anyone else who must write in the wet. It cost $4.29. I bought a pencil, because pencil takes the rain, and stood under the awning among the soaked cardboard boxes and wrote.

> Mon 6 Oct: 9 going, 12 back. Wed 8: 14 / 14. Thu 9: 15 (Z. 3B: steps 4 to 5). Tue 14: 17.

I looked at that for some time. A column of figures is the nearest thing I own to a prayer, and it was going up.

I had now been told twice in nine days that 4B did not exist: once in an office by a woman holding her keys in both hands, once on a screen by a man in front of the sea. Each had said it kindly, as a favor. And I knew what I had not said to either of them, because I have not said it to anyone, which is that I was already familiar with this sentence. In the year before my mother was diagnosed there was a sound that my father heard from the pillow beside hers, and two doctors told us it was nothing. They were wrong. The sound was exactly what it was. I know what it is to be told that something you can hear does not exist, and I know how it feels to agree, to be a good sport about it, to take the Post-it.

Under the numbers I wrote *Not deleting.* I underlined it once, as Noreen does, and put the notebook in my inside pocket, where the rain could not get at it.

By the time I came back across 31st Street the rain had gone from the kind you walk in to the kind you carry. I had left my umbrella hooked over the lobby radiator at half past eight that morning and had not thought of it for nine hours, which is the sort of thing that happens to a man who has had a day. I pushed the glass door open with my shoulder, wet to the cuffs.

Left was open.

It stood on the ground floor with its doors drawn back and its ceiling light on, and nobody in it. Nobody stood by the call button. The lobby was empty, the bench, the doorman's desk, the forty-one mailboxes, the brass H under my shoes. It was Tuesday, and Left only sticks on Mondays. It was not sticking. It was waiting, with the patience of a thing that knows you are coming.

I crossed to the radiator and took my umbrella, and when I turned, the display above the doors, which reads L when the car is at the lobby, said 4B.

It said 4B for about as long as it takes to read it. Then it said L. The doors slid shut on the empty car, and the lobby clock, six minutes slow, said 5:44, which was ten to six. I stood on the brass H with my umbrella in my hand. I did not get in.
