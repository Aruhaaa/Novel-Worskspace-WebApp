# Bernice Stays On

A beige tower hummed in a closet that smelled of warm dust and lemon drops, and Rafa stood in the doorway like a man at the edge of a pool he used to swim in.

The server closet is at the end of the basement corridor, past Rafa's shop, behind a steel door marked SERVER in strips of label-maker tape older than my employment. It holds a card table, a metal chair, a monitor with a bulging face, a green steel cabinet, a shelf with one banker's box on it and, on the floor, in a clean square of linoleum of its own, the tower. It was the beige that every computer was before I was born, a beige that was never a color anyone chose. On the front, on three strips of embossed vinyl tape, someone had pressed out BERNICE and DO NOT UNPLUG and M.D.

I had asked Rafa on Monday where Margie kept her old records. He had looked at the ceiling. On Tuesday morning he stopped at my desk, which he never does, and said to the stapler, "One o'clock. Bring your glasses." I do not need to bring my glasses. I wear them. We were two men with mended spectacles, mine in green electrical tape and his in white, the kind of equipment a building produces.

"Corvane took RentWise off every desktop at go-live," I said. "I assumed they'd taken the server."

"Nobody told it to stop." Rafa leaned on the frame. "Margie asked."

"Margie asked what?"

"That it stays on. She said, when I'm gone somebody is going to want to look at it. She said put a label." He moved the toothpick across. "She wrote the letters on a piece of paper. I pressed them out."

The old monitor took a full minute to warm, and then it lit, green on black, with a white cursor that blinked like a patient man. RENTWISE 2.4. COPYRIGHT 2003. USER: I typed DMAHAJAN, which had worked for thirteen months at my own desk, and it worked here. A banner crossed the top. READ ONLY. SUPERSEDED BY TENANTRY 9. Below it were the old function keys, F1 UNITS, F2 TENANTS, F3 LEDGER, F4 REPORTS, as plain as the buttons in an elevator. I felt a warmth for it that I had not felt for the new software, the warmth you feel for a man who never pretended to be anything but a clerk.

I typed HT-4B and pressed F6, which is History.

PD THRU 10/25 | END DATES: NONE | NAMES OLDER THAN 7 YRS MASKED PER RETENTION POLICY RP-7

I counted the rows twice. Ten. Each had a start date and not one had an end. In sixty-four years nobody had left. Nine of the names had been replaced by asterisks under a retention policy I could picture the committee for, and the tenth, the only one the rule had not reached, was a name I knew from a Christmas card Joyce could not find. Dunleavy, M., paid through October. My payment on Sunday night had gone to her. She was current.

I looked for some time at 01/16/16. It was a Saturday, as I checked later. There was no name to go with it, and I did not need one.

"There are ten of them," I said.

"I don't read the screen," said Rafa.

"No. I know."

The banker's box on the shelf said HOTEL REGISTERS in black marker. It held a dozen cloth-bound volumes with their spines flaking, and I took out Vol. 14 and opened it on the card table. The paper had gone the color of tea. The columns had been ruled in blue by a machine in a printing house that no longer existed, and the entries ran down them in the upright script of a man who had been taught penmanship as a form of discipline.

> HARWICK HOTEL REGISTER | Vol. 14 | AMBLER, T. (Mr.) | Dayton, O. | Rm 412 | Arrived 11 Apr 1961 | Departure: indef.

The line above him had left on the fourteenth of April. The line below him had left on the twelfth. Ambler, T. (Mr.) had written his city in the careful circle of a man who signs forms for a living, and under Departure someone at the desk had put indef. and a small flourish, the way a clerk puts a full stop on a sentence he does not expect to have questioned.

* * *

The green cabinet had two drawers, and the upper one carried a strip of white tape that said NOTES TO FILE. I had expected an archive. I had half expected nothing. It held three hanging folders, and the first two had tabs.

KWAN, E. had one sheet in it, yellowed, folded in thirds, with one line.

DUNLEAVY, M. had a page torn from a legal pad, covered in round upright pencil that had been pressed hard, and under it a greeting card with a cartoon pigeon in a necktie on the front, carrying a briefcase.

> Nov. 1998. Whatever you enter comes out of somewhere. E. Kwan

> 30 Aug. Keep the row. Be kind to Noreen. She is trying. M.D.

I had needed a spreadsheet and a voicemail and four missing items and two missing people to write on Sunday night what Ellis Kwan had written in one line, in the same November that stood against the seventh row of the history I had just read. I sat on the metal chair. I felt, for a moment, as one feels on being handed the answer key by a teacher who has thoughtfully put the date on it.

Inside the pigeon the printed words said WE'LL MISS YOU. Under them a dozen people had signed in a dozen inks. J. Lim, in a hand like type, Best wishes, and a space after it where she had begun a second sentence and stopped. R. Ibarra, pressed hard enough to emboss the card. A big purple gel-pen scrawl across the bottom half of the page, round, in a hand I had seen on a Post-it that said 31 MO.

> Margie, you'll be missed! (Kidding. You're not going anywhere. Ha!) N.W.

I read the *Ha!* twice. It did not improve. It had been written by a person in good spirits, or one performing good spirits for a card, and nobody in that office had asked what the joke was a joke about. There was the third folder behind the two, with no name on the tab but a word in pencil, NEXT, and nothing inside it, not a sheet. I shut the drawer on it the way you shut a door on a sleeping child.

"Rafa."

"Mm."

"You said Margie asked for the server. What else did she ask?"

He pulled out the other chair and sat, which I had never seen him do anywhere but his own stool, the way a man sits in a pew when the service is not his. The toothpick came out of his mouth. He turned it over in the fingers of the hand that stops at the knuckle.

"The thirtieth of August," he said. "A Friday. She came down to the shop at ten after four. She had her purse, and the cardigan, and no box. Her things she left in the drawer. She said, somebody will want the tin." He looked at the toothpick. "She said, 'Rafa, would you bring the key.' I said, which key. She said, 'You know which one, dear.' I asked had she eaten."

"Had she?"

"She said yes. It was a lie. She never ate lunch. She sucked those lemon candies and said it was the same." A smile came and went under the moustache. "We went up in Left. It opened on four by itself. Between the doors, where the cabinet is, there was a door. Like in an office. Beige, with the number, and a knob. I had walked by that wall for twelve years."

"What did you do?"

"I turned the key." He said it flatly, like a man reading a figure off a meter. "She said thank you. She walked in. The door stood there exactly as long as it took her to walk through, and then it shut, and I put my hand on the wall, and it was a wall. I went downstairs and I oiled the key."

"And you've oiled it every morning since."

"Every morning."

"Why?"

"In case she wants to come out." He put the toothpick back, a little roughly, as a man puts back a cigarette he has promised to give up. "You do not turn a key for a person and then let it go stiff."

I asked it as gently as I could. "Where is she now?"

"Upstairs."

"Upstairs where?"

Rafa looked at the ceiling.

* * *

Behind me the monitor had gone on glowing, and I turned the chair to it with the reluctance of a man turning back to a stove. I pressed F1 and typed HT-4B. The unit screen came up. UNIT, TENANT, RENT, BALANCE, LSTART. Below SQFT was a field I had passed for thirteen months without noticing, because I had never had a reason to, two letters wide. DR.

> RENTWISE 2.4 | HT-4B | DR: 1 | LAST EDIT: DUNLEAVY 08/30/19 16:02

DR is doors. A one, under Margie's name, at two minutes past four on the last afternoon anyone saw her. I paged through the other forty units. Every one said DR 1, last edit IMPORT, March 2003. Only HT-4B had a human hand on it.

The mapping sheet from the Tenantry migration had come to me in September in an email called MIGRATION PLAN v3 (FYI), which I had read the way I read every FYI. It said that DR would be carried across to DOOR_COUNT for rows with a valid FLOORPLAN_ID. Forty rows had one. HT-4B did not. As far as I could tell, the importer had looked at a door with no plan to hang it on and left it on BERNICE like a coat on a hook. That was why the row had gone red on the sixth of October and not in 2003, and the field it had dropped had a one in it.

I thought of the beige door in Espie's video, standing a finger's width open at 0:14 and a hose cabinet at 0:15. A door in a field nobody had cleared. An afterimage. I wrote DR=1 in the notebook and underlined it once, which is Noreen's habit, and which I caught myself in.

* * *

At twenty to three I was at my desk. The office was doing what it does at that hour. Joyce's calculator ran along like a man on a treadmill. Espie was out, showing 3D. Noreen's door was shut, and her keys silent behind it.

I opened the drawer. It stuck, and gave, with the cough. The tin and the button were where they had always been, and I took out the button and held it in my palm, a four-hole horn button the color of oatmeal with a quarter inch of thread still through one hole. It had been pulled off something, not cut. I put the tin to my ear and shook it. Two lemon drops, fused. *Somebody will want the tin*, Rafa had said, in Margie's voice, ten minutes ago, in a closet. I had been somebody. I had had it for fourteen months and thought it was rubbish.

The chair took my spine as it always does, and I knew now why it fit so well. It had not been broken in to me. It was a chair that had spent twenty years learning a different back, and had been kind enough to find the common ground.

I pulled up the PDF I had saved from the application portal in the summer of last year, the posting I applied to, and which I had read, I would have said, closely. I read everything closely. It was my best quality, and I had listed it as such. I scrolled to the top. The font was the font of a document that wants to be taken for granted.

> CORVANE PROPERTY GROUP | Position description | Rent Roll Clerk, Harwick Terrace | Budget line 4100.07 | Reports to: Property Manager | Replaces: M. Dunleavy (vacant since 08/30/19) | Posted 04/22

I could have recited the budget line. I had read that sheet on the day I applied, and again on the day I accepted, and once more, in the lobby, on the nineteenth of August, with my bag on my shoulder and my first day in front of me. I had not once looked at the name.
