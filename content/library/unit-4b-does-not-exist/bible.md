# Story bible: Unit 4B Does Not Exist

For the writers only. Readers never see this file. Everything here is binding unless the outline overrides it for a particular chapter.

## 1. Logline and premise

**Logline.** A data-entry clerk finds that the apartment missing from his building's floor plan has been paying rent for sixty-four years, and slowly learns that the rent is being paid out of him.

**Premise, in order, with the ending and every major reveal.**

Dev Mahajan, 29, is the Rent Roll Clerk for Harwick Terrace, a seven-story rental building on East 31st Street in an unnamed mid-sized American city. He works in the ground-floor management office for Corvane Property Group, which is being sold to a bigger company, Larchmont Residential Partners. On Monday 6 October, the day Corvane cuts over to new software (Tenantry 9), the validation run turns 40 rows green and one row red: HT-4B. Unit 4B has a paying tenant (OCCUPANT, $1,840 a month, never late) but no floor plan, no permit number and no door count. The fourth-floor corridor has apartments 4A and 4C and, between them, a fire-hose cabinet. Dev paces the distance between the two doors: nine paces. Walking back, twelve. Then, from inside the wall, two knocks.

His manager, Noreen Whitmore, tells him it is a migration ghost and to delete it. Her regional boss, Randall Pugh, puts it in writing: Unit 4B does not exist, and the row must be gone before the data-room freeze on 31 October. Dev does not delete it. He is a person who archives instead of deleting, and he has a private reason: when his mother first got sick, doctors told the family for months that the thing they could hear was nothing.

The audit trail then shows that his own login has posted 4B's rent at 23:58 on the 19th of every month for fourteen months, starting on his first day, with a receipt in what looks like his handwriting. He has no memory of it. A cash envelope of mixed old banknotes (a 1957 silver certificate, a 1963 five) appears in the night drop on the 20th and Joyce Lim in accounts deposits it without being able to say whose it is.

Dev learns that the whole building treats 4B the way a family treats an uncle nobody mentions. Chioma Eze in 4A leaves pencil notes on the wall and hears a radiator that asks questions. Zofia Baran in 3B, directly underneath, hears someone pacing and once sewed curtains for a window no one can see. Mr Ptolemy Grieve in 6A has put out a tea plate for "the gentleman in four" for thirty-six years. Mrs Han in 5C greets "the quiet ones" every morning in the tenant group chat. Rafa Ibarra, the super, oils a key every day for a door that does not exist.

Dev discovers the following, in roughly this order:

1. **The ledger rule.** The building obeys its rent roll. Entries made under the clerk's login are carried out physically at 02:10 each night, and every entry is balanced out of the clerk's own life. The building takes what the clerk is holding least, however much it weighs. Dev's first losses are small (a mug, spare keys), then a saved voicemail from his mother that he has never dared to play, then people. The rent for 4B is paid in the clerk's neglected life, which is why his life has been so small for fourteen months.
2. **The clerks.** The building has always grown a Rent Roll Clerk: Ellis Kwan from 1986, Margie Dunleavy from 1999, then a five-year gap in which the building visibly decayed, then Dev. A clerk who writes the building long enough runs out of things to be debited and is "filed": moved into 4B as a tenant. Margie went in on 30 August 2019. Noreen hired Dev knowing this, because the building was dying without a clerk, and because he told her in his interview that the only person who would need to be told if he got the job was his sister, "I guess."
3. **The room.** 4B is a long narrow room, eleven feet wide and twenty-two feet deep (nine of Dev's paces) between 4A and 4C, with one window in bay 2 of the façade. The window was bricked up in 1986; at night it glows. The room keeps people no one is attending to. Ten have been kept since 1961. Each lives in their own "hour", always about ten past four on a rainy afternoon in their own year, and hears the others only as sounds. Dev meets whichever one is nearest the door that day, which is whoever is being thought about hardest by someone outside.
4. **He made the door.** At the midpoint he types 1 into the required field DOOR_COUNT. At 02:10 a door grows out of the corridor wall. The room itself is old (it was Room 412 of the hotel, and the kept have been in it since 1961), but a way into it exists only while a clerk holds the door open with a door count of 1; when a clerk is filed, the door shuts behind them. RentWise still shows DR = 1 under Margie's last edit, a fossil that explains the door's afterimage on Espie's video, and Tenantry's migration dropped the field, which is why the row went red. The door did not come back because Dev found the room; it came back because he filled in the blank. Inside he meets Margie, who has been reading his entries for fourteen months. He forgets to count his paces on the way out and loses three days. Afterwards he learns the rule: count in, count out.
5. **Noreen's husband.** Gary Whitmore, a pipefitter, came into the building on Saturday 16 January 2016 to bring his wife her lunch at ten past four. She was on the phone and did not look up. He has been in 4B since. Noreen has known ever since, in the way one knows a diagnosis one has declined to have confirmed. She washes his socks each weekend and leaves them on a dryer for Thursday. She is not a murderer, she is something harder to forgive.
6. **How to get out.** A tenant can leave only when (a) the unit is a certified unit with a door that has a floor plan, a permit number and a door count, and (b) someone outside says the tenant's full name aloud at the door while that tenant's hour is the nearest, and (c) the tenant chooses to step through. Without (a), stepping through a door leads into the margin of the record, which is nowhere.
7. **The clock.** Larchmont's integration script purges any rent line without a valid unit at 02:10 on Friday 21 November, three days before closing. Purging the row unmakes the room and everyone in it.
8. **The slide.** Pugh's data-room freeze forces Dev to clear 211 data-quality flags in a week. Each edit is paid for. The clocks go back on 2 November and the nightly batch runs twice. His neglected things run out. By 5 November he is losing what he loves: his apartment at Tolliver Court, his employee record, his phone number, Noreen's and Joyce's recognition of him. His name appears as Tenant of Record on the 4B row, pending, effective 02:10 on Sunday 9 November. He is about to be filed.
9. **The vigil.** On Saturday 8 November the tenants gather in the lobby and say his name aloud until the batch runs. Noreen finds his emergency contact and phones his sister Anjali, who has forgotten she has a brother, and makes her say it. The pending status is cancelled. Anjali flies in. The building's attention, not any magic, saves him.
10. **The audit.** Woo Cho, Larchmont's analyst, walks the building on 12 November, hears Margie's voice through the wall, answers her, and sees the door. He cannot sign off a unit he cannot document, but he can mark it "under review", which buys time until the purge. Mr Grieve collapses with pleural cancer, and asks to be taken in to meet the gentleman before he dies.
11. **What Margie wants.** Margie explains the arithmetic. Certifying the unit means three required fields (floor plan, permit, door count, the last done) plus a Source of Funds: the building will ask whom the sum is being taken from. Ellis Kwan tried it in 1998 with floor plan and door count but no permit (he never found the infill permit), wrote "me" on the Paid From line of the paper rent book, and was filed; the building took him and gave nothing back, because it does not make change and does not certify two-thirds of a unit. That is the reason Dev must have all three fields before he answers the question. Margie herself is not asking to be rescued. Some of the ten want the radiator and the rain. A door is only worth having if it opens both ways.
12. **Last tea.** Mr Grieve is wheeled in as a guest on 18 November and takes tea with Thaddeus Ambler at last (two lumps). Ambler declines to leave; he is waiting for a call from his wife Lorna, who died in 1979, and Grieve, who knew, promises to take the call on Sundays for as long as he can. Grieve dies in 6A in the early hours of 19 November, attended.
13. **The choice.** On the night of 20 November Dev types the floor plan and the permit number (borrowed from the 1986 window-infill permit). Tenantry then refuses the save: LEDGER UNBALANCED, SOURCE OF FUNDS REQUIRED. The default source, PREPARER, is exhausted. A picklist offers the people he holds dear. "Self" is on it. Choosing himself would fill the room's ledger and leave no clerk to keep it. Chioma, Rafa and Noreen are needed: she says his name every morning, he cannot lose them. He chooses his sister Anjali, who is in another state and cannot stop him. Chioma tells him he is choosing her because she is far away. This is also true.
14. **The ending.** The batch runs. The corridor shrinks back to nine paces with a door in it. Larchmont's script skips a valid unit. At dawn Noreen says "Gary Michael Whitmore" at the door and Gary walks out, unaged, to a wife ten years older. Margie and Ambler stay, by choice, but now with a door. Anjali wakes with no brother; her phone's photographs have closed up around the space where he stood; she thinks the line in her mother's eulogy that Dev wrote is her own. Dev phones her at eight as he has every Sunday at six, now as a wrong number. She says he sounds like he has been up all night.
15. **Coda (March).** Dev lives in Mr Grieve's apartment, 6A. He walks the fourth floor each morning, nine paces with a door in the middle, and says the names of the kept aloud. The window in bay 2 has been unbricked and Margie sees the spring. Ambler waits for his Sunday call. Every Sunday at six Dev calls a woman in Raleigh who does not know she has a brother. She answers on the second ring and has begun to say "Oh, it's you."

**Ending note.** Disturbing and quietly sad. The reader must feel that Dev did a good thing in a cold way and that both halves are true. Nobody is redeemed for free.

## 2. World and setting

### The city
An unnamed mid-sized American city with a river, a beltway, two hospitals, a minor-league ballpark and a lot of parking structures. It is wet October, then November. Rain is specific: gutters, umbrella spokes, wet cardboard, the sound of a bus braking on a slick road. No named river, no named state. Real national things (Diwali, Halloween, the clocks going back, Raleigh, Edison NJ, Zoom) are fine.

### Harwick Terrace (map in words)
**114 East 31st Street.** A seven-story brick building, built in 1927 as the Harwick Hotel (a residential hotel) and converted to apartments in 1986. Red-brown brick with a cream terracotta band at the second and seventh floors. Five window bays across the 31st Street front (numbered bay 1 at the west end to bay 5 at the east). A cast-iron fire escape on the alley side. Roof access by a steel door that sticks.

**Lobby.** Brown-and-cream terrazzo, a worn brass H inlaid in the floor, a dead doorman's desk, a bench, and a wall of **41 brass mailboxes** (one for each line on the rent roll; the box for 4B has a name card in Margie's hand reading OCCUPANT and is always full). The lobby clock runs six minutes slow. So do all the building's clocks; no one fixes them.

**Elevators.** Two cars, called Left and Right. Left is the older, sticks on Mondays, and occasionally opens on four with nobody having called it. The floor display above Left occasionally reads 4B for a second.

**Management Office, Suite 100.** The ground-floor office, converted from former unit 1F, which is why the building has 41 positions and 40 doors. Glass door with frosted lettering. Noreen's office at the back with a vision board and a sign reading LIVE LAUGH LEASE; open-plan area with Dev's desk (the inherited desk with Margie's chair and a drawer containing a lemon-drop tin and a loose horn button), Joyce's cubicle (accounts receivable), Espie's leasing desk by the window, a candy dish of butterscotch discs, a night-drop safe, a filing room.

**Basement.** The boiler room; tenant storage cages; the **laundry room** (six washers, six dryers, one extra avocado-green washer with a brass plate reading 4B that is not on the fixed-asset register, and which runs every Thursday from 7:00 to 8:12); Rafa's shop with the **key board** (a pegboard with 41 hooks and 40 labeled keys; on hook 4B hangs a small brass key with a green tag); and the **server closet** with BERNICE, the old beige tower that ran the legacy software, a green steel cabinet labeled NOTES TO FILE and a banker's box of hotel registers.

**The fourth floor.** The only floor that matters. A central core of two elevators and the stairwell. A double-loaded corridor runs east-west. North side (the 31st Street side): 4A at the west end, then an 11-foot stretch of wall holding a red glass-fronted fire-hose cabinet, then 4C. South side (alley side): 4D, 4E, 4F. The plan shows the stretch of wall as riser R-4. On every other floor the A, B and C doors are 11 feet apart; on four, 4A to 4C is 22 feet. A stair landing at the west end has a window ledge where Mr Grieve puts his plate. Carpet: maroon with a diamond lozenge pattern, repeating every 3 feet. A faint smell of toast that comes and goes.

**Other floors that matter.** 3B (Zofia) sits directly beneath 4B. 5C (Mrs Han). 6A (Mr Grieve), a corner unit with the best view and the steno pads. 2F (Owen Fenske, graduate student, complainer). 6C (Bao Tran). 5E (Priscilla Obi, nurse, rarely seen).

**The façade at night.** From the bus shelter across 31st Street you count five bays on level four. Bay 2 is infilled with newer, lighter brick and wider mortar, per permit BP-1986-04417. At night, from about 7:40 pm, the bricked opening glows amber.

**Other places.** Halloran's Deli across the street (ham on rye, sells waterproof notebooks next to the batteries). The 14 bus (22 minutes from Dev's apartment, stop at 31st and Merrow). Tolliver Court: Dev's 1970s garden-style complex on Kessler Avenue, 2.1 miles east, apartment 3F, studio, rent $1,125. The Municipal Annex downtown (Permit Records, window 3). Merrow General Hospital, six blocks north (Chioma's work). The Gaslight (trivia bar). Corvane Plaza on the beltway (regional office, never visited).

### Corvane, Larchmont and the software
**Corvane Property Group** manages seventeen buildings. **Larchmont Residential Partners** is buying it. Key dates: data-room freeze Friday 31 October; Larchmont's physical walk-through of Harwick on Wednesday 12 November; **orphan-unit purge at 02:10 on Friday 21 November**; closing Monday 24 November. The purge deletes any rent line that fails validation.

**RentWise 2.4** is the legacy software, green on black, running on BERNICE in the basement since 2003. **Tenantry 9** is the new web software with a validation sweep (green tick, red banner), an audit trail, required fields and a rule that any entry over $1,000 needs a **Source of Funds** attestation (a dropdown that defaults to PREPARER). The Tenantry trainer is Brent, who appears only on Zoom and says "super".

### The rules (the ledger rule and its costs)
These are the book's magic. State them as Dev infers them: as an analyst, with caveats, never as a lecture. Never add a rule not on this list.

1. **The roll is the truth.** The building tries to match its rent roll. A row with a tenant paying rent is a unit. A unit with a door count has a door, for as long as the clerk who entered the count still holds the job; when a clerk is filed, the door shuts behind them and the field becomes a dead letter.
2. **Only the clerk counts.** Only edits made under the clerk login (RRCLERK, whoever or whatever holds it) have physical effect, and only inside Harwick's walls. The one exception is the clerk's own body and life: wherever he goes, the building's jurisdiction goes with him, which is how it reaches Tolliver Court. Other Tenantry roles (Noreen's manager role, accounts receivable, leasing) cannot edit tenant master data, so no one else can clear Dev's flags for him. Larchmont's integration script is provisioned under the RRCLERK service account for the migration, which is why its purge is carried out physically.
3. **The batch.** At 02:10 each night the building settles the day's entries. Pipes sing, the clocks stop for the length of the batch (about a minute), the radiators knock twice.
4. **Balance.** Every entry is paid for out of the clerk's own life. The building takes what the clerk is holding least, in order of least attention (attention, not use: a key he turns twice a day without once looking at it counts as unheld), regardless of how much it weighs. Trailing spaces in a name cost a spare key; a phone-number fix costs a gift card; a saved voicemail from a dead mother that he has not dared to play is very cheap indeed. When the neglected things run out it takes held things. The building does not make change: if the sum is bigger than the thing taken, the surplus is simply lost.
5. **Nothing is destroyed; it is filed.** What the building takes goes into 4B, turning up as physical objects (a microcassette of the voicemail, labeled in Dev's block capitals). Margie keeps a shelf of what the clerks have lost.
6. **The rent.** 4B's $1,840 a month is posted by the clerk's login at 23:58 on the 19th. It is the invoice for the clerk's month of neglect. The cash that appears in the night drop is the building's receipt.
7. **Counting.** The corridor between 4A and 4C is 22.00 feet by laser. By footsteps it lengthens as the clerk attends to the room (Dev's counts rise from nine to thirty-one). Inside the room it is nine paces from door to window. **Count in, count out**: if the number of paces going in matches coming out, you leave in the same hour. If you do not count, hours and days go missing.
8. **Seeing the door.** The door is visible to anyone who has attended to 4B with intention at least once (said good morning to the quiet ones, left a plate, knocked back). Outsiders see a hose cabinet. The door's afterimage (the RentWise DR = 1 fossil) can flicker on camera for a frame or two and is visible then only to people who have attended to 4B; to everyone else the same frame shows a cabinet.
9. **The hours.** Ten tenants share the room, each in their own year and their own ten-past-four. The one nearest the door is whoever is being attended to hardest from outside that day. The 4B tenant name in the system changes to match.
10. **Witness and filing.** The building files (kept in 4B) a person when nobody is attending to them. A person who is being named aloud by others is not filed. Mere texting does not count (nor typed comments); a live video call or livestream counts only for people who come on camera or microphone and say the name to the person's face on screen. It has to be spoken, to the person, with their full name, by someone looking at them.
11. **Leaving.** See premise item 6. The door must be certified (floor plan, permit number, door count all valid, and the Source of Funds paid), someone says the full name at the door, and the tenant chooses to step out. A tenant who steps out returns to ordinary time at the age they went in.
12. **Source of Funds.** Over a threshold, Tenantry asks whom the entry is being paid from. If the default is exhausted it presents a picklist of the clerk's attachments and the building takes the one chosen. The building asks the question in whatever the clerk is using: before RentWise it was a Paid From line in the paper rent book (Ellis wrote "me" there), and RentWise had no such field. When the migration imported the old postings it filled the new field with the default.
13. **No clerk, no building.** From 2019 to 2024 there was no clerk; occupancy fell from 98% to 81%, the elevators failed, the pipes froze twice. The clerk's life is the building's operating reserve.

### Glossary
- **Rent roll:** the table of all 41 units, tenants, rents and balances.
- **HT-4B:** the code for the problem row (HT is Harwick Terrace).
- **Validation:** Tenantry's green tick or red banner on each row.
- **FLOORPLAN_ID / PERMIT_NO / DOOR_COUNT:** the three required fields that 4B lacks.
- **Source of Funds:** the attestation field. Default PREPARER.
- **Migration ghost:** corporate term for a row left behind by a data migration; Noreen's and Pugh's name for 4B.
- **Data room / freeze:** Larchmont's due-diligence repository and the cut-off after which it is locked.
- **Orphan-unit purge:** the Larchmont script that deletes unvalidated lines at 02:10 on 21 November.
- **The batch / EOD-HT:** the nightly 02:10 run.
- **BERNICE:** the legacy server. Label: BERNICE, DO NOT UNPLUG, M.D.
- **Notes to file:** the one-page letters each clerk leaves in the green cabinet. This book is Dev's.
- **The clerk:** the Rent Roll Clerk; also the person whose life the building spends.
- **The kept:** the ten people in 4B (never "ghosts", never "prisoners").
- **An hour:** one tenant's version of the room.
- **The nearest:** the tenant whose hour is currently at the door.
- **Count in, count out:** the pacing rule.
- **The plate:** Mr Grieve's tea plate on the fourth-floor stair ledge, daily at four.
- **The quiet ones:** Mrs Han's phrase for the people nobody mentions, always in "Good morning to all, including the quiet ones."
- **Roll call:** Chioma's rule: Dev texts his name twice a day, later says it aloud.
- **The hose cabinet:** what outsiders see where the door is.
- **Bay two:** the bricked window.
- **The extra washer:** machine 4B in the laundry room.
- **Left / Right:** the elevators.
- **Slow time:** every clock in the building is six minutes slow.
- **Documented coaching:** Corvane's phrase for a performance improvement plan.
- **Lunch & Learn:** the monthly training lunch.
- **"Noted.":** Joyce's single-word reply to everything.
- **The Annex:** the Municipal Annex where permits are kept.
- **Tolliver:** Dev's apartment complex.
- **Preparer:** the default Source of Funds; the person entering.
- **The Gaslight:** the trivia bar Dev has not entered in 21 months.
- **The Rascals:** Dev's old trivia team (the Quizzy Rascals), eight members, of whom Dev will lose two names.

## 3. Cast

Naming palette: South Asian-American, Nigerian-American, Latino, Korean-American, Polish-American and white working-class. No name from the overused list.

### Devendra "Dev" Mahajan, 29 (narrator)
**Look (fixed).** About 5'9", narrow shoulders, a permanent slight stoop from desks. Round wire glasses with the left hinge mended with green electrical tape. A cowlick at the crown that rises when he is stressed. A split left thumbnail from a bike chain at fifteen.
**Speech.** Self-qualifying, precise, quick. Starts with "Technically," and "To be fair," and apologizes to furniture. Puts numbers in sentences. Jokes in an undertone to himself. "Technically it's a data quality issue." "I'm sorry, I mean, it's a drawer, I'm apologizing to a drawer." Hates conflict; says "no worries" when there are worries.
**Wants.** To do the job right and be left alone. Underneath, to be told he is fine by someone who would know.
**Fears.** That he is the kind of person nobody would miss. That his mother's last call was something he could have answered.
**Secret.** On Thursday 11 January last year at 9:41 pm, while the family kept a hospice vigil without him because he had a trivia night (the Rascals needed a ninth), his mother Kalpana called. He saw MUMMY on the screen and let it ring out because he was mid-answer. She left a 23-second voicemail. She died at 3:12 am. He has never listened to it. He quit the Rascals the next week and has not been to the Gaslight since. Anjali does not know he saw the call.
**Arc.** From a man who lets everything go unattended, including himself, to a man who understands attention as the only thing that keeps anyone from disappearing, and then spends the thing he finally holds best, in a cold clear piece of arithmetic that is also an act of love. He ends as the building's clerk with a new ritual: he says the names aloud.

### Anjali Mahajan Kowalczyk, 33 (sister)
**Look.** 5'2", strong forearms, a gap between her front teeth, a pale scar through her left eyebrow from a scooter at eight, hair in a claw clip. Pediatric occupational therapist in Raleigh. Married to Ben Kowalczyk; daughter Tia, 4.
**Speech.** Brisk, imperative, warm under it. "Okay. Okay. Here's what we do." Calls Dev "you idiot" as an endearment and "Dev-u" when frightened. He calls her "Anju".
**Wants.** For her brother to be alright, near, and on the Sunday call at six. For someone else to share the grief.
**Fears.** Being the only one who is still carrying their mother.
**Secret.** She resents that Dev was not at the hospice and has never said so; she also did not call him until 3:30 am because she was ashamed of resenting him.
**Arc.** She is the one person who has paid steady attention to Dev all his life. She is the cost. She comes to Harwick, helps, goes home, and loses him, then answers a wrong number every Sunday.

### Noreen Whitmore, 58 (manager; antagonist and tragic figure)
**Look.** 5'4", cropped burgundy-dyed hair, reading glasses on a beaded chain, a lanyard with fourteen keys that chime when she walks, a smoker's rasp six years after quitting, forever chewing nicotine gum.
**Speech.** Warm, quick, managerial. "Hon." "Let's circle back." "Let's put that in a drawer." She is funny and not at all stupid. Under strain she goes very polite and says "okay" three times.
**Wants.** The audit to pass; thirty-one months to her pension; the building to stand; Gary.
**Fears.** The look on Gary's face if she opens the door and is ten years older.
**Secret.** Her husband Gary is in 4B and she has known since 2016. She hired Dev for the building's sake. She has a spare 4B key on her lanyard.
**Arc.** From the woman who tells the clerk to delete the row to the woman who stands at the door and says her husband's full name aloud. She is not forgiven by the book, only seen.

### Chioma Eze, 34 (4A)
**Look.** 6'0", red-framed glasses, a burn scar on the inside of her right wrist from a kettle at eleven, twists dyed honey-gold at the ends, hospital clogs in green.
**Speech.** Fast, dry, bossy. "For the record," before every fact. Calls him "Mahajan". Uses a few Igbo words with her mother (biko, ewo) and never explains them. "For the record, I am not scared. I'm tired, and from the outside it looks the same."
**Job.** Respiratory therapist, nights, Merrow General.
**Wants.** Sleep; her own place; to stop being a person who talks to a wall.
**Fears.** Being told she is lonely and imagining it. Her mother's long illness taught her to distrust her own certainty.
**Secret.** For six years she has written a pencil note on the wall by her bed every night ("Rain tonight, Margie.") and has had answers: two knocks for yes. She was planning to leave Harwick for Houston in December.
**Arc.** From private ritual to public witness. She establishes the daily roll call that saves Dev. She cancels Houston. She refuses to be the name he picks and then tells him exactly why he picked someone else.

### Rafael "Rafa" Ibarra, 52 (super)
**Look.** Broad, heavy forearms, the top of his left ring finger missing, a gray-tipped moustache, a tool belt with a flashlight he never uses, reading glasses mended with white tape, always a toothpick.
**Speech.** Slow, kind, evasive. "Mira," to open a sentence. Answers questions with questions. "Depends who's asking." Occasional proverbs half-remembered.
**Wants.** Two more years and then a house outside Zacatecas.
**Fears.** Being asked to turn the key again.
**Secret.** On 30 August 2019 he turned the key for Margie and watched her go in. He has oiled it every morning since in case she wants to come out.
**Arc.** From keeper of a key to a man who walks into the room.

### Zofia Baran, 61 (3B)
**Look.** 4'11", always in a man's gray cardigan, a measuring tape around her neck, a wrist pincushion, two missing molars so that her s whistles.
**Speech.** Polish syntax, drops articles. "Is not normal, is building." Measures people on sight: "You lost two centimeter."
**Job.** Alterations from her front room.
**Wants.** Quiet. To be measured by someone once in a while.
**Fears.** Dying unnoticed in her apartment (widow, no children).
**Secret.** Her notebook of measurements of every tenant since 1998 shows who is thinning. She knows who has been thinning for years. She made the 4B curtains on a commission in 2020 and has never said whose window they are.
**Arc.** She measures Dev in decline in the slide chapters, sews his name into his jacket for the vigil, and in the coda measures him again: "Same."

### Mr Ptolemy Grieve, 84 (6A)
**Look.** Long face, large ears with whistling hearing aids, pencil moustache, brown tweed three-piece suit even to collect the mail, a cane with a brass duck-head handle, a cough like a drawer being opened.
**Speech.** Formal, exact, court-reporter pedantic. "Strictly speaking." "Quite so." "Tol-uh-mee. The P is silent, like most of my family's expectations."
**Job.** Retired court reporter (forty-one years of other people's worst days, taken down verbatim).
**Wants.** To know what sugar the gentleman takes.
**Fears.** Dying in a hospital bed with the record unfinished.
**Secret.** He wrote to Dayton in 1994 and learned that Lorna Ambler died in 1979. He has known for thirty-one years that the gentleman is waiting for a call that will never come.
**Arc.** Dying of pleural cancer. He sits at Ambler's table as a guest, takes tea, and is attended to his death. Leaves Dev his steno pads and his apartment.

### Margaret "Margie" Dunleavy, 71 (65 inside)
**Look.** Tall, big hands, an oatmeal cardigan with the third button missing (Dev has it), reading glasses on a cord, a tin of lemon drops. Hair cut short and gray.
**Speech.** Dry Midwestern. "Right then." "Dear," "I should say." Practical and exact. "The books must balance, dear. It isn't a punishment. It's accounting."
**Wants.** To be left to her tea and her radiator. She does not want rescue; she wants her tenancy to be a choice.
**Fears.** Being returned to a world where nobody can say her name; being unmade by the sale.
**Secret.** She was not taken; she stepped in. She asked Rafa to turn the key. She was tired and had never been attended to.

## 4. Relationships and conflicts

## 5. Voice guide

**Viewpoint and frame.** First person, past tense, Dev. The whole book is a **note to file** that Dev writes after the events, for whoever holds the clerk's desk next. He opens chapter 1 by saying what a note to file is. He addresses the reader as "you" rarely (the incoming clerk), at most once or twice in the middle and then at the close. He is writing from some months after the events, so he can say "later I learned" but must not spoil by hints ("little did I know").

**Tense.** Past, with present-tense asides for standing facts about the building ("The lobby clock is six minutes slow").

**Register.** Clean plain American English. Specific and slightly formal, an office worker's precision. Anxious, funny and observant. Comparisons come from offices, forms, software and small domestic objects: a spreadsheet, a laminated sign, a hole punch, a fire exit map. The humor is dry and mostly in understatement and self-correction. Early chapters are comic and the comedy thins as costs mount; by the late chapters it is a coping habit, not a texture.

**Sentence rhythm.** Mostly medium sentences with the occasional very short one to land a fact. Lists of three are allowed once in a while. Numbers appear as digits for measurements, counts and times in documents, and as words in dialogue and ordinary prose when small. Do not open consecutive paragraphs with the same word.

**Dialogue.** Double quotation marks. A new paragraph for each speaker. Dev answers questions with another question when scared. People interrupt, dodge, answer a different question, and say less than they mean. Rely on distinct speech: Chioma's "For the record,"; Rafa's "Mira," and "Depends who's asking."; Zofia's dropped articles; Mr Grieve's "Strictly speaking."; Noreen's "hon"; Joyce's "Noted."; Margie's "dear"; Woo's "Per the checklist."; Espie's "ok so". About 35 to 45 percent of most chapters is dialogue.

**Documents.** Each chapter carries at least one document, set as block quotes. A document is one or two blockquote paragraphs, each beginning with "> ", with a blank line between. Never write a document as separate lines inside one paragraph: the build joins consecutive "> " lines into one paragraph, so write a header line ("From: ... To: ... Subject: ...") as one run-on blockquote line and the body as a second. Typical documents: validation screens, Tenantry audit trail lines, emails from Pugh and Noreen, the tenant group chat (each message its own blockquote), work orders ("HT-88213: 4B radiator knocks. CLOSED, no access."), the 1961 guest register entry, notes to file, a county permit, the TikTok caption and comments, a Source of Funds dropdown, a lease clause, the final apartment listing. Documents are short, flat and bureaucratic. They carry the dread by being calm.

**Humor.** Plenty in chapters 1 to 8, steadily less after; never at the expense of the kept.

**Frightening things.** Fear comes from almost-normal details: a count that is three steps off, a stamp on an audit trail, a form that asks for a source of funds. No screaming, no jump scares, no blood, no visible monsters. Nobody in 4B is hostile.

**Sample paragraph (voice only; not part of the novel).**

I should say that I was good at my job. It is not a job people hope to be good at, but there were pleasures in it: a column of phone numbers that all had ten digits, a lease that ended on a Saturday and was moved, quietly, to the Friday before. On Monday mornings I opened the rent roll and watched the forty-one rows come up, each with its unit number and its tenant and its balance, like a school photograph in which nobody has blinked. That was how I knew something was wrong with 4B before I knew anything else. Forty rows looked at the camera. One row looked at me. I would like to tell you I closed the file. I made a coffee instead, because the coffee was free and because I am the kind of person who, offered a door or a drink, takes the drink and calls it a plan.

**Things this narrator would never write.**
1. "Little did I know", "if only I had known", or any foreshadowing that tells the reader to be afraid.
2. "Suddenly".
3. The words haunted, cursed, evil, ghost or supernatural for the room. He says "the unit", "the room", "it".
4. A summary of emotion ("I felt a wave of sadness"). He shows what he did with his hands.
5. A lecture on the rules. He states what he inferred, how he tested it, and what he is not sure of.
6. Weather as mood. Rain is a bus, a gutter, a wet sleeve.
7. Similes from horror films or fantasy.
8. An exclamation mark in narration.
9. A chapter ending on a question to the reader.
10. Em dash chains. A dash may appear now and then; commas and full stops do the work.
11. Claims of certainty he lacks. He says "as far as I could tell".
12. Mocking Noreen, Mr Grieve or the kept.

## 8. Ground rules that must stay consistent

**Dev's count log (corridor, 4A door to 4C door, in his paces).**
6 Oct: 9 going, 12 returning. 8 Oct: 14. 9 Oct: 15; Zofia's overhead pacing: five steps. 14 Oct: 17. 17 Oct: 19. 20 Oct: 21. 22 Oct, afternoon: 24. After the door, from 23 Oct: 31 total, with the door 14 paces from 4A. After 21 Nov: 9, with the door at four paces from 4A, four from 4C and one for the door itself. The laser always reads 22.00 ft.

**Inside the room.** Door to window is nine paces. Eleven feet wide. Window at the far end, overlooking 31st Street in the nearest tenant's year. Radiator under the window; a hot plate; a sink; a Murphy bed or a recliner or a bed depending on the hour; a shelf of ten teacups; the clock stuck at 4:10; always wet light and the sound of rain; the smell of toast and wet wool. Phones show 4:10 and no service. Time slips only when you do not count: Dev loses three days on 23 Oct; each later visit is counted and costs no time.

**Distances and times.** Dev's bus: 22 minutes, the 14. Tolliver Court is 2.1 miles from Harwick. Raleigh to the city: a flight of about ninety minutes, three hours door to door. Municipal Annex: 19 minutes downtown by bus. Merrow General: six blocks north, a 12-minute walk. The batch: 02:10, lasting about a minute. The laundry washer 4B runs Thursdays 7:00 to 8:12 pm.

**Dates.** All dates are for the year with Monday 6 October and Friday 21 November. Do not name the year. Diwali is Monday 20 October. Clocks go back on Sunday 2 November.

**Who knows what, and when.**
- Noreen: everything about the room and the clerks, since 2016. She does not know the Source of Funds picklist exists.
- Rafa: that the key works and that Margie is inside. He does not know about Gary until ch 17.
- Chioma: that something answers on the wall (always); the door and the rule only from ch 14 and ch 16.
- Zofia and Mrs Han: that someone is there. Neither knows who.
- Mr Grieve: that the gentleman is Ambler; that clerks vanish after a long tenure; he does not know the ledger rule.
- Joyce, Espie: only the anomalies (cash; the video). They forget Dev when he is erased and remember when named.
- Anjali: nothing until ch 20. She never learns the price. She never learns it was she.
- Woo: nothing until ch 21.
- Pugh: nothing, ever.
- Dev learns, in order: the count (1), the self-posting (2 and 3), the rituals (4 and 9), the cost rule by experiment (10), the clerks and Margie's name (11), Gary (7 and 13, confirmed 16), the exit rule (17, confirmed 22), the picklist (22 warns, 25 shows).

**What the building never does.** It never harms anyone with violence. It never takes someone who is being attended to by name. It never gives change. It never adds a rule late.

**Things that must not change.** Physical descriptions above. Noreen's burgundy hair. Chioma's red glasses. Rafa's missing finger. Margie's missing third button. Mr Grieve's duck-head cane. Dev's taped glasses and crossed sevens. The lobby clock is six minutes slow until 02:10 on 21 November. Left sticks on Mondays.

**Style.** American spelling and idiom, "apartment" not "flat". No periods after Mr, Mrs, Ms, Dr. Documents as blockquotes. No emojis anywhere. Keep real brands out of the plot; passing mentions of ordinary things are fine.
