# Bible: The Quiet Witness

## Logline and premise

**Logline.** A federal court interpreter, sworn to add nothing and omit nothing, overhears a defence lawyer teach the government's star witness to change one Spanish word, and must decide whether keeping her oath means keeping silent.

**Premise, in order.** *United States v. Hanlon et al.*, Courtroom 5B, Newark. Defendants: Gerald Hanlon, owner of Halyard Freight Services, and Calvin Pryor, his operations manager. Charges: racketeering conspiracy, bribery of terminal checkers and a federal cargo inspector, and, against Pryor alone, murder in aid of racketeering. On 14 March 2022, at 2:10 a.m., cargo surveyor Reynard Cole died in Halyard's Berth 14 yard when a container was swung into him.

The murder count rests on one phrase. At 11:48 p.m. Pryor phoned spreader operator Rigoberto Salas and said, in English, "Take care of it by morning. Get it to claims." Cole had filed a written complaint about mislabelled containers; Pryor meant: bury the paperwork. Salas, who now cooperates (manslaughter-in-aid plea, 5K1.1 letter promised), told FBI Special Agent Kyle Gaffney in November 2022 that Pryor said "encárgate de eso" (take care of *that*). Gaffney's classroom Spanish led him to ask "¿De él? ¿De Cole?" Salas, exhausted and afraid, answered "Sí, sí, lo que usted diga" ("uté diga" in his accent). Gaffney's report wrote: "take care of *him*."

The truth: Wade Sutter, Hanlon's yard boss (died January 2024), radioed "Swing it, Rigo, now." Rigo, trained to obey, swung the spreader. Cole died. Teo Quintanilla, a crane mechanic in the maintenance shed with a handheld radio, heard Sutter's voice. Teo, who maintained Crane 9, took $5,000 from Sutter to log it out of service that night so no lift record exists, and has said nothing.

On Monday 6 October 2025 Rigo testifies honestly: "eso." At lunch Hanlon's lawyer Vincent Lazzaro slips into Witness Room 5C and coaches him back to "él", because a Pryor murder conviction closes the book on the yard and Sutter's name never matters to Hanlon. His lever: the "Halyard Family Hardship Fund" pays Rigo's family's rent. Interpreter Marisol Quintanilla, eating lunch in the alcove behind the thin partition, hears everything and notes it from habit. That afternoon she must faithfully render the lie.

She checks facts quietly: the audio of Exhibit 114A, an anonymous letter to the judge, her estranged brother. Private investigator Emmett Foyle notices. Prosecutor Dana Whitcomb already knows Lazzaro visited the witness and chose silence. The jury convicts Pryor on all counts on 7 November. Marisol is provisionally suspended after an anonymous bias complaint. She goes to Dana in writing, her notes having been stolen from her flat, and on 2–4 December testifies at the Rule 33 hearing, reproducing the coaching verbatim from trained memory. Rigo recants in English: "It. *Eso.* It." Teo testifies about the radio. Foyle, who has recorded every call with Lazzaro since 1999 out of habit, hands over the file. Judge Okoro vacates the murder verdict, orders a new trial on that count and refers Lazzaro and Hanlon. Pryor's bribery convictions stand. Marisol is removed from the federal list for six months and her income collapses, Teo pleads to falsifying records, and the Administrative Office opens a review of the interpreter's duty to report. In March 2026 she is still an interpreter.

## World and setting

**Newark courthouse** (50 Walnut Street). Courtroom 5B: pale oak, fourteen jury seats, a wall clock three minutes fast. The interpreters' alcove is a former mop closet with a kettle and one chair; its plasterboard partition is shared with Witness Room 5C. Deputy Marshal Desai logs every entry to 5C on the door sheet. Cafeteria on three; Dino's Luncheonette on Market Street.

**The Ironbound.** Portuguese and Brazilian bakeries and Dominican colmados. Marisol rents the flat over a shoe-repair on Ferry Street. Panadería La Espiga, run by Nilda Cabral, sells pan con queso. **Port Newark**: Berth 14, Halyard's yard, containers stacked four high, straddle carriers, red crane lights, Meadowlands fog. **Union City**, Bergenline Avenue: the Salas flat. A Hackensack hotel serves as the marshals' safe house.

**The rules of the work.** Interpreters speak in the first person ("I told him...") and add, omit, explain or advise nothing. They may speak to the Court only about a linguistic problem. Consecutive: speaker pauses, interpreter renders from notes. Simultaneous: rendered live. Contract interpreters are on the Administrative Office's certified list, paid by the day, and can be dropped without a hearing. The Code bars disclosing anything learned in the course of the work. No rule says what to do about a crime heard through a wall; that gap is the book. Cost of reporting: loss of the list, loss of every future case, and becoming a witness, which means never interpreting that case again.

**Glossary.**
- *eso / él*: "that" / "him"; the whole case.
- *uté*: Cibao "usted"; the final s is dropped.
- *compai*: pal; Lazzaro's coaching word.
- *ñapa*: the little extra; untranslatable.
- *vaina*: Dominican all-purpose "thing".
- *tíguere*: street-smart operator.
- *colmado*: corner shop.
- notation: Marisol's symbols (circle-and-line for "world", slash for "not", arrows for cause).
- the Code: interpreters' professional code.
- the list: certified roster.
- the alcove; the door sheet.
- 302: FBI interview report.
- Exhibit 114A: the audio of that interview.
- 5K1.1 letter: prosecutor's letter to reward cooperation.
- Rule 33: new-trial motion.
- Jencks / Giglio: disclosure of witness material.
- sidebar: private bench conference.
- spreader: the frame that grips a container.
- straddle carrier: lifting vehicle.
- manifest: cargo list.
- the Fondo: the Halyard Family Hardship Fund.
- the green book: Pryor's pocket ledger of cash received.

## Cast

**Marisol Quintanilla**, 44, "Mari" to Teo, "Ms. Q" to clerks. Federal-certified since 2014. Grey streak at the right temple she will not dye; ink on her left middle finger; reading glasses on a beaded chain her mother made; a green top-bound reporter's pad. Father Puerto Rican (Efraín, Rutgers custodian, d. 2009), mother Dominican from Santiago (Altagracia, d. 2023). Speaks low, precise, finishing sentences in her head first; a habit of silently rendering other people's words; says "Let me put that another way." Example: "I can say what he said. I can't say what he meant. That's the whole of my job and the whole of my trouble." Wants to be accurate; fears being a person who is merely a pipe; hides that she skipped her mother's last month to work. Arc: from conduit to witness: she learns that faithfulness has an owner.

**Rigoberto "Rigo" Salas**, 47, spreader operator. Missing tip of left ring finger; rosary-bead bracelet; borrowed blue tie. From a hill village outside Santiago; Cibao Spanish ("puebio", dropped s). Calls Marisol "doña"; tic: "¿me entiende?" Example: "Yo no soy mala gente, doña. Yo soy gente que obedece." Wife Yocasta, daughter Yaritza (16). Wants his family safe; fears deportation and his own hands; hides that he knows "eso" is the truth. Arc: obedience to speech.

**Dana Whitcomb**, 39, AUSA, Bayonne, father a longshoreman checker. Tall, hair in a hasty clip, same navy suit, chews unlit cinnamon toothpicks. Short declaratives; "Say more." Example: "I don't need it pretty. I need it true and loud." Wants the conviction and Deputy Chief; fears sending the wrong man; hides that she heard "lo que usted diga" in August and let it ride. Arc: from winning to disclosing; she ends up leaving for the federal defender.

**Hon. Anselm Okoro**, 63, district judge, born Irvington to a Nigerian father. Grey clipped beard, reading glasses pushed up, taps a pen twice before ruling. Courtly: "Counsel will approach." Counts to three before speaking. Wants a clean record; fears reversal; hides that his father lost a case in 1979 because no interpreter was provided. Arc: stern to just.

**Vincent Lazzaro**, 58, Hanlon's lawyer. Silver hair, camel coat, cedar scent, signet ring. Smooth, warm; "mi hermano" in Spanish he learned from his Dominican housekeeper Eusebia in Bloomfield, "my friend" in English. Wants Hanlon free and the firm's debt to Hanlon forgotten; fears disbarment; hides everything. Arc: from untouchable to referred.

**Priya Desai**, 38, Deputy U.S. Marshal, Edison, Gujarati-American. Compact, black braid pinned flat, scar through her left eyebrow. Dry, clipped: "Noted." Writes times on the back of her hand. Wants a clean room; fears blame; hides that she left her post for six minutes (12:41) to take a call about her father's stroke. Arc: she documents until documenting is not enough.

**Teo (Mateo) Quintanilla**, 41, crane mechanic. Broad, burn scar on right forearm, St. Michael medal. Spanglish, jokes to deflect, "mira, look." Cared for their mother alone. Wants his pension and no trouble; fears Marisol's judgement; hides the $5,000 and the radio call. Arc: from silence to testimony.

**Emmett Foyle**, 56, investigator, ex-county detective, Kearny. Soft voice, raincoat, thermos of tea, hearing aid in the left ear. Courteous, never threatens: "I'm only here to be of use." Wants a quiet retirement; fears being useless; hides that he has recorded every call since 1999. Arc: finally listens to his own tapes.

**Calvin Pryor**, 52, operations manager. Reading glasses, grey suit, pocket Bible. Writes daily to daughter Imani (17). Clipped Baptist cadence, "Be that as it may." Wants to get home; fears she learns he took $40,000 a year to look away; hides the green book. Arc: owns his guilt and refuses to own the killing.

**Gerald "Gerry" Hanlon**, 61, owner. Broad pink face, courteous, brings pastries to clerks. Sports idioms. Wants it all to stay buried. Appears rarely; never explains himself.

**Loretta Pinckney**, 64, interpreter coordinator, Charleston-born, Panama Canal Zone Spanish. Tiny, silver braids, cardigans. "Mm-hm." Wants to protect the program; hides that she was punished for reporting a lawyer in 2009. Arc: files the report she must, then stands up for Marisol.

**Neil Banks**, 49, Pryor's court-appointed lawyer. Cheap suits, almonds in a tin, stammers when angry. Wants to save Pryor; hides that he never had the audio checked.

*Minor:* Nilda Cabral (bakery), Yocasta and Yaritza Salas, Special Agent Kyle Gaffney, Reynard Cole, widow Odessa Cole, Wade Sutter (dead), Wendell Ibarra (retired interpreter), Julio Peña-Marsh (rookie).

## Voice guide

Close third person, past tense, Marisol; seven chapters in Dana's or Rigo's close third. Register: plain, exact, dry, never showy. Sentences vary: short declaratives under pressure, one long run when she is listening. Humour: understated, mostly Teo, Nilda and Loretta. Dialogue: double quotes, new paragraph per speaker; interpreted lines in the first person as spoken; Spanish phrases are left untranslated only when the scene makes them clear.

**Sample (not part of the novel).** The witness said it in six words and Marisol took them in the way she always did, by letting go of the sound and keeping the shape. A circle with a line through it. An arrow toward the man at the defence table. The rule was to say what he said and nothing he meant, and the rule had never felt like a rule until the wall behind her had spoken. "He told me," she said to the jury, in the witness's voice, in the witness's skin, "to take care of it." Twelve people looked at the old man and not at her. That was the job. She wore it like a good coat, and it was lined with something that had begun, very faintly, to itch.

**Never writes:** (1) a stated moral; (2) "objection!" shouted or gavels pounded; (3) Spanish called musical or exotic; (4) a legal lecture delivered to a layman; (5) a hero speech in court; (6) italicised Spanish wholesale; (7) a chapter ending on a summary; (8) a villain explaining himself.

## Motifs

The green steno pad and its notation; thin walls and doors with sheets; *eso*; pan con queso and Nilda's bakery; Foyle's thermos and its steam; fog over the Meadowlands; the beaded glasses chain; red crane lights; the freezer bag of plantains where she hides her page.
