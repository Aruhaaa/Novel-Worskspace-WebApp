# Outline: The Quiet Witness

**Mystery key.** Solution: Pryor said "take care of it" about Cole's complaint paperwork. Wade Sutter radioed "Swing it, Rigo, now." Agent Gaffney's leading question turned *eso* into *él*, and Rigo, exhausted, said "Sí, sí, lo que uté diga." Hanlon's lawyer buys the change back with the Hardship Fund. Clues and where planted: *eso* (1); the coaching and the Fondo (2); "uté diga" on Exhibit 114A (6, 11); Lazzaro's cadence in cross (7); Foyle's recorder (8, 27); the door sheet (2, 12, 14); Teo's radio (15, 19, 27); the green book (14); the Fondo in a public tax return (22). Red herrings: Desai as leak (10, 12, 17), Teo as informant (15, 16, 19), Loretta as betrayer (16, 21), Dana as villain (14, 20). Cost of the Code: the list, her income, and becoming a witness.

## Chapter 01: The Oath
Marisol; Courtroom 5B, Monday 6 October, morning. Purpose: hook the reader inside a job that demands invisibility, and plant *eso*.
1) Loretta's text at 7:40: "Big day. Be a pipe." Marisol takes the chair at the witness box's left shoulder: pad, water, glasses on their beaded chain, the wall clock running three minutes fast. 2) She swears her own oath, in two languages, before Rigo Salas is sworn. 3) Dana Whitcomb's direct: container numbers, straddle carriers, Berth 14. Marisol works consecutively, first person, notation shown as craft: a circle with a line for "world", a slash for "not". The jurors watch Rigo, never her. 4) The 11:48 call. Rigo: "Me dijo: encárgate de eso." Dana: "Of what?" "De eso. La queja de Cole." Dana lays the FBI report beside him: "You told the agents 'him.'" Rigo: "No, licenciada. Eso." 5) Dana goes still; Banks half rises; Judge Okoro counts to three and calls the lunch recess. 6) In the emptying room only Calvin Pryor looks at the interpreter.
Closing: Marisol realises that one English word may decide a man's life, and her mouth still holds the shape of it. Plant: the pad; Pryor's look; the 302.

## Chapter 02: The Plasterboard
Marisol; the interpreters' alcove, 12:34-12:58 that day. Purpose: the inciting overhearing, scene by scene.
1) A former mop closet with a kettle and one chair. She eats Nilda's pan con queso and does not go to the cafeteria because the lawyers swarm it. 2) Through the partition: Deputy Marshal Desai's radio, a quick "Papa?", her boots leaving at 12:41 with nobody posted at 5C. 3) A door opens in Witness Room 5C. Lazzaro, in Cibao Spanish, warm and fast: "Compai, esta mañana te hundiste." 4) The coaching, line by line: say "de él," steady voice; Rigo's objection ("Pero doña la intérprete lo oyó todo"); "an interpreter is furniture, my brother." The Fondo pays Yocasta's rent on Bergenline, 1,350 a month, through March; "nadie te va a creer contra un papel del FBI." Rigo weeps once, quietly. 5) She writes it all in notation without deciding to, the way her hand takes down a stranger's will. 6) Lazzaro leaves with a cheerful word to nobody; Desai returns early and tries the handle of 5C.
Closing: Her pencil snaps on the last symbol and she keeps writing with the stub. Plant: "mi hermano," the Fondo, the rent, Desai's six minutes. The partition is two sheets of board.

## Chapter 03: Faithful Rendering
Marisol; Courtroom 5B, Monday afternoon, then the lobby. Purpose: she must render the lie, and chooses to start checking.
1) Rigo returns, tie loosened, eyes down. Dana tries again, gently, with the report in her hand. 2) "Me dijo: encárgate de él." Marisol's mouth must say "take care of him." She runs the Code in her head: she may ask the Court for help only on language. 3) She rises: "The interpreter requests a moment." Okoro: "Is it a linguistic matter?" She sees Lazzaro studying his signet ring. "No, Your Honor." 4) Juror 6, a retired postman, writes the word down and underlines it. Banks objects badly; cross is reserved to Tuesday. 5) Pryor's daughter Imani, in the gallery, mouths "Daddy" and turns away. 6) In the lobby Foyle holds the door, raincoat damp, thermos in hand: "After you, Ms. Quintanilla." She has not told him her name.
Closing: On the courthouse steps she writes one line in notation: *302 = him? Who checked?* She decides to check one fact before she decides anything else. Plant: Foyle; the 302.

## Chapter 04: Union City, After Dark
Rigo; the Salas flat on Bergenline Avenue, Monday night. Purpose: show the lie from inside and the pressure on a cooperating man.
1) Yocasta's rice and beans, Yaritza's chemistry homework spread on the table, the rent receipt stamped Santa Rosa on the fridge. 2) A memory from June: Lazzaro in the church lot with Rigo's cousin Fello and a manila envelope, nothing said that a judge could hold against him. 3) The night on Berth 14 told only in pieces, the radio crackle, the weight of the spreader through his hands; "Swing it" arrives and he stops the memory at the door. 4) In the bathroom mirror he practises "de él" and hates his own mouth. 5) Yocasta asks whether the government will really keep its promised letter; he cannot say. 6) He recalls the interpreter's voice that morning: the way the final s fell off his sentences as it does at home. 7) Yaritza, on her phone, asks why the old judge looks at him like that.
Closing: "La intérprete es de Santiago." He lies awake wondering whether a wall can hear and what it would do. Plant: the radio, the Fondo, Rigo's fear of the interpreter.

## Chapter 05: Pan con Queso
Marisol; Ferry Street and the courthouse cafeteria, Tuesday 7 October. Purpose: show what telling would cost.
1) Dawn at Panadería La Espiga: Nilda Cabral hands over bread without being asked, reads her face, says "Eat." The Ironbound wakes in Portuguese and Dominican radio. 2) On a bench by Ferry Street she rereads the Code: confidentiality, no exception for crime, an exception only for the Court. 3) Cafeteria, 8:10: Loretta Pinckney, tea, cardigan. Marisol asks a hypothetical about an interpreter who hears a thing in a corridor. 4) Loretta's story: Delia Renko, 2009, told a judge what a lawyer had said, was dropped from the list within a month, and the lawyer was never disciplined. 5) Loretta guesses the question is not hypothetical and, deliberately, refuses to learn more. 6) A comic touch: the cafeteria's single egg salad sandwich, endlessly rotated.
Closing: "Whatever it is, kid, don't carry it into my building." Marisol feels the pad in her bag. It is already inside. Plant: Renko; Loretta's old wound.

## Chapter 06: The Cinnamon Toothpick
Dana; the government's trial room on six, Tuesday 7 October, 6:50 a.m. Purpose: make Dana human and plant her blind spot.
1) Dana rereads yesterday's transcript beneath her father's calendar of ships. Her father was a checker at the Elizabeth terminal; she grew up on the sound of cranes. 2) Special Agent Gaffney arrives with coffee and easy confidence: "He got nervous. They do." 3) They play Exhibit 114A on her laptop. She hears the fragment: "Sí, sí, lo que uté diga." Gaffney: "Yes, yes, whatever you say. He's agreeing." She wants it to be agreement. 4) Odessa Cole, the widow, takes the second row every day with a thermos of her own; Dana remembers the promise she made her in the hallway in January. 5) Her career: the open Deputy Chief seat, the judge who likes clean files. 6) She writes *Certified retranslation?* on her pad and crosses it out.
Closing: Her toothpick snaps. Payoff later: 114A (11, 20, 27); Dana's August doubt.

## Chapter 07: Cross
Marisol; Courtroom 5B, Tuesday afternoon. Purpose: a trial set piece and the first sight of Lazzaro's craft.
1) Banks's cross is shaky: "Which is it, *eso* or *él*?" He stammers; Marisol renders the stammer faithfully, as the Code demands, and hates it. 2) Rigo, steadied: "Él. Me confundí antes." 3) Comic relief: the court reporter, hands in the air, begs for the spelling of *ñapa* and for the Spanish word for "forklift," and Okoro, deadpan, rules that the Court will accept "montacargas." 4) Lazzaro's cross, twenty minutes, honey-soft, every question tilted so the answer is yes. The rhythm is exactly the coaching rhythm she heard through the wall; twice he says "my friend" where he said "mi hermano." Her teeth set. 5) Okoro notices nothing; a juror yawns; Dana taps her pencil. 6) Pryor writes a note to Banks and underlines it twice.
Closing: Rigo, finished, leans and murmurs to her, "Perdone, doña." She renders everything: "The witness said, 'Forgive me, ma'am.'" Lazzaro looks at the interpreter for the first time. Plant: "doña."

## Chapter 08: Tea in the Afternoon
Marisol; Dino's Luncheonette on Market Street, Wednesday 8 October. Purpose: introduce the pursuer and his kindness.
1) She goes for eggs to be alone. Foyle takes the next stool with his thermos: "I'm told the coffee improves with company." 2) Newark small talk: his Kearny boyhood, the old port, the Meadowlands before the landfill. He is plainly good company. 3) He knows her brother Teo from the union hall: "A good man on a night shift." The mention is so mild she nearly misses it. 4) He tells her about an Essex County interpreter who once heard something and whom it never helped. 5) He mentions that he records everything, since 1999, a bad habit his wife complains of; a small recorder rests by the thermos like a pager. 6) He pays for her eggs, and asks nothing. That is the menace.
Closing: At the door: "Cheese is good, though. Try the guava next time." She had told no one what she ate in the alcove. Plant: Foyle's recorder; Teo.

## Chapter 09: Notation
Marisol; the Ferry Street flat, Wednesday night. Purpose: reconstruct the proof and make her first move.
1) She transcribes the coaching verbatim from the symbols, Spanish and English in two columns: craft as detective work, a circle for "world", an arrow for "because". 2) Every tic returns: "mi hermano" four times, "compai," the numbers 1,350 and "hasta marzo." 3) She realises she could do this with any twenty minutes of her life, and that this is exactly why she was hired and why she has never once been asked what she thinks. 4) The page goes into a bag of frozen plantains, behind her mother's last pastelitos. She nearly laughs; she has hidden a federal case in a freezer. 5) She phones Teo for the first time in eleven months. "Mari?" A long silence, a television in the background. "I need to ask you about the port." "Not on this phone."
Closing: He hangs up. She holds her mother's beaded chain and says "eso" aloud, and does not know which meaning she wants. Plant: the page in the freezer; Teo's fear.

## Chapter 10: A Quiet Hotel in Hackensack
Rigo; the marshals' safe house, Thursday 9 October. Purpose: red herring (Desai) and Rigo's wavering.
1) Rigo has finished testifying. Desai guards him over rummy on a sagging bedspread and does not say that her father is in a rehabilitation ward. 2) She teaches him a Gujarati greeting; he teaches her *vaina* and she writes it on her wrist. 3) An envelope under the door contains a Mass card and Yaritza's school photograph, unsigned. 4) He suspects Desai, tests her with a lie about the card, and finds her honestly baffled; she logs it with the time. 5) At night he remembers Berth 14 properly for the first time since the plea: the radio, the spreader's weight, the sound after. 6) He tries in broken English to say the word to her: "It. Not him." She nods politely and does not catch it.
Closing: He asks for a priest. She writes it on her hand. Red herring: Desai as source of the envelope and of the leak.

## Chapter 11: Ibarra's Desk
Marisol; the interpreters' office, Friday 10 October. Purpose: she hears the true audio.
1) Okoro grants Banks's motion for a neutral check of Exhibit 114A's transcript. Loretta assigns Wendell Ibarra, 66, easygoing, Chihuahua Spanish, a man who eats burritos at his desk at noon. 2) At lunch Marisol finds the file on his desk and puts in earbuds, her heart loud as a typewriter. 3) She hears Gaffney's slow "¿De él? ¿De Cole?" and Rigo's "Sí, sí, lo que uté diga," and the cracked tiredness beneath it. 4) She knows why Ibarra will miss it: the dropped s, the slurred "uté", a Cibao habit that is invisible to anyone not raised on it. 5) Ibarra returns with a pastry; she lies about a stapler. He says kindly he will have the report by Monday.
Closing: She has broken a rule for the first time, and sees that the man whose job is to fix the word is the man least able to hear it. Payoff: 114A.

## Chapter 12: What the Sheet Said
Dana; the trial room, Monday 13 October evening. Purpose: Dana chooses silence.
1) Ibarra's report lands: "substantially accurate; one phrase inaudible." Dana reads "inaudible" three times. 2) Desai arrives, stiff, with the door sheet: Lazzaro in 5C, 12:43 to 12:56 on 6 October, her own six-minute gap at 12:41 written in pencil and then over in pen. 3) Dana asks what Rigo says; Desai: nothing, only a request for a priest. 4) Dana runs the arithmetic of a mistrial: five weeks, thirty witnesses, Odessa Cole in the second row. 5) She tells herself it is a lawyer's nuisance and almost believes it. 6) Desai asks whether she should tell the judge, and admits what she was doing at 12:41: a call about her father's stroke. Dana says that is human and irrelevant. Both know it is neither.
Closing: "Log it. File it. Say nothing." Desai writes on the back of her hand, then wipes it with her thumb. Plant: the sheet; Dana's guilt.

## Chapter 13: The Letter
Marisol; Courtroom 5B and Penn Station, Tuesday 14 October. Purpose: she tries the system.
1) A short comic witness: a Spanish-speaking customs broker's clerk who speaks almost entirely in shipping jargon, so Marisol must decide how "FCL per BOL, ex-vessel, demurrage" sounds in English. The jury laughs with her, not at her. 2) Over lunch she writes the letter in plain English, no names, only a date, a room and the sentence "The testimony of a witness changed after an improper conversation." 3) She mails it from the post office at Penn Station beneath the departures board and feels sick and light. 4) She rides the 6:12 to New York for no reason, to feel a train beneath her. A man in a raincoat folds his newspaper two cars down.
Closing: She goes home by another route and still sees steam from a thermos in the window of the number 13 bus. Plant: the letter; Foyle.

## Chapter 14: The Green Book
Marisol; Courtroom 5B, Thursday 16 October. Purpose: midpoint reversal. Pryor is not innocent, and the system will not fix itself.
1) Okoro has received the letter and calls a sidebar. Marisol, seated near the bench, hears everything. 2) Lazzaro smoothly denies: he visited the witness room to ask about a sandwich. Okoro asks Dana whether she knows of any contact with the witness. "No, Your Honor." Marisol hears her lie, softly, with a toothpick behind her teeth. 3) Banks, hunting Hanlon to spare his own client, introduces Pryor's pocket ledger, the green book: forty thousand a year in Pryor's own hand. 4) Pryor looks at the interpreter and nods, as if to say *I know what I am.* The innocent man she has been protecting took the money. 5) At the break Desai whispers: "I told her on Monday. She said log it."
Closing: Marisol sees that nobody in the room will correct the record, and that the person who can is wearing the interpreter's badge. Plant: the green book; Pryor guilty of bribery, not of the killing.

## Chapter 15: What Teo Said
Marisol; Teo's house in Elizabeth, Saturday 18 October. Purpose: the sibling collision.
1) Rain, a leaking gutter, Teo's old dog; the kitchen they grew up in, now his, her mother's saints still on the shelf. 2) She asks about Berth 14. He deflects with jokes and a pot of coffee too strong to drink. 3) The quarrel: the funeral, the sale of their mother's house, the month Marisol spent interpreting at a conference in Miami while Mami died. "You want to talk about oaths? Mami died and nobody asked me what I swore." 4) He lets one thing slip: Crane 9 was down that night. "I logged it." 5) She sees his hand shake on the cup, and says nothing, because she has become a professional at saying nothing. 6) Before she goes he gives her a bag of her mother's mangú seasoning from the cupboard, as if it were a truce, and as if it were not.
Closing: He shuts the door on her; through the glass she watches him dial a number. Red herring: Teo as informant.

## Chapter 16: Complaint
Marisol; the interpreters' office, Wednesday 22 October. Purpose: the pursuit tightens and the first lie is told.
1) Loretta shows her an anonymous complaint to the Administrative Office: bias because of her Dominican origin, and "improper contact with a witness." 2) It cites her lunch hours and mentions her brother's work at the port. Marisol thinks of Teo's phone call and the number he dialled through the glass. 3) Loretta reads the Code aloud, kindly, and describes what an inquiry means: a hold on assignments, a file that follows you for years. 4) A comic breath: the cafeteria's one egg salad sandwich sits on the desk, untouched, like an exhibit. 5) Loretta admits she must forward the complaint within the week, and that she hopes she will not have to forward anything else. 6) She asks, "Is there anything you want to tell me?" The question stays open like a door. Marisol thinks of Delia Renko and of Loretta's careful hands.
Closing: "No." The first lie she has told in that building. Plant: the complaint; Loretta's duty; Teo as suspect.

## Chapter 17: The Wrong Translator
Rigo; Hackensack and a conference room at the U.S. Attorney's Office, Friday 24 October. Purpose: Rigo tries to tell and is not heard.
1) The government rests. Yocasta visits with pastelitos and tells him the Fondo has stopped paying, and she is not afraid; she has found work at a laundry in Union City and has started speaking to the landlord herself. 2) Rigo asks for Dana. She comes with Gaffney, whose classroom Spanish is all the room has, pushing her toothpick to one side. 3) He says, "It was *eso*. Always *eso*." Gaffney renders: "He says he is very sorry for the confusion." Rigo watches the English leave and cannot follow it. 4) Dana sees his face and almost understands; her phone buzzes; she leaves with her hand already on the door. 5) Desai traces the envelope Rigo thought came from her: Yocasta's priest, Father Ignacio, left it at the marshals' front desk with a prayer card. Her relief is almost funny. 6) Rigo laughs, then cannot stop.
Closing: He understands that no one in the room wants the word. Payoff: Desai's innocence.

## Chapter 18: The Elevator
Marisol; courthouse, Tuesday 28 October, then Ferry Street. Purpose: menace without a threat; the theft.
1) The defence case begins. Hanlon brings a box of cannoli to the interpreters' office and the clerks adore him; he remembers each of their children's names. 2) The elevator stalls between four and five for six minutes with Hanlon and Desai, the car's fan wheezing. 3) Hanlon, avuncular, asks about her mother, whether she has family at the port, and says kindness repays itself, usually in kind. He talks about hurling and bad knees. 4) He never says a word a judge could object to. Desai's hand stays on her radio, and Marisol sees it. 5) Home at nine: the door unmarked, the radio off, her mother's saints facing the wrong way. The freezer bag of plantains lies open.
Closing: The page is gone, and so is her printed transcript. She sits among her mother's furniture with only her memory. Payoff: the freezer; the stakes become personal.

## Chapter 19: Fog on the Meadowlands
Marisol; Port Newark, Thursday 30 October, 5:30 a.m. Purpose: set piece and Teo's partial truth.
1) She drives out in fog on Port Street, and Teo lets her in with a visitor's pass and a hard hat that does not fit. 2) Berth 14 under red crane lights, containers four high, the straddle carriers like tall insects stalking the gloom. He shows her where Cole died, a painted rectangle gone grey. 3) Teo, in a flat voice: "Wade said, 'Swing it, Rigo, now.' I heard it on the handheld in the shed. Pryor I never heard that night. Pryor I never heard once." 4) He admits the $5,000, but not yet the whole of it. He swears he told Foyle nothing. She believes him, and sees how little she has believed him in two years. 5) A white pickup with a Port Authority decal idles at the gate, hazards blinking, nobody visible.
Closing: Foyle's thermos steams on Teo's truck hood beside a note: *Good morning.* Red herring cleared: Teo as informant. Payoff: the radio.

## Chapter 20: The Closing
Dana; Courtroom 5B, Tuesday 4 November. Purpose: Dana's closing and her crack.
1) She builds her summing-up around the 11:48 call and the photographs of Cole's boots in the yard. Jurors lean forward. 2) She uses "him." The word comes out of her like a tooth. 3) In the gallery Marisol watches her, unassigned that day and sitting on her own time, and Dana sees the interpreter's lips moving silently, rendering the closing into Spanish for no one. 4) Dana stumbles, recovers, finishes strong; Banks's closing, shorter and kinder, asks the jury only to remember that one word is not a man's whole life. 5) In the corridor by the water fountain she says to Marisol: "If you have something, bring it in writing." 6) Marisol says only: "I'm not allowed to have anything."
Closing: In the washroom Dana spits out the toothpick and meets her father's eyes in the mirror. Payoff: the invitation; 114A and Dana's August doubt return.

## Chapter 21: Guilty on All Counts
Marisol; Courtroom 5B and the alcove, Friday 7 November. Purpose: the low point.
1) The verdict after two days' deliberation. Imani Pryor's cry carries to the ceiling. Odessa Cole does not move. 2) Pryor looks once at the interpreter's chair beside the witness box, empty because no Spanish-speaking witness remains. 3) Loretta, white-faced, tells Marisol the Administrative Office has placed her on provisional suspension on the strength of the anonymous complaint; Loretta forwarded it as she was obliged to, and says so, and does not apologise, and does not look away. 4) Teo phones: Foyle found the Crane 9 log with Teo's initials and told Lazzaro, and two agents are at Teo's door. 5) Marisol goes to the empty alcove, sits on the single chair and presses her ear to the plasterboard, wanting to hear the wall say anything. She hears her own breath and the hum, and understands that the profession's whole promise is this silence: a wall that tells nobody.
Closing: Nothing. Only the vending machine's hum. Payoff: Loretta's report; Teo's exposure; Dana's invitation.

## Chapter 22: In Writing
Marisol; kitchen table, then the U.S. Attorney's Office, Monday 10 November. Purpose: the legitimate route, and the proof that survives.
1) She writes the affidavit at her kitchen table; translating herself is harder than translating anyone. She refuses every adjective. 2) On a public library computer she finds the Fondo in a public tax return filed by Hanlon's wife's foundation: grants to Santa Rosa Management, the name stamped on the Salas rent receipt (4), the exact rent, through March. 3) Dana reads in her trial room beneath the ships calendar, stony, and then reads again. 4) "You'll be a witness. You understand what that does to a career." Marisol does. 5) Dana admits the door sheet and her own August doubt. They are two women who both knew, and who each decided something different. 6) Dana tells her what conceding will cost the Government, and does not flinch. She will disclose to Banks and to Rigo's new counsel today; Banks will move under Rule 33.
Closing: "Your notes?" "Gone." "Then you'll have to be the notes."

## Chapter 23: A Man With a Lawyer
Rigo; the detention centre interview room, Friday 14 November. Purpose: Rigo's decision.
1) His new lawyer, Agnes Kowalczyk, young and patient with a pen she clicks; the interpreter Julio Peña-Marsh, nervous, fresh from certification. 2) Dana, through Agnes, discloses the affidavit. 3) Rigo hears that a woman heard him through a wall. His first feeling is shame; his second is relief so deep he has to look at his hands, at the missing fingertip. 4) He asks the name. "Quintanilla." He asks Julio to say each word of his own plea back in English so he can learn it himself. 5) Agnes warns him what a recantation will cost: perjury exposure, the promised letter, his residency. He says Yaritza will have a father who said it plain. 6) Julio's hands shake on the pad, and Rigo, who has been interpreted all his life, reaches out and steadies them.
Closing: "It," he practises. "It. It. It."

## Chapter 24: The Second Oath
Marisol; Courtroom 5B, Tuesday 2 December. Purpose: the climax begins.
1) The hearing on the new-trial motion. Okoro admits her as a witness over Lazzaro's objection and explains the rule: no one may be witness and conduit at once, so she will never sit in that chair again for this case. He remarks drily that his father lost a case in 1979 for want of an interpreter, and that the Court takes the chair seriously. 2) Loretta testifies about the Code, voice steady, and says the Code did not anticipate what the Code is now being asked. 3) Teo sits in the gallery in his funeral suit; Nilda sits beside him with a paper bag of bread. 4) Marisol is sworn; she has sworn a hundred oaths for others and never one for herself. The clerk reads it slowly. 5) Dana questions her, gently. 6) Foyle sits at the back with his thermos on his knee and says nothing. Lazzaro, at the defence table, does not look at him, and Marisol notes it.
Closing: "Tell the Court what you heard." "I have never in my own words."

## Chapter 25: Verbatim
Marisol; the witness stand, Tuesday 2 December. Purpose: her testimony and the cross.
1) She gives the coaching in Spanish and English, line by line, in the original rhythm, and the courtroom hears what the wall heard. 2) Lazzaro attacks: bias, her Dominican heritage, her brother's trouble, memory, no recording. 3) She answers with trained recall, quoting his "mi hermano" four times and the rent figure to the dollar. 4) A read-back of the morning transcript confirms two details only she had: the order of Rigo's words and a cough. 5) Lazzaro objects once too often and Okoro, who has been writing, looks up. 6) The court reporter, who has been transcribing without pause, stops typing, as if to hear. Okoro asks for the read-back himself. Rigo's coached sentences, in Marisol's mouth, are the same ones the jury already heard, and the room feels the echo.
Closing: Okoro counts to three. Lazzaro does not sit. Payoff: Marisol's recall; the cadence; the Fondo.

## Chapter 26: Eso
Rigo; the witness stand, Wednesday 3 December. Purpose: the climax.
1) Rigo takes the stand in his borrowed blue tie; Julio interprets, shaking. 2) Rigo says *eso*, and Julio, flustered, renders "that" and asks leave to note a linguistic matter: *eso* is a neuter demonstrative and cannot mean "him". 3) Marisol, in the gallery, does not move; everything she is trained to do has been taken from her by the oath she swore the day before. 4) Rigo says in English, slowly, as one carries a full cup: "It. *Eso* is it." 5) Pryor lowers his head; Imani leans in. 6) Yocasta in the back row crosses herself, and Yaritza, beside her, in her school uniform, does not. Lazzaro, for the first time, asks for a recess and is refused. Okoro grants it, and the room waits while a young man learns the cost of one word. Lazzaro's cross finds no question that does not return to it.
Closing: Okoro: "The witness has spoken for himself." Payoff: *eso*.

## Chapter 27: What the Thermos Held
Marisol; Courtroom 5B and chambers, Thursday 4 and Monday 8 December. Purpose: resolution, with a cost.
1) Teo testifies about the radio and the $5,000, awkward and honest, and he does not look at his sister until the end. 2) Foyle asks to be heard and gives the Court his tapes: Lazzaro on 7 October, "Find out who was in the alcove"; on 20 October, dictating the anonymous complaint, "mention the brother"; on 27 October, asking where she keeps her papers. Foyle admits he gave her address and never learned who entered the flat. Lazzaro stands very still. 3) Okoro's ruling from the bench: new trial on the murder count; referrals for Lazzaro and Hanlon. Pryor's bribery convictions stand. 4) The Administrative Office's letter arrives by courier: six months off the list. 5) Loretta announces a working group on reporting, with Marisol invited and unpaid. Nobody says the Code was wrong; they say it was silent. 6) Dana tenders her resignation to a man who does not pretend to be sorry, and takes her father's ships calendar down from the wall. 7) Pryor, led out, tells the interpreter's empty chair, "Be that as it may," and smiles.
Closing: Marisol leaves into the fog, unmeasured.

## Chapter 28: Not Translated
Marisol; Panadería La Espiga, Saturday 14 March 2026. Purpose: land the emotional note.
1) Four years to the day. She and Teo go to Odessa Cole's memorial at the yard gate and say nothing for a long time; Odessa nods to them both. 2) Teo pleaded; he lost the pension and kept the dog. 3) Back in the Ironbound they order pan con queso from Nilda, who refuses payment. 4) He asks her to say something in their mother's Spanish and no other voice. 5) Loretta's draft of the new guidance sits on the table between the cups, her handwriting in the margin. 6) Marisol is teaching a course on consecutive interpreting at the community college, three evenings a week, and tells students on the first night that the hardest sentence is the one you must not improve.
Closing: She says "ñapa," their mother's word for the little extra given for nothing, and translates it for no one. Payoff: the untranslatable word planted in chapter 7.
