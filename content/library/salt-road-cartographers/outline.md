# Outline: The Salt Road Cartographers

**Solution and clues.** Kaie Tammaru, Keeper, redraws leaves in the vault and the land obeys on the ninth night. Clues: the Correction Book dates match Shifts (ch1-2); a pin beats a far drawing (ch1, ch9, ch13, ch21); pinholes sit 4 mm from the gutter instead of 5, a redraw re-pierces it (ch2, ch16); Kaie's dividers have a bent leg (ch4); the re-inking ink is true Founding grade (ch5); the assay shelf has lost vials, Palu's row bare (ch2, ch16); her gloves hide stained hands (ch4, ch17). Red herrings: Toomas (dismissed, seen at the vault, ch3); Mihkel (holds a key, ch2); Salme Ilves (sells the ink, ch5). Costs: forgetting, in proportion to the ground drawn.

## Chapter 01: The Lake That Walked

Reet, Dry Cairn station on the Seam's northern edge, first light, Day 1. Purpose: Hook with a prediction that comes true; plant the cost of pinning.

1. Reet rides out with a chain and a bag of vials. The lake called Kuld Mere, which sat east of the cairn yesterday, is now a half-mile west on a heath.
2. She walks it to the yard, a ritual, and finds it exactly where she expected: she copied the Correction Book entry nine nights ago (leaf re-inked, "Kuld heath, tidy").
3. Fellow rider Pille sneers: guess or luck? Reet says the number aloud and does not stammer. Banter about breakfast, the Guild's stale bread.
4. Pinning: a boundary stone is sliding into the new lake, dragged by the Atlas's old drawing. She draws it in day-ink with stood-water, swears the oath, and the stone stops: the near hand beats the far. (Day-ink holds one night only.) A yard of her memory goes quiet, a patch like a missing tooth. She has never forgotten anything.
5. She fills a vial of stood-water for the assay shelf, as every rider does.
6. She rides home with the lake behind her and the hole in her head.

Closing beat: she realises that if the Book predicted the lake, then someone drew the lake there first. She says it aloud to the horse.

## Chapter 02: The Correction Book

Reet, Karst Hollow, Guild House, Day 1. Purpose: Show the Guild and prove the pattern.

1. The Hollow at morning: stairs, lamp-boys, ink-smell. Reet climbs to the Day-book room, where she keeps copies of every Shift since she was fifteen.
2. Mihkel Org assigns her tedious chores, quoting Rule Eleven. Comedy: his mustard waistcoat, the pastry he hides.
3. She lays fourteen Shifts over fourteen "re-inking" notes. All nine nights apart. All in the last eight years.
4. Mihkel dismisses it: re-inking is maintenance. But he lets her carry Leaf 88 (Sora Crossing, her home) from the vault to the copy room, under watch. Plant: pinhole sits 4 mm from the gutter, but when she copied this leaf two years ago it was 5; a redraw re-pierces the hole. Sora's marsh drain moved last spring and her mother wrote about it. She flakes a sliver off the fresh corner and says nothing.
5. At the assay shelf she notices gaps: whole rows of vials for places named in the Book are missing, Sora's and Palu's among them.
6. Today's entry: "Leaf 163, Palu valley, re-inked." Her stomach turns: nine nights from tonight is the tenth.

Closing beat: she counts on her fingers, bare left hand against the ink-stained right, and stops on the tenth.

## Chapter 03: A Binder in Tinta Row

Toomas, Tinta Row, evening, Day 2. The only man who might know takes the case. Purpose: Inciting incident: the one man who might know takes the case.

1. Toomas binds a ledger for a fish merchant, complaining about thread. His knee. His tea. His loneliness, shown by how many cups he sets out.
2. Reet appears with a satchel and a reputation. He knows who she is: the girl with the memory the Guild brags about.
3. She states fourteen dates and the Palu entry. He says go away. She recites the Soo valley, every contour, from a pre-flood Dawn Sheet the Guild forgot to burn, which she copied at sixteen. He stops.
4. Toomas's inner view: he does not say what he knows; the reader sees his hand shake on the awl.
5. He tests her with his own map from memory, and she corrects it. He is delighted and appalled.
6. He studies her pinhole sketch. His eye goes to the 4 mm. He thinks of a bent-legged tool and a girl he trained.

7. He says a far hand can only be beaten by a near one: someone must stand in Palu. She has no one else to tell: Mihkel gave her Rule Eleven.

Closing beat: "Nine nights. Sit down. How many are left?" She says eight, and he finally puts the kettle on.

## Chapter 04: Naturally

Reet, Guild House, vault antechamber, morning, Day 3. Purpose: Introduce the antagonist; Reet lies.

1. Reet is summoned to Kaie Tammaru's rooms. Kaie is small, silver-braided, polite, in white kidskin gloves. She makes tea.
2. Kaie praises Reet's work. She ends sentences with "naturally." She measures Reet's draught of the Day-book with brass dividers that stand at a slant, like a dancer's foot.
3. Kaie asks whether Reet has spoken to Toomas Veskimae. Reet says no and stammers, three syllables. Kaie nods and takes it in.
4. Kaie benches her: leave of absence, daily reporting, Mihkel to watch her door.
5. Reet takes the satchel, the chain, and Leaf 88's tracing hidden under her shirt, and goes down by the lamp-boys' stair.
6. Toomas is waiting at the Wharf Gate, already in boots, with a bag he claims is only bread.

Closing beat: Reet says "I stammered," and Toomas says, "I know. I heard it from the next street."

## Chapter 05: Ilves Black

Toomas, Ilves Ink-works, Tinta Row, night, Day 3. Purpose: The ink clue and a new ally.

1. Toomas takes Reet to Salme Ilves, ink-mistress, whose vats breathe sulphur. Salme is blunt, smokes a thin cigar, and loves her son loudly.
2. Taavi tests the sliver Reet took from Leaf 88's re-inked corner. He hedges "technically" four times, then goes quiet: the ink is Founding grade, unmade for three hundred years, and carries Sora marsh water.
3. Salme says she knows nothing. Taavi sees her lie. Toomas sees Taavi see it.
4. Guild watchers come knocking. The escape runs through the dye room: a green vat, a ladder, a flung bucket of lamp-black.
5. Taavi jumps in after them with his vial bandolier clinking and an apology for stealing from his mother.
6. At the Wharf Gate, Salme presses a corked flask into Taavi's hand: "Don't drink it. Don't lose it. Don't tell me where." Plant: this is the pure Founding base.

Closing beat: the flask is warm, as if it had been held a long while.

## Chapter 06: Oxen Named for Officials

Reet, the Salt Company yard and the first night in the Seam, Day 3. Purpose: Introduce Aino and the Seam.

1. The yard: forty wagons, thirty-six oxen. Aino Rebane lists them like a roll call: Registrar, Guildmaster, Rule Forty, Mihkel.
2. Aino sells them passage as pilgrims to a bridge shrine. She does not believe it. She does not care. She tests Reet's ground knowledge on the road and is annoyed to be impressed.
3. The caravan crosses the Seam's edge at sunset. Reet describes how the sky goes tight, like a sail.
4. Camp: stake-and-ask with a neighbouring caravan, comic confusion about who is where. Aino tells the story of the wagon that woke in a barn, then slips off to feed her "hen" basket (plant: six pigeons, a first report to Mihkel).
5. Taavi is ground-sick; Toomas, kind, hands him mint and says nothing about his own worry.
6. At midnight the Shift: a low roar, a sensation like a ship turning.

Closing beat: Aino drives a stake: "It'll still move. The stake's just so we know where we woke up."

## Chapter 07: The Road That Ended

Toomas, the Seam, Day 4, morning. Purpose: Set piece and Toomas's old memory.

1. Dawn: the road ends at a cliff with a thousand-foot drop that was not there yesterday. The caravan masters argue, then the oxen decide.
2. Reet and Toomas ride a pony ahead, reading ground: he by grammar, she by memory. They find a ledge and then a rope bridge, rebuilt overnight by some other caravan.
3. The rope bridge: Aino is afraid. She admits it by going very quiet, then very loud. The bridge sways. Taavi is sick over the side. The ox called Registrar refuses to cross.
4. Toomas anchors the bridge's far end with his chain-staff and a long joke about moustaches.
5. His inner view: the land in daylight, fifty feet from where he last knew it. He remembers drawing a river once, and puts the memory down hard.
6. Over the cliff-lip, a plume of dust: riders, a dozen.

Closing beat: Toomas counts the riders and quietly names the one at the front by the shape of his waistcoat.

## Chapter 08: Rule Forty

Reet, a pass in the Seam, Day 4, afternoon. Purpose: A chase with comedy; her second loss.

1. Mihkel Org arrives with eleven riders, quoting the Guild's Rule Forty by number as justification for stopping caravan and pilgrims.
2. He meets the ox named Mihkel. Aino keeps a straight face for four seconds.
3. Reet and Mihkel argue; he truly believes in the Rules. He says the Keeper has gone ahead to Palu with fast horses.
4. To delay him, Reet pins the narrow pass behind them. She draws it, swears, and the pass holds for the dark. The riders turn back to search for another way.
5. The cost: she finds she can no longer remember the road to the Hollow's gate. A whole mountain's worth of ground is a hush.
6. Aino sees her hands shake and says nothing, and hands her a boiled egg.

Closing beat: Reet tries to recall how she got here, and finds only Toomas's face, which she holds like a rope.

## Chapter 09: Haldja Ford

Toomas, Haldja Ford, evening, Day 5. Purpose: Lore, an old friend, the oath-songs.

1. The river has moved a hundred yards east since last month. Veikko Pent's rope-ferry now crosses a dry field and a puddle.
2. Veikko, with one ear and a goat called Rule, is shocked that Toomas is alive. They insult each other warmly.
3. Over fish soup, Veikko sings his answer to Reet's questions: a ferrymen's song with the lines "The far hand writes, the near hand holds. Stand and say, and the water stays."
4. Taavi asks what the song means. Veikko says it means what it says. Water remembers whoever stood in it.
5. Aino tests the ferry; it holds. She apologises to the goat.
6. Toomas watches Reet copy the song's words. He realises she will find Soo soon, and does not tell her it is nearby.

Closing beat: Veikko's last verse names the eleven dead of Soo. Toomas leaves the room before the verse ends.

## Chapter 10: Eleven Stones

Reet, Soo-on-the-Slope, Day 5 night to Day 6 morning. Purpose: The hidden wound.

1. A hamlet of stone cottages built uphill from where Soo drowned. Rumour sells itself at the market stalls: a woman with eggs, a man with a lame dog.
2. Reet buys eggs, and the egg-seller spits at Toomas's name. Reet notices.
3. The ruined original village is a quarter-mile off: a shallow pond with the chimneys visible beneath. Reet walks the shore and counts them.
4. Eleven memorial stones on a rise, each with a name and a mark.
5. On the lintel of the old Soo mill there is a scratched mark: a surveyor's tick, Toomas's own.
6. Taavi finds Toomas sitting alone among the stones, tea cold, and sits beside him.

Closing beat: Reet reads the eleventh name aloud, and a sweep of her ground memory about the pond goes quietly dark, a toll for the looking.

## Chapter 11: What Ene Ots Asked

Toomas, Soo-on-the-Slope, Day 6, morning. Purpose: Confession, begun.

1. Ene Ots, eighty, on two sticks, receives them in a kitchen that smells of lye and onions. She does not offer tea. Toomas notes that she has kept the same blue crock for fifty years.
2. She asks him a single question: to name the eleven.
3. He does, in order, with their ages and trades: a baker, a cooper's widow, twin ferrymen, a boy of six. He has said them every night for twelve years.
4. Ene tells him of the first winter: the burials in frozen ground, the quarrel over who would dig. Her granddaughter was the baker's wife.
5. Reet watches from the doorway as Toomas does not defend himself, does not weep, and does not sit until Ene lets him.
6. Aino, outside, is told by a neighbour that the village will not hand over a cart or a pony for his sake; she buys them with salt.

Closing beat: Ene says, "I won't forgive you. But I'll hear the rest. Go and say it to the girl." Toomas walks out into the cold yard and begins: "Twelve years ago I redrew a river."

## Chapter 12: The Night Outside the Rope

Reet, Soo-on-the-Slope to the open Seam, Day 6, dusk to night. Purpose: Midpoint reversal.

1. Toomas tells all: he moved the river off Palu, his sister's town, with one stroke of Founding Ink. It worked. He did not think about where the water would go.
2. Reet realises that edits are real, that Toomas lied by omission, and that Kaie, his assistant, found his notebook.
3. Reet leaves the camp at dusk, furious, and walks past the stake line on purpose to test Toomas's claim that an unpinned edit lands, with her chain and a vial.
4. The Shift catches her in the open: the ground tilts, a hill rolls like a sheet, a lake of salt appears at her feet. She runs.
5. Aino grabs her by the scarf and the hair. They hold a stake together until dawn.
6. Reversal: Reet understands that edits save as well as drown. She needs Toomas not as a suspect but as the only one who knows how.

Closing beat: at first light Reet says, without a stammer, "Teach me how it's done," and Toomas says, "Not yet."

## Chapter 13: The Blank

Toomas, the Blank at the Seam's core, Day 7. Purpose: Why the land obeys.

1. Six miles of white plain: no Shift, no wind. The caravan feels the silence in its bones.
2. Veikko's songs lead them to the Hearth-Stone, a flat rock in the centre with a groove and an inscription.
3. Toomas reads it aloud: the Oath of Standing, in Hilja Orm's own square hand. "I stood here. I drew only what I saw."
4. He explains the ground rules he learned by accident: authority is testimony. The Atlas was never magic; it was a very long witness.
5. Taavi tests soil and water: the ink needs stood-water, from the place itself. The stone's carving shows two hands: the far one writing, the near one holding. With no water on the Blank, they wait for the groove to fill at noon.
6. Aino, who has pushed the oxen too hard, sits under the stone and cries once, hard.

Closing beat: Reet sets her bare hand on the groove and realises anyone could do this, not just the Guild.

## Chapter 14: What the Pigeon Carried

Reet, the Blank, Day 7 night. Purpose: Betrayal and the first real friend.

1. Taavi, looking for dry kindling, finds a wicker cage in Aino's gear: six pigeons (one missing), a roll of tissue slips, and a cipher in Mihkel's looping hand.
2. Reet confronts her. Aino does not deny it: the Guild paid her brother's gambling debt in advance, and her brother is in the Hollow with the people who hold the note.
3. A fight in the white quiet. Aino throws her salt-hook down; Reet does not step back.
4. Aino explains her shame: she has never told anyone she can't afford to be honest. Reet admits that she lied to Kaie, stammered, and was caught, and understands the wage of it.
5. Toomas settles it: "You are paid by the day, Aino, not by the sentence."
6. Reet cannot lie aloud, so Aino writes the false report herself in Mihkel's cipher: caravan lost in the Seam, all drowned, with grand details. Reet reads it back flat, no stammer, because it is only reading, and Taavi laughs until he hiccups at Aino's drowned oxen. Aino frees the bird south.

Closing beat: the pigeon flies into a sky with no landmark, and Aino says she will never name an ox for a person she respects. (Her brother's debt is still unpaid.)

## Chapter 15: Palu Under Its Bridges

Toomas, Palu, Day 8, evening. Purpose: Make the stakes human.

1. Palu hangs on forty rope bridges across a gorge. The Name-Day of the Bridge feast is under way: drums, plum brandy, ribbons tied to every cable.
2. Liisu Pold intercepts them with a map she drew of the town: every well, every goat. She corrects Reet on the mulberry, flatly.
3. Jaan Pold, the reeve, receives Toomas coldly. Toomas learns his sister, Jaan's mother, died last winter, and he arrived too late for the grave.
4. Jaan refuses the warning: the Guild's notice only asked for a "precautionary gathering on the upland" on Day 9, and Guild notices cry wolf every autumn. He will not cancel the feast.
5. Liisu shows a Guild surveyor's board she borrowed from a drunk rider: a copy-clerk's tracing of Leaf 163 with the valley drawn as a lake, Palu not on it, and a pinhole near the gutter.
6. Aino whispers to Reet that the bridges are the worst thing she has seen, and she will cross every one.

Closing beat: Toomas stands on the central bridge in the feast-light and sees, hanging from its cable, a child's map of the town he saved and then nearly cost.

## Chapter 16: Stood Water

Reet, Palu valley and gorge, Day 9, morning. Purpose: The attempt that fails and the plan that emerges.

1. Reet and Taavi test Palu's river water. It reacts as Sora's did in the sliver: far-writing runs on vials, Palu's row on the shelf was bare, so Kaie holds Palu's water. Local water will also take the ink.
2. She tries to pin the whole valley herself. It is too large. She draws one field and her mind blanks; she faints on the grass.
3. Liisu watches and says: "You can't stand in all of it. We can."
4. Reet wakes in a sheepfold and realises the answer: the people stand in what they live in. She is not the one who stood.
5. Taavi shows her what he carries: enough Founding base for a town, if mixed with stood-water. He does the sums aloud, in recipes. He hides half in a hollowed loaf for Liisu to keep and fills his flask with Ilves Black, his first deliberate lie.
6. She sets the pinhole on the stolen board, 4 mm from the gutter, against her memory of Kaie's slanted dividers. The angle of the bent leg gives exactly 4. The Keeper's hand is in the Atlas.

Closing beat: a bell rings at the gorge head. Twelve horses, a silver braid.

## Chapter 17: The Keeper Comes to Dinner

Toomas, the reeve's house, Day 9, afternoon. Purpose: The antagonist's case.

1. Kaie arrives with Mihkel and riders, in gloves. She thanks Jaan for hospitality and asks for supper.
2. Over fish stew, she lays out the numbers: the Seam will reach the Hollow in thirty-one years; Palu is the ninth edit. She will move people first.
3. Jaan refuses. Kaie is patient. "Naturally you may refuse. I will ask again."
4. Toomas and Kaie in the yard. She admits his notebook began it. She did not ask him because he would have said no.
5. Mihkel hears this, for the first time, and sits down on an upturned bucket.
6. Kaie has Reet and Toomas taken for custody. She removes her gloves to sign the order: her hands are stained to the wrist.

Closing beat: Reet sees the stains and says, "You've been drawing for years," and Kaie says, "Naturally."

## Chapter 18: The Grain Store

Reet, Palu grain store, Day 9, night. Purpose: Low point.

1. Reet and Toomas are locked among sacks of barley. Toomas coughs. Aino is chained to a cartwheel in the yard, swearing in a whisper.
2. Kaie visits alone, ungloved. She offers Reet the Keepership: with her memory, Reet would be the best the Atlas has ever had.
3. Reet argues: the Hollow's survival is real. But nobody asked Palu. Kaie says, gently, that Palu would have said no.
4. Kaie takes Taavi's flask from his bandolier, sniffs it, and pours it out onto the floorboards, satisfied. Reet watches the ink soak into the grain and does not look at Taavi, who is studying the floor.
5. Taavi sits against the door with empty hands, and Toomas, for the first time, apologises to someone for not stopping it earlier.
6. Kaie leaves. The town is quiet. Reet, who has never forgotten a thing, wishes she could forget this.

Closing beat: from below the floor comes a whistle of three notes, a child's.

## Chapter 19: Under the Floorboards

Toomas, Palu, Day 9 night to Day 10 before dawn. Purpose: Rescue and an ally's turn.

1. Liisu has dug a gap under the grain store, her private tunnel for smuggling bread to a lame neighbour. She leads them out on hands and knees.
2. Mihkel is waiting at the far end with a lantern. He has read the Rules again: Rule Forty. "No Surveyor sleeps unwatched in a shifting place." He cites the number eleven times to cover his shaking.
3. Liisu gives Taavi the loaf: the pure base is saved, and the flask Kaie poured was Taavi's decoy. Mihkel, who knows the guard rota, opens the yard gate.
4. Jaan frees Aino with a hammer. He has seen his daughter's map and changed his mind in the night.
5. Toomas insists on speaking first to Palu, to say what he did. Reet does not argue.
6. They plan: at dawn the assembly, then the whole town, on East Rise.

Closing beat: Toomas looks at the paling stars and says he has never been so frightened or so glad.

## Chapter 20: The Assembly of Bridges

Reet, Palu square, Day 10, morning. Purpose: Persuasion and consent.

1. Jaan rings the bridge-bell. Nine hundred people gather in plum-stained feast clothes. Reet has never spoken to a crowd.
2. Toomas confesses first, publicly, about Soo and the river. Silence. A woman spits. Another cries.
3. Reet explains the rules and the cost: the land will keep what they promise, and she will lose her memory of the ground. She wants every household to say where their place will be.
4. The assembly argues: boundaries, a disputed goat shed, a shared mulberry, Kaie's evacuation notice. Comedy and pain, both.
5. Liisu stands on a barrel and says what she wants the new Palu to have: a school with a map-room, and a well by the mulberry.
6. Jaan calls the vote. Aino counts hands, because someone must.

Closing beat: the bridge-bell rings once for yes, and then rings and rings, because nobody will let go of the rope.

## Chapter 21: Everyone Stands

Reet, East Rise, Day 10. Purpose: The climax: the drawing.

1. The town climbs the ridge with stools, goats and cradles. Taavi mixes the pure base with Palu's river water in a copper pot and tests it on his thumbnail.
2. Reet stands on the bare ground where Palu will be. She asks each household to show with their feet where their house will stand. They say it aloud, and she writes.
3. Kaie arrives with riders at noon. Mihkel puts himself between them. Kaie sees what the town is doing and, for a moment, does not interfere.
4. The afternoon: Reet draws it hand by hand, Liisu holding the paper flat, Toomas feeding her the contour grammar, the ridge as wide as a held breath.
5. At dusk, the oath: "We stood here. We drew what we saw. We drew what we mean to make." The ink takes without a stammer; it is a promise, not a lie.
6. Kaie speaks: "It will not hold against the Atlas." Reet answers that it does not have to hold against the Atlas; it has to be nearer.

Closing beat: Reet signs, and for a moment cannot say what her own name feels like. She writes it anyway.

## Chapter 22: The Ninth Night

Toomas, Palu and East Rise, night of Day 10. Purpose: The Shift and the cost.

1. The town waits in the dark on the ridge, singing Veikko's ferry song.
2. At midnight the ground rolls. The valley drains into a lake; the town lifts and moves like a held breath.
3. Houses arrive on East Rise in a heap and then in place, walls true. A goat is on a roof. No one is lost.
4. Kaie stands in the lake's new shallows and watches the Atlas's false lake form below her.
5. Reet wakes at dawn unable to recall the valley, the road, or the cairn. She knows only the faces around her.
6. Toomas swears his own oath for Soo: he will map every well in the hamlet, for nothing, for the rest of his life.

Closing beat: Reet asks Toomas, "What's there?" pointing at a hill, and he answers, "Mm. Say that shorter. A hill."

## Chapter 23: The Hearing in the Hollow

Toomas, Karst Hollow Assembly Hall, three weeks later. Purpose: Consequences and repair.

1. The Hollow looks smaller than he remembers. Delegations from Palu, Soo and Haldja Ford sit in the gallery on wooden benches, Veikko's goat among them.
2. Mihkel unlocks the vault in front of everyone and reads the Correction Book aloud, entry by entry, in a steady voice.
3. Kaie testifies. She does not defend herself. She repeats the numbers, takes a question from Liisu, and answers it honestly.
4. The Assembly votes to end the monopoly: any town may keep its own atlas under the oath, and the Hollow's leaves are opened to all.
5. Kaie is not imprisoned. She is made the first teacher of the oath, with bare hands, and a salary no one will envy.
6. Reet cannot find the hall without Taavi. When the Speaker asks if she remembers the vault, she wins a laugh from the gallery with an honest "I don't know." Mihkel burns the note on Aino's brother's debt.

Closing beat: Toomas takes Kaie's gloves from the table and hangs them on the vault hook, for whoever needs them next.

## Chapter 24: Ask the Ground

Reet, new Palu, spring. Purpose: Land the emotional note.

1. Reet wakes in a room under the new school roof, with a note on the wall in Aino's blocky hand telling her where the privy is. She has stopped being embarrassed.
2. She walks the town with Liisu and a new notebook. She cannot remember yesterday's lane, so she asks the baker, the cooper and a woman hanging washing. Each draws a yard of it.
3. Taavi sells honest ink in the square, apologising to every customer in advance. Aino leads the first Palu caravan out, with an ox she has named for a baker.
4. A letter from Toomas in Soo: he has mapped sixty wells and makes a joke about his moustache. Ene Ots has begun to correct his spelling.
5. A girl from a far village arrives, wanting to learn the oath. Reet is nervous; she has nothing to teach but how to ask.
6. At the field edge, Liisu points. "What's here?"

Closing beat: Reet, who cannot remember, says "Tell me," and Liisu begins.
