# Story bible: The Salt Road Cartographers (for the writers, not the readers)

## Logline and premise

A nineteen-year-old surveyor with a perfect memory for ground discovers that the land of Orrin does what the Guild's secret master atlas tells it to, and must cross the unstable Seam with a disgraced mentor to stop the Keeper from drowning a town.

**What happens, in order.** Reet Sallik, junior Dawn-Surveyor, watches a lake "walk" to exactly the spot the Guild's public Correction Book predicted nine nights earlier (ch1). She charts fourteen such matches over eight years (ch2) and finds the next entry: Leaf 163, the Palu valley, re-inked, so it will change on the ninth night after (ch2-3). She goes to Toomas Veskimae, the disgraced ex-Keeper, who recognises the signs and pretends not to (ch3). The Keeper, Kaie Tammaru, kindly benches Reet; Reet lies and stammers (ch4). Reet, Toomas and Taavi Ilves, an ink-maker's son, join Aino Rebane's salt caravan and cross the Seam (ch5-8). Master-Surveyor Mihkel Org chases them and is outwitted (ch8). At Haldja Ford, old Veikko sings the oath-songs (ch9). At Soo, the hamlet Toomas destroyed, Reet learns he is responsible: twelve years ago he secretly redrew a river to save his sister's town (Palu), and the flood went to Soo instead, killing eleven. The Guild buried it as "a lost leaf" (ch10-12). Founding base has not been made for three hundred years; the vault's last stock ran out in Toomas's edit. Kaie, his assistant, found his notebook, and has since used his method eight times, buying base secretly from Salme Ilves, who kept the recipe, always on thin-peopled land. At the Blank, the never-shifting plain at the Seam's core, they read Hilja Orm's benchmark and learn why the land obeys: testimony, not authority (ch13). Aino confesses she was paid to report on them and chooses them (ch14). Kaie's ninth edit redraws Palu's valley as a lake basin. In Palu (ch15-18) Kaie arrives with riders to evacuate the town, which refuses to leave; she jails Reet and Toomas. Liisu, Taavi's decoy flask and Mihkel free them (ch19). The town assembles, argues, and consents (ch20). On the tenth day the whole town stands on East Rise and tells Reet what its new place will hold; she draws a place that does not yet exist, without a stammer, in true Founding Ink (ch21). On the ninth night Palu moves intact (ch22). Reet pays with every memory of ground she has ever held. The Assembly ends the Guild's monopoly; Kaie becomes the first teacher of the oath; Toomas accepts Soo's sentence (ch23). In spring Reet maps by asking (ch24).

## World and setting

Orrin is a continent whose land rearranges itself after dark along the Seam, a band running east to west, about forty miles wide and widening a mile a year. North of it lies **Karst Hollow**, a Guild city of 40,000 built in a limestone sinkhole, tiered with stairs, lamp-lit, smelling of oak gall and wet stone. South lie the **Pale Flats**, salt pans. The **Salt Road** crosses the Seam; it is redrawn daily, never the same twice. Palu, a rope-bridge town of 900 on a river gorge, lies in the Seam's southern third, about six days by ox-cart from the Hollow. Distances in the Seam: a fair day is nine miles by ox, six when rerouted.

**How ground works (rules and costs).**
1. At night, unheld ground in the Seam moves. Held ground stays still.
2. Ground is changed or held for good by a drawing in Founding Ink by someone who stood on it, with the Oath of Standing: "I stood here. I drew only what I saw." Riders' day-ink (Ilves Black mixed with stood-water) can only pin a yard or two for one night.
3. If the drawing differs from the ground, the ground changes to match it. A drawing made on the spot lands that night. A drawing made far away (the vault) lands on the ninth night, "the news walks".
4. Cost: the drawer forgets the ground drawn, in proportion to its size. A boundary stone costs a yard of memory; a town costs everything.
5. Near over far: a drawing made on the spot beats a vault drawing of the same ground (planted ch1 pin, ch9 song, ch13 stone). Truthful drawing only: a lie will not take the ink. The ink also fails if not made with stood-water.
6. Far drawing needs a vial of the place's stood-water from the assay shelf, which is why vials go missing. The Guild teaches that the Atlas merely records. Only Toomas (by accident), Kaie (from his notebook) and now Reet know it decides. The Guild's monopoly was invented by Hilja's heirs, not by Hilja.

**Glossary.**
- **Seam**: the shifting band. **Shift**: a night of movement. **Ground-sick**: nausea after a Shift.
- **Dawn-Surveyor / Dawn Sheet**: riders who correct and sell the daily map.
- **Atlas**: 412 vellum leaves in the vault. **Leaf**: one page. **Gutter**: the inner margin. **Pinhole**: where the dividers pivoted (5 mm from the gutter, always).
- **Keeper**: head of the Atlas. **Correction Book**: public ledger of "re-inking" in the Atlas.
- **Founding Ink**: iron gall, Pale Flats salt, lamp-black and stood-water. **Ilves Black**: the Ilves family's ordinary ink, sold to the Guild for generations.
- **Stood-water**: a vial of local water drawn by a rider on the spot; the **assay shelf** holds thousands.
- **Pinning**: a rider's small same-night drawing that keeps a yard or two of ground still.
- **Chain**: 66 feet, 100 links. **Plumb-bob**, **chain-staff**, **dividers**.
- **Contour grammar**: the Guild's way of drawing slope in lines and ticks.
- **The Blank**: six miles of white plain at the Seam's core that has never moved. **Hearth-Stone**: Hilja Orm's benchmark there.
- **Rule Forty**: "No Surveyor sleeps unwatched in a shifting place." The Guild's Rules are numbered to 112.
- **Stake-and-ask**: caravans stake the oxen each night, then ask the next caravan where they are.
- **Name-Day of the Bridge**: Palu's autumn feast.
- Customs: never draw a place you have not stood in; never name a place twice (rename and lose the first).

## Cast

**Naming note:** Toomas and Taavi share an initial: always full names, never shortened; Toomas speaks in slow questions, Taavi in hedged clauses. The stammer is a lie-tell, treated by others as information, never mocked.

**Reet Sallik**, 19, junior Dawn-Surveyor from Sora Crossing. 5 ft, cropped dark hair she cuts herself with survey shears, grey eyes, a notch through her right eyebrow, left-handed with ink-stained fingers to the wrist, a brass plumb-bob on a cord. Fast, exact, counts in yards and links, "to the yard" is her habit. Stammers only when she lies ("I— I— I did not"). Example: "Four hundred and six paces from the cairn, and the cairn is eleven yards west of where it was." Wants: the truth to be written down correctly. Fears: being wrong, and being forgotten as a person once her memory has gone. Hides: she is lonely. Arc: from believing a perfect record is safety to trusting people to tell her what is there.

**Toomas Veskimae**, 62, ex-Atlas-Keeper, now a bookbinder in Tinta Row. Milky left eye, bad right knee, wide white moustache tea-stained, walks with a chain-staff. Slow, dry, speaks in questions and invented proverbs ("A wall remembers who leaned"). Habit: "Mm. Say that shorter." Wants: absolution, not truth. Fears: Reet's judgement. Hides: he did it. Arc: from silence to public confession and Soo's sentence.

**Aino Rebane**, 28, salt-caravan guard, 6 ft 2, shaved head, red scarf, chipped front tooth, long salt-hook. Loud, sums up in jokes, names every ox after a Guild officer. Afraid of rope bridges. Wants to clear her brother's debt. Hides: she reports to the Guild by pigeon. Arc: a hired shield becomes family.

**Taavi Ilves**, 20, ink-maker's son. Grey-blue fingertips, freckles, ears that stick out, a bandolier of tiny vials. Over-qualified sentences, "technically", apologises before he is right. Wants to leave the family trade. Fears he is dishonest like his mother. Hides what his mother sold. Arc: speaks plainly, makes the ink.

**Kaie Tammaru**, 48, Atlas-Keeper. Tiny, silver braid to her waist, white kidskin gloves that hide stained hands, bent-legged brass dividers. Quiet complete sentences, ends statements with "naturally". Wants the Hollow to survive thirty-one years of widening Seam; her answer is to push Shifts onto thin-peopled ground, and to move people first. Fears the Atlas is a lie. Hides eight edits. Arc: from certainty to teaching the oath.

**Mihkel Org**, 55, Master-Surveyor. Round, bald with a walnut-dyed fringe, mustard waistcoat. Quotes the Rules by number, bluster hiding decency. Wants order. Arc: opens the vault.

**Liisu Pold**, 11, Palu. Gap in the front teeth, ink-black thumbnail, flat declarations ("That's wrong, the well is by the mulberry."). Draws maps of Palu and ties them to the bridge. Wants to be a mapper, which the Guild forbids.

**Jaan Pold**, 41, Palu's reeve, her father. Heavy shoulders, a bell-rope callus. Stubborn, proud, speaks in short commands. Fears being the reeve who left.

**Veikko Pent**, 70, ferryman at Haldja Ford. One ear, a goat called Rule, sings his answers. Wants company. Knows the old songs.

**Salme Ilves**, 51, Taavi's mother, ink-mistress. Cigar-gruff, blunt, loves precisely. She sold the Keeper pure Founding base for eight years. Hides it.

**Ene Ots**, 80, Soo's elder. Walks with two sticks. Asks Toomas to name the eleven.

## Voice guide

Close third person, past tense, alternating Reet and Toomas (Reet leads). Register: warm, wry, exact; craft detail told through a character's habits. Sentences vary: a long surveying sentence, then a short one. Humour lives in the caravan and in Mihkel. Dialogue: double quotation marks, new paragraph per speaker, interruptions with a dash. About 40 percent dialogue.

**Sample (not part of the novel).** The ground at Dry Cairn had a way of looking innocent, like a dog beside a torn cushion. Reet put the chain down at the cairn, walked it out to the hundredth link, and stuck the pin. Eleven yards west, then. She did not write the number. Writing was for later, for the Hollow, for Mihkel's red pencil. For now she let the number sit in the place where she kept all numbers, and noticed that the place was fuller than yesterday. Someone had moved a lake. Someone had put it, as carefully as a hand sets down a cup, exactly where the Book had said.

**The narrator never writes:** (1) stock phrases such as "a testament to"; (2) villain speeches; (3) a map described as "ancient and mysterious"; (4) characters labelling emotions in full sentences; (5) magic that does not cost; (6) winks at the reader; (7) Reet reading minds; (8) the word "journey" for a trip.

## Motifs

1. Hands and ink stains. 2. Salt: tears, preserves, spoils maps. 3. Plumb-bobs and stakes: what holds. 4. Gloves versus bare hands. 5. Songs that carry old names. 6. Empty patches in memory. 7. The word "naturally". 8. Asking: the last word of each act.

## Timeline and ground rules

**Backstory.** Guild Year (GY) 1: Hilja Orm draws the Atlas. GY 292: Reet born. GY 299 (autumn, 12 years ago): Toomas, Keeper, redraws Palu's river to save his sister's town; the flood goes to Soo; eleven die; he is dismissed in 300, the leaf declared "lost". GY 302: Kaie, his assistant, becomes Keeper. GY 303: her first edit. GY 311, late autumn: story.

**Present, by Day.** Day 1: the lake walks (ch1-2); the Palu re-inking is entered. Day 2: ch3-4. Day 3: leave Hollow (ch5-6). Day 4: ch7-8. Day 5: Ford, Soo (ch9-10). Day 6: ch11-12. Day 7: the Blank (ch13-14). Day 8 evening: Palu (ch15). Day 9: ch16-19. Day 10: ch20-22; the ninth night is the night of Day 10. Weeks later: the Hearing (ch23). Spring: ch24.

**Rules to keep.** A Shift lands between midnight and first light. No Shift on the Blank. Kaie leaves the Hollow Day 4 and arrives Day 9 afternoon (Seam detours). Her notice reached Palu Day 5: a "precautionary gathering on the upland" on Day 9, which Jaan ignored because Guild notices cry wolf. The Correction Book logs edits as "re-inking" because Mihkel audits it for tidiness only. Only Reet, Toomas, Taavi, Aino, Kaie, Mihkel and Veikko know the ground rules at start; Palu learns in ch20. Aino's pigeon basket holds six birds. Reet's memory loss grows from ch1 (a yard), ch8 (a mountain pass), ch22 (all). Kaie wears her gloves in every scene until ch17.
