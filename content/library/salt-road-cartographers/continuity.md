# Continuity log: The Salt Road Cartographers

Each writer appends the facts a chapter establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: The Lake That Walked
- Day 1, first light, Dry Cairn (belt-high limestone cairn, brass datum stud). Reet rides grey mare Plover. Kuld Mere moved a half-mile west: near shore 40 chains 18 links from the cairn, bearing 2 degrees south of west, north spit against the Saar hedge.
- Reet predicted it from Book entry "Leaf 31, Kuld heath, lake basin re-inked. Tidy." (read nine mornings before, said aloud at breakfast to Pille). Two earlier chance matches noticed in four years.
- Pille Raud, 23, rider: broad, long plait, burnt nose, piebald mule Biscuit. Riders know Reet’s stammer is a lie-tell and never mock it.
- Hallik boundary stone (H and K faces) crept toward the lake as if roped. Leelo Hallik, farm-wife, watched Reet pin it with day-ink (Ilves Black plus lake water in a horn cup) and the oath. It holds one night.
- Cost: the yard of heath at the stone is a blank in Reet’s memory ("gap where a tooth has been"), her first loss. Right hand ink-dyed to the wrist, writing left hand only to the second knuckle.
- She left a stood-water vial for the assay shelf ("Kuld Mere, north shore, first light"); stammered lying to Leelo ("It’s the c— the cold").
- Ends: riding to the Hollow, she tells Plover someone drew the lake before it was there.

## Ch 2: The Correction Book
- Day 1. Karst Hollow: lamp-boys, 419 steps on the Guild stair, Plover at the rim livery. Ants, about 11, lamp-boy, is Reet’s friend. Guild House on the north wall; Day-book room at top of the east wing; her cupboard holds ledgers since age 15.
- Mihkel Org, 55: round, bald, walnut-dyed fringe, Master-Surveyor 19 years, mustard waistcoat for audits, hides sugar pastries. Rule Eleven: rider reports, Keeper interprets. Rule Nineteen: two gloves on vellum.
- Reet matched fourteen Book re-inkings (Leaf 56 Tallow Moor, 71 Kask Ford, 88 Sora Crossing) to Shifts nine nights later, all within eight years. Mihkel called it chance, let her pick Leaf 88 for the audit.
- Vault: iron door, three locks, three keys (Keeper’s, Mihkel’s, one in the Assembly strong-room); two open it. Porter Tõnis, 71, watched her and dozed.
- Leaf 88: pinhole 4 mm from gutter, was 5 mm on her tracing two years ago; new corner ink glossy and undried. She took a sliver (hidden in her glove thumb) and made two tracings, one filed, one onion-skin in her shirt.
- Assay hall (keeper Mistress Piret, ear-trumpet): Row 88 had 94 vials, now 6; Row 163 (Palu) had 112, now bare; 19 other rows gapped. "Breakage."
- Evening Book entry: "Leaf 163, Palu valley, re-inked." Reet counted nine nights and stopped on the tenth day.

## Ch 3: A Binder in Tinta Row
- Day 2 evening. Toomas Veskimae, 62, binds alone in Tinta Row (Lill & Sons herring ledger): bad knee, white left eye, tea-stained moustache, chain-staff for walking. Two cups, the second kept mouth-down. Sister Helju in Palu wrote every Midwinter; none came last Midwinter.
- Reet is the Guild’s Assay Fair memory prodigy. She recited pre-flood Soo from a Dawn Sheet copied at sixteen: three bends, nine-stone ford, mill with crooked middle sluice, fourteen roofs, ash tree and bench. His awl shook.
- He drew Palu with planted errors; she found them plus a new one: the bell-tower was rebuilt facing south. Palu: forty bridges, bare east ridge, birch hanger on its north flank.
- He saw the 4 mm pinhole and thought (unnamed) of a girl he trained whose dividers had a bent leg. He told Reet nothing of it.
- She told him of the Hallik stone and the lost yard. He said a far hand is beaten by a near one: someone must stand in Palu. Mihkel gave her Rule Eleven; she has no one else.
- Ends: "Eight" nights left; he puts the kettle on and turns up the second cup.

## Ch 4: Naturally
- Day 3 morning. Kaie Tammaru: under five feet, silver braid to her waist, pale brown eyes, white kidskin gloves with pearl buttons, salted Pale Flats tea, ends sentences "naturally". Rooms off the vault antechamber.
- Her brass dividers have a bent leg and stand slanted "like a dancer’s foot". She stepped them along Reet’s fourteen-column draught (every interval landed) and kept it.
- She had Reet’s Kuld pin-sheet and asked if the stone stopped. Asked if Reet had spoken to Toomas; Reet stammered "I— I— I have not." Kaie called Toomas her teacher.
- Leave of absence on full pay, report to Mihkel at the fourth bell, Mihkel to guard her loft door "for your protection".
- Reet left by the linen-cupboard hatch and lamp-boys’ stair (Ants helped) with satchel, chain, her mother’s last letter and the onion-skin tracing. The Kuld vial and filed tracing stay in the Hollow.
- Wharf Gate (south wall, Cold Stream, barges, ducks): Toomas in boots at noon with a heavy "bread" bag. Ends: "I stammered." "I know. I heard it from the next street."

## Ch 5: Ilves Black
- Day 3, early afternoon. Ilves Ink-works, Tinta Row: shutters nailed shut, 31 vats breathing sulphur. Salme Ilves, 51: short, wide, blue-black arms, leather cap, thin cigar, exact numbers, says "roughly" when she lies. Taavi, 20: freckles, jug ears, grey fingertips, vial bandolier, says "technically".
- Taavi tested the sliver: Founding grade, unmade for 300 years, mixed with Sora marsh water (Reet: the drain behind Kalda’s mill).
- Salme denied making it ("roughly, the odd cask years back, to a clerk"). Taavi heard the lie; Toomas saw him hear it. Toomas privately recalled a jar he once emptied to the lees.
- Guild watchers (grey Surveyor’s-office coats) came front and back. Escape via the dye room: Toomas fell into the green vat to the hip (trousers stained green), Salme flung lamp-black on a watcher, then roofs and a drainpipe.
- Taavi followed, apologising for taking his mother’s brass funnel and indigo; Reet said he was invited. He now travels with them.
- At the Wharf Gate Salme gave Taavi a stoneware flask in red thread, pure Founding base: "Don’t drink it. Don’t lose it. Don’t tell me where." Toomas held it: warm all through, long held against a body.

## Ch 6: Oxen Named for Officials
- Day 3 afternoon to midnight. Salt Company yard: 40 wagons, 36 oxen, master Hendrik Loo (never removes his hat). Aino Rebane, 28: 6 ft 2, shaved head, red scarf wound thrice, chipped front tooth, salt-hook on her back. Oxen named for officials: Registrar, Auditor, Guildmaster, Treasurer, Rule Forty, Mihkel (dun with a pale belly).
- They travel as pilgrims to the Bridge at Palu; Toomas paid eight silver marks. Aino quizzed Reet on the road (Three Pines, Kesk’s well) and was annoyed to be impressed.
- The Seam’s edge is three miles from the Wharf Gate, crossed at sunset, sky tight like a sail. Camp is a wagon ring; Mägi wool train a hundred yards off; stake-and-ask shouted across the dark.
- Aino keeps six "hens" in a covered basket; at dusk she carried it north out of the light; Reet saw it lifted, heard a clatter like shaken cards, and filed it.
- Taavi ground-sick; Toomas gave him dried mint and looked north at the Hollow glow, saying nothing.
- Midnight Shift: roar from the south, a ship-turning sensation, a hill sliding past; Reet lost no memory. Ends with Aino driving a stake: "It’ll still move. The stake’s just so we know where we woke up."

## Ch 13: The Blank
- Day 7, morning to noon. Left the new unnamed salt lake (a mile long; Reet's vial labelled "Unnamed lake. First light") at sunrise. Aino drove oxen Registrar and Mihkel (ox) nine miles without a halt; Registrar lay down at the stone but is alive. Cart (smells of eels) and unnamed borrowed pony are from Soo.
- The Blank: wind stops at a line; voices drop dead at the speaker's feet, no echo; no birds, no growth; six miles across. Hearth-Stone is at the centre, 6,200 paces from the northern lichen line. Taavi blacked Aino's, Reet's and his own eyes with lamp-black against the glare; Toomas declined (white eye).
- Hearth-Stone: black stone not local (carried in by Hilja, "on an ox"), knee-high, door-sized, top dressed flat. Brass datum stud at the north end of a hand-wide, finger-deep north-south groove (a noon-line) running to the south edge. Oath cut on the north edge in square capitals (A without crossbar, R with flicked tail): "I stood here. I drew only what I saw." Below: two carved hands (upper holds a pen over a rectangle, lower is flat on a wavy line), "THE FAR HAND WRITES. THE NEAR HAND HOLDS.", "H. ORM" and the numeral 1.
- Groove fills at noon with clear icy water welling from a hairline crack under the stud; drains about one o'clock. Taavi: base plus Soo cart-barrel water = rust scum; base plus groove water = instant clean black. The ink needs the place's own stood-water.
- Toomas's teaching: "The ground believes whoever stood on it." The Atlas is a witness, not a king; assay vials are riders' "I stood here" kept in glass and spent by a Keeper. Toomas said "Not yet" because he wanted a witness better than himself. Leaf 206 (middle of 412) is bare vellum with a small square and dot, forbidden to every Keeper. Reet used his "Say that shorter" on him.
- Aino cried once, hard, against the stone, saying "It's not the ox"; Toomas has wondered four days about the hens and kept quiet.
- Ends: Reet pulls off her left glove with her teeth, lays her bare hand in the full groove (a hand's width exactly): "It's just a hand. Anyone could do this."

## Ch 14: What the Pigeon Carried
- Day 7 dusk, camp beside the Hearth-Stone. Taavi found the pigeon cage in the false back of the kindling box under the cart seat: peeled willow, leather hood, six stalls, five grey pigeons, one empty stall (red wax ring), tissue slips, Mihkel's waxed-linen cipher square ("Keep dry. Burn if taken."), and an unsent copy of the first report ("S DOES NOT SLEEP. EATS WHAT SHE IS GIVEN. SHE COUNTS. R.").
- Aino sent only that one bird (Day 3 night) and carried the basket out three later dusks without sending. Her brother Rasmus Rebane owes 340 silver marks to a card house on Lamp Street; a Surveyor's-office grey coat paid 100 down the afternoon Reet booked passage; 240 still owed, due when Aino finishes the service. Rasmus is held in a room over a baker's.
- Reet had suspected since the Gullet and said nothing ("You gave me an egg"). Reet admitted she cannot lie and stammered to Kaie. Aino admitted she names oxen for officials so she can shout at them. Reet's list of people: Ants (11), her mother in Sora, Mihkel's pastry crusts.
- Toomas: "You are paid by the day, Aino, not by the sentence." Aino wrote a false report (all drowned: Veskimae holding his moustache above water, Sallik counting, Ilves boy apologising, oxen named) and enciphered it herself; Reet read it back without a stammer. Toomas struck "Debt to be closed".
- Grey pigeon with a white fleck at the throat released at dusk, the Blank's first bird; it circled the stone twice and flew south, the wrong way. Four pigeons remain. Aino vowed never to name an ox for anyone she respects. Debt still 240 marks.

## Ch 15: Palu Under Its Bridges
- Day 8 evening. Left the Blank at first light; arrived over a limestone shoulder. Palu: gorge about 200 ft deep, 300 ft across, forty numbered rope bridges, ribbons on every cable, Name-Day of the Bridge feast (plum brandy, drums, roast goat). Red flood line dated 299 painted 20 ft above the river on the east wall; the river is now a thin bootlace in a wide bed.
- Toomas cannot see the upper gorge (memory gap). Reet described it: turns east-north-east at 300 yards, 30-ft white fall at 450 yards, rose rock left bank, grey right, a sideways birch above the lip. He thanked her; she said "Don't".
- Liisu Pold, 11: grey smock pinned with ribbons, gap teeth, plum-coloured thumbnail, flat voice. Her map (pencil and walnut ink, contour ticks all uphill). She corrected Reet: the well is by the mulberry (7 years old); the old well moved when the Lauris' cow Marta fell in. Reet said thank you. She is Toomas's great-niece and told him Helju is behind the bell-tower under the white stone.
- Jaan Pold, reeve, 41: calls Toomas "Veskimae". Helju died the eleventh night of the dark month, the week before Midwinter, forbidding anyone to write; a pit was burned in frozen ground. Jaan refused the warning (Guild notices cry wolf: last year's survey drill, a landslip scare): "I'm not the reeve who ran." Bell Bridge is central, 210 ft, bell-tower at its south end facing south, reeve's house at the north end.
- A Guild courier in a grey coat has been drunk since Day 5 under a trestle; Liisu borrowed his lime-wood board with an onion-skin tracing of Leaf 163: valley as a clerk's-blue lake, no Palu, East Rise a hatched cliff-line, pinhole very near the gutter. Reet said she must measure it by daylight. Liisu meant to put it back.
- Aino whispered the bridges are the worst thing she has seen and she will cross all forty; Reet will count them for her. Ends: Toomas on the Bell Bridge sees a white ribbon with a child's knot and Liisu's map pinned to the cable ("PALU. Liisu Pold. Her map.").

## Ch 16: Stood Water
- Day 9. Dawn: Wet Stair (304 steps) to the river. Reet and Taavi: base in Palu river water goes black at once; any stood-water from the place works; Kaie holds Row 163's 112 vials. Liisu said Gran called the flood-line "the day somebody did" save them. Toomas went to Helju's grave before light; Aino crossed 29 bridges by one o'clock.
- Reet tried to pin the whole valley from East Rise with true ink (a spoon of base in a pint of river water); she drew only Ruus's barley field (thorn hedge, ditch, split-oak gatepost with an iron ring, stile of two flat stones), lost it from memory, then the line went grey and she fainted. Liisu: "You can't stand in all of it. We can." Ruus's field is now a blank in Reet's mind.
- Woke in a stone sheepfold above the town; understood the townspeople are the ones who stood. Reet: "I'm only the one who can draw."
- Taavi has two quarts and a gill of base; a gill of base per sixty gills of water makes the ink; the town needs far less than he has. Half the base is in a stoppered grey stoneware crock inside a hollowed rye loaf held by Liisu (she knows a place under the grain store nobody goes). He topped up Salme's red-thread flask with Ilves Black: his first deliberate lie. The flask is half real base, half Ilves Black, and still smells of clove.
- Reet measured the tracing's pinhole with her ivory rule: 4 mm. Kaie's left divider leg leans three up, four across, five along, so its pivot lands 4 mm from the gutter. The Keeper's hand is in the Atlas.
- Ends: lookout bell at the gorge head; Day 9 afternoon, twelve horses, a small rider on a pale grey with a silver braid.

## Ch 17: The Keeper Comes to Dinner
- Day 9, late afternoon and evening. Kaie crossed the Bell Bridge on foot with Mihkel and riders (horses left at the carters' stables on the south lip); white kidskin gloves with pearl buttons. She claimed Palu guest-right; Jaan: "Sit. Eat." Grayling stew with dill and cream. Liisu asked about the gloves; Kaie cited Rule Nineteen.
- Kaie's figures: Seam widens a mile a year; Hollow rim is 31 years away; in GY 303 it was 11; eight places bought 28 years; 211 people moved first at her own expense, nobody died; Palu is the ninth place. Lake on the night of the tenth, forty feet deep. She offered Orav (empty pans, 300 roofs, held by the Hollow since the sickness). Jaan: 31 of his family lie behind the tower; "No." Kaie: "Naturally you may refuse. I will ask again." Reet: "The figures are right. The sum is wrong," and she does not yet know which line.
- In the yard (rail over the gorge, upturned buckets): eight places on fourteen leaves, first was Tallow Moor, autumn 303. She found Toomas's notebook in the false drawer behind the lamp-oil receipts two years after his dismissal, at 38; she held the lamp the night he drew the river and watched his hand. She did not ask him because he would have said no. Her cost: she cannot say where Sora Crossing lies; she draws from the leaf, not the ground. The riders never knew their vials were lent.
- Mihkel overheard "the ninth" and the notebook, for the first time; no Rule came; he sat on an upturned bucket of herring heads: "It was a very tidy Book." He then cited Rule Sixty-two: custody orders must be signed bare-handed.
- Kaie ordered Reet, Toomas, Taavi and Aino held in the grain store under Rule Forty. She removed her gloves to sign: hands stained blue-black to the wrist, glossy, years deep. Mihkel had never seen them in 19 years. Aino was heard being chained to a wheel after a scuffle with a shield.
- Ends: Reet: "You've been drawing for years." Kaie: "Naturally."

## Ch 18: The Grain Store
- Day 9 night. Grain store: timber barn on staddle stones, 41 barley sacks, high slit window, bolted door, guard outside. Toomas coughs; Reet wrapped her grey Soo scarf round his mouth. Aino is chained to the near wheel of the reeve's grain cart, whispering insults and naming the riders after Guild officers. Taavi was found under the cart sacking; riders were told not to touch his things.
- After the second hour of night Kaie came alone and ungloved with a blanket and a bottle of hot salted tea. She offered Reet the Keepership (cannot lie, cannot forget; drawing from the leaf costs almost nothing). Reet: the figures are right, but no one asked Palu; "a no is information". Kaie: "Palu would have said no." Sora's marsh drain (Leaf 88) was the seventh place, moved a quarter-mile into the basin; Reet's mother's dry cellar was a consequence, and Kaie said she was sorry. Kaie will not ask again before dawn.
- Kaie took Salme's red-thread flask from the back of Taavi's bandolier, smelled the clove, poured it onto the floor; the stain is shaped like the Pale Flats. Taavi: "It's the only flask I carry." Reet: "That was all that was in the flask" (no stammer). Kaie accepted it.
- Toomas apologised to Taavi for not stopping it earlier and believes the base is lost. Reet and Taavi did not tell him about the loaf (guards listening). The real base (a quart in a grey crock) is in the loaf with Liisu.
- Reet's ground-memory keeps leaving; what people do to her stays. Kaie at the door: "I did not mean it to be so cold."
- Ends: a child's three-note whistle, rising, rising, falling, from beneath the floor.
