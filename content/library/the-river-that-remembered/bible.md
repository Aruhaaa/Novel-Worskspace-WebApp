# Story bible: The River That Remembered

For the writers, not the readers. Close third person, present tense, Thida. Mythic river fantasy, 22 chapters, about 2,640 words each.

## Logline and premise

A ferrywoman who has not touched the river's water since it took her daughter must carry a dead man's memory three hundred miles upstream in forty days, and learn that the dams she helped keep silent are what drowned the child.

**What happens, in order.** Eleven years ago, on the Closing, the Water-Lords' Warden Oluwa Sen dropped the leaf-gates of Nine Sluices during the old king's opening ceremony. The king's barge was crushed, and the surge killed 87 people in the lower channels, among them Thida's daughter Dara, eight. The Lords called it an accident. Thida, ferrying guests that day, signed the inquiry as neutral witness, half mad with grief, so she could go home. Sluice-keeper Anan Teav, who was ordered to pull the pins, fled downriver ahead of the wave and pulled the dying Dara from a collapsing stilt-house. Rule of the river: a child dying in a carrier's arms in Amaru water passes her last memory to the carrier. Anan lived eleven years under a false name in the Salt Marsh, carrying the girl's memory and his own guilt. Three weeks before the story, he wrote to Prince Dumo's widow, Channary Ruan. At the book's opening he walks into the river at Low Tamber, and the river-priest Sovann Pech draws his memory (both memories) into a jar. Thida, as ferrywoman, receives Anan's belongings and finds Dara's egret-bone hairpin.

Channary hires Thida to carry the jar to the Mother Gate, the source shrine, before the memory sours on day 40. The party: Thida; Bakary Vorn, her late husband's cousin, a disgraced sluice-wright; Ife Lamin, a 13-year-old channel-mapper; and Sovann Pech, the priest who stopped believing. Marshal Kossi Dey, Thida's former apprentice, hunts them for Oluwa Sen. At the midpoint the jar cracks and Thida tastes it: she sees that Bakary designed the "one-pull" that dropped the gates, and that Dara's memory is in the jar. The sip also takes her lullaby. Ife is exposed as an informer (his sister Mey is held at the Lid). Sovann dies at Ash Reach after teaching Thida the Setting. At the Lid, Channary (whose son Rith is held at Oluwa's court) is revealed to have bargained with Oluwa Sen to bury the dams' guilt and keep only the murder charge; the jar is taken, Thida jailed. Oluwa shows her the Great Flood that made him; Kossi turns; Bakary and Ife free her. At the Mother Gate Thida drinks the set memory, which costs her all her craft memory of the channels, and testifies under Remembrance Law. The Assembly orders the dams opened, in Seven Breaths on Thida's terms: the ferry-guild evacuates the lower channels first. Bakary dies holding the Fifth Sluice open. Low Tamber's floating market is lost, 4 die. The river gives Thida Dara's memory, with the lullaby, and the rains arrive.

## World and setting

**The Lower Reaches** is a delta kingdom of a thousand channels, stilt-houses, floating markets and rice-terraced mud. The river **Amaru** runs 296 miles from the Mother Gate to the sea. Heat, mud-smell, woodsmoke, fermented fish, green water gone brown. Dry season now: the **pulse** (highland flood) is 27 days late, the **Salt Line** has crept to mile 68, orchards die white.

**Rules.** (1) A drowned person's memory enters the river. Those who drink Amaru water may dream it back. (2) **The river keeps accounts**: every swallow of a stranger's memory displaces one of the drinker's own of equal weight, which goes into the water for someone else to dream. Cost is permanent. A sip costs small things, a draught costs great ones. (3) A **Drawing** by a priest captures a living or just-dead person's memory into a clay jar sealed with beeswax and red clay. It **sours after forty days**: dull, then nothing. (4) **Setting** is pouring the jar into the pool at the Mother Gate; the memory fixes there. (5) **Remembrance Law**: testimony of a drinker, spoken at the pool before the Assembly, cannot be overruled by any lord. A lying drinker is taken by drowning-dreams. (6) The river never lies and never answers directly. It answers sideways, in dreams, currents, objects returned. (7) **Ferry-guild neutrality**: carry all, ask nothing, side with none. Breaking it costs the guild-knot (blue thread in the wrist-cord) and berth-rights.

**Places (river miles).** Low Tamber 0 (floating market, Drowning Steps, Thida's stilt-house). Nine Sluices 24 (nine stone sluices, leaf-gates of black ironwood, the lock-basin). Salt Line 68 (Pesh Alo village). The Hen's Ladder 95 (shoals). Ketu Bend 130 (guild hall). Wat Samre 150 (half-drowned temple). Pa Tomloy 175 (floating market). The Stair of Oru 205 (rapids). Ash Reach 232 (burned stubble-country). The Lid 265 (highland dam, gaugemen camp). The Mother Gate 272 (stone weir-shrine and pool; the last mile on foot).

**Glossary.** *Pole-barge*: flat teak-ribbed freight boat. *Palm-sail*: woven rectangular sail. *Kettle-skiff*: round one-person boat. *Long Patience*: Thida's 38-foot barge, one palm-sail, a bilge eel-tank, a blue-painted tiller. *Drowning Steps*: stone stairs at Low Tamber where the dead come out. *Belongings-tin*: the dead's effects, carried to kin by ferrywomen. *Wrist-cord*: knotted guild record. *Leaf-gates*: sluice gates. *One-pull*: Bakary's emergency-release rig, one rope and nine pins. *Gaugemen*: Water-Lords' enforcers in grey sashes marked with gauge-rods. *Wardens*: the seven Water-Lords. *The Closing*: the day the gates dropped. *The Lid*: the great upper dam. *Drawer*: priest who performs Drawings. *Dream-sips*: thimbles of Amaru water sold by licensed dreamers. *Mudfish broth, pickled lime, rice-wine (tuak)*: staples. *Egret-bone pin*: Dara's hairpin, three notches Thida carved. *Seven Breaths*: the staged release. *The Assembly*: highland headmen and guildmothers at the Gate.

## Cast

**Thida Vorn**, 41, "Mistress Vorn", "Thi" to Bakary. Lean, long arms, a burn scar across her left palm, grey wrist-cord, hair cropped short since the Closing. Speaks in short flat practical sentences, answers questions with questions, never swears, hums when frightened without noticing. "Pay the toll and ask me nothing. I'm a ferry." Wants to deliver the jar and be left alone. Fears the water and fears forgetting Dara. Hides that she signed the inquiry. Arc: neutrality to taking a side; from carrying the dead to carrying the living.

**Bakary Vorn**, 52. Husband Ibou's cousin. Huge, slow, bent, missing two fingers on his right hand, a wright's calluses, a grey plait. Counts things in sevens, says "Say it plain." Speaks low and short. Wants to atone. Fears being found out. Hides that he designed the one-pull and taught it to Anan. Arc: confesses and dies opening the Fifth Sluice by hand.

**Ife Lamin**, 13. Skinny, ink-stained fingers, one ear pierced with a fishhook, wears an oversized gaugeman's coat. Fast, dry, sarcastic annotations: "Here be mud. Also here." Wants his sister Mey back. Fears being useless. Hides that he reports their route to Kossi. Arc: informer to chartmaker; becomes Thida's memory of the channels.

**Sovann Pech**, 58. Priest-Drawer. Round, shaved head, tremor, rice-wine breath, yellow robe patched. Rambling, polite, apologises before saying things. "Forgive me. It's worse than that." Wants to be proved wrong that the river listens. Fears being right. Hides that he took a bribe from Oluwa's steward to bury the first Drawing attempt eleven years ago. Arc: dies teaching the Setting.

**Channary Ruan**, 36. Prince Dumo's widow, mother of Prince Rith, 7. Tall, white-streaked braid, ink-black mourning silk, sharp hands. Formal, precise, never raises her voice. "I am asking you to be useful, not kind." Wants her son safe on the throne. Fears the dams' truth would undo the Lords who keep the realm fed. Hides her bargain with Oluwa. Arc: chooses truth at the Gate.

**Lord Oluwa Sen**, 67. Warden of Nine Sluices. Small, courteous, white goatee, cane of ironwood, damp cough. Soft-spoken, asks questions, gentle. "I would like you to be comfortable." Wants the dams kept because the Great Flood of his childhood killed 20,000. Fears the flood. Hides that he knows the river is dying. Arc: unmasked, dethroned, sits at the Steps.

**Marshal Kossi Dey**, 33. Thida's apprentice from 12 to 17. Broad shoulders, a pilot's squint, tattooed gauge on his forearm. Clipped, loyal, calls her "Mistress". Wants order and the favour of the Lords. Fears being what the Lords made him. Hides his mother's death in the Closing. Arc: refuses an order, opens the gates.

**Guildmother Yaye Ndoye**, 60. Ketu Bend. Wide-hipped, rope-calloused, rings on every finger. Hearty, proverbs reversed: "A river never sleeps and neither do I." Protects the guild, will not take a side. Arc: sends the guild south to evacuate.

**Anan Teav** (dead, in memory). 49 at death. Stooped, ledger-neat, left-handed, nets-mender's hands. Soft apologetic voice.

**Dara Vorn** (dead, in memory). Eight. Gap tooth, egret pin, sings the lullaby "Little Boat, Little Boat".

**Mey Lamin**, 8, Ife's sister, held at the Lid camp as a runner. Stubborn, tells everyone their fortune.

## Voice guide

Close third person, present tense, Thida only; river interludes in second person ("you"), 100-250 words, italic block. Register: lyrical but plain, physical, unsentimental. Grief is carried, not dwelt on; it shows in what Thida avoids. Sentence rhythm: short declaratives broken by one long river-sentence per page. Humour: dry, from Ife's annotations and Bakary's counting. Dialogue in double straight quotes, a new paragraph per speaker, few tags.

**Sample (not part of the novel):** The barge noses into the Nine Sluices basin at third bell, and the water changes. Out in the channels it moves; here it waits. Thida holds the tiller loose, the way she holds everything, and watches the leaf-gates rise on either side like the sides of a throat. Ife is already writing. "It says here the gates are forty feet," he says. "It says nothing about the smell." "What does it smell of?" He thinks. "Things that have been told to stay." She hums three notes and stops. The tiller is warm under her scarred hand. The first gaugeman waves a rod.

**This narrator never writes:** (1) a character crying on cue; (2) the river described as "angry" or "wise"; (3) the word "destiny"; (4) explaining a metaphor; (5) a summary of grief ("she felt the weight of loss"); (6) a villain's speech about power; (7) a chapter ending on an aphorism; (8) "the river remembers" as a slogan.

## Motifs

The egret-bone pin. The lullaby "Little Boat" (the melody only; words never fully given until ch22). Salt, white crust on wood. Knots: wrist-cords, Bakary's sevens, the one-pull. Hands: Thida's burned palm, Bakary's missing fingers. The sound of the gates humming under load. Ledgers and accounts. Unfinished sentences, the river's answer sideways.

## Timeline and ground rules

**Backstory.** Thida born 41 years ago; ferrying since 21. Married Ibou at 20; Dara born when Thida was 22, drowned at 8 on the Closing, 11 years ago, when Thida was 30. Ibou died of salt-lung 6 years ago. Prince Dumo died "of fever" 3 years ago; Channary suspects poison. Kossi apprenticed to Thida at 12 (age 33 now), left at 17 for the gaugemen.

**Present.** Day 0 (late Low Moon): Anan drowns at dawn, jar drawn, sours at day 40. Day 2 depart. Day 4 Nine Sluices. Day 7 Salt Line. Day 10 Hen's Ladder. Day 13 Ketu Bend. Day 15 Wat Samre. Day 17 Pa Tomloy. Day 18 jar cracks. Day 24 Stair of Oru. Day 27 Ash Reach. Day 28 Sovann dies. Day 31 the Lid. Day 33 escape. Day 35 Mother Gate. Days 35 to 41 the Seven Breaths. Day 60: the rains.

**Rules to hold.** Upstream pace is about 9 miles a day in the dry, less in rapids. Pigeon post is 2 days from Channary to anywhere on the river. Who knows what: Thida learns of the one-pull on day 18; Ife's informing is revealed on day 29; Channary's bargain on day 31. Kossi does not know the truth of the Closing until day 32. The Lords have Thida's signature on the inquiry; she tells the party only on day 24.
