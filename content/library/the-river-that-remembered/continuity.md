# Continuity log: The River That Remembered

Each writer appends the facts a chapter establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: The Dead Man’s Tin
- Day 0 dawn, Low Tamber. The Drowning Steps have eleven shelves; the river now sits below the second. Kunthea keeps the Steps (stool, slate, never rises). Fish-seller Bun saw the man walk in at first bell and say "Sorry for the trouble".
- The dead man: "Old Anan", Salt Marsh net-mender, about fifty, stooped, grey stubble, left-handed (left hand inked blue-black), no surname or village known to anyone. Slip ticked "Gave" (the three words are Taken, Lost, Gave). He asked Kunthea the evening before who had the dawn boat. Thida gave his body to the Long Quiet (slack bend below the market).
- The tin is an oblong "Old Mother Seng ginger rounds" biscuit tin. Inside: small cloth ledger (backward-leaning left-hand script, mesh-counts, prices, and in the margins hundreds of upright tally strokes in fives per page), two bone mending hooks, a wax-sealed note, the egret-bone pin. Thida told Kunthea "three things" and hid the pin; she keeps it in her belt-pouch from now on.
- The pin has a beak-carved knob and three notches Thida cut when Dara turned eight, standing for the first three notes of the Little Boat (up, up, down). Dara called Thida "Mama".
- Note (unsigned, smeared to the right by a left hand): "To the Drawer of Low Tamber. I will go into the river at first light. Draw me as I go, and seal what you draw, and give the jar to the mother of the pin. To no one else. I ask nothing more of anyone." Sovann will hand over the jar only when the mother of the pin asks; Thida has not asked. Sovann "owed Anan something" and will not say what.
- Sovann stood hip-deep with both hands on Anan to draw him; he has drawn 41 people. Jar: forearm-high, dull brown, red clay over beeswax. He offers river water by custom; Thida refuses as she has for eleven years.
- Thida avoids the river: boots to the thigh, hands high on the pole, long-handled dipper for the eel-tank, rain-barrel for washing.
- Her house is a stilt-house on twelve piles, one room plus lean-to, ladder, shutter on the water side. Dara's room is behind a faded indigo curtain; the shelf holds a one-eyed clay frog, a comb missing four teeth, a snail shell, knucklebones, a tin whistle, and a bare hand-width gap at the left end. She dusts it every seventh day.
- A gaugeman in a plain brown skiff watched her house and noted it on a tablet. Thida hums up, up, down and her throat shuts on the fourth note; she still knows it and has never let it out. Ends with her burned left palm closed on the pin.

## Ch 2: Neutral Ground
- Low Tamber guild-house: raft-hall with a Board of Knots (a hemp copy of every living ferrywoman's cord; black-tied cords of the dead at the back). Clerk Rotha is thin, ink on his lip, cuts his own hair, and ties a black knot in the cord for each death carried. Guild rule over the door: carry all, ask nothing.
- Register entry: Anan's tin goes to next of kin "Channary Ruan, the Crown Widow", entered at first light by a girl in mourning, before Anan was cold.
- Ife Lamin: about 13 ("fourteen in the wet"), sells mulberry-paper charts on the wall above the Steps. Grey wool coat, sleeves rolled four turns, hem to his shins; fishhook in left ear; ink to the second joint. A man "who said please" paid him two coppers a day for three days to count Thida's berth (eleven callers, nine the well-man). He told her because he would rather be paid by two people. He mentioned the Hen's Ladder.
- Kossi Dey, Marshal of Waters: took his hat off; grey sash with white gauge-rod, silver pin; tattoo of blue-black ticks wrist to elbow on his forearm; broad, grown into his shoulders. She calls him "Kossi" and he flinches ("Marshal, Mistress. For the men's sake. They can see my face"). He stands with his back to the Steps and never looks at them. He coils rope "the way it wants to lie".
- Thida gave Kossi only the ledger and lied: "Hooks. Two." He asked about "a pin, perhaps". He warned: "Keep your head down, Mistress. Same as eleven years." She said she did not know what he meant; he said "No. I know." Her berth is the nineteenth on the west pier.
- The Patient Cup (rice-wine raft). Sovann says Anan came to his porch "last night" at dusk; the jar "holds more than a man", something small and bright like a coin laid in with the rice. He told Thida she is the mother of the pin.
- Market gossip: the Crown Widow fed Prince Dumo his fever (or sat up with it); "the boy with his father's ears".
- Summons: black silk in a widow's knot with a tiny silver fish, brought by a girl of fifteen (unbleached cotton, black armband): attend Lady Ruan at the third bell after dark, Tamber Channel, house with the mourning cloth. Sovann: "I thought she'd be slower."
- Ends: Kossi's brown skiff leaves the pier after Thida and follows a hundred yards behind with a lit lantern all evening.

## Ch 3: A Jar for the Source
- Channary's house-barge on Tamber Channel is draped in wet black cloth. Watched by gauge-skiffs (two at the lower mouth, one at the upper) and a man fishing without a line on a laundry boat. Steward: Vuthy, old, stiff leg, black cord at wrist.
- Channary Ruan: 36, tall, thick black braid with a white streak from the left temple, ink-black silk, long sharp hands, squares every object to the table edge. Pours tea herself and drinks first. "I am asking you to be useful, not kind."
- Rith, 7, sleeps behind a screen of painted cranes; plaster on his heel; murmurs "Not the duck". Thida avoids looking at him.
- The job: one sealed jar to the pool at the Mother Gate, 272 miles, before day 40 from the dawn it was drawn. Double the river toll: half at the quay on Day 1, half on the step of the Gate. Charter under Channary's seal (a heron over water, black wax) gives carrier's peace (gaugemen may inspect, not seize).
- Anan's letter to Channary (burned by her): he held Dara; kept the pin eleven years and meant to leave it for Thida; the Warden's steward had traced him to the Salt Marsh (a man with no past, who mends nets with the wrong hand and pays in silver); he had weeks, not years. Channary says the jar holds a Drawing, she has not looked inside, and she will say no more.
- Why Thida: Anan named her; neutrality is the cover; she does not drink (dreamers have all tried to sell her her own daughter; she once put one in the water with his tray at Pa Tomloy).
- If Channary leaves Tamber Channel, Rith is "guested" at the Lords' court at Nine Sluices that hour. She has written that she is sensible of the honour.
- Berth frozen: the guild's berth-bird (speckled hen pigeon) brought a note signed "R." for the Guild, sealed with Kossi's grey wax (gauge-rod with notched head), citing "withholding of a dead man's goods". Thida infers the watching gaugeman reported the pin. She loosed her blue guild thread (hanging loose from her wrist-cord, not cut).
- She says "Yes" aloud at Dara's doorway and signs the charter with the pin as paperweight; the loose blue thread drags ink through the V of her signature.

## Ch 4: Provisions
- Day 1. Bakary Vorn: 52, enormous, bent, grey plait thick as a hawser; right hand has thumb and two fingers (Ibou said "a rope"). Counts in sevens (the barge's 42 ribs are "six sevens"). Heard of the trip from the well-man. Refused pay, then took "a seventh of what's left". Calls her "Thi"; says "Say it plain". Said "Ibou would have wanted someone with you." Left Ibou's burning six years ago before the rice.
- Stores: seven sacks of rice, 49 smoked mudfish, seven crocks of pickled lime, fourteen poles, a bladder of sweet well water, thirty pounds of live yellow eels in the bilge tank (from Pov, the well-man's brother). The market charged triple because of the frozen berth; the noodle-raft women gave her a twist of lime. Vuthy brought the first half of the toll in silver bits at noon.
- Long Patience: stern-shelter of palm thatch where Thida sleeps by the tiller; empty slatted eel-crate lashed on the foredeck; tank amidships.
- Sovann delivered at dusk by litter by Channary's servants, jar in a basket of rice-husk; robe clean, head shaved and nicked. "I have a reputation." He weeps easily. Claims a Drawing wants a warm body near it; sleeps on the foredeck with the basket as pillow.
- Ife stowed away in the eel-crate; Bakary wanted to throw him over ("He'll sell us"). Fare: a fresh chart of every mile, drawn from his own eyes; he sleeps in the crate and does not touch the tiller. Coat "fell off a hook in a laundry". He heard Sovann say "a man and a coin" at the Cup.
- A brown gaugeman's skiff lay at the pier-end again that day. Thida wears the black silk token round her wrist beside the cord with the loosed blue.
- Departure Day 2 at dawn. Low Tamber's boats stand silent, no waving. Kunthea stands for the first time, slate held to her chest. Thida lets the first pole-stroke fall.

## Ch 5: Leaf-Gates
- Day 4, wind dies; four miles of dead water below Nine Sluices. Nine stone piers, leaf-gates of black ironwood forty feet high, lifted and dropped vertically; they hum under load. Thida goes grey, her scar tightens, she hums the three notes.
- The Fifth Sluice is the lock: a stone basin about 200 feet long with a gate at each end (lower gate comes down, upper lifts by stages), a wheel-house with a great wooden wheel on the north coping, and forty yards of paler rebuilt coping on the east wall. Lock-hands warp boats by chain.
- Inspection by Gate-Sergeant Sokun (stout, boil on neck, soft sorry voice). He knows the charter seal. Eel-tank water is taxed (seven gauges). Thida: "Pay the toll and ask me nothing. I'm a ferry."
- The jar was hidden in the eel-tank under the eels; Bakary put it there with his arm in to the shoulder because Thida could not move her hand. Sokun's rod knocked on it; Bakary said "ballast, the eels like a stone". Sovann gave the false name "Brother Phirun of Wat Samre" and wept; Sokun waved it off.
- At the wheel-house cleat hangs a tarred rope thick as a forearm with Bakary's double-sevens knot (seven turns, seven back, tail tucked). Thida recognised it; Bakary noticed her notice. He muttered: "Takes a man's whole weight, that wheel... Gate'll come up by stages if somebody holds it. Stage and hold." then "Chain's wet." Ife heard and wrote it down.
- Thida was on the upper side of the Fifth in a line of ferries at the Closing and remembers only the sound ("a row of doors slammed in a house the size of the sky").
- Locked through at dusk into the upper pool (a mile across). A plain brown skiff with one lantern lit and a grey-sashed rower slipped into the basin behind them as they left.

## Ch 6: Salt Line
- Day 7, Pesh Alo, mile 68. Pomelo orchard white with salt to the hip. A painted post at the landing records each year's Salt Line; this year's mark is above head height. Forty-two iron pans distil well-water a spoon an hour. Ibou died of salt-lung.
- Fanta: widow, about 55, laugh like a dropped pot, four front teeth missing. Grandson Pich, 5, silent cough. Traded four strings of smoked snakehead and a basket of duck eggs for ginger, clove, willow-bark, poppy and Sovann's honey. Showed the drowned willow whose roots have turned to ringing salt crystal. She told of the Great Flood (20,000 dead, her grandmother's time); the Warden's man "has a form".
- Dreamer Sambath: bald, smooth, copper licence badge with gauge-rod; thimbles tied with twine (storm, wedding, good dinner, first kiss, mother's kitchen, night with no pain); offered "Closing Reach" waters; Thida refused. Ife mocked drinking the dead, then slipped out at night and drank a fisher's storm.
- Bakary's sister lives in Pesh Alo in a little house with a red door; he will not go up ("She'd ask me what I've been doing. And I'd tell her."). He quietly paid the lane's well-man about seven silver bits to dig past the clay to a sweet vein a man's height deeper.
- Dawn Day 8: Ife cannot write his mother's name in the cartouche of his charts ("Drawn by Ife, son of"). He remembers her face, hair, how she banged the pot twice, the song at the stove, but not the name. He is thrilled by the stored storm (green boat with an eye on the bow, a beard, a wave).
- Sovann explains the rule: each swallow displaces a memory of equal weight, which goes into the water; a thimble takes small things, a draught great ones; the river weighs, it does not value. Nobody knows if it can be got back.
- Thida nearly struck Ife, put her hand down, and cooked him six duck eggs with pickled lime. Bakary counted the last two. She notices Ife's coat is cut for a small person (shoulders sit true, wool unfaded under the collar, hem let down to grow into) and puts it away.
- Left late Day 8; tied up three miles above the village. Ife asked whether Thida knows his mother's name from the dead she has carried (about 400 tins). She said "Ask me in the morning." She does not know it.
