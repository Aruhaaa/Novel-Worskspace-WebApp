# Outline: The River That Remembered

Viewpoint is Thida, close third, present tense, unless stated. "Interlude" means a short italic river passage in second person ("you"), set off by a scene break, 100-250 words. Days count from Anan's drowning (Day 0). Each chapter aims for about 2,640 words.

**The solution.** On the Closing, Warden Oluwa Sen signalled the one-pull to drop all nine leaf-gates while the king's barge was in the basin, to kill King Ketema, who meant to open the dams for good. The surge killed 87 below, including Dara. Anan Teav, pin-keeper, refused the last pins but was overruled; Bakary designed the one-pull and taught Anan, believing it was for floods. Clues: pin (ch1), Bakary's wright-knot on the Fifth Sluice (ch5), Kossi's "you signed" (ch8), Sovann's bribe (ch9), "Pin-Man" (ch10), the taste (ch11). Red herrings: Yaye's guild (ch8), Sovann as thief (ch9), Channary as Prince Dumo's poisoner (ch3, ch17). **Costs:** a sip takes a small memory (Thida's lullaby, Ife's mother's name); a draught takes a great one (Thida's channel craft).

## Chapter 01: The Dead Man's Tin
Low Tamber, dawn, Day 0.
*Purpose: hook us with a ferrywoman who carries the dead and the pin that should not be in a stranger's pocket.*
- Thida poles *Long Patience* to the Drowning Steps for a drowned man's body; the crowd is thin and the priest Sovann Pech is already kneeling with a clay jar.
- She signs the tin: a stranger, a net-mender called Old Anan, drowned himself on purpose.
- Inventory of the belongings-tin: a ledger, two hooks, a wax-sealed note, and an egret-bone pin with three notches.
- She knows the notches. She carved them. She does not breathe.
- She asks Sovann how a drowned man came by her daughter's pin. He says "Forgive me" and does not answer.
- Anan's wax-sealed note, in a left-handed ledger hand, asks only that "the Drawer of Low Tamber" draw him and that the jar go to "the mother of the pin". Sovann, who owed Anan something he will not name, did as asked.
- She refuses the river water the priest offers to calm her, as she has for eleven years.
- Scene two: she takes the pin to her stilt-house and puts it on Dara's old shelf. A grey-sashed gaugeman is watching from a skiff.
Plant: the pin; the jar; the gaugeman.
Interlude: the river addresses Thida, who will not drink.
Closing beat: she closes her burned palm around the pin and hears herself humming three notes she cannot finish.

## Chapter 02: Neutral Ground
Low Tamber, morning and afternoon, Day 0.
*Purpose: show the cost of neutrality and bring Kossi and Ife.*
- Thida visits the guild-house to log the death; the clerk says Anan's tin goes to the "next of kin": the Crown Widow, Channary Ruan.
- A boy in a gaugeman's coat, Ife Lamin, sells charts at the Steps. He tells her someone paid him to count her berth.
- Marshal Kossi Dey meets her on the dock. He is courteous. He asks to see the tin. She hands over the ledger, not the pin.
- They spar over old times: he calls her "Mistress", she calls him "Kossi" in the old way, and he flinches.
- Kossi warns her, "Keep your head down, same as eleven years."
- Scene two: Sovann Pech in a rice-wine shop admits he drew Anan "at his own request", and that the jar holds more than a man.
- A messenger arrives with a black-silk token. She is summoned.
Plant: Ife counting; the jar holds "more".
Closing beat: Kossi's skiff pulls away but its lantern stays lit behind her all evening.

## Chapter 03: A Jar for the Source
A floating house on Tamber Channel, night, Day 0.
*Purpose: inciting incident. The hire.*
- Channary Ruan receives Thida on a barge draped with mourning cloth, with her son Rith asleep behind a screen.
- The widow lays out the job: carry one sealed jar to the Mother Gate, 272 miles upstream, before day 40.
- She will not say what is in it. Thida refuses; ferrying is for the dead, not for secrets.
- Channary names the pin. "He kept it eleven years. He wrote to me that he held your daughter."
- The widow offers a charter, double the toll, and the guild's protection. Thida asks why her. Anan named her in his letter, and a ferry on charter is the one vessel the gaugemen may inspect but not seize from: neutrality is Channary's cover. "And because you cannot be bribed by water." Anan wrote because Oluwa's steward had traced him to the Salt Marsh; he had weeks, not years.
- Channary cannot go herself: her household is watched, and the Lords have invited her son Rith, asleep behind the screen, to be "guested" at their court at Nine Sluices once the barge leaves, which she calls an honour
- Scene two: a pigeon brings word that the guild has frozen her berth. Kossi's work.
- Thida goes home and stands in Dara's doorway. She says yes aloud to an empty room.
Plant: forty days; Channary's want; the frozen berth.
Closing beat: she signs the charter with the blue guild-knot hanging loose from her wrist.

## Chapter 04: Provisions
Low Tamber, Day 1 to dawn of Day 2.
*Purpose: assemble the crew with comedy and friction before the road.*
- Bakary Vorn arrives, enormous, with a sack of rope and a refusal to explain. He counts the barge's ribs in sevens.
- The two haggle over his cut. He offers to work for nothing. She smells guilt.
- Sovann Pech is delivered by Channary's servants like a parcel, holding the jar in a basket of rice-husk.
- Ife stows away inside the eel-tank crate. Bakary wants to throw him over. Thida makes him pay in charts.
- They stock the hold: mudfish, rice, pickled lime, extra poles, a bladder of fresh water.
- Argument over who sleeps where; the priest sleeps on the jar.
- Scene two: dawn departure. Low Tamber's floating market watches without waving.
Plant: Bakary's missing fingers; Ife's coat.
Interlude: the river counts the weight of the boat.
Closing beat: Thida lets the first pole-stroke fall and the barge slides out of sight of the Steps.

## Chapter 05: Leaf-Gates
Nine Sluices lock-basin, Day 4.
*Purpose: a tense set piece at the dam; plant the one-pull.*
- Wind turns the palm-sail slack; they pole through four miles of dead water before the black bulk of the sluices rises.
- Thida, who hasn't been here since the Closing, goes grey at the first gate's hum.
- Gaugemen inspect every boat. Thida hides the jar in the eel-tank, under a layer of live eels.
- Bakary is silent and keeps his eyes on his boots. Ife narrates, "The gates are forty feet and the guards are four feet from asking."
- A gaugeman demands the priest's name; Sovann lies badly and gets away with it by crying.
- Inside the basin, Thida sees the Fifth Sluice's rope. Tied to it is a wright's double-sevens knot. Bakary notices her notice.
- Bakary, hauling the lock-chain, mutters that the wheel-house at the Fifth takes a man's whole weight to turn, and that gates can be lifted by stages if someone holds the wheel. He stops talking.
- Locked through at dusk, with the water rising behind them like a held breath.
Plant: wright-knot; Bakary's flinch; the hum.
Closing beat: from upstream, she looks back and sees a grey-sashed skiff slip into the basin behind them.

## Chapter 06: Salt Line
Pesh Alo village, mile 68, Day 7.
*Purpose: show what the dams are doing, and the price of a sip.*
- Pesh Alo's orchard rows are white-crusted. Villagers boil seawater out of their wells.
- A widow, Fanta, trades fish for medicine. She shows them a drowned willow whose roots have turned to salt.
- A licensed dreamer sells dream-sips. Thida refuses. Ife pretends to refuse, drinks one at night.
- At dawn Ife cannot say his mother's name. He is delighted with a drowned fisher's memory of a storm; the priest explains the ledger.
- Thida wants to hit the boy and instead cooks him eggs.
- Bakary quietly pays Fanta's well-man to dig deeper.
- Ife's gaugeman coat: Thida notices it fits too well.
Plant: the ledger rule; Ife's lost name; his coat.
Closing beat: Ife asks for his mother's name. Thida says, "Ask me in the morning," and doesn't know it either.

## Chapter 07: Low Water
The Hen's Ladder shoals, Day 10.
*Purpose: physical comedy and a quiet talk about the dead.*
- Long Patience grounds on a sandbar in the dry channel; the crew spend a day hauling her over with villagers and bamboo rollers.
- Ife's chart saves them from three false channels and he is insufferable about it.
- Bakary and Thida talk at night about Ibou. He says Ibou asked him once to stay out of river work. He doesn't say why. Later, asleep, Bakary counts pins aloud to nine and stops at seven.
- The priest tries to teach Thida the lullaby of the drowned. She walks away.
- A gaugeman's skiff sits in the reeds a mile back.
- Ife sneaks off to the bank at night to leave a mark on a post.
- Thida catches the priest watering the jar with river-wine; he claims it eases the memory.
Plant: Ife's mark; Bakary's evasions.
Closing beat: the jar, in the dark, hums one note. Sovann says it is only the heat.

## Chapter 08: The Guild at Ketu Bend
Ketu Bend guild-hall, Day 13.
*Purpose: neutrality tested; Kossi's claim on Thida's past.*
- Guildmother Yaye Ndoye receives them in a hall of mooring-knots and rings. She refuses sanctuary: neutrality.
- Thida argues in vain. A guild-sister, Nimol, passes her a night-pass.
- Kossi arrives, polite. He calls on Thida alone and tells her the Lords hold her signature on the inquiry.
- She did sign. She tells him she wanted to go home.
- He says he lost his mother in the Closing too, and has done every job since to keep that from happening again.
- Yaye asks whether the guild may "lose track" of Thida. A deal: the guild looks away for one night.
- Night: they slip out through the old tow-canal.
Plant: the signature; Kossi's mother.
Interlude: the river asks, "Why did you sign?"
Closing beat: the guild-hall's lamps go out one by one behind them, a decision made by lamplight.

## Chapter 09: Dry Thunder
Wat Samre, half-drowned temple, mile 150, Day 15.
*Purpose: quiet and uneasy talk; reveal Sovann's bribe.*
- A storm of heat-lightning and no rain drives them into the temple, its lower hall full of water.
- At midnight the water in the lower hall drops a yard in an hour, as if a plug were pulled: the Lid breathing. Thida and Bakary haul the barge off the temple steps before it grounds. Bakary says only "Someone upstream has a hand on this."
- Sovann tells how a Drawing is done and how the jar sours: the memory dulls from the edges.
- The jar's wax shows a hairline craze; he says it will last.
- Thida, sleepless, finds the priest by a shrine and he confesses: eleven years ago, on the Closing night, he drew a dying gate-hand who had seen the Warden signal the drop, and a steward of Oluwa Sen bought both the jar and his silence with a purse.
- Thida wants to throw him in the water. Bakary holds her back.
- Ife listens from the roof and writes something in his chart.
- The rain doesn't come.
Plant: craze in the wax; Sovann's purse; Ife's secret report.
Closing beat: Thida lays her palm on the temple's drowned floor and does not drink.

## Chapter 10: Market on the Water
Pa Tomloy floating market, mile 175, Day 17.
*Purpose: a bright set piece, a sharp turn.*
- Pa Tomloy: a thousand moored boats trading in crowded lanes; rice-wine, lanterns, eels.
- Thida hunts a new tiller-pin; Ife haggles and wins.
- Bakary is recognised by an old wright, who calls him "the Pin-Man". He laughs it off.
- Thida eavesdrops and hears nothing. Bakary changes the subject at dinner.
- A courier brings Channary's pigeon note: "Do not stop. They know your route." The courier wears a grey sash under his cloak, and the note's wax is not Channary's usual red: a clue that her post passes through the Lords' hands.
- Ife is away for two hours, returns with a bandaged wrist and a stolen bag of figs.
- Night: a fire on the market's edge. Gaugemen are searching boats.
- Memorable image: lantern-lit eel-sellers hanging paper fish over their stalls, so the whole lane glitters like a shoal. Comedy: Ife sells Sovann a "blessed" fig.
Plant: "Pin-Man"; Ife's absence.
Closing beat: Thida snuffs her lantern and the whole market, boat by boat, goes dark.

## Chapter 11: What the Jar Holds
The channel above Pa Tomloy, Day 18.
*Purpose: midpoint reversal. The truth about Bakary, and what is in the jar.*
- A gaugemen skiff rams *Long Patience* at dawn; the jar falls and cracks.
- A pole-fight; Bakary holds the skiff off with a boathook; Ife bails.
- The crack leaks. Through it, faint, Thida hears a child hum. She knows the cost (Sovann has told her) and still cups the leak in her scarred palm and drinks, to keep Dara from running into the river unheard. Her choice, not an accident.
- She sees it: the basin, the king's barge, a hand on the rope with a wright's double-sevens knot, Bakary young, teaching Anan.
- Then a smaller memory: a gap-toothed girl, singing.
- She comes to on the deck. The gauge-men have gone. She tries to hum the lullaby. She cannot find it.
- Bakary has not moved. He says, "Say it plain, Thi." She says, "You built it."
Payoff: the knot; the pin.
Interlude: the river says, "You asked."
Closing beat: Thida takes the pin from her belt and holds it out to Bakary like a blade.

## Chapter 12: The Pin-Man
A backwater, miles 185 to 200, Days 19 to 20.
*Purpose: confrontation; decide whether to keep going with him.*
- Thida puts Bakary ashore. He stands in the mud and doesn't plead.
- Ife argues for him: he can read a sluice. Sovann argues too. She ignores them.
- Bakary tells it plain: Oluwa asked for an emergency release against a flood. He built the one-pull. He taught Anan. He did not know. Asked if the rig can be undone, he says the one-pull is built to fall in one pull and rise in seven, by hand at the Fifth wheel, never tried; Sovann adds that the Assembly can order a staged release, "seven breaths", if a drinker testifies.
- He walked away that day. He has not built anything since.
- Thida asks him why he came. "To carry something heavier than rope."
- Thida cannot forgive him. She needs a wright at the Lid. She takes him back.
- Night: she sits at the tiller trying to remember the lullaby.
- Ife cries in his sleep and Bakary wakes first.
Plant: Bakary will be needed at the sluices.
Closing beat: Thida hums the first three notes, then silence where the fourth should be.

## Chapter 13: The Stair of Oru
The rapids at mile 205, Days 22 to 24.
*Purpose: a physical set piece and a confession.*
- The Stair is eleven shelves of white water; barges must be hauled by rope-teams from the cliffs.
- Villagers of Oru sell hauling, at a high price and in advance. Thida pays with her own boat-lamp.
- The haul: forty people, a thousand feet of rope, the barge lifted shelf by shelf. Ife's chart is wrong; a shelf has shifted.
- Bakary takes a rope-end and holds the barge by hand when a line parts, losing the nail of a third finger.
- At the top, Thida tells them that she signed the inquiry.
- Sovann, shaken, says the Drawing may fail in the last days.
- Ife says nothing and looks at the water.
Plant: day count slipping to 24; Ife's silence.
Closing beat: Bakary kneels to wash his hand in the river, and Thida, for the first time, hands him her cloth.

## Chapter 14: Ash Reach
Stubble-country above the Stair, Day 27.
*Purpose: moral choice; neutrality broken.*
- The valleys are burned. Gaugemen have fired the villages that sold hay to the barge.
- A line of families wait at a burned jetty, children first.
- Thida breaks guild law by ferrying forty across to the unburnt far bank in two crossings, losing half a day.
- Sovann is hit by an arrow while helping a child aboard.
- Kossi arrives with six skiffs and finds Thida with children; he lets her pass, saying the order covers "a jar" only.
- She cuts her guild-knot with a knife and drops it in the water.
- Sovann, bleeding, says, "Forgive me. I am dying, and it is worse than that."
- Image: a burned fig tree holding one unburnt branch, and a child asleep under it.
Plant: Kossi's mercy; the Setting.
Closing beat: the blue knot sinks, tugged out of sight, and a heron lifts from the reeds.

## Chapter 15: A Drawer's Last Rite
A burned jetty and the barge, Day 28.
*Purpose: Sovann's death and the lesson of the Setting.*
- Sovann lies on the deck. He teaches Thida the Setting words: three lines, one gesture, a pool.
- He tells her what it costs to testify: the drinker must keep the memory through the telling, and the river keeps accounts.
- He gives her his own wrist-cord, a priest's grey.
- Thida asks if the river listens. He says it answers; he just did not like the answer.
- The refugees are landed at a far-bank village that will take them. Thida finds Sovann's tuak flask holding only river water: he had stopped drinking and said nothing.
- Sovann dies at dusk. They give him to the water.
- Thida carries the empty bed to the stern and sits there.
- Ife can no longer meet her eyes.
Payoff: the bribe's redemption.
Interlude: the river remembers him.
Closing beat: Thida, who has not drunk in eleven years, scoops a handful of river and lets it run out between her fingers.

## Chapter 16: What Ife Sold
The barge, miles 240 to 250, Day 29.
*Purpose: Ife's betrayal exposed.*
- Thida finds Ife's chart notes; each night's position written in code.
- Confronted, Ife breaks: Kossi's men hold Mey at the Lid camp. He has reported every stop since Nine Sluices.
- Bakary is for sending him ashore. Thida says no.
- Ife swears he did not tell about the Setting. He says Kossi has a hundred men at the Lid.
- Thida decides to use him: send a false position.
- Ife's plan: a charted route that doesn't exist, a back-channel through the reeds.
- Night: Thida tells him about Dara's pin and his mother's name.
- He is lent the pin to hold.
- Comic relief: Bakary tries to cook and burns the rice; Ife writes "famine" on the chart beside it.
- Image: the reed-maze at dusk, every stalk bent the same way by wind that is not there.
Plant: the back-channel.
Closing beat: Ife draws a line on a blank page, and writes his mother's name at its end.

## Chapter 17: The Lid
The Lid dam camp, mile 265, Days 31 to 32.
*Purpose: low point.*
- The Lid: a wall of mud and stone, two hundred feet high, a camp of tents on the spillway.
- The back-channel works, then fails: Channary herself stands at the camp, unbound, among gaugemen.
- She admits her bargain with Oluwa Sen: he will confirm the old king's murder as Prince Dumo's, charge it to a dead man, and secure Rith's claim, but the dams stay. Her son is already in his house. The grey-sashed courier of ch10 was hers to answer for.
- Thida refuses. Channary orders the jar taken.
- Kossi seizes the jar and the party; Bakary fights and is clubbed.
- Mey is brought out, safe, and Ife is released to her.
- Thida is jailed in a wooden gauge-house above the water.
- She hears the Lid hum all night, a note she can almost remember.
Plant: the gauge-house's brass wheels.
Closing beat: through the plank-floor, she sees the river far below, black and waiting.

## Chapter 18: Lord Oluwa's Table
The Warden's pavilion, Day 32.
*Purpose: the antagonist's case; Kossi's turn.*
- Oluwa Sen has her brought tea. He apologises for the discomfort.
- He asks her what she remembers of the Great Flood. She doesn't. He tells her about his grandparents, his village, a night of drowning.
- He admits the dams are killing the delta slowly. He says slow is better than sudden.
- He offers her a bargain: return home, her berth restored, her silence kept.
- She asks what he did on the Closing. "I saved the lower delta from a king who would have loosed it." He does not say how. Of Channary he says only, kindly, that the boy Rith will be "raised among Wardens, and very comfortable".
- Kossi stands guard. Oluwa says "Marshal, escort her." Kossi's hand shakes on the rod.
- In the dark, Kossi unlocks the cell and says he stood guard the day Thida signed the inquiry, watched her weep over a paper she could not read, and has known since then it was a lie.
Payoff: Kossi's mother.
Closing beat: Kossi gives her back the pin and says, "I never ordered the gate."

## Chapter 19: Night of the Pins
The gauge-house and the Lid, Day 33.
*Purpose: escape set piece; Bakary's wrench.*
- Kossi gets the jar; Ife and Mey hide in the chart-room.
- Bakary, free, leads them through the machinery under the spillway.
- A gaugeman captain notices; a fight in the dark among iron wheels.
- Bakary blocks the one-pull with a pole and his own body to give them time.
- They run across the dam in the open, a half-mile of mud under searchlights of oil.
- Channary stops them at the end. Thida shows her the pin and repeats Oluwa's words about Rith: raised among Wardens, comfortable. Channary understands the bargain buys her son's cage. She lets them go and weeps.
- Kossi stays behind to hold the gate; the boat slips out under the Lid.
- Image: the iron wheels turning in lamplight, each tooth a hand's width, and the great gates humming above them like a held note.
Plant: Bakary's bruises; the pole.
Interlude: the river says, "Closer."
Closing beat: dawn, mile 270, and the shrine's pale weir seen from the water.

## Chapter 20: The Mother Gate
The Mother Gate, mile 272, Day 35.
*Purpose: climax; the Setting and the testimony.*
- The last mile is on foot, with Mey carried; the jar's seal is half powder.
- At the pool, the Assembly sits: headmen and guildmothers, among them Yaye.
- Thida performs the Setting with Sovann's words; the water goes grey and then clear.
- She drinks a full draught to testify. She sees the Closing from Anan's eyes. Dara's last memory is there too and the river does not hand it over: it stays in the pool, set.
- The river takes her craft: every channel, every depth, every name of the dead she has carried.
- She testifies. Oluwa Sen arrives under guard and denies it; the pool's water rises at his feet.
- Channary stands and gives her own testimony, with the bargain.
- The Assembly orders the dams opened, and Thida names her terms: evacuate first.
Payoff: the pin; Sovann's words; Ife's chart.
Closing beat: Thida looks at the pool and cannot recall the way she came.

## Chapter 21: Water Let Go
The lower river, Days 35 to 42.
*Purpose: resolution, the price.*
- The guild fleet launches downriver to clear stilt-houses before the release.
- Thida, who has lost her craft, steers by Ife's charts; he reads her the channels aloud.
- At the Lid, Kossi opens the first gates. At Nine Sluices, the Fifth jams.
- Bakary climbs the wheel-house and pulls the one-pull in reverse. The counterweight takes him.
- The Seven Breaths run through the channels, each higher than the last.
- Low Tamber's floating market breaks free and drifts out. Four die.
- Thida and Ife carry people to the high ground.
- On the final night she gives Bakary to the water at Nine Sluices.
- Scene two: in the aftermath Ife steers by his own charts while Thida sits blind to the channels, learning to trust the boy.
Payoff: the one-pull; the knot.
Interlude: the river speaks of weight.
Closing beat: the sea reaches the Salt Line and, for the first time in years, withdraws.

## Chapter 22: What the River Gave
Low Tamber and the Drowning Steps, Day 60.
*Purpose: the emotional landing.*
- The rains arrive. Low Tamber is rebuilt on stilts higher than before.
- Oluwa Sen is stripped of office and sits at the Steps, watching the water.
- Channary comes with Rith to ask the ferry for the dead; Thida refuses to take her side again.
- Thida carries Bakary's tin to his sister at Pesh Alo, mile 68, where the well he paid for now runs sweet.
- Ife is her chartmaker now, with Mey sleeping on deck.
- That night, on the quiet river, Thida finally drinks.
- The river gives her what the pool held: Dara's last memory, the surge, a stranger's arms, the lullaby.
- She sings it to the end, every word, the first time in eleven years.
- Comedy: Mey reads the fortune of every goat in Low Tamber and is never wrong.
Payoff: the lullaby, the pin.
Closing beat: Thida lays the egret pin on the water, and it does not sink.
