# The Ninth Floor: chapter outline

Every chapter is narrated by Ruth Szymanski in first person, past tense. "Viewpoint" is therefore always Ruth, so each entry gives place and time instead. Each chapter must carry about 2075 words: at least two scenes, a turn, and a closing beat from inside the story. Opening images are all different; do not repeat them.

### The solution (for writers only)

Ambrose Quill, trustee of the Calder Estate Trust, killed Declan Maher on the ninth floor on the night of 4/5 November 1952. He meant to put Maher to sleep with chloral hydrate from his own prescription in his rye, search him and the room for the original *Esther Vane* manifest, and make him talk when he woke. Maher, who had a weak heart and four whiskies in him, died. Quill and Lambert moved the body to 807 and Quill told Teddy there was no ninth floor. Quill killed Lambert on 11 November when Lambert tried to confess. He framed Teddy with a brown bottle bought on the same prescription, and got a confession from him by threatening Bronia. The manifest, with thirty-one "cases, personal effects" signed for the Estate by A. Quill in green-black ink (the Estate's insurers require the Trustee's own signature, and Quill cannot falsify a receipt), is behind the Certificate of Inspection in the cab.

### The clues, and where they are planted

1. **Two glasses on the ninth, none in 807** (Teddy, ch. 1; two clean rings in the dust, ch. 14).
2. **Shoes off on nine, shoes on in 807; the hole in the left sock heel** (Teddy, ch. 1; the property envelope, ch. 3).
3. **No dent in the bedspread** (Pearl, ch. 2).
4. **The certificate says "Landings served: 9"** (ch. 2; Pomeroy's ledger confirms, ch. 15).
5. **Maher read the certificate "a good minute" and called it "the only honest thing in the building"** (Lambert, ch. 2; recalled, ch. 24).
6. **Quill clears his throat twice before speaking** (ch. 2; the dry voice on nine, ch. 7; his tea, ch. 12).
7. **The cane's tip is perfect, not worn on one side** (ch. 2; noted again ch. 12; deduced, ch. 22–23).
8. **Quill's green-black ink dries rust-brown** (the visitor's pass, ch. 2; the hatband note, ch. 16; the manifest signature, ch. 24).
9. **The word "Mezzanine"** (Quill, ch. 12; Maher's note says "Nine", Quill says "Mezzanine" when he forgets himself, ch. 12 and ch. 26).
10. **The brass dial over Quill's door** (ch. 12; the needle moves to the blank on Ruth's visit, ch. 14; his arrival in the lobby, ch. 24).
11. **The dark red quilted dressing gown on the back of the door in 806** (ch. 12; Yvette's tall old man with a tray, ch. 18).
12. **Quill's alibi**: left the party at 11:50, in 806, call to Santa Barbara 2:05 a.m. logged by Teddy at the desk (ch. 13). Broken by the toll ticket (75 minutes) and Miss Calder's ten minutes (ch. 23).
13. **The brown bottle**: torn label, "Sorkin's" and "Rx 4187_ Oct 28" (ch. 20); Sorkin's book gives Rx 41872 to A. Quill (ch. 23).
14. **Quill's visit to Teddy in the cells at 4:05 p.m. on 20 November** (the visitors' log, ch. 22).
15. **The $500 envelope is Estate stationery, watermarked C.E.T.** (ch. 3; noticed, ch. 19).
16. **"Do Domu" in the book, and "Dom" meaning the Estate** (ch. 18).
17. **Plum says "Maher"** before the press knows the name (ch. 6).
18. **The key switch is cut out after Ruth's visit** (ch. 15): somebody saw the needle.

### Red herrings

- **Ferrante**: menace, motive (Maher bled him), orange peel on her desk. Alibi: forty witnesses at the Shape Hall. Cleared in ch. 17.
- **Plum**: motive, the slip, the pier. Cleared when he proves too frightened and too stupid (ch. 16).
- **Swain**: signs for the Estate, brings the $500. Cleared when he is seen to be afraid of Ferrante (ch. 16) and by the Pacific Room alibi (ch. 19).
- **Yvette Marchand**: in the building at 12:10, lied. Cleared by her account (ch. 18) and by Pearl.
- **Teddy**: found the body, kept the key, lied. Framed in ch. 20.
- **Dolan**: the Pier 6 gap suggests he is dirty. His real guilt is smaller and older.
- **Cordelia Plum**: a Calder cousin by marriage, so "the Estate" looks like her family.

### Costs

- **The key and the cab**: the car stays on the blank for as long as it stays up; the needle is a witness. Quill can read his own dial.
- **The book**: whoever holds it holds the names of three hundred and eleven people who can be deported. It cannot be used as evidence.
- **The carbon**: Ruth's leverage over Dolan costs her his friendship, and costs Dolan his career when she gives it up.
- **Teddy's confession**: it buys the safety of the names at the price of his life.

## Chapter 01: The Blank on the Dial

**Place and time**: Ruth's office (room 309, Selkirk Building, Cutter and Second), then a drugstore telephone on Cutter, then Front Street. Wednesday 5 November 1952, 7:40 a.m., rain.

**Purpose**: Hook the reader: a client says a body was found on a floor that does not exist, and a dead man has already chosen Ruth.

**Opens on**: the lobby radio at the Selkirk announcing Eisenhower's landslide, and a pink eviction notice on Ruth's door.

**Beats**:
- Ruth has $11.40, two months' rent owing, and a landlord's notice. Teddy Wozniak is waiting on the bench in the hall, wet, holding his hat in both hands. Present: Ruth, Teddy.
- Teddy tells it in order: he is night clerk at the Harbor Hotel. From ten to one the guest car had stopped answering the lobby buttons (Lambert said it was sulking, and the few late guests took the service lift). At ten past two Teddy saw the needle sitting on the blank past eight; at ten past four it was still there. He turned his key at the lobby panel, which brought the car down, and rode up. He found a man on a cot in a long low room, barefoot, with a hole in the heel of his left sock, and two glasses on a card table. By six the police had found the same man in room 807, shoes on, and Mr. Quill, the resident auditor, had told Teddy kindly that the Pacific Room party had run till two and a man on his feet for thirty hours sees things.
- Ruth tests him. How many buttons? Eight. How did he reach nine? "There is a key." He will not say whose, or what it turns. She notes his pillbox rattling.
- Teddy produces the blotter sheet from the desk: the dead guest, "Mr. Delaney", wrote Ruth's name and address on it in pencil. "He said you used to be on the police wire. He said you were honest." Ruth has never heard of a Delaney.
- She takes twenty dollars and tells him to go home and sleep. He says he has to stay near the cab.
- Ruth telephones Frank Dolan from the drugstore on Cutter. Dolan: "Natural. Heart. Room 807, where else would a man die in a hotel, Ruth?" She does not tell him about Teddy's version.
- She walks to Front Street and counts the hotel's floors from the opposite kerb: eight rows of windows, and above them the fourteen-foot terracotta Frieze of waves and fish. She counts again, and the second time includes the Frieze.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth takes the case and withholds it from her oldest friend. Closing beat: the second count, and the first low moan of the Heifer from Calder Point.

**Plants and payoffs**: plants the dial, the Frieze, the key, two glasses, the sock, the blotter, the pillbox, Dolan's dismissal; payoffs in ch. 2 (dial, plug), 3 (sock), 14 (glasses), 21 (pillbox), 24 (blotter).

## Chapter 02: Paint on the Brass

**Place and time**: the Harbor Hotel, lobby, elevator and eighth floor. Wednesday 5 November, 10:30 a.m. to about 1 p.m.

**Purpose**: Show the hotel's impossibility and meet its keepers: Quill's gentle denial, Pearl's precision, Lambert's warning.

**Opens on**: a man vacuuming confetti from the marble under a "PLUM FOR THE PEOPLE" banner, the lobby clock three minutes slow.

**Beats**:
- The lobby after the Councilman's party: crepe, a deflated balloon arch, the day clerk (Miss Dorrie Nash) chewing gum and refusing to say anything about Teddy except that he is "a lovely man for a shift". Present: Ruth, Dorrie, the man with the vacuum.
- Ambrose Quill receives her in a small office behind the desk. Cane, silver hair parted in the middle, pale hands, tea in thin cups. He is gracious and sorrowful: poor Teddy has had a long night; there is no ninth floor; the hotel has eight, "as the plans, the licence and the Board of Underwriters will tell you". He clears his throat twice before speaking. He signs her a visitor's pass in green-black ink that dries rust-brown, "like old blood", and says nothing in the building is locked to anyone who asks. Ruth notices the cane's rubber tip is as clean as a new pencil eraser, and that he carries the cane across his forearm and only taps it on the floor when someone is watching.
- She rides the elevator with Joe Lambert, the porter, who hums "Abide with Me". The cab: eight buttons (L, 2 to 8), the brass arc over the door with a needle and an unnumbered ninth stop, the framed Certificate of Inspection (she reads it while the car climbs: "Landings served: 9"; Lambert says the fellow in 807 read it for a good minute on Monday and called it "the only honest thing in the building").
- On the eighth floor, Pearl Hightower, head housekeeper, is stripping 807 with a maid. Pearl: the spread was smooth; a man who dies on a bed leaves a dent, and this man had been laid on it. "I do not guess, Miss Szymanski. I make beds." Pearl shuts the conversation like a drawer.
- The linen cupboard at the east end of the corridor has a padlock new enough to shine.
- Back in the cab alone, Ruth finds under the "8" button a round brass plug the size of a half dollar, painted the same cream as the panel. She presses it. The paint is wet.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth now has one physical fact that contradicts the hotel. Closing beat: the cream paint on her thumb, wet at eleven on a morning when nothing had ever been there to paint, and Lambert in the doorway saying quietly, "Don't, Miss."

**Plants and payoffs**: Quill's throat-clearing, ink, cane tip; the certificate; Pearl's dent; the linen cupboard; the plug. Payoffs: ch. 7 (voice), 12 (cane, throat), 16 (ink), 24 (certificate), 14 (cupboard and stair).

## Chapter 03: Property of the Deceased

**Place and time**: the county morgue at Municipal Hospital, then Ruth's office. Thursday 6 November, 9 a.m. and 3 p.m.

**Purpose**: Inciting incident. The dead man has a real name that touches Ruth's past, and someone pays $500 for her to stop.

**Opens on**: Orville Pugh peeling a pickled egg over a body bag.

**Beats**:
- Dolan, grudging, takes her to the morgue because "you called twice yesterday and a woman who calls twice is working". Dr. Ostrander's report says heart, alcohol. Present: Ruth, Dolan, Pugh.
- Pugh names his drawers for baseball clubs; the man in the Cardinals drawer is "Delaney". His hotel register was false: the Customs credential case in his coat gives him as Declan Maher, tally clerk (dismissed 1950). Pier 6 pass, wallet with $212, a pawn ticket. Ruth sees the property envelope: shoes with laces tied in a granny knot; the left sock has a hole at the heel. "His shoes are on." "Men wear shoes, Ruth."
- Ruth knows the name Maher and cannot find where. Dolan flinches at the word Pier 6. She does not tell him that Teddy found Maher barefoot.
- At three Ruth returns to the Selkirk and finds Gilbert Swain in her chair: hair oil, bay rum, anchor cufflinks, rhetorical questions. He represents Councilman Plum, who is distressed that a death at the hotel on his night "might be misconstrued". He offers $500 in an Estate envelope to prepare a report concluding that Mr. Wozniak was mistaken. Present: Ruth, Swain.
- Ruth, behind on rent, takes it, telling herself she can serve two clients until one asks for a lie. Swain: "Would I pay you five hundred dollars to look, Miss Szymanski, if there were anything to find?"
- After he leaves, Ruth opens the false bottom of her typewriter case and takes out the 1948 dispatch carbon. Among the entries for Sunday 14 March, at 11:14 p.m.: "Caller D. Maher, Customs tally, asks if cars will clear Pier 6 gate." Her own typing, four years old.

**What changes / closing beat**: A dead man chose her because he knew what she kept; and two clients now want opposite things. Closing beat: the name on the carbon in her own typing.

**Plants and payoffs**: the sock, the shoes, the envelope's watermark (C.E.T.), the carbon. Payoffs: ch. 12 (shoes), 19 (envelope), 16 and 28 (carbon).

## Chapter 04: Over the Bakery

**Place and time**: Teddy's flat above Sobczak's Bakery, 1131 Hake Street, and Ruth's mother's kitchen at 1127. Thursday 6 November, 8 p.m. to midnight.

**Purpose**: Teddy lies for the first time on the page, and Ruth discovers her mother knows his family.

**Opens on**: Mrs. Sobczak's ovens warming the stairwell wall at 8 p.m.

**Beats**:
- Teddy's two rooms: a crucifix, a narrow bed, a shelf of Polish books, a hot plate, and a photograph of a heavy man in a sexton's black coat beside a small woman with rimless spectacles. "My sister's husband. Dead." Present: Ruth, Teddy.
- Ruth says the dead man was Declan Maher, a Customs man. Teddy says he never saw him before Friday. She asks why Maher wrote her name on the blotter. Teddy says Maher asked on Monday whether he knew a private detective who was honest; he said no; Maher said, then I know one. She notices Teddy's hands squaring a pencil on a blotter that is already square.
- She asks again how he reached nine. "I will tell you when I know I can." The pillbox rattles. He asks her not to go near the cab, because Mr. Quill will take it as an insult.
- Ruth's mother Helena has supper waiting: cabbage rolls, a lecture on Eisenhower ("a general is a man who asks other men to die and then writes a book"). Present: Ruth, Helena.
- Helena, hearing Teddy's name: "Tadeusz Wozniak? Bronia Kowal's brother. Stefan Kowal, the sexton, who kept the St. Raphael box, God rest him. A saint with a temper." Ruth hasn't known any of it.
- At the kitchen window at 11:20 she can see over the roofs to Front Street. In the dark band above the hotel's eighth floor, where no floor was, a light moves left to right along the louvres and goes out.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth knows her client lied about knowing Maher and has a Polish church web behind him. Closing beat: the light in the Frieze and the Heifer's two notes.

**Plants and payoffs**: Stefan, Bronia, the St. Raphael box, Teddy's pencils, the light. Payoffs: ch. 9 (church), 13 (Teddy's confession), 14 (the floor).

## Chapter 05: The Empty Folder

**Place and time**: the Harbor Commission records room (Customs Annex, Front and Cutter) and Fong & Son, 14 Anchor Alley. Friday 7 November, 9:30 a.m. to 1 p.m.

**Purpose**: Establish the *Esther Vane* manifest as the missing thing, and give Ruth her best ally.

**Opens on**: Lucille Fong stamping a form at the counter and saying "Number, please" without looking up.

**Beats**:
- Lucille, Ruth's friend from the old switchboard (the wire room, 1941 to 1948), is Senior Clerk of Vessel Records. She and Ruth trade insults in the manner of two women who once shared a headset. Present: Ruth, Lucille, a junior clerk named Mr. Tibbets.
- Maher was a Customs tally clerk until September 1950, dismissed for "irregularities". Lucille pulls the Harbor Commission's file for the Liberty ship *Esther Vane*, March 1948: the arrival sheet lists seventy-one crew, of which thirty-one are noted "discharged ashore by mutual consent". Lucille: "A Liberty carries forty. Nobody loses thirty-one men. That's a mutiny or a lie." The sheet is typed on different paper from the rest of the folder.
- Lucille offers to pull the 1926 building permit for the Harbor Hotel: she can't show Ruth the plans in the room, but will bring a copy.
- Lucille takes Ruth to lunch at her father's shop. Walter Fong, tailor, 66, measures Ruth with his eyes before speaking. Present: Ruth, Lucille, Walter Fong.
- Fong remembers March 1948: a hurried order, thirty-one men fitted for sea in one night in a pier shed, all in cash, the buyer a man with a clerk's voice whom he did not look at. "In my trade you do not look at the man who pays cash for thirty-one coats." He keeps his order books.
- On the wall of the shop, a framed certificate in another surname. Lucille changes the subject too quickly.
- Outside, across Anchor Alley, a man in a snapped-brim hat peels an orange in one spiral and does not take his eyes off Ruth.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth has a named ship, a number (31), and someone watching. Closing beat: the orange peel dropping from the man's hand to the wet cobbles.

**Plants and payoffs**: the retyped sheet; the 31; Fong's order books; the orange; Fong's certificate (Lucille's secret). Payoffs: ch. 6 (Ferrante), 17 (the origin), 23 (Fong's book), 28 (Lucille).

## Chapter 06: Victory at the Elks

**Place and time**: Elks Lodge banquet room on Lamplighter Hill; then the Selkirk Building. Friday 7 November, 1:30 p.m. to 9 p.m.

**Purpose**: Meet Plum and Ferrante. Comedy and menace in one room.

**Opens on**: a four-foot sheet cake in the shape of City Hall, with a sparkler in the dome.

**Beats**:
- The Friends of the Waterfront victory luncheon. Swain has got Ruth in, "in your best hat". Plum is round and pink, dabbing his lip, introducing her to the room as "a daughter of the working classes and an alumna of our fine police family". The microphone howls. Present: Ruth, Plum, Swain, Cordelia Plum, two hundred guests.
- Cordelia, a Calder cousin by marriage, tells Ruth over the punch that Everett thinks the harbor is a pond he owns. Plum's speech about "a tragic incident on a night of joy"; afterwards, privately, he says "poor Maher" and, when Ruth echoes "Who?", corrects himself to "Delaney" too quickly. The Beacon has printed only the name Delaney.
- Aldo Ferrante, in the back, peels an orange. He comes over, courteous, and calls Ruth "Anton's girl": he carried her father's coffin. "Pier 6 is a bad place for a woman who sits in the dark." Present: Ruth, Ferrante.
- Back at the Selkirk at nine, the office door is unlocked. Files on the floor, her phone book gone. The typewriter case is open but untouched: the intruders did not look for a false bottom. On the desk a single orange peel in one long spiral.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth knows Plum knew Maher, and that the union has noticed her. Closing beat: the orange peel on the blotter, laid down carefully, "somebody who wanted me to know he'd been polite".

**Plants and payoffs**: Plum's slip; Ferrante's finger and orange; Cordelia as a Calder. Payoffs: ch. 10, 16, 17.

## Chapter 07: The Frieze

**Place and time**: Tommy's Lunch Counter across from the hotel, then the rear light well and fire escape of the Harbor, Ferry Slip Row. Saturday 8 November, 2 p.m. and 9 p.m., rain.

**Purpose**: Physical proof that the ninth floor exists and is being searched.

**Opens on**: a copy of the 1926 building permit unrolled on the lunch counter under a ketchup bottle.

**Beats**:
- Lucille brings the permit copy to Tommy's. Elevation: eight rows of windows under a cornice. Section: "Attic, tanks and machinery, 9'0" clear". Ruth measures: the Frieze is fourteen feet. Present: Ruth, Lucille, Tommy the counterman.
- Lucille's quiet warning: the hotel is owned by the Calder Estate and the pier too; she had to sign out the permit under a false reason. "Don't make me file for you again."
- 9 p.m.: the rear light well, rain on corrugated iron. The fire escape stops at eight. A rung ladder leads up to the foot of the Frieze. Ruth climbs it, counting rungs (eleven). Present: Ruth.
- A louvre in the terracotta has a scratched corner in the black paint. She looks in. A lantern on a card table; Joe Lambert's back; a second man in the dark who speaks dryly and clears his throat twice: "It isn't on him, it isn't in the room, and it isn't up here. Paper doesn't walk off, Joe. Somebody put it somewhere." Lambert: "Yes, sir." The dry voice: "Mind the stair."
- Her foot slips on wet iron; a rung rattles; the lantern goes out. She hangs by her hands while a door below shuts, then drops six feet into a bin of kitchen peelings. She counts to forty by the Heifer.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth has heard the ninth floor in use and two voices, one of them Lambert's. Closing beat: the potato peelings and the sound of a key turning inside the Frieze.

**Plants and payoffs**: the Frieze; the search for paper; the dry voice; Lambert's complicity. Payoffs: ch. 10 (Lambert's call), 12 (the voice at tea), 24 (the paper).

## Chapter 08: A Name Like Marchand

**Place and time**: the Cormorant Room, Brine Street. Saturday 8 November, 11:30 p.m. to 2 a.m.

**Purpose**: Introduce Yvette and give the ninth floor a human face before anyone sees it.

**Opens on**: a trumpet mute dropped on a stage and a blue spotlight on Yvette Marchand's white gloves.

**Beats**:
- Ruth, wet and smelling of peelings, is lent a towel by Deacon Tolliver at the piano, who says "Mm-hm" to everything. Yvette sings in French; on one word the Polish comes through. Present: Ruth, Deacon, Yvette, customers.
- In the dressing room Yvette denies knowing Maher. Ruth says, in her mother's kitchen Polish, "Co słychać?" Yvette flinches and covers it. "Marchand. Like people who sell things. Write it however you like, Miss Szymanski, the police do." She wants Ruth out.
- In the mirror frame, a snapshot: Yvette younger, on a low-ceilinged landing with a rail of child-high coat hooks behind her. Ruth studies the ceiling and the hooks.
- After the last set, Deacon walks Ruth to the stage door. He says Yvette left during the 11 o'clock set on Tuesday night and returned at ten past one with wet hair. "Everybody needs a walk, Miss Szymanski. Some folks walk farther." Present: Ruth, Deacon.
- A man in a snapped-brim hat follows Ruth out of the club. She loses him through the kitchen, a cook with a cleaver shouting at them both.
- Yvette is waiting under the awning. "The police do not need to know I walk." Ruth: "Who else knows?" Yvette says nothing and leaves.

**What changes / closing beat**: Yvette becomes a suspect and a mystery; Ruth knows she was out near the hotel. Closing beat: Yvette gone into the rain and one white cotton glove left hanging on the awning rope, dripping.

**Plants and payoffs**: the snapshot with the low ceiling and hooks; Yvette's time away; Deacon. Payoffs: ch. 14 (the floor matches the snapshot), 18 (Yvette's account).

## Chapter 09: High Mass

**Place and time**: St. Casimir's Church, Hake Street and Eleventh; the basement coffee hour; Ruth's kitchen. Sunday 9 November, 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m.

**Purpose**: Show the Polish community that will carry the book, and Teddy's sister.

**Opens on**: the bell and the smell of wet wool as Ruth is pulled by the sleeve up the nave.

**Beats**:
- Helena marches Ruth to the fourth pew. Father Dziedzic preaches on "the stranger within your gates" and sees Ruth in the pew and looks away. Across the aisle, Bronia Kowal in black, a mother-of-pearl rosary, a black handbag held on her lap with both hands; she takes a black book with a brass clasp from the bag for the Gospel. Present: Ruth, Helena, Bronia, Teddy (at the back), parishioners.
- Coffee hour in the basement: plum cake, gossip, three women arguing about Eisenhower's wife. Helena corners Bronia over the urn. Bronia to Ruth: "Teddy is a good boy. He is fifty-four and a good boy. Leave him be."
- Father Dziedzic, asked about the St. Raphael box, says it contains "a few dollars for travellers' Masses". Ruth: "Which travellers?" He mentions Stefan's "good works" and ends the conversation.
- Sunday dinner. Helena, over roast pork, remembers 1949, when the women of the parish whispered about a house for newcomers; "nobody said where, and nobody asked". Ruth puts it beside the child-high hooks in Yvette's snapshot and the light in the Frieze, and asks the question straight: was there a house? Helena gets up to see to the gravy and stays at the stove with her back turned. It is the first time Ruth has watched her mother not answer.
- Walking Helena up the stairs afterward, Ruth finds Teddy waiting in the rain at the street door. "Leave Bronia out." "Out of what?" He shakes his head and goes.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth suspects the ninth floor was a hiding place for people, and finds that her own mother and the whole parish close like a hand around the question. She has seen the book and met its keeper without knowing. Closing beat: Teddy walking away without his umbrella, and Ruth noticing he has put his pillbox hand in his pocket.

**Plants and payoffs**: Bronia's black book and clasp; the St. Raphael box; the rumour of 1949; "Leave Bronia out." Payoffs: ch. 13, 17, 18, 22, 28.

## Chapter 10: The Shape-Up

**Place and time**: the Shape Hall, Capstan Row, then Ruth's office, then a drugstore booth. Monday 10 November, 5:45 a.m., 2 p.m., 6 p.m.

**Purpose**: Ferrante's side of the story, and Lambert's call.

**Opens on**: three hundred men standing in a ring in fog, breath rising, waiting for a toothpick.

**Beats**:
- Ruth, in a man's overcoat, stands at the edge of the shape-up and watches Ferrante nod men into work with a toothpick in his mouth. He sees her; he has her brought in. Present: Ruth, Ferrante, two hall men.
- In his office, Ferrante peels an orange. He says Maher bled him for two years, came again last week for two thousand dollars. He claims to have been counting election returns in the hall from eight till three, with forty men and a radio. "Ask any of them. Ask the radio." He mentions her father: "Your father died on my pier. I paid for the funeral. Remember I carried it." He tells her to stay out of Pier 6.
- Two o'clock: Dolan at the Selkirk with doughnuts and a warning. Rafferty retires on the fifteenth and doesn't want noise. Dolan says the lab found nothing; Ruth: "Did anyone ask the lab to look?" He chews a matchstick. Present: Ruth, Dolan.
- Dolan's hand drifts toward the typewriter case as if he knows. They both pretend not to see.
- Six o'clock: a drugstore booth on Cutter. A telephone call at the booth, Lambert's voice: "Miss Szymanski, I'm the porter at the Harbor. I carried him, Miss. I can tell you. Tomorrow night, after the parade, down by the boiler, midnight." The line goes dead. The Heifer starts.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ferrante is not the killer; Ruth now has an inside witness. Closing beat: the dial tone and the fog bell.

**Plants and payoffs**: Maher's blackmail; Ferrante's alibi; Dolan and the carbon; Lambert's call. Payoffs: ch. 11, 12, 17, 22.

## Chapter 11: Armistice Night

**Place and time**: Front Street during the parade; Tommy's counter; the Harbor Hotel basement and lobby. Tuesday 11 November, 11 a.m. and 11 p.m.

**Purpose**: The second body: set piece in the dark with the elevator.

**Opens on**: a drum corps in the rain, a one-armed veteran in a garrison cap holding an umbrella with his one good hand over a boy.

**Beats**:
- The parade down Front Street with Lucille under one umbrella: Legionnaires, Girl Scouts, a Sea Scout band out of tune. Lucille tells Ruth the Commission's Esther Vane sheet has vanished from its shelf overnight. Present: Ruth, Lucille.
- Dolan passes in a squad car and does not look.
- 11:30 p.m.: fog. The hotel's service entrance on Ferry Slip Row. Teddy lets Ruth in; he has the desk. Pearl is not at her post. Present: Ruth, Teddy.
- The boiler room in the basement: Lambert's lantern is lit, his thermos open, his cap on the floor, no Lambert. The car at the lobby has a hand-lettered sign: OUT OF ORDER. The cab is stuck between 3 and 4.
- Ruth and Teddy open the hoistway door at the basement and look up and down: the pit, seven feet below, lantern light on a pair of shoes pointing up. Ruth counts them, two. They are shoes of a man lying on his back.
- Ruth goes down the pit ladder. Lambert is dead, his head turned wrong. A heavy brass oiler beside him. She takes the service key on its shoelace from his neck without knowing why.

**What changes / closing beat**: the only inside witness is dead and Ruth has his key. Closing beat: the key warm from her pocket, and Teddy overhead, saying "Joe" over and over in the dark.

**Plants and payoffs**: Lambert's key; the oiler; the stalled cab. Payoffs: ch. 12 (hands), 14 (key), 22 (the oiler as weapon).

## Chapter 12: The Pit

**Place and time**: the Harbor basement and lobby; room 806. Wednesday 12 November, 12:20 a.m. to 10 a.m.

**Purpose**: Make Quill the best-mannered man in the case.

**Opens on**: a policeman's flashlight swinging across the pit wall, and a grey rat that does not run.

**Beats**:
- Dolan and Karras arrive at 12:20. Karras measures, writes, and is polite to Teddy. Dr. Ostrander, called from his bed, says a fall. Ruth, looking at Lambert's hands: they have no grease on them, though the oiler beside him is full of it. Present: Ruth, Dolan, Karras, Ostrander, Teddy.
- Dolan: "You were here at midnight. Why?" Ruth lies: a friend of Mr. Wozniak asked her to look at the cab. Dolan doesn't believe her. Karras notes it.
- Quill comes down in the small hours in his quilted dressing gown, a single tear on his cheek, the cane over his arm like an umbrella. He asks to speak to Ruth in the morning.
- 9 a.m., room 806: tea in thin cups, a view of the sound. A brass dial over the door, a sister of the one in the cab, and beneath it a small brass toggle under a glass cap, like a fire-alarm switch. A dark red quilted dressing gown on the hook. ## Chapter 13: What Teddy Left Out

**Place and time**: Teddy's flat over the bakery; the Hake Street stairwell. Wednesday 12 November, 7 p.m. to 11 p.m.

**Purpose**: Teddy's confession of the shelter; the first half of the midpoint turn.

**Beats**:

## Chapter 14: A Floor Nobody Built

**Place and time**: the Harbor Hotel elevator, the ninth floor and the hidden stair. Thursday 13 November, 1 a.m. to 3 a.m.

**Purpose**: Midpoint reversal. The ninth floor is not a murder scene; it is a sanctuary, and Ruth's job changes.

**Opens on**: a brass key that fits a square hole nobody has painted yet in the dark.

**Beats**:
- 1 a.m., the lobby empty, Teddy absent from the desk. Ruth rides alone, lifts the plug with her thumbnail, inserts Lambert's key, presses 8, counts three, and the car climbs the extra six seconds; the needle swings onto the blank. The door opens on fourteen paces of a low corridor. Present: Ruth.
- The floor: eight-foot-six ceiling, forty-two iron cots, a rail of child-height coat hooks, a kitchenette, a copper laundry boiler, a wall telephone without a dial. In the stair-head, a wall of chalked first names with dates: Irena 21/10/49, Wacław, Halina, three small names under a drawing of a ship. A calendar of saints' days. A child's pencil drawing of a train. The snapshot's landing matches. She stands for a long time.

Her question changes from "who killed a stranger" to "who is protecting what". Closing beat: Pearl's key ring held silent in her fist at the service door.

**Plants and payoffs**: the floor, the circles in the dust, the hidden stair, the safe. Payoffs: ch. 15, 18, 26, 27.

## Chapter 17: Fish Market, Before Light

**Place and time**: the municipal fish market, Front Street pier; Bronia's flat on Eleventh. Sunday 16 November, 4 a.m. and 11 a.m.

**Purpose**: Ferrante's account of the racket's origin; the book changes hands.

**Opens on**: crushed ice, gulls and the steel table where Ferrante cleans a cod.

**Beats**:

## Chapter 18: Pod Twoją Obronę

## Chapter 22: Requiem for a Night Clerk

## Chapter 26: The Safe in the Card Room

**Place and time**: the ninth floor. Wednesday 26 November, 7:05 p.m. to 7:45 p.m.

**Purpose**: Confrontation, with the fewest words from the villain.

**Opens on**: the smell of lamp oil in a closed space.

**Beats**:
- Ruth climbs the hidden stair by flashlight and crosses the empty dormitories first, past the cots, the copper and the chalk wall, with the wall telephone dead (its wire cut that afternoon). Quill is on the ninth in his dinner jacket, in the Calder Club, with the Prohibition safe open and a green ledger on a baize table. A single lantern. A can of lamp oil by his foot. The cane across his knees. Present: Ruth, Quill.
- "My dear Miss Szymanski." He clears his throat twice. "It was the Mezzanine, in my day." He says almost nothing else about the dead. She tells him what she knows: the open line, the chloral, the ink, the tray, the shoes. He answers every one with a single sentence: "Conjecture." "Hearsay." "Possible." He does not explain.
- She asks about the thirty-one cases. He says, "I invoiced them." She asks about Teddy. He says, "The Estate has outlived four generations of Calders. It can outlive a night clerk." That is the nearest thing he gives to a motive.
- The Heifer sounds; Ruth uses its forty-second gap as a clock. She says Dolan is outside. Quill says he doubts it. She says the manifest is in the District Attorney's safe. He says nothing for several seconds. Then he picks up the can.
- He pours oil along the baize and the cots. She kicks the lantern toward him; it breaks and the baize catches. He steps through the stair door and drops an iron cot rail across it from the stair side. She hears the click of the bar, and his tread going down, unhurried. He had said, as he poured, that fire in a roof space is a thing one insures against, and that the brigade would be at the cornice in six minutes.
- Ruth grabs the green ledger from the table, shoves it inside her coat, and finds the stair door will not move. She tries the safe, the laundry copper, the kitchenette window, the dead telephone. The cots catch. The chalk wall begins to blacken.

**What changes / closing beat**: the villain leaves her to burn and takes his silence with him. Closing beat: the sound of the cane's tip tapping evenly down the stair, fourteen steps, no limp.

**Plants and payoffs**: payoff of the cane, the throat, the ledger; Quill's few words. Payoffs: ch. 27–28.

## Chapter 27: The Cornice

**Place and time**: the Frieze, the hotel's rear light well and fire escape, Ferry Slip Row. Wednesday 26 November, 7:45 p.m. to 9 p.m., fog.

**Purpose**: Escape and capture.

**Opens on**: black paint blistering on a louvre.

**Beats**:
- Ruth kicks the louvre out of its frame. The Frieze's ledge is two feet wide, wet terracotta with fish on it. Below is the eight-floor drop. She goes out sideways, ledger inside her coat, burning her hands on the louvre, counting terracotta fish, the fog thick enough to lean on.
- Behind her the fire finds the oil. The Frieze lights from inside like a lantern.
- She works along to the east corner and the top of the fire escape, where the rung ladder starts. Her hands are blistered; she goes down it with her forearms. Present: Ruth.
- Inside the hotel, Pearl rings the fire bell and empties the floors in order of her own, ninth guests first, though none on nine. Present: Pearl, guests, Lucille (who arrives from home with a coat).
- Quill, by the service door onto the light well, tries to leave unhurried. At the bottom of the fire escape Ruth drops to her feet in front of him. He raises the cane, as if to tip a hat, and then, seeing her face, lowers it.
- Dolan's voice from the alley: "Mr. Quill. I count nine." Karras's cuffs. Quill: "The plans say eight, Sergeant." Dolan: "Plans lie." The cane tip lies on the wet cobble, flawless.
- On Front Street, the fire brigade pumps water on the Frieze. As the louvres glow and fall, the names go too.

**What changes / closing beat**: Quill is taken; the chalk wall is burned. Ruth is glad of the second and ashamed of being glad. Closing beat: ash falling like paper snow on Front Street.

**Plants and payoffs**: ledger, hands, the Frieze. Payoffs: ch. 28.

## Chapter 28: What the Keeper Keeps

**Place and time**: Ruth's kitchen; Central Station steps; Union Station; St. Casimir's; the roof of 1127 Hake; Front Street. Friday 28 November to Sunday 30 November (First Sunday of Advent), with a coda a week later.

**Purpose**: Resolution at a cost.

**Opens on**: Ruth's bandaged hands holding a fork across a plate at Helena's table.

**Beats**:
- Friday: Dolan hands the DA the carbon in a sealed envelope; Rafferty is to be served; Dolan will be dismissed by Christmas. He and Ruth sit on the steps of Central Station and run the roll call: Quill held for Maher, Lambert and fraud, and the Beacon prints a retraction, "Wozniak Cleared"; Swain turns state's evidence and names the two hall men he hired at Quill's word; Plum resigns; Ferrante is indicted; Pomeroy returns his hams. Ruth's licence is reinstated on a letter from the Board. "Didn't you want to be a lieutenant?" "I wanted to be somebody I could stand." Present: Ruth, Dolan.
- Saturday: Union Station, the night train north. Yvette, in a new coat and no gloves, with a new name she will not tell. She hugs Helena. She gives Ruth her stage gloves. Lucille on the platform, quietly: "A paper son's daughter would keep it. I'm only saying." Present: Ruth, Helena, Yvette, Lucille.
- Sunday: after Mass Dolan gives Bronia the tin pillbox from the property bag at the church door, and she holds it without speaking. Bronia takes Ruth's wrists: "A book wants a keeper, not a shelf." Ruth says she will put it where it cannot be found. Odessa Lambert is waiting by the gate; Ruth gives her Joe's key on its shoelace and tells her all of it; Odessa says "He hummed 'Abide with Me' for six years straight, and I never once asked him why." Present: Ruth, Bronia, Helena, Dolan, Odessa.
- That night Ruth takes the book to the roof of 1127 Hake and, in the light of the Heifer's lighthouse, reads the first page, Stefan's: "Pod Twoją Obronę." She counts the names. She stops counting. She begins again.
- Coda, a week later: the State Division of Elevators makes the Harbor correct its certificate and fit a ninth brass button marked 9 under the 8, for a charred floor with sky showing through the louvres. Pearl rides up to see it. Ruth stays on the sidewalk, looking up at the empty louvres, and does not count.

**What changes / closing beat**: Ruth keeps the book and gives the carbon; she is a keeper, not a hoarder. Closing beat: Ruth, on the sidewalk, no longer counting the floors.

**Plants and payoffs**: pays off the carbon (to Dolan), the book (to Ruth), the dial (the ninth button), the counting motif (stops), Lucille's secret, Yvette's gloves, Bronia, Teddy (the pillbox returned to Bronia by Dolan), Lambert (the key to his widow), the hall men (Swain).
