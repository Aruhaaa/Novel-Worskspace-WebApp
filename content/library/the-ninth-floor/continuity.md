# Continuity log: The Ninth Floor

Each chapter's writer appends the facts it establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: The Blank on the Dial

- Wed 5 Nov 1952, rain. Ruth owes $84 on a pink pay-or-quit notice tacked across her door (309, Selkirk); she had $11.40, now $31.40 after taking $20 (a ten and two fives) from Teddy. The Selkirk elevator is out (card on the gate); 52 steps up counting half-landings. Her office phone is a party line with a dentist and a corset-seller, so she phones from a drugstore booth on Cutter. Fee: $15 a day plus expenses. She takes notes on the back of the pink notice.
- Teddy: maroon jacket, nameplate T. WOZNIAK, gold tooth top left, moth-wing eyebrows, tin pillbox rattling in his waistcoat pocket; night clerk 11 to 7, 13 years at the desk, 21 in the building. He says: guest car stopped answering at ten to one (Lambert said it was "sulking", guests used the service lift); needle on the blank at 2:10 and still at 4:10 by the lobby clock, which runs three minutes slow; at 4:10 he turned his key at the lobby panel to bring the car down, then rode up. He will not say whose key or what it turns ("There is a key").
- Teddy's account of nine: a low corridor, a long low room, a man on a cot in stocking feet, no shoes seen, hole in the heel of the left sock, two empty tumblers on a card table. He phoned Mr. Quill, was told to wait at the desk; a little before six the police went to 807 and found "Delaney of Portland, dried fruit" (registered since Friday) on the bed, shoes on, spread over his legs; a doctor's man said heart.
- Quill, "the resident auditor" with a room on eight, gave Teddy tea in the office behind the desk and said the Pacific Room party ran till two and a man awake thirty hours sees things. Teddy calls him "a gentleman". Ruth has not met Quill. Teddy says Quill also holds that the Harbor has eight floors.
- Ruth holds the desk-blotter top sheet: Delaney wrote "Ruth Szymanski. Selkirk Bldg., Cutter & Second. Rm. 309." in pencil on Monday night, 3 Nov (clerk's hand, crossed sevens), and told Teddy she used to be on the police wire and was honest. Ruth has never heard of a Delaney; she glances at the typewriter case on her filing cabinet whenever anyone says "honest".
- Teddy insists he must "stay near the cab". Ruth took the case and told Dolan nothing of Teddy's version.
- Dolan (Central Station, Detective Bureau, ~9 a.m.): "Natural. Heart. Room 807." He got the call at 5:20 a.m.; Ostrander's man looked at the body and signed, "heart, and a skinful". He told her to "find somebody's husband" and called her "Ruthie". He signed her 1949 licence application; they have not discussed why. Ruth phoned him once this morning.
- From the water-side kerb of Front Street Ruth counted eight rows of windows (the lobby row is the first), judged the Frieze (so named on the hotel's postcards) about fourteen feet, "a storey and a bit", and on a second count including it reached nine.
- Ending: the Heifer sounded for the first time that day (thick rain at Calder Point, not named fog); Ruth counted forty and it sounded again.

## Ch 3: Property of the Deceased

- Thu 6 Nov, 9 a.m., county morgue (white tile, three rows of twelve steel drawers). Orville Pugh: long, stooped, sixties, bald as a candle, rubber apron over brown cardigan, pickled eggs in a tin sandwich box; will not talk to anyone who interrupts (Dolan does). Drawers named for ball clubs: top row National League, middle American, bottom Coast League (for bodies from the bay); Maher is in the CARDINALS drawer (middle row). Pugh allows no hats on the table.
- Dolan drove Ruth up in a Plymouth (heater works on his side only); she had phoned him twice on 5 Nov. He knew the dead man's real name from the Customs file by 7 p.m. on the 5th and looked at the drain, not the face. When Ruth said "Pier 6" his matchstick stopped. Ruth told him nothing of Teddy's account, the ninth, the glasses or the bare feet.
- Ostrander's report: cardiac failure, contributing acute alcoholism, natural. He kept the stomach contents in a jar on the shelf, "a week in case anyone wants it"; Pugh says nobody has asked for a jar since the war (plant for ch 16).
- Property envelope: brown pigskin wallet with $212 (ten twenties, a ten, two ones); comb; handkerchief; grey felt hat in a paper bag (not examined); pawn ticket for 7x50 field glasses, $14, dated 28 Oct, unredeemed; Pier 6 gate pass (Calder Pier & Terminal Co., valid to 31 Dec 1952); leather Customs card case: DECLAN F. MAHER, TALLY CLERK, stamped SEPARATED 9-50. Maher: 47, pale worried face, small clean hands, pen callus with a blue stain on right second finger.
- Shoes: black cap-toed, size nine, resoled once, tied in a granny knot over the creases of a bow (the wearer's own habit). Socks grey wool; left heel has a hole; both soles grey and felted with dust that Ruth says is not from any hotel bed. Dolan's answers: "Men wear shoes" and "a bellhop, a maid, hotels are full of people who tie things".
- 3 p.m., Selkirk: Gilbert Swain waited in Ruth's chair (the superintendent let him in). About 40, plump cheeks, narrow eyes, patent-shiny hair, bay rum, gold anchor cufflinks, tan polished shoes, soft rising voice, rhetorical questions. Acts for Plum and "the owners" (will not say "Estate"). Pays $500 in ten fifties in a cream envelope with no crest; watermark of three letters C.E.T. which Ruth sees against the window and files without understanding; envelope kept in her desk drawer with the pink notice. She agreed to "write what I find". Swain knew of Teddy via Quill ("a lady had called").
- Swain invited Ruth to Plum's Friends of the Waterfront luncheon, Elks Lodge on Lamplighter Hill, Fri 7 Nov 1:30 p.m., "wear your best hat".
- The carbon: Ruth's 1948 wire-room log copy is under a felt-lined board in the bottom of her Royal portable's case on the filing cabinet; three sheets of blue-violet carbon onionskin folded in four, station copy sheet 6 of 9, headed SUNDAY 14 MARCH 1948. Entry: "2314 Caller D. Maher, Customs tally, asks if cars will clear Pier 6 gate." Ruth advised him there was no information; he thanked her. Ending: the name in her own typing, rain tapping the pipe outside.

## Ch 4: Over the Bakery

- Thu 6 Nov, 8 p.m. to 11:20 p.m. Teddy lives at 1131 Hake, two rooms over Sobczak's Bakery, 18 stairs, one board that sounds a note; Mrs. Sobczak shapes the next day's loaves in the evening. Front room: oilcloth table, hot plate, crucifix, shelf of Polish books (missal, a paperbound Pan Tadeusz with a broken back), narrow tight-made iron bed in a closet room, green blotter on the table. Photograph in black frame: Stefan Kowal (heavy, sexton's long black coat, shovel hands) and a small woman in rimless spectacles holding a handbag in both hands. Teddy: "My sister's husband. Dead." and "My sister." (does not name them).
- Teddy's lie: says he never saw Maher/Delaney before Friday and had no idea of the name Maher. His story for the blotter: on Monday night Delaney asked whether he knew an honest private detective; Teddy said no; Delaney said "then I know one" and wrote Ruth's name. Teddy makes Polish coffee in a glass. He squares a pencil on the blotter twice; pillbox rattles. He still refuses to say how he reached nine ("I will tell you when I know I can"). He asks Ruth not to go near the cab because Quill would be hurt by distrust; she answers only "I'll stay out of the cab".
- Ruth has not told Teddy about the wet paint on the plug or about Swain's $500 (both withheld). She told him Maher's real name.
- Helena at 1127 Hake, one flight up over a Lithuanian cobbler who sings: head scarf, glasses on string, darning a sock on a wooden egg. Supper: cabbage rolls, potatoes with dill, cucumbers in sour cream. She voted "twice, in my heart" for Stevenson (the man with the hole in his shoe). She saw Ruth enter Sobczak's and described Teddy: buys a rye on Saturdays, sits in the last pew at the six o'clock, leaves before the blessing.
- Helena's facts: Teddy is Bronia Kowal's brother; Bronia (small, black, rimless glasses, rosary on wrist, handbag in both hands) a widow seventeen months; Stefan Kowal the sexton kept the St. Raphael box (Raphael patron of travellers, for Masses and sailors), emptied it every Friday and never said where it went; once chased the bishop's driver down Eleventh Avenue with a brass candle snuffer. Helena asked none of the questions Ruth expected; said only "The Kowals were people who kept their doors open." Ruth noticed.
- 11:20 p.m.: from the kitchen window at 1127 (faces north over Flats roofs, ferry slip red lamp, hotel ten blocks off) Ruth sees a small yellow light slide left to right along the black band above the eighth floor for eleven seconds, then go out. Teddy had left at ten for his 11 p.m. shift, so it was not him. Heifer sounded twice. Ending on the dark band.

## Ch 5: The Empty Folder

- Fri 7 Nov, 9:30 a.m. to about 1 p.m. Lucille Fong, 34, Senior Clerk of Vessel Records, Harbor Commission records room, ground floor Customs Annex at Front and Cutter (sign: NO FOOD NO SMOKING NO EXCEPTIONS). Tortoiseshell glasses (pushed up her forehead when angry), chipped tooth, pencil in hair. Wire room 1941-48 sharing a headset with Ruth; Ruth left in June 1948 with Lucille's good chair "in a taxi". Chief Clerk is a Mr. Pruitt, who Lucille has been passed over for. Junior clerk Mr. Tibbets (green eyeshade, pimple). Lucille hinted she thought about why Ruth left in June 1948: "That's your month."
- Maher: Tally Clerk Grade Three, separated Sept 1950 for "irregularities". He telephoned the Commission twice in October asking for Vessel Records; Tibbets said Lucille was at lunch both times.
- The Esther Vane folder: Liberty ship, American flag, from Antwerp via Halifax, docked Slip 3, 9:10 p.m. Sun 14 March 1948, sailed Tuesday; Master Captain Oakes. Folder has tabs ARRIVAL, CREW, MANIFEST, TALLY, CLEARANCE; only ARRIVAL holds a sheet, the rest empty. The sheet is white watermarked bond (rest of folder is buff pulp), typed on an Underwood pica whose capital E sits low; lists 71 crew; pencilled note and figure "31 discharged ashore by mutual consent". Lucille: "A Liberty carries forty... a mutiny or a lie." Ruth put her thumb on the pencilled 31.
- Lucille will bring a copy of the 1926 Harbor Hotel building permit (Commission holds permits inside the pier-head line for the fireboat easement; the Harbor is four feet inside) in her handbag; Ruth owes her a slice of pie at Tommy's.
- Fong & Son, Tailors to the Sea, 14 Anchor Alley (gilt on black sign; "the Son is aspirational"; no brother mentioned). Walter Fong, 66: thin, upright, cropped white hair, half-glasses, tape measure round neck, needle-scarred left fingers. Measured Ruth by eye: 37 chest, 36 sleeve, left shoulder low, "Sears has never met you". He fitted 31 persons for pea coats and watch caps in a Pier 6 shed by lantern on a Sunday in March 1948, paid cash by a man with a smooth clerk's voice he did not look at; "most of them were men"; none spoke. He has kept his order books since 1912, in the back of the shop, and will not discard a measurement.
- On his wall: a gilt-on-black Canton Guild of Merchant Tailors apprenticeship certificate, 1903, in the name LAU WAI-LOK (the sign says FONG). Lucille: "Have a bun." Ruth said nothing.
- Ending: a man in a long dark coat and snapped-brim hat, in the steam laundry's loading door across the alley, peels an orange in one spiral, face pointed at Ruth; after a count of ten the coil drops whole to the wet cobbles.

## Ch 6: Victory at the Elks

- Fri 7 Nov, 1:30 p.m. Elks Lodge banquet room on Lamplighter Hill: oak panelling, elk heads, four-foot City Hall cake with a sparkler in the dome, about 200 guests, a howling microphone. Plum introduces Ruth as "a daughter of the working classes and an alumna of our fine police family". Swain met her at the door.
- Everett Plum: round, pink, dyed ginger hair, pea-sized diamond stickpin, damp lip dabbed with a handkerchief from his cuff; mixes metaphors. In the cloakroom corridor he said "poor Maher", stopped, said "Delaney", then pleaded a cousin with Maher's Hardware in Sacramento. Swain steered him away. The Beacon printed only "Delaney" (page three, under a dog-show notice).
- Cordelia Plum: tall, thin, faded, brushed-wrong mink stole, hat with a dusty wing, guards the punch. A Calder cousin ("my mother's sister married a Calder"); a lawyer's Christmas letter says she is still a Calder. "Everett thinks the harbor is a pond he owns." Fond of him, tired of him.
- Aldo Ferrante sits at the back with his hat on and two silent men: black cropped hair, handsome tired face, broken nose, thick forearms, left ring finger missing its top joint, orange and pocketknife. He is the man from Anchor Alley (came first by car). Calls Ruth "Anton's girl"; carried Anton's coffin, front left; "Pier 6 is a bad place for a woman who sits in the dark... I heard you sat very still for nine years." Offered her an orange segment (declined); left a loose peel on his chair arm.
- Ruth paid the Selkirk superintendent $84 of Swain's money at 5 p.m. (receipt on a seed packet with a beet); about $450 of the $500 remains in her hands. Ate at Tommy's; studied the hotel from across Front Street.
- 9 p.m.: her locked office was open; filing drawers emptied on the floor, desk drawers upturned on the desk, chair overturned; the typewriter case open with the Royal untouched and the carbon still under the felt (she checked). Her black address book with a rubber band (Helena, Lucille, Dolan, the Harbor night desk) is gone. A single unbroken orange peel lies centred on the blotter. Ends on "somebody who wanted me to know he'd been polite."

## Ch 7: The Frieze

- Sat 8 Nov, Tommy Lunch Counter on Front Street (twelve stools, pie clock runs fast; Tommy big, damp, dishrag, grey paper cap). Ruth fitted a Yale lock on 309.
- Permit 7312 (Jan 1926, Lindqvist and Pratt, owner "the Calder Estate"): Frieze 14 ft; attic "TANKS AND MACHINERY, 9 ft 0 in CLEAR", plan blank beyond two tanks. Lucille signed it out as "fireboat easement", also signed the Pier 6 deed index: "Do not make me file for you again."
- Tommy saw Marchand (white gloves, Cormorant Room singer) stand ten minutes across from the hotel just after midnight on election night, then cross over.
- Fire escape from the kitchen yard: 91 steps to a grating outside the eighth-floor end window (LINEN door visible); eleven iron rungs up to black-painted louvres; the fifth has a scraped corner.
- Inside: lantern, card tables, Lambert searching and humming; an unseen man who clears his throat twice: "It is not on him, it is not in the room, and it is not up here. Paper does not walk off, Joe." Then "Mind the stair."
- Ruth slipped, the lantern went out, a door shut; she fell into the potato bin. A key turned once inside the Frieze after she had gone.

## Ch 8: A Name Like Marchand

- Sat 8 Nov, 11:25 p.m. to 2 a.m., Cormorant Room (ex-chandlery, stuffed cormorant with a hatpin eye). Deacon Tolliver says "Mm-hm" until he trusts Ruth, then full sentences; lent a cormorant towel.
- Yvette Marchand: pale, dark coil, grey-green eyes, chin scar, white gloves, bitten nails on her bare hand, a Polish l in her French. She flinched at "Co slychac?" and said "Marchand. Like people who sell things. Write it however you like, Miss Szymanski, the police do."
- Mirror-frame snapshot: younger Yvette on a landing with a ceiling touching her head and coat hooks at knee height. Ruth studied it; Yvette saw.
- Deacon: on election night Yvette left by the stage door during the eleven o clock set and returned at ten past one, hair wet.
- A man in a snapped-brim hat and dark overcoat, untouched beer, followed Ruth; she lost him through the kitchen. Unidentified.
- Yvette under the awning: "The police do not need to know I walk." No answer to "Who else knows?" Her white glove hangs dripping from the awning rope.

## Ch 9: High Mass

- Sun 9 Nov, St. Casimir High Mass: the Szymanski pew is the fourth; Father Dziedzic preached on the stranger and looked away from Ruth. Teddy in the last pew (pillbox rattle).
- Bronia Kowal: black felt hat, rimless glasses, rosary twice round her wrist, steel-clasped handbag. At the Gospel she held a small black book with a brass clasp against her chest.
- Bronia: "Teddy is a good boy. He is fifty-four and a good boy. Leave him be." She and Helena are twenty-year rivals at the coffee urn.
- Father Dziedzic on the St. Raphael box (oak, iron bands): "Travellers Masses"; Stefan did "many good works" and emptied it every Friday; he fled to a baptism.
- Dinner at 1127: Helena said parish women whispered in 1949 of a house for newcomers, "nobody said where and nobody asked"; asked outright she turned to the gravy and did not answer.
- Teddy at the street door: "Leave Bronia out." He left his umbrella on the stoop rail, hand on the pillbox.

## Ch 10: The Shape-Up

- Mon 10 Nov, 5:45 a.m., shape-up at the Hall, 300 men; Ferrante hires with a toothpick. Ruth wore Anton brown overcoat (patch on the left elbow); an old stevedore: "Anton girl."
- Ferrante (office with St. Joseph and a ledger of names): Maher bled him two years ($100 a month) and asked $2,000 last week. Alibi: the Hall, forty men and a radio, 8 p.m. to 3 a.m. "Remember that I carried it." Stay out of Pier 6.
- 2 p.m. Dolan at 309 with doughnuts. Rafferty retires Sat 15 Nov and wants quiet. Lab found nothing in 807. Ruth mentioned the stomach jar on Ostrander shelf; Dolan hand drifted to the typewriter case and withdrew. A priest had telephoned him.
- 5:50 p.m. Gundersen Drug on Cutter: Lambert phoned (card from Dorrie desk). "I carried him down." Midnight Tue, boiler room, Ferry Slip door, kitchen bell, "Bring nobody." A machine ticked behind him.
- Ending: dial tone and the Heifer.

## Ch 11: Armistice Night

- Tue 11 Nov parade. Lucille: the Esther Vane arrival sheet vanished overnight (Mon 5 p.m. to Tue 8 a.m.), five empty tabs, three keys to the room, lock not forced. Dolan and Karras drove by; Dolan did not turn his head.
- 11:30 p.m., fog. Teddy opened the kitchen door unaware Lambert had asked her; pillbox rattled at "carried". Lobby repeater stuck between 3 and 4; OUT OF ORDER on the lift doors since seven. Pearl went home at ten with a "headache".
- Boiler room: Lambert lantern lit, cap on the floor, thermos open and cold. Pit door ajar; pit seven feet below, seven rungs, trouble-lamp, iron buffer.
- Lambert dead on his back, head turned wrong, brass oiler upright by his right hand, neck warm in the crease.
- Ruth took his key (long, square head, OTIS INSP.) on its shoelace; it was warm.
- Ending: Teddy at the pit door saying "Joe" over and over.

## Ch 12: The Pit

- Wed 12 Nov, 12:20 a.m.: Dolan, Karras (calls Teddy sir), Dr. Ostrander rule an accidental fall onto the buffer. Ruth: palms clean, oiler full to the spout, a man slipping off oily rungs grabs.
- Ruth lied that Teddy asked her to look at the cab; Teddy backed her. Dolan: "You were never any good at it, Ruth." Karras underlined one word.
- Quill came down in a dark red quilted dressing gown, cane on his arm, one tear; cleared his throat twice; asked her to tea at nine.
- 806: lacquered screen, squat green safe, brass dial over the door with a glass-capped toggle beneath, the gown on a hook. Quill admitted the ninth floor ("the Mezzanine", old Calder Club); he moved Maher to 807 with "a man" (Ruth: Lambert; he: "I did not say so"), for sixty jobs.
- Quill asked Ruth to find the killer of Maher and Joe, $15 a day. She told him of Swain $500; he is "only the Estate auditor".
- Ending: eleven perfectly round cane dents in the corridor nap; Lambert key cold in her pocket; she likes Quill and cannot say why it worries her.

## Ch 13: What Teddy Left Out

- Wed 12 Nov, 7 to 10:45 p.m., Teddy's flat (18 stairs, the board that sings). Teddy is stunned that Quill spoke of the ninth: in winter 1947 Quill told him only that he had "a great respect for the tank room above the Frieze" and wished not to be told what he need not know. Teddy believes Quill moved the body to protect the shelter ("He loved Joe"); he does not suspect him.
- Teddy's history: Dec 1946 Stefan Kowal (died June 1951, aged 61, in the church vestibule) brought a man, woman and two girls off a Greek freighter; Teddy hid them nine nights in the eighth-floor linen cupboard, then pushed the back wall of the shelves (he had known since 1932 that it opens) to the stair and the floor above. Pearl fed them (soup under pillowcases), Joe kept the car, Stefan carried up iron cots with laundry tags. 311 people in six years, "all with names". Teddy let slip "It is written down" (the book). Last family left 17 Oct.
- Maher was Stefan's friend at Customs, kind to them two years, "afterward something else"; Teddy will not say what.
- Keys: three exist (Joe's, now Ruth's; Teddy's, watch pocket; Quill's, in his safe). Lobby panel keyhole beside the call plate recalls the car "like a dog called in". In the cab: lift the painted plug with a thumbnail, key, press 8, hold a slow count of three, six extra seconds. Teddy does not know of the toggle in 806. Joe told Teddy the car was "sulking" and lied.
- Teddy lied because Bronia keeps a book (Ruth named it: black, brass clasp). "If you read it, you will burn it." Ruth: "I don't burn things."
- Yvette: she asked Teddy for "Mr. Delaney" at 12:10 on 4 Nov; he said Delaney had retired; looked down at the register and did not see which way she went; later told Dolan no one had asked. He neither confirms nor denies she was hidden upstairs.
- Quill's alibi as Teddy gives it: Quill left the party 11:50 for 806; at 2:05 a.m. the operator rang "Santa Barbara for 806", Teddy plugged it and wrote it in the toll book (time, room, Miss Calder). Elder niece is 78 and deaf, Quill "obliged to shout". Teddy saw the 806 lamp lit on the board at 2:45 and dark at 3:30. Ruth wrote "2:05, Santa Barbara, 806, lamp 2:45, dark 3:30" on the back of the pink notice.
- Relief clerk Okonski had the desk till midnight Wed; Teddy spent an hour at Mt. Zion with the deacons. Teddy knows Ruth has Joe's key (Pearl said the shoelace was missing). Ended on the landing: "Don't go up." / "If you go, count the steps."

## Ch 14: A Floor Nobody Built

- Thu 13 Nov, 1:00 a.m. Ruth rode up alone. Desk card: NIGHT CLERK AWAY FROM DESK, PLEASE RING. Teddy sat in the boiler room in Joe's chair; Pearl, asked by Teddy by phone at eleven, sat up in the linen room. Plug was dry cream paint over the keyway (split Ruth's thumbnail). Ruth bought a nickel torch at Gundersen's, $1.19.
- Ninth floor: grey felt runner over boards; palm lies flat on the ceiling; corridor 14 paces; at the 13th pace a steel girder drops the ceiling to about six feet and the corridor widens a yard. Knee-high rail of brass hooks on the right (a red child's mitten, thumb darned green). Left wall chalked with first names and dd/mm/yy dates (Wacław, Halina, Józef, Rūta 3.XI.47, Andris, Oksana 12/1/50, Irena 21/10/49; a ship drawing with JANEK, BASIA, ZUZIA). Nineteen names dated 15/3/48 on one stretch, the rest rubbed away.
- Five doors (three left, two right), 42 numbered iron cots (9, 8, 8, 9, 8; numbers in Pearl's hand), kitchenette with gas ring and enamel mugs inked with names, copper boiler, saints' calendar stopped at 13 Nov, child's pencil drawing of a train. Black wall telephone with no dial was not touched.
- Club room: card tables stacked under baize, chandelier swathed in grey sheet, bottle-green safe in the east wall (dial, gold lettering worn off), one cot pulled out with a body dent in the mattress, two clean circles of tumbler size in the dust on the third table, a trodden path from cot to the east wall.
- The car was recalled at 1:51 a.m. (Pearl's clock) while Ruth was on nine; no call button on that landing. Ruth thought of the glass-capped toggle in 806 but said nothing aloud. Hidden stair: hinged oak panel by the safe with a painted thumb-catch; 14 bare steps; the sixth tread is loose; barrel bolt, oiled, on the stair side of the door into the linen cupboard.
- Ruth now sees Maher bled the hidden people and that Teddy lied rightly; the chalk date 15/3/48 ties the thirty-one to her own wire night (Sunday 14 March, her call from Maher). Her question changes to who is keeping this and who is hurt if it comes out.
- Pearl (called Miss Hightower) waited in the cupboard: "I do not guess. I counted the car going up." Would not say who sent it down. "They were children." Took Ruth by the elbow down staff stairs to the service door on Ferry Slip Row: "Go home and be sorry." Ended on her key ring silent in her fist.

## Ch 15: What the Certificate Says

- Fri 14 Nov. Hubert Pomeroy, 58, State Division of Elevators (third floor, State Building): stout, pink scalp, brass elevator-car charm on watch chain, model railway (the Overland Limited, tunnel of E-12 folders). Register "Passenger Elevators, District 4": Harbor certificate 4471 shows Landings 9 every March 1938 to 1952. He rode to nine each March with Joe (key, count, six seconds), never stepped out. Annual 11-lb Virginia ham from the Trustee. Division counts landings, Building Dept counts floors. Gave Ruth a stamped typed extract; will testify only if his name stays out of the papers.
- 12:20 p.m. Ruth showed Dolan; from the kerb he counted nine and took his hat off. Dolan carries an evidence envelope (Maher's hat) in his breast pocket; Karras waited in the Ford.
- Lobby: Dorrie Nash wears a black ribbon for Joe. Quill tapped his cane once for Dolan (never for Ruth). He said the ninth is a "tank loft" that the State calls a landing and that he gave it a pet name in a moment of grief.
- In the cab the plug is polished brass; under it a solid filed brass disc, no keyway. Quill: Otis sealed it Thursday morning (he rang at eight, a man came at nine), and he hopes Joe's key "is in kind hands" while looking at Ruth. Swain appeared in the lobby and demanded a warrant; Quill pretended to be overruled.
- Dolan: he needs a judge; the ledger page is a paperwork matter and she has a trespass she cannot admit. Asked for 48 hours to try to get Rafferty to sign a request; ordered her to stay out of the lift; gave her a spare seat at Rafferty's dinner (Pacific Room, Sat 8 p.m., "wear a dress").
- 6 p.m. from Tommy's: a lantern moves behind a louvre in the Frieze; Karras sits in the squad car with his notebook closed. Ended on the new brass in the keyway.

## Ch 16: Under the Hatband

- Sat 15 Nov, clear and cold, no fog, no Heifer. Pacific Room dinner for Rafferty (60, born in Dayton, Ohio; white hair cut square, face like rare beef). Police Benevolent Association Band. Rafferty toasted Ruth ("the best voice on the wire"). Ruth in navy wool and her Plum hat; Dolan in a rented dinner jacket. Present: DA Hollis Barnwell and wife, Plum, Swain, Ferrante (orange on his plate), Quill (white dinner jacket and carnation), Karras.
- Quill showed Ruth the silver-framed photograph on the Pacific Room piano: Hallam Calder, wife and nieces Miss Honor and Miss Verity, 1913 (Quill held the plate). They are in Santa Barbara, "the last of the line", have not seen Port Calder in twenty years. "Miss Honor is somewhat deaf." Cane tip a perfect round.
- Plum in the cloakroom corridor: terrified, cannot hold a water glass, "inadvertical", "conflagration of circumstance". Ruth rates him too frightened and stupid to be the killer.
- Men's cloakroom: slip under the sweatband of Maher's grey felt hat, ruled paper torn from a pad, block capitals in rust-brown ink: "NINE. HALF PAST MIDNIGHT. BRING IT." Dolan found it Friday after Lambert. Ruth said "Ink like old blood" and did not connect it. She recalled the dry voice in the Frieze: "Paper does not walk off, Joe."
- Lab found chloral hydrate in Ostrander's jar; Rafferty said it will keep till Monday. Dolan asked if Ruth still has the carbon and told her to burn it; she refused ("That's why I didn't"). Quiet quarrel.
- 12:45 a.m.: Ruth followed Swain's Packard on foot to the Pier 6 gate; hid behind cable reels. Swain handed Ferrante a cream envelope ($500, goodwill). "Two men dead and you bring me an envelope." "A trust has no face." "Then it's got no neck." Ferrante: "Fish market. Four o'clock Sunday. Come alone, or don't come." Ended on his pocketknife opening and closing.

## Ch 17: Fish Market, Before Light

- Sun 16 Nov, 4 a.m., municipal fish market; Ferrante in a rubber apron cleaning cod for his mother. His account: Stefan came to the Shape Hall winter 1946 for work chits; March 1948 the Esther Vane's thirty-one; price $400 a head: $150 hall, $100 Plum's company, $150 "the House" (he believed Plum's wife's Calder relatives). Maher bled him $100 a month from 1950; last week asked $2,000 for the ship's master's copy "with a signature that would hang the House". Ferrante admits he thought of killing Maher; alibi stands (Shape Hall, forty men). He fears he is next.
- Ferrante: Stefan kept the names in a prayer book in pencil ("God keeps accounts"); he never saw it. Swain's envelope of last night is what the Estate pays to find Maher's paper and keep his mouth shut, paid since the fourth. His boys searched Ruth's office on the 7th and the Kowal flat on Tue and Thu; called off. He paid Rafferty $1,500 for the Pier 6 gap on 14 March 1948 ("Your wire went quiet because I paid Rafferty"). Ruth said nothing.
- Helena's note: Mrs. Wach says the Kowal door is kicked in. Kowal flat: Eleventh Avenue behind the church, three rooms over a cobbler's, outside stair; chairs slit, carpet rolled, boards lifted, Sacred Heart face down, Stefan's photo glass cracked. Bronia: "A Pole would have taken the sugar."
- Bronia gave the prayer book to Helena (black leather, brass clasp, spine mended with thread, printed Krakow 1911): "Stefan said, if there is trouble, give it to a woman who has never let anything go." Bronia said Ruth would read it "with her head". Helena wept; Ruth took it.
- A man in a long dark coat read a Courier upside down at Eleventh and Hake (unidentified). Ruth rode the Number 14 to Cutter and back with the book unopened on her lap, then moved it to her coat pocket where the clasp clicked against Joe's key.

## Ch 18: Pod Twoją Obronę

- Sun 16 Nov, 8 p.m. onward, Ruth's kitchen under the pulled-down lamp. Helena read the back leaves: pencil, Stefan's hand, saints' days for dates, columns for place, ship, "dni na gorze", then from March 1948 "ofiara" and "do Domu". 28 lines to a page, eleven full pages and a short twelfth (311). First entry: Nowak family of four, Greek ship Aegina, St Lucy (13 Dec 1946), nine days, offering nothing, "Pan Bog zaplac". Margins: "chora", "chrzest", one "urodzona na gorze". A line from Feb 1949, "u Sz., 7 dni, 4 dusze": Helena stopped and did not read it; Ruth did not ask (Helena's secret not yet spoken).
- 14 March 1948 (St Matilda), "Okret Esther Vane... Trzydziesci jeden dusz": 31 at $400, $150 each to the House ($4,650 that night). Stefan wrote "Panie, przebacz mi." Last entries are in Teddy's square hand with crossed sevens; last: Lukaszewicz, five persons, 3 Oct, left 17 Oct 1952. Faint pencil margin note beside the first fee page: "Dom = Hotel. T. mowi."
- 10 p.m. Yvette arrived with Stefan's knock (three and one), one white glove, a cardboard suitcase; Dolan had come to the Cormorant Room at 8 asking where she was on Tuesday night, and Deacon sent her to Hake Street. Helena spoke Polish to her; both wept. She stays the night in Ruth's bed.
- Yvette's page: "Wolska, Irena, Pacific Tern, St Ursula (21 Oct 1949), 19 days, 400, House 150"; paid with her mother's three rings in Antwerp; Lyon laundress papers. Maher took $40 a month from 1 July 1951 via envelopes at Mrs. Abbott's: 16 payments, $640.
- Yvette on 4 Nov: carried $300 to buy her page; Teddy at the desk looked at the register so as not to see her; she went up the stairs, knocked at 807, no answer. She saw from behind a tall old man in a dark quilted dressing gown, shiny at the shoulders, pale hair, carrying a tray with two glasses and something tall; he walked fast, did not try the handle, had the key already out, and went into the last door on the right (LINEN). She never saw his face. She told no one else.
- Ruth silently counted Teddy, Pearl, Joe, Quill as the four with a cupboard key and crossed off three by height or sex; did not name Quill aloud. She weighs the dressing gown against Quill's Santa Barbara alibi and has only "a back and a tray".
- Helena put a bread knife under her pillow. At 1:45 a.m. Ruth found the margin note: the House is the Hotel. She will telephone Lucille Fong in the morning to ask whose name is on the paper for the pier. Ended on Helena in her nightgown: "A name is a small thing to carry. Carry it."

## Ch 25: Thanksgiving Eve

- Wed 26 Nov. 6:40 a.m. Dolan eats Helena's eggs at 1127 Hake. Pearl's sack came by the Harbor linen van (Dodge, HARBOR HOTEL in gilt) at 5:50: twelve damask (Helena washes them, hangs them from lines across the kitchen) with the manifest, the C.E.T. receipt half-sheet and Maher's note rolled in a pillowcase. Dolan carries Maher's hatband slip in an evidence envelope and matches the rust-brown ink. Karras developed Ruth's roll of Fong's order book in the station darkroom (31 lines, "Paid from Pier 6 office, order of the Estate").
- 9 a.m., County Building (2nd floor, top of Lamplighter Hill): DA Hollis Barnwell, 52, long, grey, heavy lids, hat crown-down on the blotter, speaks in conditionals ("If this were mine"). Reads 11 minutes with a glass. Will not ask for "the others" ("a prosecutor who has not asked cannot be made to tell"). Keeps manifest and note in his safe. Wants the Estate's receipt ledger: warrant for the Calder Building and the hotel (806 and above the eighth floor), to be heard at Judge Pardoe's house at four; Pardoe keeps Thanksgiving with his sister at Stannard's Ferry (no telephone). "A man like that does not burn his books. He files them." Nobody to enter the hotel before the warrant.
- Miss Aldous, Barnwell's secretary (about 40, green cardigan), telephones "Gil" (Swain): "not till four". Dolan says he will have her moved to the files.
- Ruth sleeps till 2; the book stays in the oven under the roasting pan; Helena ties a grey wool scarf on her ("A street is a very small river"). Tommy's counter (CLOSED THURS.) from about 5; fog from 5:15, the Heifer from 5:40.
- 6:20 Swain's Packard: camel coat, leather bag left on the lobby desk with Dorrie Nash. 6:45 a light behind a louvre near the Ferry Slip end of the Frieze. Ruth phones Dolan from Tommy's: warrant unsigned, Barnwell gone after Pardoe, "We'll be there at seven-thirty" (Karras and two uniformed men). She also phones Walter Fong: tell Lucille to bring a coat to the Harbor kitchen yard at eight; "I will see to it."
- Kitchen (22 turkeys on the steel table): Pearl says Quill, in a dinner jacket, took Swain's bag (six or seven green cloth ledgers with leather spines) and a five-pint lamp-oil can from the pantry up the service lift at 6:40; padlock on the linen cupboard hangs open; back of the shelves swings in from the cupboard side. Pearl has 26 guests and will ring her bell only when she smells smoke. Ruth takes Pearl's nickel-plated, dented flashlight (new batteries since the 14th). She does not fetch the .32 (Cutter St desk, bottom drawer).
- Ruth climbs 138 staff steps to eight, pushes through the cupboard back and starts up the hidden stair; the sixth tread cracks under her and she says "Six" aloud. Ends there.

## Ch 26: The Safe in the Card Room

- Wed 26 Nov, about 7:03 to 7:45 p.m., ninth floor. Lamp oil already tastes on the stair. The panel at the stair head is open; Quill, in a black dinner jacket and stiff white collar, sits at the card table nearest the safe, cane across his knees, a green cloth ledger open with a silver pencil laid in the gutter, a five-pint can by his right foot, the empty leather bag by his left, a hurricane lantern on the floor. The Prohibition safe stands open and empty. He says "The sixth, I think. I have always meant to have it seen to" and "It was the Mezzanine, in my day."
- He audits Ruth's read-back, ticking the ledger margin: 75-minute call "Conjecture"; Rx 41872 "Hearsay"; ink "a very old formula... Dorset"; gown and tray, and Maher's shoes "Possible"; cane tip "I take care of my things"; Lambert "I was very fond of Joseph"; the 31 cases "I invoiced them... The insurers required a signature"; Teddy "The Estate has outlived four generations of Calders. It can outlive a night clerk."
- Waltham read 7:27. Quill knows Judge Pardoe is at his sister's with no telephone ("Mr. Swain was good enough to let me know"). Ruth says the manifest is in Barnwell's safe. He picks up the can: "A fire in a roof space is a thing one insures against, my dear. The brigade will be at the cornice in six minutes." Pours on the baize, the table, Maher's cot (dent in the mattress). The dormitories were oiled earlier; the wall telephone cord was cut that afternoon.
- Ruth kicks the lantern; the baize ignites; Quill steps through the panel, pulls it to, and an iron rail drops into brackets on the stair side. Ruth takes the green receipt ledger (hot at one corner) inside her coat. The panel gives a finger's width.
- She tries panel, safe, corridor, telephone, kitchenette (louvre a yard above reach) while counting fourteen taps of the cane going down; cots catch; the chalk wall browns and goes (Wacław, Halina, Józef, Rūta, Irena with her date, JANEK BASIA ZUZIA). The loose sixth tread cracks under him and he does not falter. Ends on "There was no limp in him anywhere."

## Ch 27: The Cornice

- Wed 26 Nov, 7:40 to 9 p.m., fog. Ruth kicks out the kitchenette louvre (south face, Ferry Slip Row side; slats of fired clay in an iron frame); wraps Helena's grey scarf round her right hand, grips the hot frame bare with her left; both palms burned and blistered. The ledge is two feet wide with a carp-like terracotta fish every foot; she counts 31 fish east to the corner, then the eleven rungs to the eighth-floor grating and the 91-step fire escape.
- Pearl's brass hand bell; Pearl emptied floors from the ninth down ("the habit of six years"); 46 people in the yard with the kitchen staff.
- Quill leaves by the service door beside the laundry chute, cane on his arm, no bag. Ruth drops six feet in front of him; he clears his throat once only; the cane rises and lowers; he sees the ledger bulge in her coat. Dolan (arrives 7:52, hat back, no matchstick) in the alley mouth: "I count nine." Quill: "The plans say eight, Sergeant." Dolan: "Plans lie." Karras cuffs him; the cane falls to the cobbles, tip perfect. Dolan holds him for the deaths of Declan Maher and Josiah Lambert and for arson, and says to get a lawyer who isn't Swain. Quill says nothing.
- Pearl to Quill: "Forty-six, with the kitchen. Every one counted out by me. I shall count them in." Pardoe signed the warrant on his sister's porch step with a pencil at 7:40; it came half an hour late. Ruth hands Dolan the green ledger: "Footed. I believe it balances."
- Lucille arrives at 7:56 in her father's overcoat with a grey herringbone coat (black sateen lining) that Walter Fong cut the day Ruth came to lunch, left shoulder let out; Ruth wears it. Lucille sits on an upturned bucket.
- Engine Company Four at the cornice six minutes after the first bell; louvres fall in the hose water; the chalk names are gone. Ruth is glad (no evidence to photograph) and ashamed. Ash falls like paper snow from about 9. She never learns whether the light in the Frieze was bait.

## Ch 28: What the Keeper Keeps

- Fri 28 Nov: Ruth's hands in gauze; Helena fed her a Friday turkey (29 cents the pound). 10 a.m. Central Station steps with Dolan. Ruth told Dolan on Thursday night, in the Municipal ward, where the carbon was (the Royal, under the felt); Dolan fetched it with the Selkirk superintendent witnessing and gave it to Barnwell, who keeps it beside the manifest ("a very well typed log"). Roll call: Quill arraigned 8 a.m., held without bail for Maher, Lambert, arson and fraud, given a pencil, wrote nothing. Swain talked three hours with his own lawyer and named hall men Mulcahy and Reese, hired through a hiring boss at Quill's word to wreck 1127 Hake. Plum resigned by telegram at six. Ferrante went to Barnwell's door on Thursday with an orange and a lawyer; the grand jury sat Friday at ten. Pomeroy sent the receiver a cheque for $63.14 (14 hams of 11 lb at 41 cents). The Beacon ran WOZNIAK CLEARED at the foot of page one. Swain withdrew his complaint and the Board's letter reinstating Ruth comes by the thin woman in grey. Rafferty to be served in Santa Cruz that afternoon. Chief Haskell told Dolan he goes at Christmas with no pension. "I wanted to be somebody I could stand."
- Sat 29 Nov, 11 p.m., Union Station, Track Four, night train north: Yvette in an oatmeal coat, no gloves, will tell no one her new name; Helena gave her bread; Yvette gave Ruth her white cotton stage gloves (washed, starched, folded), which stay in Ruth's coat pocket; Lucille said "Lau" aloud for the first time and Ruth read it back ("Lau. Say again. Lau." "Received.").
- Sun 30 Nov, First Sunday of Advent: Dolan stood outside St. Casimir's through High Mass; gave Bronia the tin pillbox in a property bag tagged T. WOZNIAK; she shook it once and put it in her handbag where the book had been. "He said you were a polite policeman." "I was a slow one." "Yes." Bronia to Ruth: "A book wants a keeper, not a shelf." Ruth: "I'll put it where it can't be found." Bronia: "By them. Not by you."
- At the gate Ruth gave Odessa Lambert Joe's key on its shoelace and told her all of it. "He hummed 'Abide with Me' for six years straight, and I never once asked him why." Helena broke the first Advent wafer with Ruth that evening.
- That night on the roof of 1127 Hake (14 stairs, 8 iron rungs to the scuttle), by the Calder Point light, Ruth reads the first entry (Nowak, four souls, Aegina, St Lucy's day, nine days upstairs, offering none, "Pan Bóg zapłać"), counts 311 (28 a page, eleven pages and three lines), stops, and begins again reading aloud: "Nowak. Say again. Nowak." Ruth keeps the book.
- Coda, a week later: Pomeroy and an Otis man fit a ninth brass button marked 9 under the 8 (where the plug was) and paint a 9 on the dial; the certificate reads LANDINGS SERVED: 9, FLOORS OPEN TO THE PUBLIC: 8. The Estate is in the hands of receiver Gruber; Pearl is acting manager by Tuesday; her forty keys now chime. The ninth is a charred floor with sky through the empty louvres. Ruth stays on the pavement and does not count.
