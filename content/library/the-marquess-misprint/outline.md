# The Marquess's Misprint: Chapter Outline

Twenty-four chapters of about 2,420 words. Dates are 1816. Weather is rain unless stated.

**Shape.** Hook in chapter 1; inciting incident (the bargain) in chapter 3; complications through chapter 11; midpoint reversal in chapter 12 (the kiss and the double confession, after which the pretence is that they are not in love); the Kent chapters 13 to 17 raise the stakes and end in the flood; the low point in chapter 18 (the Crier prints the Articles; Julian's secret attempt to buy the notes); recovery 19 to 22; climax 23 (the Saturday number); resolution 24.

**Secrets, clues and red herrings (the book's one light mystery: who is selling the engagement to the Crier? A reader who attends can suspect Agnes from chapter 8 and is sure by chapter 16, when the narrator shows her with the letter; the characters learn it in 19 and 20. The pleasure is in the ladder of fair clues and in how the answer lands on people we like, not in a surprise.).**
- *Solution.* Peregrine Pry, the Crier's gossip columnist, is Agnes Fell. She copied Winifred's counterpart of the Articles in the estate office at Stanmere Court on the night of 3 August, where Winifred had left it out of its case; she posted the copy on 15 August to Lovejoy, who has bought up her brother Matthias's debt and writes of it with perfect civility. The carter Sowerby told Lovejoy's clerk that "the young lady stood in the arch and said nothing" on the morning of 6 June. Lovejoy deduced the mechanism of the error himself from the Crooked W.
- *Clues, with chapters.* An ink stain on Agnes's right middle finger, mittens, and a night-candle burning until two (4). Pry's copy arrives at the Phoenix Press in a copyist's hand (6). Pry's report of the rout (Saturday's Crier, read aloud by Toby in 8) quotes the Duchess's whispered remark about Mrs Chalmers, which only the sofa heard: Constance, Winifred, Verity, Rupert, and Agnes a pace behind with the shawl (7, 8); the column also uses a phrase, "I beg your pardon, but", that is Agnes's own. In the flooded kitchen Agnes says softly, "I beg your pardon, but I think the turtle is not dead" (11), and the Crier of 6 July repeats the line word for word. Agnes standing at the press-room door in the night counting sheets, and Winifred's counterpart lying on the table (14). Agnes receiving a letter in Lovejoy's hand about her brother (16). Dawn post at Yalding (17). The Crier of 31 August opens "I may be quite wrong, but", and Constance, who has heard that phrase over breakfast for twelve years, hears it (18, 19).
- *Red herrings.* Minton (his fingers are inky, he is anxious, he has a motive of shame) is suspected by Julian after the Crier of 6 July, and cleared by a sketchbook of forty fond caricatures of his employer; the apology is told on the road in 13. Rupert (owes Lovejoy £800, is asked for a favour, was near the sofa) is suspected by Winifred in 8 and again in 15, and cleared when he tells her himself. Mrs Bunting, Constance's cook, is dismissed for a day after the flood report and reinstated (13).
- *How Lovejoy learns it was Winifred's mistake.* The Crooked W: the "W." in the printed Misprint prints with a hairline gap in the left foot, as does the W in her fair-copy advertisement in the same number; the two lines are of one measure; and an engagement announced by a Marquess's own secretary would never name the bride's trade address.

**Costs and rules.** There is no magic. The costs are these: Clause 9 (the contract and £500 bond), the Articles (no money, no direct falsehood, sole right to end it at Michaelmas), the Michaelmas notes (£740, then £1,140), and the cost of the lie to the people who believe it (Barnaby, Underhill, Verity's mother).

## Chapter 01: A Page in Pie

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. Pargeter's, Hand and Pen Court. Wednesday 5 June, five in the afternoon, to Thursday 6 June, a quarter past five in the morning.

**Purpose.** To make the error, make us love the shop and the woman, and end on the choice that will cost her.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the Drip, a steady fall of rainwater from a broken slate onto the imposing stone, and the basin Toby is made to empty every quarter-hour. The narrator introduces the shop, the year, the cold, and the reader.
- Five o'clock: Mr Minton's boy brings the Gazette copy, a damp slip in a clerk's fair hand. Winifred, Orme (sets in the corner, reads backwards), Toby (eating a pie), Barnaby on the speaking-tube from his bed, bellowing instructions and jokes. She sets the Fashionable Intelligence in a good humour, making fun of the paragraph about Lady Quarles's daughter while she works.
- The rain gets in. The copy for the Marquess's paragraph is blotted. Mr Ingram the reader is ill; Mr Underhill's rule is no proofs after midnight.
- Eleven at night: Toby, a pie in one hand, the page of type in the other, trips over Brevier the cat and drops it. The page goes into pie. Orme sleeps. Fairweather and the second pressman Dick Sparrow are already inking the Albion for the first side.
- Winifred recovers the page from the heap, line by line, by measure and memory. Two lines of the same measure lie together. She sets what she sees. A compositor, the narrator explains, does not read; she sees letters, as a baker sees flour.
- Quarter to one: forme locked, no proof. The two presses begin. At 4.40 Fairweather hands her one of the first sheets, hung to dry since two, with a heel of bread. She reads the paragraph. She finds her own name in it. She does the arithmetic of stopping the cart: reset, reprint eleven hundred, deliver three hours late, and Clause 6, under which a second late cart voids the contract (the first was the rain-bound cart of 11 May, and Mr Underhill has been waiting). She sees that she can avoid Clause 9, which punishes a printed correction, only by breaking Clause 6.
- The cart in the arch; Sowerby the carter flicks his whip; Barnaby's bell rings upstairs.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred has made the error and decided to let it go. Closing image: she stands very still in the arch with the sheet in her hand, which is a thing compositors do well, while the cart goes out into Paternoster Row. She had good boots and did not use them.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the Drip; the Crooked W (in her advert line, seen as a hairline gap); Clause 9 and Clause 6; the bond; the brass stick (her mother's); Orme's backwards reading; Sowerby seeing her stand; Toby's secret (he dropped it); Brevier the cat; Mrs Hobday's hymns. Pays off later in 12 (her confession), 22 (the hearing), 23 (she rides the cart).

## Chapter 02: Cards on the Salver

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Julian. Stanmere House, Curzon Street. Thursday 6 June, eight in the morning.

**Purpose.** To show the man, his predicament and his temptation, and to put Lady Constance on the board.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a silver salver of visiting cards, carried in by Gantry, in a drift like a card trick. Julian has been up since five, eating a cold mutton chop and proofreading the Gazette with a pencil, because he cannot read anything without correcting it.
- He reads the paragraph and reads it again. The narrator tells us what he thinks in the first ten seconds (it is a joke) and the next ten (it is not). The "Pargeter" is the name on the imprint of the proofs of the Farmer's letters. He keeps a pocket-book with the margin queries signed W.P.
- Mr Minton arrives grey-faced: he sent the denial; the paper has printed its opposite. Julian's reaction is exactly civil. Minton wants to write a retraction. Julian says "In a moment."
- Lady Constance arrives with Blücher the pug and her ear-trumpet: "Who is Miss W. Pargeter?" She orders the salver counted. Forty-one cards the day after the Quarles rumour; this morning nine, and three of those are letters of regret. A note from Verity Quarles, in a square hand: "Thank you. V.Q."
- Lord Rupert Tremayne strolls in laughing, with the Gazette folded to the page. Rupert's questions (is she handsome? is she rich? is it true?) bring out Julian's temper and the fact that the Marquess has not once said he would correct it.
- Constance: "You will not correct it." "I have not said I would." "No. You have been not saying it for ten minutes. It is how your father began." She lets fall that nothing in ten years has made Lady Quarles leave a house with her hat crooked.
- Julian lays his pencil on the page above "well founded" and does not mark it.

**What changes / closing beat.** He has not decided to let it stand, but he has decided to look. Closing line: "Minton," he said, "I should like to know everything there is to know about the firm of Pargeter." "The printers, my lord?" "The printers."

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Julian's pocket-book of W.P. queries; the signet ring turned; the Farmer's identity (the narrator lets the reader in at once, and keeps it from Winifred); Constance's ear-trumpet; Blücher; Rupert's debts (a remark about a tailor). Pays off in 5, 8, 12, 24.

## Chapter 03: Articles of Agreement

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Winifred then Julian. Pargeter's. Friday 7 June, ten in the morning.

**Purpose.** The inciting incident: the bargain.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: Mrs Hobday's breakfast at the long table, with hymns and a rasher. Barnaby, who was told last night, has taken it with a joke and then a silence and then a second helping. A note from Underhill arrives overflowing with joy: the Gazette's scoop of the Season, circulation up by six hundred, a second edition at the Row. Clause 9 will not be invoked unless the engagement is exposed as false.
- Winifred has a letter ready to send to Curzon Street. The Marquess arrives first, on foot with an umbrella and Minton carrying a second one, and is taken by Toby to be a bailiff.
- In the composing room, Winifred owns the error to his face: "I made it; my father did not." Julian asks, as a proofreader would, who will be inconvenienced by a correction and by how much. Their first conversation is an argument about a comma.
- He proposes that it stand: stet, until the end of the Season. She is outraged, then counts. She asks for Michaelmas, because that is when the notes fall due. She asks for the sole right to end it. She will not tell a lie aloud.
- She sets the terms in type, standing at the case, while he watches her hands. The Articles are pulled on the Albion in one proof, signed by both, with a second proof for the second counterpart. Barnaby is called down on his gouty foot to witness; he reads Article 6 aloud and weeps with laughter.
- Julian signs Article 6 with a tolerable laugh: "I never fall in love; it is a family trait."
- At the door Toby asks, "Are you really going to marry our Win?" Julian: "I am assured it is in the Gazette." Winifred, overhearing, takes the phrase.

**What changes / closing beat.** The lie is now an agreement. Closing image: Winifred pulls the counterpart off the forme and sees Julian's thumb has left an inky print on Article 6, which she does not wipe.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: all seven Articles (printed in full in the chapter); the brass stick; "It is in the Gazette"; the thumbprint; Michaelmas as the end date; Article 3 (no money), which Julian will break in 18; Article 5 (the right to end it), which Winifred will use in 18 and 23.

## Chapter 04: Lady Constance Inspects the Goods

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Winifred, with a closing page of Agnes. Hertford Street. Monday 10 June, eleven in the morning.

**Purpose.** To bring Winifred into the world she must pass through, and to show Constance as ally and adversary, and plant Agnes.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: Lady Constance's morning-room, so cold and so full of stuffed birds that Winifred feels she has been admitted to an expensive larder. Blücher inspects her ankle. Miss Agnes Fell pours tea and apologises to the pot.
- Constance's questions, through the ear-trumpet: dance? (country dances from her aunt in Hackney). French? (reads it; she set a menu in French once). Horse? (no). Music? (no). Read? (upside down). To prove it Winifred reads Constance's desk across the room: a letter with a Florence postmark and a woman's hand. Constance turns it face down. The room cools.
- Constance has known the engagement was false since eight on Thursday morning; no Dyer announces anything. If it is to be done, it will be done well.
- Dress: Winifred refuses a gift of gowns and will wear no new silk bought with the Marquess's money. Compromise: Constance's circular for the Society for the Relief of Distressed Gentlewomen is to be printed at Pargeter's at ordinary rates, and Winifred may spend that fee on a dress. Constance respects this and says so by pretending to be annoyed.
- Constance's lecture on gloves, the use of the word "Marchioness", and the art of being looked at. "He will behave like a man who intends to be jilted. Do not let him enjoy it."
- Walking home Winifred meets a print-shop window in St James's: a caricature of a Marquess with a pencil and a girl with a composing stick like a lance, captioned THE MISPRINT. She is a public joke. She laughs, then does not.
- Closing page: Agnes in her attic room under Constance's roof sharpens a pen under a night-candle, and the narrator says only that she was very good at being overlooked.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred accepts a role, on her own terms; Constance is an ally by the end of the chapter, and Agnes is a note in the margin. Closing line: "She had a way of sharpening a pen as though she were apologising to it."

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Agnes's mittens, ink-stained right finger, night-candle until two; the Florence-postmarked letter (it is the March letter Constance could not burn; pays off 19); the Society circular fee (pays for the dress; the fashionable orders it leads to pay for the Old Lady in 9); the caricature shop (Mr Treacher's); Constance's rule "gloves exist" (Winifred's thumb).

## Chapter 05: A Marquess in the Composing Room

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Julian. Pargeter's. Thursday 13 June, ten in the morning to dusk.

**Purpose.** To put Julian in Winifred's world, deepen the first knot of the Farmer secret, and bring in Lovejoy.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a Marquess in a hackney rehearsing "How do you do" for a printing house. He has brought a ham, which Mrs Hobday accepts as a pagan accepts a sacrifice.
- He is put in an apron. Winifred teaches him to set a line in the stick: type is upside down and backwards, and he sets his own name wrong end first. Orme takes him for a new hand and compliments his spacing. Toby takes him for a bailiff. Fairweather says "Aye."
- Julian watches the Gazette routine: five o'clock copy, the wet copy dried at the fire, the Drip. He finds, in Winifred's case drawer, a hatbox of old Farmer proofs with her queries in the margin and the Farmer's replies, in a clerk's fair hand, that came back to her through the Gazette's reader; she has never learned the author's name. She keeps all of them. She speaks of the Farmer with open admiration, and he finds his own prose being praised by a woman who thinks he is somebody else. He nearly says it. He doesn't, because she admires the Farmer and he wants, for once, to be liked as himself.
- Barnaby, with his foot on a stool, tells Julian about Naomi: a compositor's daughter who set the Psalms at nineteen, and whose brass composing stick Winifred uses. He asks Julian his intentions; Julian says "honourable"; Barnaby says "Quite right; nobody else's are ever any use."
- Lovejoy arrives to pay his compliments. He is exquisite: "Congratulations, my lord." He admires the Albion. He buys a copy of the Gazette off Toby for sevenpence, folds it with a magnifying glass peeping out, and says to Winifred, "A very fine W."
- Barnaby goes pale. Winifred stands very still. Julian, who understands nothing, asks.

**What changes / closing beat.** Julian learns that Pargeter's owes Lovejoy money, and that Lovejoy is, in some way he does not understand, dangerous; he also learns that he cannot help, because he has signed an Article forbidding it. Closing: he stood in a printer's yard with his hands in his pockets, obeying a contract he had himself signed, and found it was the least comfortable thing he had ever done for anyone.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the hatbox of proofs; the Farmer's identity; Lovejoy's magnifier; the W; the £740 (named by Barnaby); Julian's flaw (arranging); Naomi's stick; Orme mistaking him for a hand (pays off 23 when Orme sets the Erratum beside him).

## Chapter 06: The Crooked W

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Lovejoy then Winifred. The Phoenix Press (Ave Maria Lane), Pomfret Yard, Hand and Pen Court. Friday 14 June.

**Purpose.** To make the antagonist a craftsman, show his method, plant Mrs Rudge, and reveal the money.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a magnifying glass over a column of type at seven in the morning, and a very small man scrubbing his hands pink. Lovejoy lays the Thursday Gazette beside the previous Tuesday's, and finds the nicked foot of the W in "Miss W. Pargeter, of Paternoster Row" matches the W in her advertisement. Two lines of one measure. He sees the whole night as clearly as if he had been in the room. He is, for a moment, almost sorry.
- Backstory in two sentences: seven years as Barnaby's apprentice, and not once did Barnaby make him wash his own hands.
- Lovejoy calls at Pomfret Yard on a pretext and meets Underhill, who is blowing like a whale with pride and has doubled the print run. In the corner an old woman in grey with a pencil reads the paper, ignored. This is Mrs Rudge. She says, to nobody, "Does thee know the engagement is well founded, Ezekiel, or only that it has been printed?" Underhill bristles: "The Marquess authorised it, madam." She: "A newspaper that cannot say it was wrong cannot be trusted when it says it is right." Lovejoy watches. He decides to wait: "A fruit is picked when it falls into the hand."
- On the way home he sends his clerk, Mr Fogg, to find out who carted the sheets from Hand and Pen Court that morning, and to buy the man a quart at the Goose and Gridiron. A fruit, he remarks, is better for being asked after before it falls.
- Back at the Phoenix Press he sets the Crier himself, Friday night as always: the columns of Peregrine Pry arrive in a copyist's hand, via a boy who knows nothing. He writes "More" on the margin.
- Hand and Pen Court that evening: Winifred and Barnaby in the kitchen with the account-books. The notes: £740 at Michaelmas, payable at once if the Gazette contract is lost. Barnaby has not told her that the bond was secured on the house. The scene is quiet and awful and funny, because Mrs Hobday keeps entering with soup.
- A note from Lovejoy, delivered by a boy: "My compliments on your daughter's engagement. I find I shall require the full sum at Michaelmas. H.L."
- Closing: Lovejoy, alone, at his own case, sets one line, MISS W. PARGETER, with the Crooked W, takes a proof, folds it small, and puts it in a drawer.

**What changes / closing beat.** The reader knows Lovejoy knows; the Pargeters do not know how much; Winifred decides to use the Season to pay the notes. Closing image: the folded proof in the drawer, with a hairline gap in the W.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Mrs Rudge's saying (paid off 22); the notes; the bond on the house; Pry's copyist's hand (clue); Lovejoy's rule against lies; the folded proof (paid off 24 when he gives her a new W). Establishes: Phoenix Press; Pomfret Yard; Sowerby (a glimpse).

## Chapter 07: A Rout in the Rain

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. Ormsgill House, Piccadilly. Tuesday 18 June, the first anniversary of Waterloo, evening.

**Purpose.** The first public performance: Winifred meets society, makes two allies and an adversary, and earns the Duchess's attention.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: Winifred in Constance's altered blue-grey silk and her mother's seed-pearl brooch, rehearsing aloud in the hackney to Barnaby, who is going no further than the carriage step: "Go and be looked at." Her left thumb is in a glove two sizes too small.
- The staircase: staring, whispering, the guns in Hyde Park firing wet powder for the anniversary. Julian finds her at the head of the stairs and says, "You look as though you were about to be set in type." The first time they both laugh.
- The test of "It is in the Gazette": asked by three people in a row if she is engaged to the Marquess, Winifred says only that, and it works. Julian, asked, says the same, with a straight face. By the end of the evening it is thought a delicate reticence and by midnight a famous one.
- Lady Quarles, in raspberry silk, prowls. Verity Quarles takes Winifred behind a pillar: "I'm the previous engagement. I only wished to say thank you, and that if you are ever in want of a dull and honest friend, I'm available." Winifred has found her first ally.
- Rupert Tremayne arrives with a quizzing glass he does not need. He guesses everything in four sentences, tells her so with a bow, and says he intends to court her as a rival, honestly. She tells him to leave his hat on the table.
- The Dowager Duchess of Ormsgill summons her to the sofa. She asks if the girl sets type; does she read it backwards? She slides a card face-down on her knee. Winifred reads the back of the card from across the pillow: "Mrs Chalmers is a bore." Agnes, in her mittens, sits a pace behind the sofa holding Constance's shawl, as she always sits, and is not looked at. The Duchess laughs. "Constance. You will give a dinner. I wish to see her eat."
- The ballroom ceiling lets in the rain: a footman sets down a basin, and the plink is the Drip. Winifred begins to laugh, and Julian hears it across the room.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred is accepted by two people who matter and noticed by everyone; a dinner is ordered; Rupert has declared himself. Closing beat: Julian, across the room, turned his ring on his finger and did not know he was doing it.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the dinner; Verity's offer; Rupert's guess; the Duchess; the card, read aloud only to the sofa (Pry will quote it: clue); the formula "It is in the Gazette". Pays off in 11, 15, 21.

## Chapter 08: Queries in the Margin

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. Pargeter's and Hyde Park. Saturday 22 June.

**Purpose.** Romance by correspondence: the proof-margin conversation; Rupert enters as a rival; the first jealousy.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a table in the composing room heaped with letters addressed to "The Hon. Miss Pargeter" (there is no such title): begging letters, proposals from three curates and an eccentric of Hoxton who signs himself the Emperor of Tartary, and an offer from a lady at Cheltenham to buy her wedding-gown in advance. Toby reads them aloud. Winifred answers none.
- Saturday's Crier is among the post, and Toby reads aloud the new column by "Peregrine Pry" on the Waterloo rout, which quotes the Duchess's remark about Mrs Chalmers. Winifred stops setting. Only the sofa heard that. She runs through the sofa in her head: Constance, Verity, Rupert. She does not like where her mind rests.
- Mr Ingram of the Gazette (recovered, wheezing) brings the order to reprint the Farmer's nine letters as a pamphlet for private circulation, with the usual request for her queries in the margin. She sets her queries: "You say the labourer's bread; your figures are for the quartern loaf. Which? Also, the commas." Julian, present for his Article 2 call, stands at the stone and watches his prose being corrected by a woman who has no idea.
- She tells him she wants to meet the Farmer. "I should like to make him say he was wrong. Once." Julian says the Farmer is a bore in person. They argue about whether a man who has never been wrong writes better than one who has.
- Rupert arrives with Covent Garden pinks and an offer of a turn in Hyde Park. Winifred accepts, because the Articles want her seen, and Julian says "Quite", which is his word for No.
- The drive: a curricle in a drizzle, Rupert admitting he knows, she admitting nothing, he laughing. He is good company. He mentions Lovejoy's moneylending and that he owes the man eight hundred, as lightly as other men mention the weather.
- Back at the shop Julian is at the Albion with ink to the elbow, pretending to help Fairweather. "He is a rake." "He is a debtor. It isn't the same. I should prefer to be called a rake."
- Winifred notices that Julian, glancing at the stone, says "The quartern, not the peck" about the very figure she queried and has not yet read aloud.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred begins to like the Marquess; she has a small suspicion and puts it away. Closing: "It was a very small thing. Compositors, however, are trained to notice very small things."

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the margin queries; the Farmer's identity; Rupert's debt to Lovejoy (£800); Julian's jealousy and his word "Quite". Pays off in 12 (the pocket-book) and 15 (Rupert tells her the favour).

## Chapter 09: The Old Lady Runs Away

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Julian then Winifred. Hand and Pen Court, Ludgate Hill. Thursday 27 June, from ten in the morning.

**Purpose.** The set piece of comedy and action; the trade rallies; Winifred redeems the Old Lady herself; Julian learns what it is to do nothing and be thanked.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the sound of a bailiff's knock on a door that has not been knocked at that way in years. Mr Gribble, Lovejoy's man, with a bill of sale on the Old Lady for £60 and a dray in the court. Lovejoy has called the bill now because the sudden fashionable orders are cards and invitations that would once have gone to the Phoenix Press. Without the Old Lady Friday's Gazette run takes six hours and the cart will be late.
- Winifred argues the law; Gribble has a writ. Julian is present for his Thursday call. He wants to pay; Article 3. He stands in the yard and is useless on principle. His one weapon is his title, and it frightens Gribble about as much as a hat.
- The Old Lady is loaded; Toby insists on riding to guard her; Brevier leaps on at the last moment. The dray goes out through the arch and down Ludgate Hill, where a street organ and a drove of Smithfield sheep spook the horse. The lashings part. The wooden press, eight hundredweight of oak, is loose, and the dray is running.
- Julian runs after, Winifred after him in pattens, which she kicks off. Julian gets a hand on the tail-board and a foot on the drag chain. Toby is screaming "Your Marquessship!" The dray, press and all, fetches up in the front of Mr Mutlow's pastrycook's shop in a wave of pies.
- The crowd. A print-seller's boy sketches. Printers' apprentices from every house in the Row pour down the hill, lift the Old Lady onto their shoulders and carry her back in a procession singing a trade ditty, with Orme claiming chapel law over Gribble.
- That evening Gribble returns with the writ. Winifred pays the £60 and costs out of the cash-box, where three weeks of sudden fashionable orders (visiting cards, invitations, a bishop's sermon) have put £74, in coin, counting, with Julian watching and not paying. "You may tell Mr Lovejoy the Old Lady is redeemed and is grateful for the outing."
- Julian: "I did not pay it." "You did not. Thank you." It is the first thank-you of his life for doing nothing, and he finds he likes the feeling.

**What changes / closing beat.** The Old Lady is Winifred's; the town loves the story (Saturday's Crier will print it as a comedy); Lovejoy's first blow has made them famous. Closing: the Marquess of Stanmere, with a pie on his collar and a smear of ink across his nose, watching a woman count out sixty pounds, and not once reaching for his purse.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the Old Lady redeemed by Winifred's own earned money (pays off 14, 17); the Chapel's customs; Julian's learning not to arrange; Lovejoy's methods (attrition by delay). Mutlow's pies (motif).

## Chapter 10: A Red Sky Over Curzon Street

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Julian. Stanmere House. Sunday 30 June, evening.

**Purpose.** A quiet chapter: the first dinner at his table (Article 2), the shelf of errata, the pale rectangle on the wall, their first real talk about their dead and absent mothers.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the sunset, which is copper-red for the first time in a month of grey and has all of London out on its steps looking up. Gantry says it is an omen. Julian says it is dust.
- Winifred and Barnaby arrive in a hired hackney; Barnaby inspects the library with the gait of a man in a cathedral and falls asleep in the best chair. Minton eats with them, silently, and removes himself. The dinner itself is crossed in one paragraph (Gantry's soup, Barnaby explaining the Albion to a footman, the Drip's absence felt like a missing tooth), because the chapter proper begins when the cloth is drawn and Barnaby sleeps.
- Julian shows her his shelf of ephemera: ballads, almanacs, a Vinegar Bible leaf, and the errata slips he collects. "As a boy I thought a thing printed was true. Then I found an errata slip at the back of my father's Gibbon, and understood print could say it was sorry."
- She notices the pale rectangle on the wall between the windows, and a portrait of the sixth Marquess over the fire. She does not ask. He tells her: his mother lives in Florence; his parents' marriage was a long quarrel conducted at dinner in good manners in front of a child. "I decided at nine that I would not marry unless it were impossible not to."
- Winifred tells about Naomi, the brass stick, and the consumption that took her in the spring of 1807; the Row came to the funeral and set a hymn in type overnight.
- Rupert's courtship is discussed with great restraint. "He brought pinks." "I know. Your father told me; at length."
- At the window, the sky darkens to oxblood. She says the Farmer ought to write about it. "What would he say?" "That nobody can explain it and everybody is pretending to."

**What changes / closing beat.** Each knows one true thing about the other that no one else does. Closing: he stood on the step in the rain after the hackney had gone with the afterglow behind him, and remembered that he had signed Article 6.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the pale rectangle (Florence); the errata slips; "I decided at nine"; the red sky motif; Naomi. Pays off in 12, 19, 24.

## Chapter 11: A Dinner Afloat

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. Hertford Street. Thursday 4 July, from four to eleven at night.

**Purpose.** The set piece: the dinner at which Winifred outshines everyone.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the kitchen at Hertford Street ankle-deep in water, because a gutter has overflowed. Sixteen are to sit down at eight, and the Duchess has announced that she will arrive at half past seven, a thing nobody has seen her do in thirty years. The cook, Mrs Bunting, weeps over a dying turtle; the borrowed French hand, Monsieur Gaspard, has thrown a copper pan; Constance stands on the stairs like Moses.
- Agnes, ankle-deep and unnoticed by the stove, murmurs to nobody, "I beg your pardon, but I think the turtle is not dead." It is not. Winifred, who is passing with a bucket, hears it and is amused.
- Winifred, arrived early to be coached, takes command by the only method she knows: a printer's. Planks over the flood, buckets in a chain, the range emptied of everything that can wait, menu cards hand-lettered in ten minutes. She sets the placement cards by shape. Julian, arriving, is made to carry the turtle.
- The table: Underhill (invited by Constance to keep the Gazette sweet) seated beside her, who says "Pargeter's! My printer! The Gazette has been a matchmaker!" and takes credit for the engagement; Sir Marmaduke Crake, M.P., on the corn laws; Lady Quarles; Verity; Rupert; the Duchess at her right hand.
- Sir Marmaduke pronounces on the price of wheat. Winifred corrects his figures, politely, with the Farmer's numbers. "Madam, who is your authority?" "A Kentish farmer. I set his type." Julian chokes on a bone.
- Constance's printed menu cards (from her stationer in Bond Street, not from Pargeter's, a point of pride) have three errors: "Sallad", a stray comma after the soup, "pheasent". Winifred sees them across the table at a glance, and a fourth: every card says Hartford Street. She says nothing, and moves her thumb across the card, and lets it stand. The Duchess, who sees her do it, says in her carrying whisper: "You saw the street." "I saw it." "And?" "It is her house." The Duchess, delighted, tells the table that Constance has overdone the turtle. Constance, behind her trumpet, is proud and furious.
- Dessert: the moulded ice, which for the first time in the history of Hertford Street does not melt, because the dining-room is at fifty-two degrees. The Duchess announces she will send her carriage to the Row to engage Pargeter's for her cards.
- After the guests, Constance says only: "Well." The Duchess whispers: "Which of them knows?" "Neither." "Good. It is much the best way."
- Two days later the Crier will describe the flood in the kitchen, which only the household could have known.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred's standing is made: the Duchess's patronage will bring work. Closing: Winifred puts her gloves back on over a thumb that is still faintly blue, and finds she does not mind who sees it.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Agnes's turtle line, which Pry will print word for word (clue); the Duchess's patronage (pays off 23, 24); the Farmer's figures; the brass stick (passed round); Underhill's pride; the first stet, of a friend's error left to stand (pays off 24).

## Chapter 12: Let It Stand

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, alternating Winifred and Julian. A carriage; the arch of Hand and Pen Court. Thursday 4 July, midnight to two in the morning.

**Purpose.** The midpoint reversal. They exchange their secrets, kiss, and change what the lie is for.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the Stanmere carriage with its lamps lit, stopped by a flooded Piccadilly, two people sitting in the dark with the rain drumming on the roof.
- The coachman, Pegg, climbs down to lead the horses through the flood, which leaves the carriage a lit box with the blinds drawn. The narrator remarks that this was entirely Pegg's doing and that he will be remembered for his tact at Michaelmas.
- Winifred says she has something to tell him that is not covered by the Articles. She saw the error at 4.40 and let the cart go. She tells it plainly, all of it. Julian is silent, then says: "Then we are the same sort of coward." He tells her that when he read the paragraph he laid a pencil on it and did not mark it.
- He asks to be allowed to show her something. He takes out his pocket-book: her marginal queries, a year of them, kept and dated. She sees his commas, his figures, and knows. "You are the Farmer." "I am the Farmer." She had written to him for nine months in the margins of a sheet.
- She is moved and furious in the same breath: "You came to the Row the day after, to find out who had been correcting your commas." "And to ask who had set the type. The one reason was a pretext for the other. I have not decided which." She laughs.
- They reach the arch. The Drip is falling in the dark where the cart went out. He kisses her. Closed-door: a sentence and a half.
- She stops. "We have broken an Article." "Which?" "Six." She will not say yes tonight: "While my father's notes are Lovejoy's I will not be a woman who was bought by a dinner. Ask me at Michaelmas, when I do not need you." He agrees. "Stet." He asks her not to tell anyone the Farmer's name.
- Barnaby's voice down the speaking-tube: "Win? Is that rain or the Marquess?" "It is rain, Father."

**What changes / closing beat.** The engagement is no longer a performance; the performance is now that they are not in love. They are bound to the Articles until Michaelmas by her pride, not by the contract. Closing: a light burning in a window in Ave Maria Lane at two in the morning, where a small man was counting something.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants/payoffs: the pocket-book (planted 2, paid 12); the Farmer's name now with her (promise paid 14, 22); Michaelmas as the date of the real question (paid 23); "Stet" (paid 24). Lovejoy's lit window.

## Chapter 13: The Road to Yalding

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. The Maidstone road and Stanmere Court. Tuesday 30 July.

**Purpose.** Change of scene; the house party; Winifred sees Julian among his own people.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a two-line summary of four weeks that went by in the Row (the Season guttered out; Parliament rose; the Gazette filled August with nothing) and two people expert in not touching. Then: eleven hours on the Maidstone road in the rain.
- The party: two coaches and a cart bearing the Old Lady and two cases of type, reams of paper, Toby, Brevier in a hamper. Barnaby with his gouty foot in a bucket of warm bran; Constance with Blücher; Agnes with her writing case; Minton; Rupert and the Quarleses behind; Verity driving herself in a gig. They pick up Dr Orlando Pym at the Wrotham inn: he blames the lightning-rods on St Paul's for the weather.
- Minton, wet through in the ditch, at last has his apology: after the Crier of 6 July printed the flood in Constance's kitchen, Julian suspected him for a week (inky forefinger, anxiety, a motive of shame) and put the question baldly on the 9th; Minton cleared himself by producing a sketchbook of forty caricatures of his employer, every one of them fond. Constance dismissed Mrs Bunting for a day and reinstated her. The Marquess's apology is two sentences long and Minton keeps it like a relic.
- The coach goes into a ditch on Wrotham Hill. Julian and Rupert put their shoulders to it. The luggage cart's axle breaks; Winifred splints it with a composing rule and a gallon of beer.
- Stanmere Court at dusk: the red brick, the oast-houses with their white cowls, the drowned meadows below. No fires lit because the wood is wet. Mrs Pickett, and Mr Wragg the steward, who calls Winifred "the printer woman".
- At the gate a cluster of tenants' wives stares at the carriage. Mrs Fryer, who is eighty and cannot read, asks Winifred if she is the lady in the paper. "It is in the Gazette," says Winifred, and Mrs Fryer answers, "Then it's a lie, my dear, for I can't read it." The formula that has served her all Season cannot reach a woman who cannot read it, and for the first time it sounds like what it is. Julian gets down. He knows every name, every child, every cow. He is a different man in Kent: shorter in speech, longer in listening.
- Night: Winifred sets up the Old Lady in the old brewhouse that is the estate office, by lantern, because she cannot sleep in a house without a press. Julian crosses the yard with a lantern and does not come in.

**What changes / closing beat.** The scene has shifted from London performance to a place where the lie matters; Winifred sees that the Farmer is a landlord who knows hunger by name. Closing image: two lanterns, one in the pressroom and one in the yard, and neither moving.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the Old Lady in Kent (pays off 14, 17); Dr Pym; Wragg's hostility; the drowned meadows (pays off 17); Verity's gig; Barnaby's foot.

## Chapter 14: The Weald Relief Subscription

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Winifred and Julian. Stanmere Court. Wednesday 31 July to Saturday 3 August.

**Purpose.** Show what the Farmer's work is; give the press a purpose beyond the lie; plant Agnes's opportunity; put Trigg's account in Lovejoy's hands.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a tithe barn at six in the morning with sixty tenants and labourers in wet clothes, and a Marquess standing on a hay-cart reading a table of figures. The quartern loaf stands at thirteenpence; the hops have mouldered on the bine; Wragg grim at his elbow.
- Constance's idea: a Weald Relief Subscription, to be printed at Stanmere Court and sent to every Gazette reader and landowner in Kent. Winifred designs the broadside: a table, a plain appeal, the Farmer's figures. Barnaby oversees the Old Lady, Toby inks, Winifred pulls the bar. Orme's letter from London: Trigg the paper-merchant has sold his account to Lovejoy. The notes now stand at £1,140.
- Winifred urges Julian to sign the Farmer's letters with his name; the Marquess carries a weight a farmer doesn't. He refuses: a peer on corn is called a landlord pleading for his rents, a farmer an honest man. They quarrel kindly. She does not tell, as promised.
- Wragg is won by Winifred correcting the misprint on the tithe-barn notice that has been wrong for eleven years; he sulks, then touches his hat.
- Dr Pym lectures at dinner on lightning-rods. Verity asks him how many rods there were in 1810 and how much it rained. Rupert asks Verity to dance in a hall with the carpets up. Agnes says almost nothing.
- Midnight: the first pull of the Subscription. A hush. The narrator on the sound of a hand press: like a very slow heart. They stand either side of the stone and do not touch.
- Closing: Agnes in her shawl at the pressroom door with a candle, counting the sheets, as she counts everything. On the table lies Winifred's counterpart of the Articles, which she has left out of its case for the first time, having taken it out at midnight to look at a thumbprint. The narrator says Agnes was very good at counting, and leaves it at that.

**What changes / closing beat.** The press now serves the poor, not only the lie; the price of the debt rises; Agnes has seen the paper. Closing: Agnes counted the sheets, then the lines on the top one, then the lines on the paper beside it.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the counterpart on the table (clue); Trigg's account (£400 more); Wragg won; the Farmer's name (pays off 19, 22); "a peer on corn".

## Chapter 15: Rupert in the Hop Garden

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. Hop gardens and the estate office. Monday 5 August.

**Purpose.** The rival suitor's honest proposal; Rupert's warning; Julian's direct, adult jealousy; the paper she should have burned.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a hop garden in August, the bines blackened and fallen from the poles, and a line of Sussex women picking mould by hand for sixpence a day.
- Rupert asks to walk with her. He lays it out: £2,300 of debt (eight hundred of it to Lovejoy), an Earl who will pay no more, a Verity he likes too well to marry for money. Marry him instead. A sensible match: Lady Rupert, the press protected by an earl's name, the Marquess's blame spared. He is entirely serious, and very kind about it.
- She says no, and why: she will not be a convenient thing twice. "You are a good sort of rascal." He is delighted to be refused by her: "It is more interesting than being accepted by anyone else."
- He warns her: Lovejoy has asked him for a favour in return for his debt, and has asked about "a paper". He has not decided what to do. She should burn the Articles. She says a printer does not destroy a record.
- Julian walks up. There is no misunderstanding: "Rupert, are you asking Miss Pargeter to marry you?" "I was." "Did she accept?" "She said I was a good sort of rascal." "Quite." The two old friends argue, brotherly, for a minute and a half, in the mud; Julian tells him to go and drown himself in the river; Rupert says it is already in the river, it is the whole county.
- Alone, Julian: "Fifty-five days." "Fifty-five?" "To Michaelmas. I counted." She does not answer; she does not need to.
- Verity and Rupert, seen quarrelling happily across the hop garden.
- That night Winifred locks the counterpart in the lining of her mother's brass stick-case.

**What changes / closing beat.** Winifred knows a danger exists but not whence; Julian says he will wait, and means it; Rupert has done the right thing and is the better for it. Closing: she locked the paper in her mother's case, which was, as the narrator is bound to admit, two nights too late.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Rupert's warning; "a paper"; the stick-case; the countdown (pays off 23); Rupert and Verity (pays off 21). Red herring: Rupert as the leaker (cleared when he tells her himself).

## Chapter 16: A Wet Day for Forfeits

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, an ensemble with Agnes and Constance at the centre. The drawing-room at Stanmere Court. Wednesday 14 August, afternoon and evening.

**Purpose.** The comedic ensemble chapter and the hinge of Agnes's moral choice; Constance's forfeit gives away her secret.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the thirty-first consecutive day of rain, counted on the library chimney-piece by Dr Pym, with a pencil, in a column. Fires lit in August.
- Agnes receives, at breakfast, a letter in Lovejoy's hand: Lovejoy has quietly bought up Matthias's £312 and writes, with perfect civility, that he would be sorry to see it called in before Michaelmas; the marshal's fee; a date. She has the copy of the Articles in her trunk. She has not sent it. The narrator takes her through the whole decision without ever naming it.
- Constance, bored, calls for Forfeits. Winifred teaches the guests to set type: teams compete to set the line "The quick brown fox" backwards in the stick. Barnaby against Dr Pym; Verity against Rupert (she wins and keeps his signet as a forfeit); Julian against Toby (Julian loses to a boy of thirteen and is delighted).

**What changes / closing beat.** The warmth of the house is at its fullest at the moment it is about to be flooded; Agnes has chosen nothing yet; Constance has come within a sentence of her secret. Closing: Dr Pym, still holding his pencil, said, "That will be the rods," and nobody laughed.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: Lovejoy's letter to Agnes (clue); Constance's "regret" (pays off 19); Verity holding Rupert's signet (pays off 21); the setting of type backwards; the rising river (pays off 17).

## Chapter 17: High Water

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Julian and Winifred. Yalding meadows and the Court. Wednesday 14 August, from nine at night, to dawn on Thursday 15 August.

**Purpose.** The second-act set piece: the flood; competence under pressure; the lie and the love are tested by something real; Agnes posts the letter.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the Medway, black and wide, coming up the lawn in the light of forty lanterns.
- Julian organises: cattle off the Ham, the Fryer family on the roof of a hop-kiln. Constance commands the kitchen like an admiral. Verity counts heads. Dr Pym, to his own surprise, is useful: he has a tide-gauge in his portmanteau. Rupert and Wragg launch a hay-boat. Barnaby, in a chair on the lawn with his foot up, designs a rope-and-pulley rig for the ford out of the Old Lady's tackle.
- Winifred goes to the estate office and prints a handbill on the Old Lady: THE WATER IS UP. ALL TO HIGHER GROUND. THE CHURCH BELL WILL RING. Two hundred copies in forty minutes; Toby runs them to every door in a washtub with Brevier. It is the first time anyone has seen print do something quickly.
- The rescue: old Mrs Fryer (who cannot read, and told Winifred in 13 that the paper was a lie) and two children off the kiln roof. Julian goes into the water. He slips and is dragged under the pulley rope. Winifred hauls him out by the collar with Wragg. He coughs up half the Weald and says, "Quite."
- 3 a.m., in the tithe barn under horse blankets, the Fryers asleep on straw. Julian and Winifred hold hands in the dark; nothing is said about Michaelmas. Barnaby, wrung out, tells Julian over a bowl of broth that he would rather ask the Marquess than let his girl carry it alone, and Julian says he is bound by an Article not to; Barnaby says the Articles are wiser than they look.
- Dawn: the sky goes copper over the drowned meadows.
- Agnes, who has not slept, hands a letter to the Yalding post boy, addressed to H. Lovejoy, Ave Maria Lane. She thinks a little more sin will hardly show.

**What changes / closing beat.** The household now stands together; the engagement is real in every way but its contract; and the leak has been sent. Closing: it seemed to her that a little more sin would hardly show.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants/payoffs: the Old Lady's tackle and the Fryers' kiln; Winifred's handbill (the press as service, paid off in 23); the post boy; Barnaby's insight about the Articles; Dr Pym's gauge.

## Chapter 18: The Town Crier Cries

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, Winifred then Julian. Hand and Pen Court. Saturday 31 August.

**Purpose.** The low point: public exposure, loss of the contract, the notes called, the quarrel that breaks the engagement.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: the cry in St Paul's Churchyard at seven in the morning: THE MARQUESS'S MISPRINT! THE ARTICLES OF AGREEMENT IN FULL! Hawkers in the rain with armfuls of a sheet. (Summary, one paragraph: the last two weeks in Kent, the return to London on 22 August, three sweet weeks of Julian calling at the shop with perfect decorum.)
- Dick Sparrow, the second pressman, has not come in; Fairweather says "Aye" about this, with unusual weight. Toby brings the Crier into the composing room. The full text of the seven Articles, with "The Quizzing-Glass" by Peregrine Pry, and Sowerby's account that the young lady stood in the arch and said nothing.
- Underhill arrives in a hackney, pink and trembling: the Gazette has been made a laughingstock; Clause 9; the contract is void; the bond forfeit. He will not print a correction ("The Gazette does not retract"). Mrs Rudge is at Bath and he will not write to her. Orme sets his stick down for the first time in fifty-two years.
- Lovejoy calls, courteous: the notes, by the acceleration clause, are payable now. He will, as a kindness, wait until Michaelmas.
- Julian arrives from Curzon Street, white. He asks her to marry him on Monday by special licence; the Crier cannot libel a wife. She says a special licence is what a lord uses to make a mess disappear; she will not be disappeared.
- Then Lovejoy, leaving, mentions with a bow that the Marquess called on Tuesday in person at Ave Maria Lane and offered twice the sum for her father's paper. He refused. Julian does not deny it. (He had found at three that morning that Article 3 forbade lending but not buying, and by four hated the argument; he went anyway. It is also, Lovejoy lets fall, why the Crier appears today and not at Michaelmas: a creditor who sees a marquess coming with a cheque-book prints early.) "You signed Article 3." "I signed it before I—" "Before you what?" He cannot say it.
- She will keep the Articles to Michaelmas, because she keeps her word, and she does not wish to see him before. Julian goes out and walks the whole length of Cheapside in the rain.
- Closing image: the composing room, Barnaby asleep, the Drip falling, Winifred with her mother's stick in her hand not setting a line, and Toby, who has never in his life been known to offer food, offering her half his pie.

**What changes / closing beat.** The engagement is publicly a fraud; the contract is gone; the notes are called; Winifred and Julian are apart. Closing beat: Toby's half-pie.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants/payoffs: Article 3 (broken in spirit by Julian); Article 5 (her right to end it, held until Michaelmas by her word); Pry's column (the clue is now open); Sowerby; "disappeared".

## Chapter 19: What Lady Constance Burned

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Julian, with a page of Constance. Hertford Street and the General Post Office. Sunday 1 September.

**Purpose.** The emotional turn: Julian sees his pattern, receives his mother's last letter, and decides to act in the open.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: Julian at seven in the morning in a closed carriage outside Hertford Street while the church bells go. He goes in, unannounced.
- Constance in her wrapper, with the Crier on her knee and Blücher at her feet. She has recognised a turn of phrase in Pry's column ("I may be quite wrong, but") and has said nothing yet. They quarrel: he bought up the paper behind Winifred's back; she says that is exactly what she would have done. "You arrange people, Aunt." "Yes."
- She tells him: for twenty-one years she burned his mother's letters, one a year on his birthday, from 1795 (Cecilia addressed them to Constance, trusting her to give them to him); the first burned at his father's order, the rest from habit and fear. She kept the last, March 1816, four lines. She gives it to him. "I am at fourteen Via dei Bardi. The door is open. C." The pale rectangle on the wall has a meaning, and so does the Florence postmark Winifred read.
- His reaction is not rage; it is a slow, awful recognition. He had built a life on the belief that she never wrote. He has been letting things stand since he was nine. He sees that his own habit of arranging things for people is Constance's burning in a better coat.
- He goes to the General Post Office and writes to his mother. "Madam" is crossed out, then "Mother". Dear Mother. There has been a misprint.
- He writes his own erratum that afternoon: the tenth Farmer letter, to be signed with his name. He will not send it to the Pargeters; he will send it to Mrs Rudge.
- Constance, left alone, writes a short note to Agnes asking her to come to the morning-room.

**What changes / closing beat.** Julian understands his own pattern and stops arranging; he will act in the open and ask, not buy. Closing: the clerk at the Post Office said, "Florence, my lord? That will be two and sixpence. It is a long way." "Yes," said the Marquess. "I know."

**Plants and payoffs.** Pays off: the Florence-postmarked letter (4); the pale rectangle (10); Constance's "regret" (16); Julian's "I decided at nine" (10). Plants: the tenth Farmer letter; the letter to Cecilia (pays off 24).

## Chapter 20: Miss Fell Takes Off Her Hat

**Purpose.** Agnes's confession and redemption; the plan against Lovejoy's next stroke.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: Agnes's attic room in the early morning; a pen; a sealed letter; her white streak in the glass.
- Brief history of Peregrine Pry: two years ago, Constance's thrift, Matthias's debts, a column on the patronesses at three guineas, and the discovery that she was good. Her pride in the prose.

She hands Agnes a composing stick and a case of Great Primer and says, "Set what you have come to say." ## Chapter 21: A Brewer's Terms

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. The Quarles Black Bull Brewery, Bankside. Thursday 12 September.

**Purpose.** A comedic chapter about money earned honestly; Verity's practical friendship; Rupert and Verity's engagement; Lovejoy and Winifred's courteous collision.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: malt smell and a dray-horse the size of a church; Verity in the counting-house in her brown dress taking Winifred through the ledgers with a pencil. She has found Mr Halloran's skimming and is quietly dealing with it.
- Sir Ephraim: broad, bald, "plain terms". He likes Winifred in four minutes. The loan: £1,200 at six per cent against the Albion and the stock, and a three-year contract for all Quarles labels and handbills at a fair price, not a charity. "Is there a Marquess in this?" "There is not." "Good, I distrust them." - Rupert arrives, in an old coat, to propose to Verity, and is told to carry barrels for a month to see whether he likes it. She will buy his debts, at five per cent. "He will never forgive me, which is the foundation of a happy marriage." Her signet-forfeit from 16 is on her thumb.

He has heard about the Quarles labels.

Closing: the Black Bull strongbox, cashed that afternoon and carried home on the dray to lie under Mrs Hobday's bed beside the hymn-book, and the thought that the person she most wanted to tell was the one she had asked not to come.

**Plants and payoffs.** Plants: the loan (pays off 23); Rupert and Verity (pay off 24); the carter; Lovejoy's pause (pays off 23).

## Chapter 22: The Quaker and the Clause

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, close on Winifred. The counting-room at Pomfret Yard. Tuesday 24 September.

**Purpose.** The hearing: Winifred tells the truth, publicly, in her own name.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: a post-chaise from Bath in the yard, and a very small old woman in Quaker grey being lifted out like a parcel. Mrs Rudge is back (Winifred's letter reached her on the fifth). Underhill is already inside, distressed and pink. Lovejoy attends, by Underhill's invitation, with a document. Julian sits outside on a bale on the wet steps, because Winifred has refused to have him in the room.
- Underhill presents: the contract is void, the bond forfeit; Lovejoy offers to take the work at a lower price. Mrs Rudge asks Winifred to tell it.
- Winifred tells everything: the Drip, the wet copy, Toby and the cat, the pied page, the two lines of one measure, 4.40, the arithmetic of stopping, the cart. "I let it go." Lovejoy: "The compositor's honesty is touching, madam, and arrives four months late." "Yes."
- Mrs Rudge: "Why now?" "Because the Gazette is the only paper in London whose owner reads it with a pencil."
- Mrs Rudge's ruling: Clause 9 is a rule that teaches printers to hide; it is struck. The Gazette will print its first erratum in nineteen years on Saturday; the compositor who made the error will set it and sign it. The Farmer's tenth letter will run beside it, if the Farmer cares to sign. Underhill protests. "Then thee will have the honour of doing it for the first time."
- Lovejoy says the Crier will print Sowerby's account at six on Saturday. Mrs Rudge: "Print it, Hector Lovejoy. Thee has not told a lie in thirty years; I have watched." The Gazette will be carted at a quarter past four, an hour early, and be on the streets before the Crier's six. That has never been done.
- Outside, Julian on his bale. "Well?" "I have been given until Saturday to say I was wrong in type the size of a door." "May I—" "Dick Sparrow has gone to Lovejoy. We are short a pressman. You may pull the bar." "I should be honoured." "You will be paid nothing." "I haven't forgiven you," she says. "I know." "I have forgiven the bar-pulling."

**What changes / closing beat.** The contract is restored on better terms; the cost is Winifred's name on the record; the race is set. Closing: he took off his coat and put it over the bale to keep the rain off a stranger's newspaper.

**Plants and payoffs.** Pays off Mrs Rudge's saying (6); Winifred's secret (1, 12); the Farmer's name (14). Plants: the quarter-past-four cart; Dick Sparrow's defection; the bar.

## Chapter 23: The Saturday Number

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, an ensemble led by Winifred and Julian. Pargeter's. Friday 27 September, from five in the afternoon, to Saturday 28 September, about half past six in the morning.

**Purpose.** The climax: the whole book's cast, the roof, the press, the proofs and the proposal.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: two in the afternoon (Mrs Rudge has ordered the copy early), the copy arriving in a pile on the stone like the pieces of a puzzle. Winifred's own Erratum. The Farmer's tenth letter, signed JULIAN, MARQUESS OF STANMERE, in Minton's fair copy with the Marquess's commas restored. Agnes's column, A. FELL. Underhill's grudging notice. The rain is back. Barnaby has hung a bucket on a nail under the Drip.
- The crew: Orme composing, deaf and perfect; Fairweather and Julian at the Albion; Barnaby inking; Mrs Hobday with tea and hymns; Toby with a paper hat and a fresh pie; Agnes as reader. Verity arrives at ten with a hamper of porter and a ledger, to witness whatever Lovejoy brings. Rupert carries paper. Constance arrives at midnight with Blücher and takes the chair by the fire. The Duchess's footmen wait in the court: "My footmen are idle. Send them to the corners."
- 1.10 a.m.: a slate goes. The Drip becomes a deluge onto the stone. Winifred and Julian hold the oilcloth over the forme with their backs and their arms; two sorts are drowned; she resets them by feel.
- 2 a.m.: the Albion's toggle collar cracks. Barnaby is on his knees; there is nothing in the shop of the right metal. Julian takes off his signet ring and wedges it in the collar. It holds.
- 2.30: Lovejoy arrives with Gribble and a formal demand under the acceleration clause. (The law forbids Gribble to touch the Albion before sunrise, as Gribble says apologetically; the stroke is delay: a receipt to be written, interest reckoned to the day, an old man's signature.) Winifred, who expected it, brings the Black Bull strongbox from under Mrs Hobday's bed and counts the sum out on the stone, aloud, beside Lovejoy, who counts it back; it is correct. Verity writes the receipt. He turns to go, then comes back and tells Winifred that the word-spacing in her third column is uneven, and no reader will notice and he cannot bear it. She respaces it. He nods. He leaves.
- The Erratum, in Great Primer, set by Winifred and Toby (his first line) and Orme, proofed by Agnes: the text is given in full. It names the error, names the compositor, says she saw it and did not stop it, and begs the Marquess's pardon and the readers'. The Gazette of that Saturday is the only newspaper in London in which the Marquess of Stanmere is, at the hour of printing, correctly engaged to no lady whatever; by half past six that will have changed.
- 4.15: the last sheet; the cart; Sowerby on the box. Winifred climbs up beside him with the first copy on her knees. Sowerby, red to the ears, says he only told the clerk what he saw and wishes he had kept his tongue and his quart. "You said what was true," says Winifred. "That is why it has been so difficult." This time she goes with it.
- Dawn: she returns on foot from Pomfret Yard with the sun a pale disc over the dome. In the pressroom Julian has already asked Barnaby, in the kitchen over the dregs of the tea; she finds him sitting on the stairs with a bent ring in his hand. He asks properly: by banns, in her parish and his, three Sundays, her father giving her away, no one paying anything. She says it is the first time anyone has asked her instead of arranging.

**What changes / closing beat.** The record is set straight; the debt is paid by her own hand; the question is asked when she does not need the answer. Closing line: "Yes," said Winifred.

**Plants and payoffs.** Pays off: the Drip (1); the cart (1); the Crooked W (6); Lovejoy's craft (6); the Duchess's carriage (11); Verity's loan (21); the brass stick (3); the ring (2, 5, 10); Orme taking Julian for a hand (5); Toby's secret (1); the Old Lady's tackle (17); Julian's learning not to arrange (9).

## Chapter 24: If Any of You Know Cause

**Viewpoint / place / when.** Arch narrator, wide, then close on Winifred. St Vedast, Foster Lane; Stanmere House; the vestry. Sunday 29 September to Thursday 17 October.

**Purpose.** The resolution; the emotional landing; everyone paid off.

**Beats.**
- Opening image: St Vedast on Michaelmas Sunday, the clerk reading the banns for the first time in a wet silence (the vicar, Mr Lathom, was fetched from his dinner on the Saturday by Toby and Barnaby to take the notice, and has not stopped smiling since). "If any of you know cause or just impediment." Lovejoy in the back pew. Nobody speaks. After the service he hands Winifred a flat parcel: a single new capital W cast in best type metal. "The old one has a cracked foot."
- Saturday's Gazette sold out by nine and was reprinted on Monday; by the wedding the circulation stands at three thousand. The Crier's account of Sowerby appears at six and nobody wants it. Underhill has framed the Erratum in gilt. Mrs Rudge appoints Agnes weekly columnist, A. Fell. The Duchess's cards are on order. The Farmer's name is now Julian's and the Weald Subscription has reached nineteen hundred pounds.
- The banns are read on the same three Sundays (29 September, 6 and 13 October) in both St Vedast and St George's, Hanover Square; Julian's mother's arrival on the 15th, a tall woman in a travelling-cloak at the Curzon Street door. "You are taller." Constance with Blücher in her arms and her eyes shut, then open.
- The wedding, Thursday 17 October, eleven o'clock. Barnaby in a new coat gives her away. Toby carries the ring on a composing-stick tray. Brevier in the vestry. Orme has set the wedding card; Fairweather will play the fiddle at the breakfast. Mrs Rudge stands in the porch under an umbrella, because Friends do not go into steeple-houses and she would not miss it. The Duchess, Verity and Rupert, Agnes, Minton, Dr Pym (who blames the rods for the lull in the rain). The narrator promises sun and refuses to lie: it did not shine. It stopped raining for the length of the vows, which in 1816 amounted to nearly the same thing.
- The vestry: the register. The clerk has written "Pargiter". Julian, whose whole life has been pencil in hand, reaches for the pen to correct it. Winifred puts her inky left thumb on his hand. "Stet." One error stays on the record by choice.
- The wedding breakfast, in the printing house, because she would not have it elsewhere. A final glance at the Drip, which has been mended, and which she finds she misses.

**What changes / closing beat.** Everything is paid. The final emotional note: the woman who dels everything lets one error stand, and the man who lets everything stand corrects himself in public. Closing image: the entry in the register, the one misspelled name, left as it was, and the last word of the book is "stet".

**Plants and payoffs.** Pays off: stet (3, 12); the pocket-book; the letter from Florence (19); the new W (6); the Duchess (7, 11); Verity and Rupert (21); Agnes (20); the Drip (1); the lull in the rain; "It is in the register".
