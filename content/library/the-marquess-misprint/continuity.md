# Continuity log: The Marquess's Misprint

Each chapter's writer appends the facts it establishes: names, descriptions, places, dates, objects, promises, who knows what.

## Ch 1: A Page in Pie
- Wed 5 June 1816, 5 p.m., to about 5.15 a.m. Thu 6 June. Minton's boy (about 11, coat cut for a brother of 15) brings the copy slip, damp, clerk's fair hand, 5 p.m. The second Drip (8 p.m.) blots the name-line; Winifred sets the rest from having read it aloud.
- The Drip: copper preserving-pan on the imposing stone, falls every four seconds from a broken slate, eleven feet; Toby empties it into the pail by the door every quarter hour; Mrs Hobday says grace over it. Brevier: grey, torn ear.
- Winifred's own standing advert (since Lady Day, foot of third column): FAIR COPYING in a plain hand, for authors, lawyers and composers of music / Miss W. Pargeter, of Paternoster Row. The W has a nicked left foot, hairline gap; a new sort is fourpence; she has meant to replace it since March.
- Ingram has a quinsy and mustard plaster; Underhill's rule is no proofs to Pomfret Yard after midnight; compositors proof their own this week. No proof was pulled: forme locked about 12.45, presses began 1.30, last sheet 4.10. Winifred told Barnaby down the tube "It is as clean a page as I have ever set."
- Toby dropped page two at 11 p.m. (pie in one hand, loose cord Winifred told him on Monday to reknot, tripped over or by Brevier). He told only Brevier, in the coal-hole. Winifred thinks the cord parted. Mutlow's beefsteak pie landed face down on the page.
- Pressmen: Fairweather (enormous, says "Aye") and Dick Sparrow (whistles through a gap in his front teeth; lately wears a clean neckcloth the Chapel has remarked on and he has not explained). Orme slept through the fall.
- 4.40 a.m.: Fairweather hands her a sheet hung since two and a heel of bread; she reads the Misprint and counts: a reprint means a cart at about nine, Clause 6 (first breach 11 May, in rain; Underhill dined three times by Lovejoy since Lady Day) against Clause 9. The narrator promises she tells what she thought once, plainly, to the one with a right to it.
- The cart: Sowerby (bent, face like an old saddle, oilskin, horse Jehu, 19 years on the box) asks "Is there anything the matter with the load?" She stands in the arch with the sheet and says nothing; he looks back once from the corner of Ave Maria Lane. Barnaby's hand-bell rings upstairs (tea and Gazette news, every morning at that hour).
- Nobody knows her choice; Sowerby saw her stand silent. Closing line: "She had good boots and did not use them."

## Ch 2: Cards on the Salver
- Thu 6 June, 8 a.m., Stanmere House breakfast-room. Gantry (60) brings the salver: nine cards yesterday, three of them letters; forty-one on the Monday before Lady Quarles began to talk. Julian up since five, cold mutton chop, Corn Returns; Gazette came at seven by a running boy from Pomfret Yard; he reads with an H pencil sharpened at five.
- Julian's looks as in the bible; he corrects signboards under his breath. Turns the griffin signet when thinking and does not know he does it.
- The Farmer: first letter 14 Sept 1815, nine so far; manuscripts go by Minton in plain cover to Ingram, thence to the Row. W.P.'s queries come back in a small upright hand; Julian pictured a man of sixty with a cold. Pocket-book: green morocco, about ninety queries cut from nine sets of proofs with nail-scissors, each pasted on a leaf and dated by him ("A semicolon is not a courtesy"; "Delete 'deplorable'").
- Minton: 35, immaculate, ink on first finger of right hand, watch checked every four minutes. He sent the denial straight to the Row, not by Pomfret Yard, because Julian altered the last sentence till a quarter to four and the counting-room shuts at four. His draft correction begins "Sir, the Marquess of Stanmere, having observed". Julian says "In a moment" twice.
- Constance: arrives with one-eyed Blücher, lorgnette and tortoiseshell trumpet; "It is a question of arithmetic"; "Arrangement is for people who cannot wait"; she is at home to nobody at Hertford Street until she has thought, and the name of the thing is Pargeter. "It is how your father began."
- Verity's note: "Thank you. V.Q." in a square upright hand, red wafer, brought from Bankside by a very small brewery boy on a dray-horse. Lady Quarles lives in Brook Street.
- Rupert (29): lemon silk waistcoat, tosses a guinea, a bowing tailor is a tailor about to sue. Present with Constance; assumes Julian will correct. Julian: "I have been engaged since seven o'clock this morning."
- Julian laid his pencil flat across "entirely well founded" and made no dele. Closing: "Minton, I should like to know everything there is to know about the firm of Pargeter." "The printers, my lord?" "The printers."

## Ch 3: Articles of Agreement
- Fri 7 June, 10 a.m. Breakfast in the basement kitchen; Mrs Hobday serves rashers by hymn verse. Barnaby was told Thu evening in the parlour: a joke ("marry above the stone"), a silence, a second helping; then "Win. Whatever you did. I should have mended the roof." He knows she made the error; does not know she saw it.
- Underhill's letter 10.10: sold out in the Strand by nine, 600 more ordered from the standing forme, stopped by two bishops; Clause 9 cannot bite while the engagement is not shown false.
- Julian calls on foot with Minton, two umbrellas; Toby takes them for bailiffs. Winifred: "I made it. My father did not." Julian: "I assumed it from the W." He corrects the comma after "Lordship" in her letter and slips: "A semicolon is a courtesy" (W.P.'s line turned round). She does not catch it yet.
- The bargain: Julian proposes "stet", "Let it stand"; she asks for Michaelmas (29 Sept, "a Quarter Day", true but not her reason) and the sole right to end it; he is to be jilted by a printer's daughter. "I do not tell lies aloud. It is a rule."
- Winifred sets the Articles at the case without copy (she has set eleven novels in three volumes for the libraries). She put "Miss Pargeter" for "lady" and refused a fund at Child's. Orme and Fairweather told: "The Chapel keeps it." Article 6 set last, in plain Pica. Heading: "ARTICLES OF AGREEMENT, made at Hand and Pen Court, Paternoster Row, on the seventh day of June, 1816, between the Most Noble Julian, Marquess of Stanmere, and Miss Winifred Pargeter, printer." Imprint: Printed by B. Pargeter, Hand and Pen Court, Paternoster Row. The seven Articles are printed in full in the chapter, as in the bible.
- Two counterparts pulled on the Albion at noon on foolscap (Fairweather pulled the second); Barnaby witnessed and wept with laughter at Article 6. Winifred keeps the second, to lie flat in the lid of her mother's brass stick-case; Julian has the first.
- Julian signed with "I never fall in love; it is a family trait." His left thumb left an inky print beside Article 6 on Winifred's counterpart; she did not wipe it. She has been "engaged for twenty-nine hours".
- Toby at the door: "Are you really going to marry our Win?" Julian: "I am assured that it is in the Gazette." Winifred adopts the phrase.

## Ch 4: Lady Constance Inspects the Goods
- Mon 10 June, 11.15 a.m., Hertford Street morning-room: cold, 140 stuffed birds shot by the late General Sir Hugh Dyer (griffon vulture, two flamingoes gone the colour of old soap). Butler unnamed. Constance summoned Winifred by note ("She is not asked whether she is free"); copy arrives at three. Blücher rests his chin on Winifred's ankle.
- Agnes Fell: 41, thin, long neck, white streak at left temple, mittens indoors, apologises to the teapot ("I beg your pardon"), twelve years in the house. She heard everything said in the morning-room, including Constance saying the engagement is not true; she does not know the terms.
- Winifred reads Constance's desk from nine yards: poulterer's bill (Leadenhall, 2 pounds 11 shillings, sent twice), the sweep's, and a cover with a FIRENZE postmark in a lady's hand running uphill, directed to Lady Constance Dyer, Hertford Street, "My dear". Constance turns it face down; Agnes's cup clinks. (This is the March 1816 letter.)
- Constance has known since 8 a.m. Thursday; does not ask; "I inform you that it will be done well." She coaches "It is in the Gazette" (a small pause before "Gazette").
- Winifred refuses eight gowns. Compromise: the Society for the Relief of Distressed Gentlewomen circular (to 1,500 persons; the Bond Street printer misspelt "distressed"), 6 pounds 10 shillings at ordinary rates; Winifred plans a blue-grey silk at 4s 6d the yard from a Cheapside man. Constance's lessons: gloves ("They exist"; wear them always; hand at side; remove at table when a lady does), never say "Marchioness", do not dance, choose a point to rest the eyes on. "He will behave like a man who intends to be jilted. Do not let him enjoy it."
- Mr Treacher's print-shop, St James's Street (Winifred set his handbills): THE MISPRINT, hand-coloured: heron-faced Marquess correcting Venus with a pencil; short girl in sabots on a flying Gazette with a composing stick couched like a lance, hair like a mop; balloon "My dear Madam, a semicolon!"; "Entirely Well Founded. Price One Shilling Plain, Two Coloured." A lavender boy of twelve recognises her by her hair. Rain stopped that afternoon.
- Winifred's left thumb is hidden by a glove two sizes too small; the print did not draw the thumb.
- Agnes's attic: top of the last flight; night-candle burns till two; right mitten off shows a finger stained blue-black to the second joint and a callus; penknife, goose quill. Closing line: "She had a way of sharpening a pen as though she were apologising to it."

## Ch 5: A Marquess in the Composing Room
- Thu 13 June. Julian's call under Article 2 (the first, Tue 11 June, lasted eleven minutes). Hackney up Ludgate Hill; he rehearses "How do you do" twenty times. Brings a 14-lb York ham (elevenpence the pound, Leadenhall): Article 3 says money, not swine. Mrs Hobday receives it ("Cast thy bread upon the waters"). Minton's inquiries: house founded 1781, two presses, a singing Methodist cook; no debts found.
- Julian in Barnaby's leather apron sets "Eremnats Nailuj"; Orme takes him for a new hand ("space like a man taught by somebody that hated rivers"; "Clerkenwell?" "Harrow"); Julian keeps the proof folded behind his watch. Toby makes him Keeper of the Pan. Fairweather lets him pull the bar on the Society circular (spelt correctly). Right cuff inked grey-black wrist to button; he leaves it.
- The hatbox: faded green pasteboard bonnet-box tied with tape, lid "K.F. 9", in the long drawer under Winifred's frame. Nine bundles in red tape, her queries (she counts ninety-one), Minton's slip "The author is obliged. He means I.", her unsent pencil "Not at all.", marginal "True." and "I have seen the loaf." She pictures the Farmer as a large red-faced man with a dog (or a clergyman with gout); Ingram says only "a gentleman in the Weald with a head for figures".
- She quotes the Farmer: "The price of corn is spoken of in this country as we speak of the weather..." and says she wanted a semicolon after "weather"; he refused; she was right to lose. Julian nearly says "The Farmer is..." and says "a very fortunate man. To be so read." She still does not know his name. He wants once to be liked for something he did badly in good faith.
- Barnaby: Naomi Lamb set the Psalms at nineteen in Long Primer, back room in Warwick Lane over a chandler's, four months; Barnaby read the proofs for a fortnight; she died spring 1807 of a consumption; Winifred took the stick the week after the funeral, aged 17. "What are your intentions?" "Honourable." "Quite right. Nobody else's are ever any use."
- Lovejoy calls at 3.45 (snuff-coloured coat, two pairs of spectacles on forehead, magnifier on a ribbon, hands scrubbed pink): apprentice seven years, neighbour twenty-six; admires the Albion ("handsomest folly in London"); nods to the Old Lady; buys Toby's relic copy of the Gazette of 6 June (Toby sells them at a shilling) for sevenpence counted coin by coin; folds it with the glass showing; says "A very fine W."
- Barnaby tells Julian: 740 pounds to Lovejoy at six per cent, falling due Michaelmas. Julian's hand goes to his cheque-book on Child's; Winifred says "Article the Third" without looking up. She says "Quite" to him in his own voice.
- Closing: Julian alone by the pump, ink on his cuff, hands in pockets, bound by his own Article; "the least comfortable thing he had ever done for anyone."

## Ch 6: The Crooked W
- Fri 14 June, 7 a.m. Lovejoy (48, five feet two, yellow soap, hands washed three times) in the counting-room above the Phoenix Press (bow window of 112 panes, six presses, forty hands). Compares Gazettes of Tue 4, Thu 6, Tue 11 June; the Crooked W in the Misprint matches the W in her advert; two lines of one measure; he reconstructs the whole night. Barnaby bought the fount new in 1782; Lovejoy bound to him at fifteen in 1783. His clerk is Mr Fogg (11 years, long, damp, agreeable).
- Pomfret Yard: Lovejoy places a 4-guinea, seven-line advertisement as a pretext. Underhill (55, pink, organ voice): run 1,100 plus 600; "The Gazette does not retract. It clarifies"; "nineteen years and a month"; hints Pargeter's "two presses and a roof" are narrow; means to double the run at Michaelmas.
- Mrs Rudge (grey, white cap, better ear turned, pencil, hard chair by the stove; Lovejoy has known her by sight six years and was never introduced): "Does thee know the engagement is well founded, Ezekiel, or only that it has been printed?" and "A newspaper that cannot say it was wrong cannot be trusted when it says it is right." Lovejoy decides to wait: "A fruit is picked when it falls into the hand."
- Lovejoy sends Fogg to buy Sowerby a quart at the Goose and Gridiron, St Paul's Churchyard, and talk about horses; Fogg may name Lovejoy if asked. Sowerby was in the court at five on the morning of the 6th.
- Pry: The Quizzing-Glass arrives 4 p.m. every Friday at the Phoenix Press from a boy of ten, fetched from a hole in a wall in Cursitor Street, in a law-stationer's round hand. Lovejoy has never asked who Pry is. This week's column mocks Treacher's print (411 impressions sold). He corrects a comma and writes "More."
- Kitchen, 4 p.m.: cash-box 11 pounds 4s 7d. Three notes: 300 pounds (29 Sept 1814), 240 (Lady Day 1815), 200 (Michaelmas 1815) = 740 at six per cent, due this Michaelmas, renewed in March. Clause: payable on demand if Barnaby "shall cease, by whatever cause, to be the printer of the Morning Gazette". The 500-pound bond is secured on the house (signed 1812); Barnaby fails to tell Winifred, interrupted by Mrs Hobday (pease, loaf, ham bone and turnip).
- Lovejoy's note, 4.40 p.m., by a boy with a phoenix button: "Mr Pargeter,—My compliments on your daughter's engagement. I find I shall require the full sum at Michaelmas. H.L." Winifred counts 107 days and resolves to print a Season: cards, dinner-lists, funeral tickets; "I shall leave a card." Barnaby still has not told her about the bond.
- Lovejoy, 1 a.m., nicks a Long Primer W by hand (eleven minutes, three trials), sets MISS W. PARGETER, pulls a proof, folds it to wafer size and locks it in the drawer with the three notes. Only Lovejoy knows how the error happened, of those outside the shop.

## Ch 7: A Rout in the Rain
- Tue 18 June, evening, Ormsgill House, Piccadilly; the Park guns fire wet powder. Barnaby rides to the carriage step only: "Go and be looked at, Win. It is what a title-page is for."
- Winifred in the blue-grey silk remade by Constance’s dressmaker, her mother’s seed-pearl brooch, her mother’s brass rule through her hair ("A pin is a pin"), left glove two sizes too small. Stair of 41 marble steps; gossips quote Treacher’s print.
- Julian at the head of the stairs: "You look as though you were about to be set in type." "Imposed." "Justified." Their first shared laugh. Both answer every question with "It is in the Gazette"; by 11 p.m. a "famous reticence". Mrs Chalmers: broad, puce, wears Colonel Chalmers’s Waterloo medal.
- Lady Quarles: 50, raspberry silk, faints at routs. Verity (brown dress, spectacles on a chain, keeps the brewery books): "I’m the previous engagement ... a dull and honest friend." Winifred told Verity of the 740 pounds debt. Verity knows.
- Rupert guessed everything in four sentences ("a W where a Q ought to have been"); Winifred admitted nothing; he will court her "honestly, as a rival"; "Leave your hat on the table." Rupert knows.
- The Duchess wrote "Mrs Chalmers is a bore" on a card with a gold pencil; Winifred read it aloud in a murmur. Duchess’s whisper: "She has been a bore since Trafalgar, and tonight she is a bore with a medal." At the sofa: Constance, Verity, Rupert, and Agnes a pace behind holding Constance’s shawl, unregarded.
- Duchess: "I have forgotten more engagements than you have attended"; orders Constance’s dinner for Thursday 4 July, arriving 7.30. The ballroom ceiling drips every four seconds into a silver basin; Winifred laughs; Julian hears across the room, turning his signet unknowingly.

## Ch 8: Queries in the Margin
- Sat 22 June. Letters to "The Hon. Miss Pargeter" (no such title) go in a box marked "Hon." under the stone: curates, the Emperor of Tartary of Hoxton, Mrs Hepplethwaite of Cheltenham. A Walworth milliner asks an apprenticeship for her daughter, 14; Winifred pocketed that letter, unanswered.
- The Crier of 22 June, "The Quizzing-Glass" by Peregrine Pry: mocks her brass rule ("who is to be ruled"), uses "I beg your pardon, but", quotes the Duchess’s Mrs Chalmers remark verbatim. Winifred counts the sofa as Constance, Verity, Rupert (not the shawl-holder) and her suspicion rests on Rupert.
- Ingram (long, yellow, quinsy, muffler) brings the order: the nine Farmer letters reprinted as a pamphlet, 300 copies, Pica, with her queries; he plainly knows Julian is the Farmer. Julian called "a call in arrears".
- Winifred has queried the Farmer 91 times; three concessions, all commas; "a man who has never been wrong in print is a wall"; she wants him to say he was wrong, once. Her query on letter VII (peck against quartern loaf) was sealed in a plain cover, read aloud to nobody. Julian cannot read upside down.
- Rupert, with Covent Garden pinks, drove her to Hyde Park (borrowed greys, tiger of nine); Julian said "Quite" (meaning no). Rupert said he owes Lovejoy 800 pounds and reads the Crier. She asked nothing.
- At the shop Julian, in shirtsleeves, helped Fairweather at the Albion. Leaving, he said of the sealed cover, "The quartern, not the peck." She noticed and put it away.

## Ch 9: The Old Lady Runs Away
- Thu 27 June. Gribble (stout, civil, brown coat) serves Lovejoy’s bill of sale (14 July 1815, 60 pounds) on the Old Lady; the notice of 10 June sat unopened under Brevier. Tender could be made only at the Phoenix Press, and Lovejoy was at Pomfret Yard. Cash-box 54 pounds 11s; the Bishop’s chaplain paid 19 pounds 10s at three.
- Julian, bound by Article 3, stood useless ("I am the Marquess of Stanmere" frightened Gribble "about as much as a hat"). Dick Sparrow whistled by the pump in a very clean neckcloth and did not help; Fairweather noticed.
- The dray (Old Lady, Toby, Brevier) was bound for Lovejoy’s warehouse in Bridge Street; a street organ and sheep spooked the horse on Ludgate Hill; Julian held the drag chain; crash into Mutlow’s pastrycook’s (41 pies). Apprentices carried the Old Lady home to a ditty; Orme claimed a solace of a gallon of porter against Gribble.
- Evening: Winifred paid 60 pounds (57 guineas and 3s) and 2 pounds costs out of 74 pounds 1s; about 12 pounds left. "The Old Lady is redeemed, and is grateful for the outing."
- Julian: "I did not pay it." "You did not. Thank you." His first thanks for doing nothing. Pie on his collar, ink across his nose.

## Ch 10: A Red Sky Over Curzon Street
- Sun 30 June, about 8.45 p.m. First copper-red sunset (first sun since 11 May). Gantry (60) says omen, Julian says dust. Stanmere footmen are all called John. Dinner at Stanmere House for Winifred and Barnaby (Constance absent); Barnaby asleep in the library wing chair.
- Library: shelf of ephemera, Josiah Tapp’s Last Dying Speech ("since been Reprieved"), a leaf of the Vinegar Bible, a box of 211 errata slips. At nine Julian found an erratum slip in his father’s Gibbon: "print could say it was sorry."
- A pale rectangle between the windows; his mother’s portrait lies in a crate in the cellar. Mother in Florence since autumn 1794, "I am told, well" (by Constance); "Does she write?" "No." His parents’ "long quarrel conducted at dinner"; "I decided, at nine, that I would not marry unless it were impossible not to."
- Winifred: the rule in her hair was her mother Naomi’s; Naomi set the Psalms at 19 and died March 1807; the Row set a hymn the night before the funeral in eleven founts; Winifred keeps the sheet in her Bible. She took off her gloves; the thumb is blue-black.
- At the window: the Farmer would say "that nobody can explain it and everybody is pretending to." Julian had thought the same and said nothing.
- Closing: Julian alone on the step in the rain remembered Article 6; his laugh was "used up".

## Ch 11: A Dinner Afloat
- Thu 4 July, Hertford Street; the kitchen under 11 inches of water. Cook Mrs Bunting; M. Gaspard (cook borrowed from Ormsgill). Winifred ran the rescue like a printing house; Verity ran the bucket chain; place-cards lettered on hat-box lids. The Duchess arrived at 7.29 (early) as Julian carried the live turtle across the hall.
- Agnes, to nobody: "I beg your pardon, but I think the turtle is not dead." Only Winifred heard. The narrator notes that the Crier of Sat 6 July will describe the flood.
- Sixteen at table, 52 degrees, stuffed heron: Constance, Duchess, Winifred (Underhill on her other hand), Julian opposite, Sir Marmaduke Crake M.P. and lady, Sir Ephraim and Lady Quarles, Verity, Rupert, Mr and Mrs Underhill, two widows, Colonel Fenn (one arm), Agnes at the foot. Barnaby stayed home with the Drip.
- Underhill: "The Gazette has been a matchmaker." Winifred to Sir Marmaduke: loaf 13d; labourer 9s; 7s 7d of bread leaves 1s 5d; "A Kentish farmer. I set his type." Julian choked on a pheasant bone.
- Menu cards (Pratt of Bond Street): Hartford Street, a stray comma, Pheasent, Sallad. Winifred’s thumb made no dele: her first stet. Duchess: "You saw the street." "It is her house." "Constance, you have overdone the turtle." The Duchess will engage Pargeter’s for her cards.
- Hall, after eleven: Duchess to Constance, "Which of them knows?" "Neither." "Good. It is much the best way." Constance sends Julian to take Winifred home in the Stanmere carriage. Winifred’s thumb is still faintly blue and she does not mind who sees.

## Ch 12: Let It Stand
- Midnight to 2 a.m., 5 July. The Stanmere carriage stalls in flooded Piccadilly; coachman Pegg (50, walnut face) wades to lead the horses, leaving the blinds drawn; he will be remembered at Michaelmas for his tact.
- Winifred confessed: she saw the error at 4.40 on 6 June, did the arithmetic of Clauses 6 and 9, let the cart go; Sowerby asked about the load and looked back once. Barnaby does not know she saw it.
- Julian: "Then we are the same sort of coward"; he laid a pencil on the paragraph and said "In a moment" twice. He showed her the green pocket-book (103 of her queries); she knows he is the Farmer. He asked her to tell no one the name, not even Barnaby. Minton’s slip "He means I" was his idea.
- Under the arch of Hand and Pen Court, about 1.30 a.m., Julian kissed her; she kept her mother’s rule. "We have broken an Article." "Which?" "Six."
- Winifred will not say yes while her father’s notes are Lovejoy’s: "Ask me at Michaelmas. When I do not need you." Julian: "Stet." 87 days to Michaelmas. She now understands the Duchess’s "Which of them knows?"
- Barnaby on the speaking-tube: "Is that rain, or the Marquess?" "It is rain, Father." Julian was in the court.
- Closing: at 2 a.m. a candle burns in the Phoenix Press window; Lovejoy is counting something.

## Ch 13: The Road to Yalding
- Tue 30 July, left Hand and Pen Court 5 a.m.; 61 days to Michaelmas (60 after midnight). July: Season guttered out, Julian called Tue and Fri with perfect decorum (once touched her sleeve, not her hand). Mrs Hobday, Orme, Fairweather, Sparrow stay in London to keep the shop and the Gazette.
- Seating: Stanmere travelling-coach (Pegg): Constance, Blücher, Agnes with her writing-case, Julian. Hired yellow second coach: Barnaby (gouty foot in a bucket of warm bran), Winifred, Minton, later Dr Pym. Waggon of Heaver, carrier of Bermondsey: the Old Lady, two cases (Pica, Great Primer), eleven reams of paper, Toby, Brevier in a hamper. Quarleses follow, Rupert on a borrowed hack, Verity drives her own gig.
- Dr Orlando Pym: long, mild, fifty-odd, green-black clergyman's coat, living on the Medway; joined at the Bull, Wrotham, about 2 p.m.; portmanteau that clanks (his tide-gauge: brass staff, float and bell, used in ch 17); went to petition St Paul's to take down its lightning-rods and met a verger; says it has rained 86 days of 91 since 1 May.
- Coach slid into the ditch half-way down Wrotham Hill; Minton fell in, his watch stopped at five to three. Julian and Rupert lifted the coach; Sir Ephraim directed ("Plain terms, gentlemen, together").
- Minton told Winifred: Julian suspected him of being Pry after the Crier of 6 July and asked baldly on Tue 9 July; Minton produced a sketchbook of forty fond caricatures (Julian as heron, as Atlas with a ham, correcting the Archbishop's signboard). Julian's apology: "I accused you on no evidence but your fingers, which are inky because you work for me. I am sorry for it, and I should like the one with the ham, if you can spare it." Constance dismissed Mrs Bunting for a day. Winifred still does not know who Pry is.
- Cart axle broke at the foot of the hill at 4 p.m. Winifred splinted it with two hop-poles, wet page-cord, wedges driven like quoins, and her mother's brass rule laid along the break (rule unharmed, back in her hair); six idle labourers lifted it for a gallon of Black Bull Entire from Sir Ephraim.
- Reached Yalding at dusk; no fires lit (wood wet, coal not come). Mr Wragg (58, grey, says "Ah", calls her "the printer woman"); Mrs Pickett (keys forty years); two footmen both called John. At the lodge Mrs Fryer (80, man's coat, sack hood, stick) asked if she was the lady in the paper; Winifred said "It is in the Gazette"; "Then it's a lie, my dear, for I can't read it." The formula failed for the first time.
- Julian in Kent: short speech, long listening; knows names and ailments (Jem Fryer's fever, Mrs Pankhurst's youngest, Pankhurst's cow Duchess gone dry). He did NOT use the formula: "Miss Pargeter is a very good printer, and I am more fortunate than I deserve to be seen in her company." Mrs Fryer: "That's better than a newspaper."
- Cart came in at 10 p.m. The Old Lady set up in the old brewhouse (steward's office: copper like a bath, high desk, eleven bundles of leases), block and fall hung from the great beam; Toby sleeps on the settle with Brevier. After midnight Julian stood in the yard with a lantern ten yards from the door and did not come in; two lanterns, neither moved.

## Ch 14: The Weald Relief Subscription
- Wed 31 July, 6 a.m., tithe barn (120 ft, oak and stone): Julian on a hay-cart read the figures: wheat 78s the quarter at Maidstone, quartern loaf 13d, labourer 9s the week, seven loaves 7s 7d, 1s 5d left; nine of eleven farms have no hops; Wragg: "Eleven farms, my lord. Not a third full." Amos Pankhurst of Hope Farm asked what was to be done; Julian: "I do not know yet." He promised nothing.
- Constance proposed the Weald Relief Subscription (to every Gazette reader in Kent and every name in the Kent Directory). Winifred designed the broadside for the parishes of Yalding, Nettlestead and Hunton, ending with the Farmer's sentence from Letter V: "The price of bread is the one arithmetic from which the poor are never excused." A KENTISH FARMER. Subscriptions to Wragg and the Overseers; imprint B. Pargeter. Printed on the Old Lady in the brewhouse.
- Winifred urged Julian to sign the Farmer's letters with his name; he refused for now ("A peer on corn is a landlord pleading for his rents"; "Not yet. I will think of it"). She keeps the name secret.
- The tithe-barn notice had read "G. RAGG, STEWARD" since 1805 (Maidstone printer; the W lost). Wragg's first name is Gideon. Winifred reset it and nailed it up on Thu 1 Aug; Wragg sulked until dusk, touched his hat, thereafter "Miss Pargeter".
- Thu: Orme's letter: Trigg sold his £400 paper account to Lovejoy; the debt is now £1,140 at Michaelmas; "Sparrow is punctual and clean, which I do not like." Barnaby told Winifred not to tell Stanmere (Article 3); she did not.
- Sat 3 Aug: first fire lit in the dining-room (Mrs Pickett dried wood in the bakehouse oven); Pym on the rods; Verity asked how many rods on St Paul's in 1810 and how much it rained ("I should say the rod was a constant"); Rupert and Verity walked about in time in the hall; Agnes at the pianoforte said eleven words all evening.
- Midnight 3/4 Aug: first proof pulled on the Old Lady (Winifred at the bar, Barnaby inking, Toby on the balls; present Julian and Verity); the press runs from Monday. Julian and Winifred stood either side of the stone and did not touch.
- Earlier that evening Winifred took her counterpart of the Articles out of her mother's stick-case lid to look at Julian's thumb smudge at the foot of the sixth Article, and left it on the table beside the case. Agnes came in with a night-candle after they left, counted nine proofs, then the lines of the Articles lying out of its case. (She copies it that night.)

## Ch 15: Rupert in the Hop Garden
- Mon 5 Aug, hop garden at Hope Farm: bines rotted off the poles, Sussex women picking mould for sixpence a day. The Old Lady had pulled 200 Subscriptions since seven.
- Rupert proposed to Winifred honestly: owes £2,300 (£800 to Lovejoy); the Earl wrote that he will pay no more; likes Verity too well to marry for her brewery. He tossed his guinea, then put it away (sign of earnest). Refused: "I will not be a convenient thing twice." "You are a good sort of rascal." He was delighted.
- Rupert told her Lovejoy called at his Albany lodgings a week ago Friday (26 July) and asked whether he had seen "a paper, signed by two parties, in a printer's type"; Rupert said he would think and has instead told her. "A printer does not destroy a record." Rupert: lock it up. Winifred no longer suspects Rupert.
- Julian came up and overheard: "Rupert, are you asking Miss Pargeter to marry you?" "I was." "Did she accept?" "She said I was a good sort of rascal." "Quite." Julian told him to drown himself in the river; "It is already in the river. It is the whole county."
- Julian to Winifred: "Fifty-five days. To Michaelmas. I counted." He said he was extremely jealous. Winifred did not tell Julian about Lovejoy's question about a paper.
- Verity and Rupert seen across the garden quarrelling happily (she laying out prices, he delighted).
- That night Winifred locked the second counterpart under the green baize flap in the lid of her mother's stick-case with its small brass lock; key on a ribbon round her neck. Two nights too late (Agnes copied it on 3 Aug).

## Ch 16: A Wet Day for Forfeits
- Wed 14 Aug, 31st consecutive wet day; Dr Pym's pencil strokes on the library chimney-piece oak (since 14 July); fires lit in August.
- Lovejoy's letter to Agnes at breakfast (first direct one; he knows she is Pry): he has bought Matthias Fell's £312 from Mr Pountney, hosier, of the Borough; he would be sorry to see it called in before Michaelmas; writ need not issue before Mon 19 Aug; Marshal's fee £11; P.S. last Quizzing-Glass was thin in the second paragraph. Lovejoy's hand: small, upright, clean. Agnes's decision is never named; her copy of the Articles (a single foolscap sheet in a law-stationer's round hand, copied 3 Aug in the brewhouse) is in her trunk under three flannel petticoats, unsent.
- Forfeits in the drawing-room from 2.30 p.m.: set "The quick brown fox" in the stick. Barnaby beat Pym (40 s); Verity beat Rupert (51 s v 3 min 10) and holds his gold signet on her thumb; Toby beat Julian (38 s v 2 min 10); Toby's prize is a pie, "I'll wait." Constance (backed Pym) lost her lorgnette; Minton's forfeit was his stopped watch; Lady Quarles swooned at Verity's hands in the type.
- Constance's penalty, to say a thing she regrets: "My hat... The one with the cherries." Her hand shook and she nearly said more; only Agnes noticed.
- Julian and Winifred's penalty (his watch-seal, her left glove): tell how they met. "It was in the Gazette," and, for him alone, "He came to the shop to find out who had been correcting his commas." Room laughed; Julian did not; Rupert looked at the ceiling.
- In the long gallery Agnes began to confess to Constance; Constance lowered the trumpet and said, "You are the only person in this house who never asks me for anything," and went into her room. Agnes sat with the copy on her knee till dinner, then put it back unsent.
- About 9.50 p.m. Wragg came in hatless: the Medway up two feet since dinner, over the Ham, coming up the Beult. Pym: "That will be the rods." Nobody laughed.

## Ch 17: High Water
- Wed 14 Aug, 10.15 p.m., to dawn Thu 15 Aug. The Medway came up the lawn; forty lanterns; cattle brought off the Ham to the home paddock; tithe barn opened; Constance commanded the kitchen; Verity counted 43 persons and a dog at the barn; Pym planted his tide-gauge at the foot of the terrace steps and read it aloud. Rupert and Wragg's hay-boat was swept away at the hedge (Rupert lost his coat, Wragg a boot) but they got a line to the kiln.
- Winifred printed a handbill (Great Primer, two-up, 200 in 40 minutes): THE WATER IS UP. ALL TO HIGHER GROUND. THE CHURCH BELL WILL RING. Barnaby inked, Toby beat the balls, Agnes cut the sheets with scissors; Toby delivered by washtub with Brevier. Jem Fryer (10) read it to his grandmother; the Fryers climbed Fryer's kiln (round oast with white cowl across the ford of the Beult) when the lane flooded.
- On the kiln roof: Mrs Fryer, Jem and Patience (a girl). Barnaby's rig: line from the oak at the ha-ha to the kiln, the Old Lady's block and fall, a traveller, and a sling from her girth-strap. Julian went out on a bowline, brought the children, then Mrs Fryer ("I'll not be put in a basket like a cheese"); the wet line sagged and the current took him under; Winifred cried "Julian!" (the first time she has said his name), waded in, and with Wragg hauled him out by collar and belt. He coughed up the Weald and said "Quite."
- Mrs Fryer kept the wet handbill in her fist: "I can't read it, but Jem read it me. It said the water was up. It was."
- 3 a.m. in the tithe barn: Julian and Winifred held hands under a horse-blanket, nothing said of Michaelmas; she slept (45 days left). Barnaby told Julian over broth he would rather ask the Marquess about the notes (some eleven hundred pounds) than let Win carry it alone; Julian: bound by Article the Third; Barnaby: the Articles are wiser than they look, "I gave her a printing house."
- Dawn: sky a copper stain in the east (no sun disc), floodwater drawn back to the ha-ha with Fryer's kiln standing in it.
- About six, Agnes (up all night: boiled the kettle eleven times, cut four hundred slices of bread and butter) gave the Yalding post-boy (14, pony, sack coat; Yalding post office under three feet of water; mail goes from Wateringbury at seven) a shilling and a sealed packet: one foolscap sheet, directed in a round law-stationer's hand to H. Lovejoy, Esq., Ave Maria Lane. "A little more sin would hardly show."

## Ch 18: The Town Crier Cries
- Sat 31 Aug. The Subscription raised £611 by 22 Aug; flood mark on the tithe barn at the height of Pym's chin; party back in London Thu 22 Aug. Julian called only on Article 2 days, at the far end of the room, with perfect decorum.
- Dick Sparrow did not come in at 1.30 a.m.; Fairweather said "Aye" with weight. Winifred and Fairweather pulled the inner forme two hands short; the cart left at 5.15 with eleven minutes to spare (no breach). Sparrow is presumed at the Phoenix Press.
- The Crier (twopence, hawked from 6-7 a.m. in St Paul's Churchyard): "The Quizzing-Glass" by Peregrine Pry, opening "I may be quite wrong, but"; all seven Articles in Long Primer, imprint changed to "Printed by H. Lovejoy, Phoenix Press, Ave Maria Lane"; the carter's account (Sowerby, unnamed in print): the young lady stood in the arch at a quarter past five with a wet sheet and said nothing and let the cart go. Winifred's counterpart is still locked in the case: the Crier's text was copied, not taken. She does not know who.
- Underhill (9 a.m., hackney, coat buttoned wrong): Clause 9 "requiring" (not "printing") a correction is satisfied; contract void, £500 bond forfeit, secured on the Pargeters' house. Winifred learned of the house security only now; Barnaby: "I meant to tell you on the fourteenth of June. Mrs Hobday came in with the soup." Underhill will not print a correction or write to Mrs Rudge (at Bath for her chest). Orme laid down his stick for the first time since 1764.
- Lovejoy (10 a.m.): the notes are payable on demand under the third clause (£1,140 with Trigg's account); he does not demand and will wait until Michaelmas. "I never print a lie. I print a true thing in a place where it will do the most harm." He examined Tuesday's forme with his glass while Julian and Winifred spoke.
- Julian (10.15, walked from Curzon Street without a hat; Gantry brought the Crier on the salver) offered a special licence from Doctors' Commons by noon Monday. Winifred: "It is what a lord uses to make a mess disappear. I will not be disappeared"; "I am not an erratum to be corrected by matrimony." Julian says "Miss Pargeter", she "my lord" throughout.
- Lovejoy revealed Julian called at Ave Maria Lane on Tue 27 Aug at eleven and offered £2,280 (twice the sum) for Barnaby's paper; refused; this is why the Crier appeared early. Julian admitted it; "I signed it before I—" and could not say the word. Winifred: of seven Articles the third was the one she asked for. She will keep all Articles to Michaelmas and released him from the second; asked not to see him before. He walked the whole length of Cheapside.
- Closing, about 4 p.m.: composing room empty but for Barnaby asleep with the Crier over his face; Orme gone home; the Drip pan not emptied; Winifred holding her mother's stick without setting a line; Toby offered her the larger half of a Mutlow's pie.
