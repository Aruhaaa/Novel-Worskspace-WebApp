# A Wet Day for Forfeits

On the morning of Wednesday the fourteenth of August, Dr Orlando Pym stood at the library chimney-piece at Stanmere Court, where a fire burned, in August, and added with a pencil one stroke to a column of strokes that he had been keeping on the oak since the fourteenth of July, to the quiet horror of Mrs Pickett. There were thirty-one. He regarded them with the sorrow of an honest man who has been proved right and would have preferred not to be. Behind him the library windows ran like the sides of a fish-tank; and beyond them, where in a decent year there would have been a lawn and a ha-ha and the Ham meadows going down to the Medway, lay a grey and enormous wetness in which a few trees stood about like people who had come to the wrong party.

In the breakfast-room, at the end of the table nearest the urn, Miss Agnes Fell opened her letters. There was one. It was directed to *Miss Fell, Stanmere Court, Yalding, Kent*, in a small upright extremely clean hand, each letter standing like a recruit; and she knew the hand, though she had never had a letter in it, for she had seen it in a margin, when a boy from a hole in a wall in Cursitor Street brought her column back with a single word written beside the last line. The word was *More*.

She read it under cover of the toast-rack.

> Madam,—I have lately had the honour to acquire from Mr Pountney, hosier, of the Borough, an obligation of Lieutenant Matthias Fell, half-pay, for three hundred and twelve pounds with interest. I mention it only that you may know in whose hands it lies. I should be sorry to see it called in before Michaelmas, and I do not think I shall have to. The fee of the Marshal of the King's Bench on a commitment is eleven pounds; it falls due when the writ issues; and the writ need not issue before Monday the nineteenth. I am, madam, your obedient servant, H. Lovejoy. P.S. The last Quizzing-Glass was thin in the second paragraph. I do not think it was the fault of the prose.

She folded it in four and put it into the pocket of her gown, with the care of a woman putting away a snake in a handkerchief. For two years she had supposed herself a name in a column. It had not occurred to her that a man who could tell a letter by its foot could tell a writer by her commas; and she sat for a minute with her hands in her lap and her mittens on, at a table where nobody was looking, and the thing she had supposed for two years was taken out of her, quietly, like a tooth.

She did not decide anything. She buttered a piece of toast and ate it, and poured Lady Constance's chocolate, and said "I beg your pardon" to the milk-jug. The narrator, who would like to be fair to Miss Fell, and has been offered the choice of being merciful or accurate, has chosen to be both by declining to say what went on in her head that morning, except that it went through the house from attic to cellar, touching things. There was a sum in it, which was three hundred and twelve and eleven. There was a cough, in a room in the Rules. There was Lady Constance's shawl, which she had held for twelve years, and a pair of mittens, and a candle that burned till two; and there was a thing in the bottom of her trunk upstairs, under three flannel petticoats, folded in four, which she did not call by any name at all. It had not been sent. That was the whole of the case in her favour, and she knew exactly how much it weighed.

* * *

Lady Constance, to whom boredom was a personal insult, struck the drawing-room table with her trumpet at half past two and called for Forfeits.

"The house has been indoors since the thirtieth of July," she said. "This is the thirty-first day of the rain; Dr Pym has it on the chimney. I will not play at Consequences, I am too old to be rude on paper, and I will not play at Charades, I have seen General Dyer attempt a camel. Forfeits. Everyone will give up a trifle."

They gave them up. Verity Quarles, who was the one person in the room who could be trusted to keep an account, took them into Sir Ephraim's hat: a lorgnette, a snuff-box, a watch-seal, a glove, a pencil, a spectacle-case, Lord Rupert's signet, and from Toby Mudge, who had been summoned as an extra and sat on a hassock chewing, a button. Mr Minton, applied to for a trifle, was found to have nothing in his pockets but a stopped watch full of the ditch at Wrotham, which Verity accepted on the ground that it was the one thing in the room that had stopped on principle. It was a long room, with a fire at each end, and the faces of six Marquesses looking down from the panels on a company of persons in various stages of boredom; and the windows streamed. Winifred, appealed to for something to do with her hands, produced from her mother's case two sticks and a handful of Pica, and taught the company in four minutes the first and greatest difficulty of her trade, which is that type is set upside down and backwards and read, as it were, in a looking-glass. The line was to be *The quick brown fox*.

"Quick, madam?" said Dr Pym. "This year no fox in Kent has been quick. They are up to their hocks in the Beult."

"Then set it slowly, Doctor," said Barnaby, with his foot on the stool and a stick in his fist, "but set it."

He did it in forty seconds, talking all the while, of a Psalter he had known in 'seventy-nine, and laid the stick upon the card-table with the air of a man laying a wreath. Dr Pym took two minutes and a quarter, and then, in carrying the line to the table, tipped the stick, so that it went on the Turkey carpet in a fall, which Winifred noted was the first time that summer a page had gone into pie in the presence of a clergyman and nobody had been to blame. Lady Constance, who had backed the Doctor on the ground that a clergyman ought to be able to do something with his hands, forfeited her lorgnette.

Verity set it in fifty-one seconds, counting under her breath, with her large capable hands moving among the case like a person sorting coin. Her mother, who had been sitting very upright in a raspberry shawl, swooned lightly at the sight of those hands among the type, and was brought round by Sir Ephraim with a glass of the product. Rupert took three minutes and ten, set the *x* upside down, and said that it was an improvement; and he staked his signet against her to make the thing interesting, and lost it. She weighed it in her palm. She said it was about four guineas of gold. And she put it upon her thumb, where it rode like a curtain-ring, and he looked at it with an expression that his friends would not have known.

The last pair was Julian and Toby. Toby set the line in thirty-eight seconds, with a pie in his other hand that he had somehow acquired in the interval, and the sorts went into the stick as if called. Julian took two minutes and ten, devoting most of them to the spacing; and he laid down his stick beside the boy's, looked at the two lines side by side, and said, in tones of the deepest satisfaction, "He has beaten me." It was a thing no one in that room had seen the Marquess say before, and several would not have thought possible. "Your Marquessship," said Toby, "the space after *quick* is wide." "I know," said Julian. "I was trying to make it fit."

"What will you have for the prize, Toby?" said Constance, through the trumpet, as one asks a bishop.

"A pie, my lady."

"I have not one about me," said Julian.

"I'll wait," said Toby, and went on chewing the one he had.

* * *

"Heavy, heavy hangs over thy head," said Verity, drawing from the hat the lorgnette, and holding it up. "Fine or superfine?"

"What is the penalty?" said Lady Constance, who was a woman of her word.

"Let her say aloud a thing she regrets," said Rupert from the hearth-rug, with the cheerfulness of a man who has lost a signet and cannot bear that anyone else should be exempt. The room laughed. Lady Constance sat up. She lowered the trumpet to her lap, which in itself was an event. Behind her chair, a pace back, with the shawl over her arm, Agnes saw that the hand that held the mouthpiece was shaking, very slightly, like a leaf in a house where a door has been shut.

"My hat," said Lady Constance.

The room laughed. There was a moment in which she seemed about to say more. Her mouth opened on "And—"; and then she said, "The one with the cherries," and they laughed again, harder, and she took back her lorgnette and sat as straight as a poker and put the trumpet to her ear; and the moment went by on the other side of a hedge, like a coach with its lamps lit.

Verity went in again. "A seal and a glove."

They were Julian's watch-seal and Winifred's left glove. She had taken it off for the type, and her thumb lay in her lap in full view of the room, a little blue. "Heavy, heavy," said Verity, rather quickly. "They are to tell the company how they met," said Rupert, who had no mercy, and she did not look at him.

"It was in the *Gazette*," said Winifred.

The company laughed. It had come to be a joke, and a good one; it was the thing she said, and had been said so often at so many tables that it was thought the cleverest in London. And under cover of the laughter, with her face turned a quarter toward him, she said in a voice not meant for the room: "He came to the shop to find out who had been correcting his commas."

The room laughed. Julian did not. He sat with his long hand on his knee, and the laughter went round him like water round a post; and Rupert, who missed nothing, looked at the ceiling.

* * *

At five they broke up to dress, and Agnes went up the stair a pace behind Lady Constance with the shawl. The long gallery at Stanmere Court is lined with the portraits of Marquesses who were each, in his day, certain of having got matters right. At the end of it she said, "Lady Constance."

The trumpet did not come up. Lady Constance stopped, with her back to her, and stood.

"I beg your pardon," said Agnes. "It is only that there is something I ought to say. I have been meaning to. I have been—" Her hands, in their mittens, had found one another. "I think you ought to know that I—"

She was never to know afterward what her face did. Lady Constance turned and looked at her: a tall rigid woman in black bombazine, with a purple ribbon, and under it a face that had held a house together for forty years by declining to be moved. She looked at a thin woman of forty-one with a streak of white at her temple. What she saw is not recorded.

"You are the only person in this house," said Lady Constance, "who never asks me for anything."

It was said as one states a fact in arithmetic. She went on down the gallery, and her door shut. Agnes stood with the shawl over her arm, in the cold, understanding that she had been handed something, and that she had not the least idea where to put it.

She went up to the attic, which was at the top of the last flight, and took the packet out of the trunk from under three flannel petticoats, and unfolded it on her knee. It was a single sheet of foolscap, in a round law-stationer's hand that was not her own, which she had learned in a year of copying; it had taken her four hours on the night of the third, by a night-candle, in the brewhouse, to get it down to the smudge at the foot of the sixth paragraph, and she had made the smudge too. She sat with it on her knee until the dressing-bell, and then until the dinner-bell; and then, for she was a punctual woman, she put it back in the trunk, and went down.

They dined at eight. There was a leg of mutton, and the Doctor upon the dome; and afterwards in the drawing-room they played Blind Man's Buff, as a game suited to the rain, and the Marquess had been blinded with Verity's handkerchief and was groping toward the sound of Toby's cough when the door opened and Mr Wragg came in, hatless, with the water running off him, and stood on the Turkey carpet.

"My lord. The river's up. Two foot since dinner. It's over the Ham, and it's coming up the Beult at the back."

The game fell over. Julian took off the handkerchief. Nobody spoke, and in the silence the fire spat, and on all the windows the rain went on, which no one had listened to since the thirtieth of July. Dr Pym, who had come in from the library with the pencil still in his hand, said:

"That will be the rods."

And nobody laughed.
